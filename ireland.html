Week of Aug 22 to Aug 29, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Dáil was recalled early from summer recess on Friday to pass a financial resolution deferring planned excise increases on petrol and diesel.
  • The deferral keeps the fuel excise cuts in place to November, tapering out by end-February, at a cost of roughly €430 million to the exchequer.
  • Opposition TDs objected that only an hour was set aside for debate, with Richard Boyd Barrett calling it a “disgrace” and Michael Collins accusing the Government of guillotining the discussion.
  • Commentators framed the fuel and medicines moves as awkward pre-Budget positioning for the Coalition ahead of Budget 2027.
  • The HSE confirmed a reimbursement deal with Biogen for Skyclarys, a Friedreich’s Ataxia treatment, at an estimated €20–30 million.

Economics (Ireland)

  • The Irish Fiscal Advisory Council criticised repeated spending overruns, warning that the eventual Budget 2027 package will likely exceed what is announced on budget day.
  • IFAC chair Seamus Coffey argued the economy is strong enough that it does not need stimulus from the coming budget.
  • Inflation for 2026 is now tracking nearer 3.75%, above the 3.3% spring forecast, while employment is expected to grow about 1.8% this year.
  • The general government surplus projection has been revised up from roughly €5bn to €9bn, intensifying pressure for spending increases.
  • Finance Minister Simon Harris has signalled a personal income tax package is certain in Budget 2027, alongside childcare and energy measures.

Military / Defence Forces

  • The Government continues to push the Defence (Amendment) Bill 2026, which removes the UN Security Council leg of the Triple Lock; enactment is targeted before year end, making it a live issue as the Dáil returns.
  • Under the Bill, overseas deployments of more than 12 personnel would need only Government and Oireachtas approval.
  • Ministers insist Ireland remains militarily unaligned, while the Opposition maintains the change erodes neutrality — expect a substantial Dáil row in September.
  • Ireland’s lapsed participation in Operation IRINI in the Mediterranean remains the Government’s working example of why the mechanism is being changed.
  • Naval Service powers to board suspected Russian “shadow fleet” tankers in Irish waters remain part of the same legislative push.

Technology

  • The Irish Times reported the data centre boom is turning niche Irish specialists into multimillion-euro players, with AI-driven construction demand reshaping the supply chain.
  • Dublin-listed Kingspan agreed to pay up to €900 million for BMC, which makes low-voltage switchgear and power distribution kit for data centres.
  • Dublin’s C&W O’Brien Architects opened a London “data centre hub”; data centres now supply over 30% of turnover, heading toward the low 40s.
  • A recurring warning in the coverage: Irish contractors are increasingly following their multinational clients abroad, so little of the projected AI capex lands here.
  • OpenAI’s previously announced 250 Dublin jobs and move to the Tropical Fruit Warehouse remain on track for late 2026.

Sports

  • John Caulfield began his second spell as Galway United manager with an FAI Cup second-round tie against Shelbourne at Eamonn Deacy Park on Friday night.
  • The GAA Rounders All-Ireland finals were staged on Sunday with live streaming and commentary announced by Galway Bay FM.
  • Galway’s summer remains the story of the county’s year: a first senior All-Ireland women’s football title since 2004, beating Kerry at Croke Park.
  • Cork edged Galway again in the All-Ireland camogie final, 2-14 to 0-19, a third straight final meeting.
  • Club championship action dominated the weekend across Galway and Mayo as the inter-county season wound down and provincial rugby squads returned to pre-season.

Music

  • Electric Picnic takes over Stradbally this weekend, with CMAT and Gorillaz among the headline draws and a heavy Irish presence down the bill.
  • Bray grunge-pop quartet Florence Road are among the most-tipped Irish acts at the festival, off the back of support slots with Olivia Rodrigo and The Last Dinner Party.
  • The Irish Times rounded up new Irish albums from Miriam Ingram, Paahto & the Bull, Shakalak and Macdara Yeates, the last pairing old folk songs with production by Gilla Band’s Daniel Fox.
  • The Script’s album The User’s Guide to Being Human landed on 14 August, with Dublin and Belfast dates to follow in October and November.
  • The Dunfords announced nationwide autumn dates behind debut album The Stone Man, including two nights at Dublin’s Workman’s Club.

Books

  • The Irish Times published its autumn books preview on Saturday — a heavy season for Irish fiction.
  • Sebastian Barry’s The Newer World (10 September) closes a loose American trilogy, following a freed man through post-Civil War Nashville and Victorian England.
  • Louise Kennedy follows Trespasses with Stations (24 September), on emigration, addiction and a fractured family.
  • Dubliner Djamel White remains the sole Irish name on the Booker longlist for debut All Them Dogs; the shortlist is announced on 22 September.

Galway news

  • IKEA opened its new mini-store at Gateway Retail Park, Knocknacarra, on Thursday 27 August.

  • The city’s high-visibility policing plan continues, with up to 13 gardaí a day on foot patrol across seven designated areas from 8am to 4am.
  • Legal challenges to the Galway City Ring Road planning decision remain before the courts.

Kilkelly / East Mayo

  • The developer describes the scheme as a “high-quality, sustainable and attractive residential neighbourhood”, a notable scale of build for a village of Kilkelly’s size.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Jamie McIntyre
Music · Galway

Jamie McIntyre started out busking on Shop Street and is now one of the most-streamed independent artists in the country, entirely without a label behind him. His song “Over Galway Town” — written after a night out with friends — spread through Irish TikTok before it spread anywhere else, racking up tens of millions of video views and millions of Spotify streams on the strength of people filming themselves singing it in pubs and on nights out. That grassroots momentum turned into real rooms: support slots for Gavin James in Paris, work with Imelda May, and sold-out headline dates across Ireland and the UK behind his EP For Folk Sake. What resonates is how specific it is — a song about one city, sung in an unvarnished Galway accent, that turned out to travel further precisely because it never tried to. He remains a fixture on the Galway Folk Festival bill, which is roughly where the whole thing began.

Week of Aug 15 to Aug 22, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Dáil is being recalled early from summer recess, as soon as Friday 28 August, to legislate for the postponement of planned excise duty increases on fuel.
  • Government confirmed the planned excise reinstatements for 1 September and 1 October will not go ahead, citing Middle East instability and its knock-on effect on pump prices.
  • The plan to bar under-18s from e-scooters has been deferred on Attorney General advice that primary legislation is required; mandatory helmets and hi-vis rules still take effect by end of August.
  • Leaving Certificate results landed on 21 August with grade deflation continuing — H1s fell from 11.7% to 10.1% — keeping pressure on the Department of Education over the pace of the post-Covid unwind. CAO first-round offers come at 2pm on 26 August.

Economics (Ireland)

  • The fuel excise pause is costing the exchequer roughly €100 million a month, a live constraint on the Budget arithmetic.
  • Budget 2027 is shaping up as the tightest in years: day-to-day and capital spending rises by €7bn to €125.5bn, with an €8.5bn package including €1.5bn in tax measures.
  • Income tax cuts and further childcare fee reductions remain the two near-certainties for October, with Finance Minister Harris "crystal clear" on a personal tax package.
  • Inflation is running near 3.3–3.5% for 2026, with the Central Bank warning of 5% in 2027 under a severe scenario. Employment growth is moderating to about 1.8% this year as job ads soften.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Ireland has named its first-ever defence attachés, colonel-rank officers headed for London, Paris and Washington DC.
  • The officers take up their posts this autumn, working alongside Ireland's diplomatic missions on defence diplomacy and strategic engagement.
  • Chief of Staff Lt Gen Rossa Mulcahy welcomed the appointments as a step up in cooperation with partner armed forces; the network was approved by Cabinet in March at the request of Minister Helen McEntee.
  • Background: the attaché network sits alongside the wider modernisation programme, including the purchase of hundreds of French armoured vehicles and the first National Maritime Security Strategy.

Technology

  • Ireland now has 107 data centres operating with nine more under construction, supporting about 19,500 jobs — roughly 60% of them in construction — and the debate over grid capacity shows no sign of cooling.
  • Alibaba is hiring IT and asset managers in Dublin for its first Irish data centre, having registered its first Irish company.
  • Jobs news: NTT Data is adding 50 roles in Dublin, while Australian crypto platform CoinJar plans 30 Irish jobs on a €5m European hub.
  • Equinix has begun work on a $92m Dublin facility, and a west Dublin off-grid site run by Pure Data Centres is now buying biomethane to power its own on-site plant.
  • On the homegrown side, GAA streaming startup Clubber took a strategic investment from Mediahuis Ireland this month, giving the grassroots-broadcast platform a national media backer.

Sports

  • Club championship season is in full swing across the counties following an intercounty calendar that finished unusually early — the All-Ireland hurling final was played on 19 July, Limerick beating Galway 1-29 to 1-18 for a sixth Liam MacCarthy in nine seasons.
  • Galway's camogie side came up just short earlier this month, losing the All-Ireland final to Cork 2-14 to 0-19 in front of a record 33,419 at Croke Park — the third Cork–Galway final in a row.
  • Galway's footballers had earlier claimed bragging rights over Mayo in both the Connacht senior final and the TG4 All-Ireland series, setting the tone for a strong Tribe year.
  • Rugby is in the quiet weeks before the new United Rugby Championship campaign, with Connacht back in pre-season after reaching last season's playoffs.

Music

  • The Script released their new album The User's Guide to Being Human on 14 August, with Irish shows to follow.
  • Electric Picnic closes out the month at Stradbally with CMAT and Gorillaz among the headline draws.
  • RTÉ Culture published a well-timed guide to the 10 Irish bands worth seeing live right now, a useful map of where the scene is at heading into autumn.
  • Cork's Cardinals continue to draw praise for debut album Masquerade, while Dublin six-piece Thumper line up Irish dates in September behind Sleeping with the Light On.

Books

  • Dubliner Djamel White is the only Irish writer on the 2026 Booker longlist, and at 28 the youngest author on it, for his debut All Them Dogs.
  • The novel follows a young gangster returning to west Dublin after five years in England; judges called it "mouthy, ballsy and bracingly unsentimental".
  • White, a former tattoo-shop manager, told The Journal that "if I was trying to write a book for everyone, I'd write a sh*t book". The shortlist lands 22 September; the winner is announced 9 November.
  • Elsewhere, Helen Macdonald gave a striking Irish Times interview, and the International Booker prize fund has doubled.

Galway news

  • Minister of State Seán Canney said the State "made mistakes" in handling the 54 homes under compulsory purchase for the Galway City Ring Road, with families saying they have been in limbo for 13 years.
  • National Heritage Week wrapped up today with more than 150 free walks, talks, exhibitions and concerts staged across the city and county.
  • Gardaí seized close to €250,000 worth of drugs in two raids in Galway city, with two people arrested.
  • Galway County Council and the Local Sports Partnership open public consultation on a new Local Sports Plan, with meetings from 25–27 August in Oughterard, Ballinasloe, Gort, Athenry and Tuam.

Kilkelly news (east Mayo)

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Florence Road
Music · Bray, Co Wicklow

Four friends who met at school on Florence Road in Bray and started writing songs in a garden shed are now the Irish band everyone is quietly betting on. Lily Aron, Emma Brandon, Ailbhe Barry and Hannah Kelly built their audience the slow organic way — fish-eye phone videos and covers on TikTok — before the 2025 single "Heavy" turned casual scrollers into a fanbase. That momentum carried them onto the BBC Sound of 2026 longlist, then to a sold-out UK headline tour and their first American dates supporting The Last Dinner Party, with public nods from Olivia Rodrigo and beabadoobee along the way. What makes the story unusual is the order of it: they have a mixtape and two EPs, arena-sized support slots and a Rolling Stone profile — and still no debut album. For a scene used to exporting singer-songwriters, a teenage guitar band from a Wicklow commuter town going global on their own footage is the thing Irish music fans keep talking about.
