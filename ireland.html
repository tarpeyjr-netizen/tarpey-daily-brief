Week of Sat 3 October to Sat 10 October, 2026

Politics

  • Tánaiste and Finance Minister Simon Harris and Jack Chambers delivered Budget 2027 in the Dáil on Tuesday, with Harris saying it sets the country on a path forward.
  • Opposition criticism was sharp, with Labour leader Ivana Bacik calling the budget “pathetic”.
  • The Irish Times reported the Minister playing down tension with Chambers over the budget.

Economics

  • Budget 2027 is an €8.5bn package, with a larger tax package than expected (about €1.65bn). The standard-rate band rises €2,500 to €46,500 and main credits rise €125.
  • Other measures: weekly welfare up €10, childcare fees capped at €550 a month, and capital gains tax cut from 33% to 31%. The new-home help-to-buy grant rises €5,000, but first-time-buyer stamp duty on second-hand homes was not scrapped.
  • The CSO says 149,500 were unemployed in September, with the rate steady at 5%. Annual inflation was 3.7% in August.

Military / Defence Forces

  • No major new Defence Forces announcements surfaced this week in the sources searched.
  • Background: a September report said the Defence Forces face budget pressure affecting rations and naval radar plans, with ministers seeking targeted support in Budget 2027.
  • The planned renaming of the Air Corps and Naval Service to the Irish Air Force and Irish Navy remains part of the reform agenda.

Technology

  • The Irish Examiner looks at how Irish firms are taking the data centre boom around the world.
  • The debate over data centres continues: they use over a fifth of metered electricity while employing relatively few people directly, and the economic value claimed for them is contested.
  • An International AI Summit is listed for Dublin on 14 October.

Sports

  • Ireland drew 1-1 with Israel on 4 October in a game marred by tempers and an alleged spitting incident. Heimir Hallgrímsson was relieved the difficult window is over; Ireland also lost to Kosovo.
  • Hallgrímsson has argued that Uefa should keep Ireland and Israel apart in future competitions.
  • No Connacht, GAA or Galway club results were found in this week’s search.

Music

  • Four Men and a Dog play the Irish Cultural Centre on 10 October, and Tommy Sands and Bernadette Devlin McAliskey appear there on 22 October.
  • Tulua’s In The Snug album launch tour ran through the summer, including a Róisín Dubh date in Galway.
  • No confirmed major Irish album release for October turned up in this week’s search.

Books

  • The John McGahern Annual Book Prize winner will be announced on 18 October. The shortlist is Gethan Dick, Seán Farrell and Liadan Ní Chuinn, and Colm Tóibín picks the winner.
  • Frank Shovlin’s authorised biography John McGahern: A Writing Life is due from Faber this month.
  • Dubliner Djamel White’s debut All Them Dogs was on the Booker Prize 2026 longlist; the winner is announced in November.

Galway news

  • Galway Bay FM reports that HSE West is warning of significant service disruption during next week’s strikes, with nurses also protesting at University Hospital Galway.
  • Galway Bay FM also ran a story on men who planned a Galway mosque attack being radicalised by anti-immigrant groups.
  • The Baboró children’s arts festival runs 9–18 October, with the Galway Comedy Festival on 20–26 October and Halloween events to follow. Leisureland pool is temporarily closed for repairs, and a status yellow rain warning applied.

Kilkelly news

  • Nothing current from Kilkelly itself. The Western People’s east Mayo local notes remain the best source.
  • A Killasser tractor run on 5 October thanked its sponsors, including a Charlestown business.
  • Work on the 19th Kilkelly Kilmovee parish magazine is under way, with submissions invited.

Tarpey news

Week of Sat 26 September to Sat 3 October, 2026

Politics

  • Ireland's Budget 2027 will be presented to the Dáil on Tuesday 6 October by Tánaiste and Finance Minister Simon Harris and Jack Chambers.
  • The Dáil spent the week on the Strategic Lawsuits against Public Participation Bill 2026 and a motion on public transport fare increases.
  • France's finance minister, in Dublin for EU talks under Ireland's Council presidency, said Ireland should share its tech tax billions.

Economics

  • The CSO reported inflation rising to 3.9% in September, driven by energy prices.
  • Bank of Ireland expects inflation to peak around 4% this year, with AI-related investment buoying GDP forecasts of 3.1% growth in 2027.
  • Budget 2027 is expected to be an €8.5 billion package: about €7bn extra spending and €1.5bn of tax cuts.
  • The ESRI's quarterly report says the AI boom meets an energy squeeze, pulling the economy in two directions.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Irish Army personnel deployed to California, Orlando and New York, reported 27 September.
  • Commentary on 23 September questioned whether bigger defence budgets actually improve Ireland's defence capability.
  • Nothing further of note surfaced this week; earlier stories covered idle armoured vehicles and a new Defence Forces Training Centre commander.

Technology

  • Stripe will add 200 jobs at its Dublin headquarters, RTÉ reports; it now employs over 1,000 in Ireland, up more than 35% this year.
  • Dublin hosts the International AI Summit on 14 October, part of Ireland's EU presidency, with OpenAI, Microsoft, Cisco and others expected.
  • Computer services exports keep rising, suggesting Irish exporters are well placed for continued data-centre investment.

Books
