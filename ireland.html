Week of Aug 9 to Aug 16, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Fianna Fáil–Fine Gael coalition under Taoiseach Micheál Martin is in its summer recess, with the Dáil due back ahead of Budget 2027 on 6 October, to be delivered by Tánaiste and Finance Minister Simon Harris with Jack Chambers on public expenditure.
  • Government signals this week suggested the timetable for restoring excise cuts on fuel may be revisited again, a lingering consequence of April's fuel protests and elevated energy prices.
  • The vast majority of the Fianna Fáil parliamentary party signed a letter raising "serious concerns" about a HSE recommendation to refuse funding for the Friedreich's ataxia drug Skyclarys — an unusual show of backbench pressure on a health decision.
  • Gardaí reported hate crimes and hate-related incidents rose 15% last year, adding fuel to opposition calls for faster implementation of hate crime legislation.
  • Sinn Féin continued to press for Irish unity planning to be a central strand of Ireland's forthcoming EU Council presidency preparations.

Economics (Ireland)

  • Inflation forecasts have been revised upward on the back of Middle East energy disruption, with CPI now expected to average around 3.3% in 2026 before easing towards 2.6% in 2027.
  • Home heating oil costs remain the standout pressure point, having risen roughly 63% — about treble the European average — keeping Ireland among the higher-inflation euro area economies.
  • Unemployment ticked up to about 5%, suggesting job creation is moderating after several years of expansion, though employment growth stays positive at roughly 1.5%.
  • The Department of Finance revised its projected general government surplus up to €9.2 billion for 2026, giving headroom for further energy supports if needed.
  • Headline GDP is forecast to contract this year after 2025's distorted 12.3% surge, a reminder of how multinational accounting flatters and then flatters-in-reverse the national accounts.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Defence Minister Helen McEntee announced Ireland's first-ever defence attachés, to be posted to the embassies in Washington DC, London and Paris.
  • The three posts, understood to be at Colonel rank, were filled through an internal Defence Forces selection process and are described as "a significant milestone in strengthening Ireland's international defence engagement".
  • The move is part of a broader push to raise Ireland's defence diplomacy profile as European security cooperation deepens, and follows sustained debate over recruitment and retention shortfalls.
  • Ongoing recruitment remains the core structural challenge, with establishment strength still well below the target set out in the Commission on the Defence Forces roadmap.

Technology

  • 2026 is on track to be the worst year for Irish tech lay-offs in at least five years, with cuts increasingly driven by the largest multinationals rather than start-ups.
  • Meta's 350 Dublin job losses, up to 720 roles at risk at contractor Covalen, TikTok's 300 termination offers and Microsoft's global 2.1% cut have all landed on Irish payrolls this year.
  • An Irish Times opinion piece this week argued Irish tech workers are learning the hard way about the risks of an economy so concentrated in a handful of US employers.
  • Underneath the multinational churn, the indigenous base holds up: roughly 1,468 active start-ups, about 28 per 100,000 people, and six unicorns.
  • AI restructuring is the stated rationale for much of the cutting — TikTok explicitly tied its Dublin changes to supporting "rapid business growth in the GenAI space".

🔥 Trending in Ireland
MOIO — "Moments"
Music · Dublin

MOIO is a Nigerian-Irish singer, producer and multi-instrumentalist from Dublin whose 2023 song "Moments" has become one of the most improbable Irish streaming stories of the year. The track's revival began with nothing more engineered than a bedroom clip of MOIO himself vibing to his own song on TikTok, which racked up over 6.7 million views and dragged a two-year-old release back into circulation. From there it went to No. 1 on Spotify's Viral 50 in seven countries — the US, UK, Ireland, Canada, Norway, Denmark and New Zealand — and past 25 million streams. Musically it is a genuine hybrid: 2000s indie-rock guitars in the vein of The Killers, layered over the moody introspection of Kid Cudi-era hip-hop, which is a large part of why it travels so easily across playlists. What makes it resonate at home is that it is entirely self-made and unmistakably Dublin, with MOIO writing, producing and performing the whole thing himself and talking openly about wanting Black Irish kids to see someone who looks like them making music on their own terms.
