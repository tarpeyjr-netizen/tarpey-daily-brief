Week of Sat 19 to Sat 25, 2026

Politics

  • The Dáil has risen for the summer recess, but a row over the US Ambassador's visit to a controversial venue has kept tempers hot, with the Rape Crisis Centre warning it will fuel protests planned around President Trump's September visit to Ireland.
  • Former Taoiseach Leo Varadkar waded into the row, saying the Rape Crisis Centre "spoke for a lot of people in the country."
  • Health Minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill drew praise in mid-year assessments for holding firm with Rotunda Hospital consultants on the public-only contract.
  • Ireland continues preparations to host the Ninth European Political Community Summit in November during its EU Council Presidency.

Economics

  • Bank of Ireland lifted its domestic growth forecast to 3.5% for this year and 2.5% for 2027, though inflation and softening consumer sentiment are expected to weigh on the second half.
  • PwC pencilled in a 2.5% headline GDP decline this year, cautioning that GDP remains distorted by multinational IP and aircraft-leasing transactions.
  • Just under 40,000 homes are on track to be built this year, up from 36,000 in 2025.
  • Oil trading near $90 a barrel, down from a post-Iran-war peak near $118, has eased inflation pressure.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Minister McEntee welcomed new statutory powers for the Defence Forces coming into effect on 24 July, alongside the reappointment of the Ombudsman for the Defence Forces.
  • The Department of Defence published its Strategy Statement 2026-2029 on 16 July, setting priorities for the force over the coming three years.
  • Brigadier General Louis Flynn has been appointed General Officer Commanding of the Defence Forces Training Centre.
  • Personnel numbers remain strained: establishment strength sits just over 9,700, roughly 2,000 short of target and 3,800 below the Commission on the Defence Forces' 2028 recommendation.

Technology

  • Intel confirmed a $5.7 billion AI-driven investment to upgrade its Leixlip campus, boosting Intel 3 wafer output and linking it to other plants on site.
  • IDA Ireland logged 190 new investments in H1 2026, with Novo Nordisk and Qualcomm leading a €600m+ tech and life-sciences surge; Qualcomm alone is putting €125m into a Cork AI hub.
  • Foreign multinationals committed to 10,400 new jobs in H1 2026, a slight rise on last year, with North American firms still dominant.
  • Ireland's startup base has grown to 2,099 companies employing 45,652 people, over half of the jobs now based outside Dublin.

Sports

  • The 2026 All-Ireland Senior Football Championship nears its conclusion, with Kerry, Armagh and Roscommon among the provincial champions still in contention.
  • The 2026 All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship wrapped its provincial rounds on 19 July ahead of the knockout stages.
  • Ireland's rugby underage side finished ninth at the U20 World Championship with a win over Italy, while the senior side eyes a first-ever win over the All Blacks at Eden Park.
  • The 2026 All-Island Cup, pooling clubs from both sides of the border, reached its final on 18 July.

Music

  • Dermot Kennedy became the first Irish solo artist to headline Aviva Stadium, playing two nights on 11 and 12 July with Amble and Aaron Rowe supporting.
  • Bon Jovi is set to play Aviva Stadium on 26 July, closing out a busy month of stadium shows.
  • Waterford's Heat Wave festival ran 24-25 July headlined by Moncrieff, with a rising-star stage spotlighting local bands.
  • Irish trad-fusion group Cara released their new album "Our Ship is Ready," blending Irish, Scottish, English and Balkan traditions.

Books

  • The 2026 Booker Prize longlist is due Tuesday, with speculation swirling over whether an Irish author will make the cut after Claire Adam was the sole Irish name last year.
  • The John McGahern Annual Book Prize shortlist was unveiled, celebrating the year's best Irish debuts.
  • Liadan Ní Chuinn's "Every One Still Here" was shortlisted for the Orwell Prize for Political Fiction and the Sunday Times Young Writer of the Year award.

Galway

  • A row erupted in Ballinasloe over poor-quality grasscutting, with a councillor proposing the contract be scrapped in favour of direct council staff.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Dove Ellis
Music · Ireland

Dove Ellis is the Irish singer-songwriter behind the debut album Blizzard, a dreamy, '70s-influenced indie record that's built momentum entirely from the ground up. Rather than a label push, her rise has come from an organic cult following and constant word-of-mouth chatter online, the kind of grassroots buzz that tastemaker outlets are now flagging as a breakout signal for 2026. It fits a wider shift in Irish music this year: fans burned out on polished, algorithm-driven pop are gravitating toward artists who feel raw and unfiltered, and Ellis's slow-building, listener-led spread is being cited as a blueprint for how an Irish indie act can go from niche to festival-ready without a major marketing budget.

Week of Sat 12 to Sat 18, 2026

Politics

  • The Dáil rose for summer recess after a final round of Leaders' Questions and statements on the cost of a united Ireland; Taoiseach Micheál Martin bid the chamber farewell until autumn.
  • Martin said he is "leaning towards" a full ban on e-scooters after a string of deaths and serious injuries prompted debate in the Dáil.
  • Minister for Justice Jim O'Callaghan is holding firm in a dispute over criminal legal aid solicitor fees, with more than 100 solicitors threatening to resign from the panel.
  • Figures given to the Public Accounts Committee showed the State paid over €1 million for a single deportation flight returning 42 people to South Africa in June.
  • Sinn Féin has opened disciplinary proceedings against a member who publicly criticised the party's selection of Galway West candidate Mark Lohan.

Economics

  • CSO figures show the domestic economy grew 4.7% in 2025 on the modified domestic demand measure, with GDP up 8%.
  • The economy contracted 7.1% in the first quarter of 2026 as a tariff-driven export surge from late 2025 unwound.
  • PwC expects growth to settle at a "steadier" pace for the rest of 2026 as global risks weigh on the outlook.
  • The Central Bank raised its inflation forecasts through 2028, citing energy and food price pressure tied to Middle East volatility since the US-Israeli strikes on Iran.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Ukraine has rejected an Irish offer of 27 mothballed light tactical armoured vehicles; they may now be scrapped.
  • 344 Irish troops are training ahead of deployment to Lebanon with UNIFIL, described as the end of an era as the mission's mandate winds down next year.
  • Minister for Defence Helen McEntee says anti-drone technology will be in place by next summer, with a radar system to follow by 2028.

Technology

  • The Irish unit of US software firm PTC paid just $151,000 in tax after pre-tax profit soared to $240 million.
  • A survey found 70% of US multinationals' headquarters plan further Irish investment over five years, with two-thirds planning to boost AI and automation spend.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Pale Harbour
Music · Enniscorthy, Co. Wexford

Pale Harbour, a band from Enniscorthy, have become one of the most talked-about names in Ireland's indie rock scene after recording their debut LP in a converted shed outside town and releasing it on Faction Records, a small Dublin imprint that has quietly become the most-watched label in the country. The record's DIY origin story — homemade, low-budget, made far from any major studio — has resonated with listeners tired of over-produced pop, and it has since picked up coverage in the UK and continental European press. Faction's willingness to back unproven acts is part of a wider pattern this year: a genuine cluster of small Irish bands rising together rather than a single manufactured breakout, which is part of why the momentum feels organic rather than industry-engineered.
