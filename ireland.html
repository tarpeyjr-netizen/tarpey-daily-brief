Week of Sat 29 Aug to Sat 5 Sep, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Dáil remains in summer recess and is not due back until Wednesday 16 September, leaving the week to committee prep and pre-budget positioning.
  • Budget 2027 has been fixed for 6 October, to be delivered by Tánaiste and Minister for Finance Simon Harris — the second budget of the Fianna Fáil–Fine Gael coalition led by Taoiseach Micheál Martin.
  • Ireland's EU Council presidency dominated the political diary, with an informal meeting of EU defence ministers hosted at Druids Glen in Co Wicklow.
  • Pre-budget lobbying intensified across housing, childcare and cost-of-living groups, with ministers signalling a tighter package than last year given slower growth forecasts.

Economics (Ireland)

  • Inflation is running at roughly 3%, pushed up again in August by energy prices tied to renewed Middle East conflict; the Department of Finance has flagged a full-year figure as high as 3.75%.
  • The Central Bank has warned that in a severe scenario inflation could reach 5% in 2027, a caution now feeding directly into budget arithmetic.
  • Employment growth is moderating: job advertisements have fallen and unemployment has edged up, though the overall workforce continues to expand.
  • The exchequer position remains strong, with a surplus of around €9.2bn forecast for 2026, but economists again warned about concentration risk in corporation tax.
  • Analysis this week highlighted that foreign-owned multinationals now account for close to €3 of every €10 the State collects in tax and PRSI.

Military / Defence Forces

  • A Royal Navy warship and a submarine-hunting helicopter were deployed off Co Wicklow at Irish request to provide air cover for the EU defence ministers' meeting.
  • The deployment renewed scrutiny of Ireland's lack of any indigenous long-range air defence capability, with the State again relying on a partner military for cover.
  • Counter-drone technology promised in advance of the EU presidency has slipped once more, with former TD and Army Ranger Wing officer Cathal Berry calling the timeline "a farce".
  • Broader reporting continued on delays and half-funded procurement plans across the Defence Forces transformation programme.

Technology

  • Salesforce's $3.6bn acquisition of Fin (formerly Intercom) remains the reference point for Irish scale-up ambition, and continued to shape sector commentary this week.
  • Technology Ireland launched its 34th annual industry awards across 11 categories, with winners to be named on 20 November and the EU presidency framing the sector as central to Europe's digital competitiveness.
  • Tax analysts revisited Ireland's multinational dependency, noting the share of income tax and VAT receipts originating in foreign-owned tech, pharma and financial services.
  • Domestic coverage focused on how US tech employers — Google, Meta, Salesforce — have reset pay, hybrid working and hiring norms well beyond the tech sector itself.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Clubber
Tech / Sport · Ireland (GAA clubs nationwide)

Clubber is a homegrown streaming platform built for the least glamorous and most passionately followed sport in the country: club GAA. Founded by Jimmy Doyle, it puts cheap, reliable cameras and a broadcast pipeline into the hands of ordinary clubs, so a junior championship quarter-final in a field in Mayo can be watched by emigrants in Boston or Perth for the price of a pint. The momentum has been entirely organic — club by club, county board by county board, driven by diaspora demand and by parents who cannot get to every game. That grassroots traction turned commercial in August when Mediahuis Ireland took a significant stake in the company, a deal read across the sector as validation of Clubber's ambition to move beyond GAA into other sports and export the model abroad. It sits alongside a small cluster of Irish grassroots sports-tech firms, including Dublin's Anyscor live-scoring app, betting that community-level sport deserves the same digital experience as the professional game.

Week of Aug 22 to Aug 29, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Dáil was recalled early from summer recess on Friday to pass a financial resolution deferring planned excise increases on petrol and diesel.
  • The deferral keeps the fuel excise cuts in place to November, tapering out by end-February, at a cost of roughly €430 million to the exchequer.
  • Opposition TDs objected that only an hour was set aside for debate, with Richard Boyd Barrett calling it a “disgrace” and Michael Collins accusing the Government of guillotining the discussion.
  • Commentators framed the fuel and medicines moves as awkward pre-Budget positioning for the Coalition ahead of Budget 2027.
  • The HSE confirmed a reimbursement deal with Biogen for Skyclarys, a Friedreich’s Ataxia treatment, at an estimated €20–30 million.

Economics (Ireland)

  • The Irish Fiscal Advisory Council criticised repeated spending overruns, warning that the eventual Budget 2027 package will likely exceed what is announced on budget day.
  • IFAC chair Seamus Coffey argued the economy is strong enough that it does not need stimulus from the coming budget.
  • Inflation for 2026 is now tracking nearer 3.75%, above the 3.3% spring forecast, while employment is expected to grow about 1.8% this year.
  • The general government surplus projection has been revised up from roughly €5bn to €9bn, intensifying pressure for spending increases.
  • Finance Minister Simon Harris has signalled a personal income tax package is certain in Budget 2027, alongside childcare and energy measures.

Military / Defence Forces

  • The Government continues to push the Defence (Amendment) Bill 2026, which removes the UN Security Council leg of the Triple Lock; enactment is targeted before year end, making it a live issue as the Dáil returns.
  • Under the Bill, overseas deployments of more than 12 personnel would need only Government and Oireachtas approval.
  • Ministers insist Ireland remains militarily unaligned, while the Opposition maintains the change erodes neutrality — expect a substantial Dáil row in September.
  • Ireland’s lapsed participation in Operation IRINI in the Mediterranean remains the Government’s working example of why the mechanism is being changed.
  • Naval Service powers to board suspected Russian “shadow fleet” tankers in Irish waters remain part of the same legislative push.

Technology

  • Dublin-listed Kingspan agreed to pay up to €900 million for BMC, which makes low-voltage switchgear and power distribution kit for data centres.
  • Dublin’s C&W O’Brien Architects opened a London “data centre hub”; data centres now supply over 30% of turnover, heading toward the low 40s.
  • A recurring warning in the coverage: Irish contractors are increasingly following their multinational clients abroad, so little of the projected AI capex lands here.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Jamie McIntyre
Music · Galway

Jamie McIntyre started out busking on Shop Street and is now one of the most-streamed independent artists in the country, entirely without a label behind him. His song “Over Galway Town” — written after a night out with friends — spread through Irish TikTok before it spread anywhere else, racking up tens of millions of video views and millions of Spotify streams on the strength of people filming themselves singing it in pubs and on nights out. That grassroots momentum turned into real rooms: support slots for Gavin James in Paris, work with Imelda May, and sold-out headline dates across Ireland and the UK behind his EP For Folk Sake. What resonates is how specific it is — a song about one city, sung in an unvarnished Galway accent, that turned out to travel further precisely because it never tried to. He remains a fixture on the Galway Folk Festival bill, which is roughly where the whole thing began.
