Week of Sat 1 to Sat 8 Aug, 2026

Politics

  • Attention shifts to Budget 2027, now confirmed for 6 October, with the coalition under pressure to keep a lid on spending even as tax receipts run hot.
  • Preparations continue for Ireland's EU Council presidency, including hosting the 9th European Political Community Summit in November.
  • Housing remains the dominant domestic fault line, with Sinn Féin pushing its 300,000-home plan and the government defending delivery numbers against opposition attacks.
  • Commentary this week revisited the coalition maths, with the Irish Times arguing Sinn Féin's Northern stance still points to Fianna Fáil as its natural partner.

Economics

  • Exchequer figures show tax receipts continuing to roll in, with multinationals paying an extra €1bn in corporation tax in July under the new 15% minimum rate.
  • Unemployment is anchored near a historic low of 5%, though youth unemployment has crept up to almost 14% as AI-exposed sectors thin out.
  • The Irish Times flagged poor productivity in domestic business, particularly small construction firms, as a drag on the wider economy.
  • Bank of Ireland is still forecasting 3.5% domestic growth this year, helped by AI-driven data-centre spend and Eli Lilly / Novo Nordisk expansions.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Ireland has appointed its first-ever defence attachés to London, Paris and Washington, a significant shift in Irish defence diplomacy signed off by Minister Helen McEntee.
  • The new colonel-rank posts are framed as part of a broader push to modernise the Defence Forces and deepen bilateral security ties with key partners.
  • The Ombudsman's earlier criticism of the volume of internal Defence Forces complaints continues to shape recruitment and culture reforms this year.

Technology

  • Analysis this week highlighted just how central foreign firms are: roughly €1 in every €5 of income tax, USC, PRSI and VAT now comes from foreign-owned manufacturing, tech and financial services.
  • The tech sector accounts for 9.6% of wages, 6.4% of employment and roughly a quarter of corporation tax receipts, underlining the concentration risk.
  • RTÉ Brainstorm warned that AI's business problems could be bad news for Irish jobs, with employment in high-AI-risk roles down 20% among under-30s since 2023.
  • The Irish Times argued the enduring pull for US multinationals is now clustering, not just tax, four decades into the FDI story.

Sports

  • Mayo's surprise run to the 2026 All-Ireland Senior Football Championship final is dominating GAA conversation across Connacht.
  • At club level, U19 A North Football Championship fixtures rolled on, including Corofin v Claregalway earlier in the week.
  • The Galway United season continues with some home fixtures being staged at Pearse Stadium while drainage works finish at Eamonn Deacy Park.
  • Connacht Rugby are ramping up pre-season at the Sportsground as the new campaign gets under way.

Music

  • The Irish Times event guide flags Tom Jones and Sepultura among the big gigs this week, alongside a strong Kilkenny Arts Festival programme.
  • Kilkenny Arts Festival's music strand runs Gráda, Caroline Keane, Rattling Ark and Emma Langford across the coming days.
  • RTÉ is leaning into arts coverage this August, with new commissions and live broadcasts from festivals nationwide.
  • The National Concert Hall's 2026/27 classical season also launched this week, with a heavy Irish presence in the opening months.

Books

  • Dubliner Djamel White remains the story of the summer after his debut All Them Dogs made the 2026 Booker Prize longlist — at 28 he's the youngest name on the list.
  • Sally Rooney's Intermezzo and Niamh Ní Mhaoleoin's Ordinary Saints are both still in contention for the €100,000 Dublin Literary Award.
  • The new Nero New Writers Prize, launched with Brunel University of London, is now open to unpublished writers in Ireland and Britain.
  • The John McGahern Book Prize shortlist continues to spotlight strong Irish debuts ahead of its autumn award.

Galway

Tarpey

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Jamie McIntyre — “Over Galway Town”
Music · Galway

Galway singer-songwriter Jamie McIntyre is having the breakout summer of 2026 with his single “Over Galway Town”, a folk-tinged love letter to the city that has quietly snowballed into a genuine national moment. The track has crossed 2.5 million Spotify streams and briefly sat alongside global superstars on Ireland's daily chart, driven almost entirely by word-of-mouth, TikTok covers from Galway pubs, and heavy rotation on regional radio rather than major-label marketing. Its rise mirrors the wider grassroots wave of Irish indie acts — Curtisy, Dove Ellis, MOIO — who are turning small-town origin stories into international streaming numbers, and it has cemented Galway's status this year as the country's most quietly influential music town.

Week of Sat 25 to Sat Aug 1, 2026

Politics (Ireland)

  • The Dáil rose for summer recess until mid-September, capping a tumultuous first half of the year for the coalition.
  • Ministers face decisions during the break on fuel excise rates and the future of the Aughinish Alumina plant in Co Limerick, plus several other sticky issues.
  • Next week's Summer Economic Statement, one of the year's key fiscal documents, is due despite the recess, alongside two Cabinet meetings.
  • Fine Gael's poll numbers have steadied after a byelection win, while leadership speculation within Fianna Fáil has eased.

Economics (Ireland)

  • Bank of Ireland is forecasting 3.5% domestic growth, with inflation revised down to 3.1% this year as oil prices ease.
  • Tech sector employment has contracted 11% over the past year even as overall employment growth is expected to hold at 1.1%.
  • Housing completions are projected to rise to 39,600 this year, still well short of the 50,000–60,000 units economists say are needed annually.
  • Asking prices outside Dublin rose 5.4% in Q2, outpacing the capital's 4.5% increase.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Government has cleared the way to acquire long-range military radar, with first systems due next year and full capability by end-2028.
  • Ireland will deploy its first defence attachés to the US, Britain and France.
  • A tribunal is to examine allegations of abuse and misconduct raised by a group of women Defence Forces veterans.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Florence Road (Flo Ro)
Music · Ireland

Florence Road, an Irish four-piece known as Flo Ro, built a following the modern way — posting viral TikTok covers that turned into a genuine fanbase before labels came calling. The organic traction led to a signing with Warner Records in 2024, and the band has since become a fixture on Irish festival bills and toured alongside major acts. Their rise mirrors a wider resurgence of guitar music in Ireland this year, with venues from Limerick basements to main festival stages backing homegrown, unpolished acts over polished pop. It's a reminder that in 2026, an Irish band's biggest stage is often a phone screen before it's a festival field.
