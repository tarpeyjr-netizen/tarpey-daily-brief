Week of Nov 07 to Nov 14, 2026

Politics

  • Dublin hosted the European Political Community summit on 12 November, the showcase event of Ireland's EU Council presidency, after threatened public-sector strikes were averted.
  • The opposition attacked the summit's cost, saying the Government had "rolled out the red carpet" while households struggle; Micheál Martin and Mary Lou McDonald traded "despicable" barbs in the Dáil.
  • Housing remained the flashpoint, with the Social Democrats' Rory Hearne and the Taoiseach exchanging bitter accusations, and the Soc Dems tabling a motion on public transport fare rises.
  • The Government is backing a proposed law that would specifically criminalise drink spiking; TheJournal's winners and losers rounds up the week.

Economics

  • Inflation hit a three-year high of 4.3% in October, up from 4.1% in September, driven by energy, housing and fuel costs.
  • The housing, water, electricity, gas and fuels category rose 10.7% year on year, and mortgage interest costs are at their highest since 2008 after the ECB lifted rates to 2.5%.
  • A €5bn public-sector pay deal worth 10.75% over to 2029 for more than 420,000 workers ended the threat of strikes; economists warn it may run above inflation next year.
  • AIB's autumn outlook still sees domestic demand growing 3.8% this year, with inflation expected to peak this quarter before easing.

Military / Defence Forces

  • More than 700 soldiers, sailors and aircrew took part in Operation Fianna, the security operation for the Dublin summit, with LÉ James Joyce off the coast.
  • A legal block on the military using force in support of gardaí has been removed, replacing a 1967 framework and letting the Naval Service seize ships in Ireland's EEZ.
  • Officers' body RACO accused the Government of "defence on the cheap" because senior officers were denied allowances paid to junior personnel on the same operation.
  • RTÉ's Prime Time examined who is watching Dublin's skies and seas, reviving questions about Ireland's limited air and naval capacity.

Technology

  • Around 2,000 people marched in Dublin on 7 November calling for a moratorium on new data centres, citing pressure on the grid and water.
  • Hiring is mixed: Stripe plans 200 Dublin roles and Anthropic and OpenAI are expanding in Dublin, while Oracle, Workday and TikTok trim Irish staff, per the Irish Times tracker.
  • Nearly 5% of Irish job postings now mention data centres, the highest share of ten European countries in Indeed's analysis.

Sports

  • Rugby: after Hugo Keenan's hat-trick in a 42-12 win over Argentina, Ireland face Fiji at the Aviva tonight (8.10pm), with Bundee Aki starting and South Africa up next weekend.
  • Soccer: Ireland are in Linz tonight against Austria chasing Nations League promotion, then host Kosovo on Tuesday; Séamus Coleman is in camp in a "hybrid" player-coach role.
  • GAA: Loughrea retained the Galway senior hurling title and move on to the All-Ireland club series; Galway's Sylane play Mayo's Moytura in Tuam today.
  • Connacht have four players in the Ireland squad for Fiji, but Sean Jansen is out for the rest of the Nations Championship with a knee injury.

Music

  • Bell X1's ninth album Good Bones is out now, with Vicar Street dates on 27–28 November.

  • Fontaines D.C.'s Dopamine Chamber holds No.1 on the Irish albums chart for a third week; Rua Rí plays Limerick tonight on his debut-album tour.

🔥 Trending in Ireland
CeolConnect
Tech / Music · Dublin (DCU)

CeolConnect is a homegrown platform for booking live music. It began as a final-year student project at DCU and was set to launch on the app stores. It tackles a long-running grassroots problem: venues and pubs struggle to find acts, and independent musicians struggle to get paid gigs, so the app puts both sides in one place. It appeals to Irish musicians at a time when homegrown acts are pushing Ireland's streaming market to record highs while small gigs are getting harder to book.

  • The chief whip has published the priority legislation list to Christmas, including e-scooters and a citizenship-tightening bill.
