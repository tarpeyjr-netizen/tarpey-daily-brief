Week of Nov 14 to Nov 21, 2026

Politics

  • The Government will consider Willie O'Dea's proposed amendment to its Triple Lock reform, which the Taoiseach backs in essence; the Coalition wants the law enacted by year's end.
  • The Dáil defeated a Sinn Féin motion seeking a referendum on the change, and some 224 amendments await the defence committee — battle lines are drawn.
  • Micheál Martin vowed to find whoever is behind bomb threats against RTÉ and other media and flight alerts.
  • The Taoiseach will meet the Cork University Hospital CEO on Monday amid a staffing controversy, while rejecting calls for an independent staffing review.

Economics

  • The European Commission now forecasts Irish GDP to contract 0.4% in 2026, largely on pharma base effects from early 2025, with growth returning in 2027.
  • The State's budget surplus is set to halve by 2028, from 1.4% of GDP to 0.6%, as spending outpaces revenue.
  • House price inflation slowed to 4.7% in September, a three-year low, though the median home price topped €400,000 for the first time.
  • Daft says rental supply is up 47% year on year as the market cools; SIPTU, Fórsa and the TUI are recommending the 10.75% public pay deal, with ballots closing 17 December.

Military / Defence Forces

  • Ireland was unable to send soldiers to train Ukrainian troops after no one volunteered amid an allowance dispute.
  • The Government says it has "no intention" of sending Defence Forces personnel onto Ukrainian territory under the EU training mission.
  • Officers' body RACO accused the Government of running EU security operations on the cheap over allowances.
  • The Defence Forces Tribunal resumes at the end of November after hearings on how harassment complaints, including those of the Women of Honour, were handled.

Technology

  • Irish-founded End Close raised $3.5m in seed funding, and Irish co-founded Pretxt raised $5m to supervise AI agents, per Silicon Republic.
  • The IVCA reports Irish venture funding hit its lowest Q3 level in ten years.
  • Auxilion announced 50 jobs across Dublin, Belfast and Sheffield, adding to an autumn of mixed signals: AI hiring by Stripe, OpenAI and Anthropic versus cuts at Meta, Oracle and Workday.

Sports

  • Ireland face South Africa at the Aviva today after an unconvincing 43-15 win over Fiji, powered by a 26-0 second half.
  • The Republic lost 1-0 to Austria in Linz, then beat Kosovo 3-0 to take second in their Nations League group and a March play-off against Croatia.
  • Galway get a bye to the 2027 Connacht football semi-finals and will meet All-Ireland champions Mayo or Roscommon.
  • Salthill-Knocknacarra beat St Brigid's to reach the Connacht club football final against Castlebar; Galway United re-signed goalkeeper Hugo Da Cunha.

Music

  • Bell X1 released their ninth album, Good Bones, on 13 November, ahead of Vicar Street shows on 27–28 November.
  • U2 released Carnaval de Luz, and say their 2027 tour will wrap in Ireland, with no dates yet.
  • Other Voices runs this week with The Waterboys and Gilla Band, and Phoebe Bridgers plays two sold-out 3Arena nights on 23–24 November (event guide).
  • Madra Salach won breakthrough artist at the Rolling Stone UK Awards; Hot Press has this week's new Irish songs.

Books

  • The late Manchán Magan's Seeing What's Hidden won Waterstones Irish Book of the Year, a posthumous honour.
  • The An Post Irish Book Awards winners are announced on 1 December; the Novel of the Year shortlist includes Maggie O'Farrell, Louise Kennedy, Donal Ryan, Sebastian Barry and John Boyne.
  • Louise Kennedy was named 2027 IPUT Writer-in-Residence.

Galway news

  • No Galway Ring Road works will begin until 2029 now that the completion timeframe is confirmed, after the last named legal objector withdrew their challenge.
  • Councillors backed a motion to bring back pay-and-display parking machines in the city, per Galway Bay FM.
  • A Galway nursing home has halted new admissions after a HIQA inspection, and mediation talks have begun over the Merlin Woods tree-felling dispute (Connacht Tribune).
  • Gardaí appealed for help finding a man missing from Claregalway.

Kilkelly news

  • No Kilkelly-specific stories surfaced this week. In east Mayo, councillors want a safety review of the N17 between Knock and Charlestown, where one camera logged over 11,000 speeding detections in 11 months.
  • Senator Mark Duffy says NTA funding is being sought for a north Mayo–Knock airport bus that would add a Charlestown shuttle serving Kilkelly and Kiltimagh.
  • An East Mayo councillor has called for more footpath funding in Swinford and Charlestown after pedestrians reported falls (Western People).

🔥 Trending in Ireland
Aaron Rowe
Music · Dublin

Dublin-raised Nigerian-Irish singer-songwriter Aaron Rowe went from small rooms to Ed Sheeran's North American tour in about a year, with unreleased tracks catching on across social media before they were even out. His rise has been driven by word of mouth and raw live shows. He was added late to Electric Picnic, sang Amhrán na bhFiann at the Aviva, and has now added a second headline night at the 3Olympia in December, his biggest shows yet. Fans are drawn to his old-soul voice and his story as a homegrown act who built a following without a major-label push.

Week of Nov 07 to Nov 14, 2026

Politics

  • Dublin hosted the European Political Community summit on 12 November, the showcase event of Ireland's EU Council presidency, after threatened public-sector strikes were averted.
  • The opposition attacked the summit's cost, saying the Government had "rolled out the red carpet" while households struggle; Micheál Martin and Mary Lou McDonald traded "despicable" barbs in the Dáil.
  • Housing remained the flashpoint, with the Social Democrats' Rory Hearne and the Taoiseach exchanging bitter accusations, and the Soc Dems tabling a motion on public transport fare rises.
  • The Government is backing a proposed law that would specifically criminalise drink spiking; TheJournal's winners and losers rounds up the week.

Economics

  • Inflation hit a three-year high of 4.3% in October, up from 4.1% in September, driven by energy, housing and fuel costs.
  • The housing, water, electricity, gas and fuels category rose 10.7% year on year, and mortgage interest costs are at their highest since 2008 after the ECB lifted rates to 2.5%.
  • A €5bn public-sector pay deal worth 10.75% over to 2029 for more than 420,000 workers ended the threat of strikes; economists warn it may run above inflation next year.
  • AIB's autumn outlook still sees domestic demand growing 3.8% this year, with inflation expected to peak this quarter before easing.

Military / Defence Forces

  • More than 700 soldiers, sailors and aircrew took part in Operation Fianna, the security operation for the Dublin summit, with LÉ James Joyce off the coast.
  • A legal block on the military using force in support of gardaí has been removed, replacing a 1967 framework and letting the Naval Service seize ships in Ireland's EEZ.
  • Officers' body RACO accused the Government of "defence on the cheap" because senior officers were denied allowances paid to junior personnel on the same operation.
  • RTÉ's Prime Time examined who is watching Dublin's skies and seas, reviving questions about Ireland's limited air and naval capacity.

Technology

  • Around 2,000 people marched in Dublin on 7 November calling for a moratorium on new data centres, citing pressure on the grid and water.
  • Hiring is mixed: Stripe plans 200 Dublin roles and Anthropic and OpenAI are expanding in Dublin, while Oracle, Workday and TikTok trim Irish staff, per the Irish Times tracker.
  • Nearly 5% of Irish job postings now mention data centres, the highest share of ten European countries in Indeed's analysis.

Sports

  • Rugby: after Hugo Keenan's hat-trick in a 42-12 win over Argentina, Ireland face Fiji at the Aviva tonight (8.10pm), with Bundee Aki starting and South Africa up next weekend.
  • Soccer: Ireland are in Linz tonight against Austria chasing Nations League promotion, then host Kosovo on Tuesday; Séamus Coleman is in camp in a "hybrid" player-coach role.
  • GAA: Loughrea retained the Galway senior hurling title and move on to the All-Ireland club series; Galway's Sylane play Mayo's Moytura in Tuam today.
  • Connacht have four players in the Ireland squad for Fiji, but Sean Jansen is out for the rest of the Nations Championship with a knee injury.

Music

  • U2 released Carnaval de Luz on 13 November and say their next tour will finish in Ireland, though no dates are set yet.
  • Bell X1's ninth album Good Bones is out now, with Vicar Street dates on 27–28 November.
  • The Shane MacGowan tribute album 20th Century Paddy is out, featuring Bruce Springsteen and Damien Dempsey.
  • Fontaines D.C.'s Dopamine Chamber holds No.1 on the Irish albums chart for a third week; Rua Rí plays Limerick tonight on his debut-album tour.

Books

  • The An Post Irish Book Awards 2026 shortlist is out, with Maggie O'Farrell's Land up for Novel of the Year and the late Manchán Magan also recognised.
  • Jan Carson and John Boyne are among the other shortlisted names; winners have not yet been announced.

Galway news

  • The last objector withdrew a High Court challenge to the N6 Galway city ring road; construction is now estimated to start in 2028.
  • Galway County Council is demanding urgent action as the number of women waiting over four years for gynaecology treatment at UHG tripled.
  • Cllr Alan Cheevers has taken the fight over Merlin Woods tree felling to the High Court.
  • The council is still weighing the parking problem at Oranmore station, and 64 graduate nurses will get permanent contracts in Galway.

Kilkelly news

  • East Mayo: Charlestown Sarsfields secured Mayo Junior 'A' status for 2027, and Swinford and Charlestown councillors are pushing for more footpath funding (Charlestown notes).

🔥 Trending in Ireland
CeolConnect
Tech / Music · Dublin (DCU)

CeolConnect is a homegrown platform for booking live music. It began as a final-year student project at DCU and was set to launch on the app stores. It tackles a long-running grassroots problem: venues and pubs struggle to find acts, and independent musicians struggle to get paid gigs, so the app puts both sides in one place. It appeals to Irish musicians at a time when homegrown acts are pushing Ireland's streaming market to record highs while small gigs are getting harder to book.
