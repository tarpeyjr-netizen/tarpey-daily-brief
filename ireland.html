Week of Sat 19 Sep to Sat 26 Sep, 2026

Politics

  • A premature sports capital grants announcement has stirred coalition tensions, with backbench TDs competing for credit ahead of the next election.
  • New polling shows little movement: Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael and Sinn Féin each dropped a point, while the Social Democrats and People Before Profit each gained one.
  • 68% of voters say the government should begin preparing for possible Irish unity, according to the same poll.
  • Energy and home heating costs are the top issue voters want addressed in Budget 2027.

Economics

  • House price inflation slowed to 5.5% nationally in the year to July, with apartment prices rising faster than houses.
  • Consumer prices rose 3.7% year-on-year, with energy, housing and transport the biggest drivers of the increase.
  • Tech employment has contracted 11% over the past year, and professional and financial services jobs are also being shed, nudging unemployment up to 5.3%.

Military / Defence Forces

  • The 128th Infantry Battalion completed final training ahead of deployment to UNIFIL in Lebanon, as the decades-long peacekeeping mission winds down.
  • Brigadier General Louis Flynn has been appointed the new General Officer Commanding of the Defence Forces Training Centre.
  • The 27 Infantry Battalion Sniper Team won Best International Team at the 2026 US International Sniper Competition.

Technology

  • New EU transparency filings show Apple and Microsoft accounted for 98% of tens of billions of euro in Irish corporate tax receipts.
  • Apple posted €209.3bn in Irish revenue, roughly double the profit of the rest of the top 10 companies combined.
  • Meta trimmed its Irish headcount to 1,738, down from just under 2,200 a year earlier, while still posting €2.9bn in profit on €85.3bn of revenue.
  • Ireland's media regulator Coimisiún na Meán opened an investigation into X over its age assurance and parental control measures.

Sports

  • Galway football manager Pádraic Joyce is stepping down after seven years, having won four straight Connacht titles and reached two All-Ireland finals.
  • Galway's hurlers remain in action in the Forvis Mazars Galway Senior Hurling Championship, with RTÉ analyst Liam Sheedy tipping the county as a genuine All-Ireland SHC contender.
  • Galway's camogie side added the Centra Camogie League Division 1A title to last year's All-Ireland championship.

Music

  • Gilla Band released their fourth album, Pugnello, opening with the screamed-vocal, noise-rock track “The Angelus.”
  • Trá Pháidín released Cloch ‘ Cláí, blending improvised traditional music with ambient textures on the track “Mionnán.”
  • Radie Peat of Lankum released her debut solo single, a reinterpretation of the traditional love song “Still I Love Him.”
  • Dublin/Kildare artist Chelle released the new single “Same Blue Sky.”

Books

  • Gráinne O'Hare won the Kerry Group Novel of the Year for her debut Thirst Trap at Listowel Writers' Week.
  • Liadan Ní Chuinn's Everyone Still Here was shortlisted for the Orwell Prize for Political Fiction, alongside Susan Choi, Ben Lerner and Douglas Stuart.
  • Chris Haughton was named Ireland's eighth Laureate na nÓg (Children's Literature Laureate), a post he holds until 2029.
  • Djamel White's All Them Dogs missed the cut for the 2026 Booker Prize shortlist; the winner is announced November 9 in London.

Galway news

  • Works have begun on the €19 million redevelopment of Galway City Museum.
  • The Misleór Festival of Nomadic Cultures returns to Galway city September 24–27, bringing Traveller and nomadic artists to the city.
  • University of Galway's Arts in Action 2026–2027 programme launches this week with free events and workshops.
  • Galway County Council is consulting the public on a preferred design for the Portumna Swimming Area.

Kilkelly news

  • Weekly Eucharistic Adoration resumes across the parish, including a Thursday session in Kilkelly.
  • An east Mayo councillor has called for more footpath funding in Swinford and Charlestown after several pedestrians fell, with councillors saying the council's repair budget is inadequate.

Tarpey news

  • No news items specifically naming Tarpey surfaced in Irish coverage this week; University of Galway's Tarpey Scholarship, endowed in memory of sisters Hazel and Tanya Tarpey, continues to support nursing and medicine students.
🔥 Trending in Ireland
F3miii
Music · Dublin

F3miii (Richard Oluwafemi Adebusuyi), a Dublin-born artist of Nigerian and Irish heritage who taught himself guitar from online videos at age 12, is having a genuine grassroots breakout with his single “Noble.” The track spread first through TikTok rather than radio or label push, and has since amassed more than 200 million Spotify streams, cracked the US Billboard Hot 100, and charted in the UK, Ireland and Australia. He got his start in the Dublin hip-hop collective Gracepark before going solo in 2020, and describes “Noble” as influenced by 2013-era Frank Ocean and Childish Gambino. It is the kind of slow-build, fan-driven momentum — built one clip at a time rather than a single viral moment — that keeps propelling him further up the charts.
