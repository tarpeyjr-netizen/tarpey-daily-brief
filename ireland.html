Week of Sat 17 to Sat 24 Oct 2026

Politics

  • The week was dominated by the aftermath of Budget 2027, delivered on 6 October; Simon Harris said it sets the country on a path forward, while Sinn Féin dismissed it as “gone before you get it”.
  • The Irish Times reported the Government playing down tension between ministers over the budget.
  • Unions called the budget a missed opportunity, citing public sector pay.
  • Search coverage of this week’s Daíl business was thin; no other dated political stories were confirmed.

Economics

  • Budget 2027 is a package of roughly €8.5–8.65bn (reports differ), with about €1.65bn in income tax measures and the minimum wage rising 79 cent to €14.94.
  • The fiscal watchdog Ifac warned the measures put the public finances on a worse trajectory.
  • New personal investment accounts will allow €12,000 a year to be invested tax-free.
  • The Irish Times noted the public finances are cash-rich but spending has ballooned.

Military / Defence Forces

  • No dated Defence Forces stories from this week were found.
  • Background: the Irish Examiner reports an officers’ association saying Navy recruitment is too slow to hit the Government’s target, and called the October budget a test for defence spending (€1.49bn in 2026, target ~€1.8bn by 2028).

Technology

  • Irish firms are exporting data-centre know-how worldwide; data centres used 22% of metered electricity in 2024, with the share expected to pass 30% by 2030.
  • Friends of the Earth continues to call for a moratorium on new data centres.
  • Ireland is hosting AI Innovation Month, running until 17 November, following the International AI Summit in Dublin on 14 October.

Sports

  • Andy Farrell named a 36-player Ireland squad on 21 October for the November internationals, including Ulster’s uncapped Jamie Benson and James McNabney.
  • No confirmed GAA, League of Ireland or Galway team results from this week turned up in search; the club and county seasons are largely over.

Music

  • Bell X1 announced their ninth album, Good Bones, for November, with an autumn/winter tour taking in Wexford, Dublin, Cork, Derry, Belfast and Killarney.
  • Gurriers bring their debut album Come and See to Barcelona on 4 November.
  • Kingfishr’s “Killeagh” has been back at number one on the Irish Singles Chart per Hot Press (date of the latest run unconfirmed).

Books

  • Frank Shovlin’s authorised McGahern biography, A Writing Life, is out from Faber.
  • Dubliner Djamel White’s debut All Them Dogs was the only Irish title on the Booker longlist; the winner is announced in November.

Week of Sat 10 to Sat 17 Oct 2026

Politics

  • Budget 2027 was presented on 6 October; a larger tax package than expected drew mixed reaction in the Dáil.
  • Labour leader Ivana Bacik called it a "KitKat budget from a TikTok Tánaiste", while Sinn Féin said it lacked ambition.
  • The Fiscal Advisory Council warned the measures put public finances on a worse trajectory; Taoiseach Micheál Martin rejected the criticism.
  • The Taoiseach said the public sector pay dispute is "unwarranted" given the Government's willingness to talk.

Economics

  • Consumer inflation rose to 4.1% in the latest CSO figures, with housing, water and energy up 9.5%; an economist warns it will "wash away" budget measures.
  • Budget 2027 indexes income tax bands and raises the USC threshold, but opposition finance spokesperson Pearse Doherty says the gains will be wiped out before people receive them.
  • Unemployment was reported at 5% in September, and the ESRI sees it staying low while flagging possible long-term effects of AI on jobs.
  • Indeed says almost 5% of Irish job postings now mention data centres, though its economist questions whether the momentum will last.

Military / Defence Forces

  • A mid-September digest reported deep cuts pressures, including smaller soldiers' rations and a halt to naval radar plans ahead of Budget 2027.
  • This sits alongside a €1.7 billion capital plan launched by Defence Minister Helen McEntee, a 55% increase in capital funding.
  • No specific Defence Forces news dated this week turned up in searches; check gov.ie or An Cosantóir for updates.

Technology

  • PayPal is cutting roughly 164 Irish roles, about 12% of its staff here, with redundancies running into October (secondary-source roundup).
  • A September roundup also reported OpenAI deepening its Dublin presence after a July EU HQ opening of 250 jobs; treat as unconfirmed.
  • Data centre hiring continues to climb, with postings at 2.1 times early-2020 levels per Indeed.

Sports

  • Soccer: Galway United beat Shamrock Rovers 2-0 at Eamonn Deacy Park on Friday, their first win over Rovers since 2006, keeping the title race open.
  • Rugby: all four Irish provinces lost in URC Round 1, with Connacht beaten 29-15 by the Stormers; Round 2 results were not found.
  • Upcoming: Connacht host Zebre Parma on 23 October and Leinster on 30 October at Dexcom Stadium.

Music

  • The Irish Times reviewed new Irish albums from Basht, Meryl Streek, Morgana, Wasps vs Humans and Whozyerman? on 13 October.
  • RTÉ profiled Fontaines D.C., who have a new album due this October.
  • Foy Vance plays Galway Town Hall Theatre on 30 October.

Books

  • The John McGahern Annual Book Prize winner's reading is on Sunday 18 October at the Liverpool Literary Festival; the shortlist is Gethan Dick, Seán Farrell and Liadan Ní Chuinn.
  • Dubliner Djamel White's debut All Them Dogs was longlisted for the Booker Prize, with the winner to be revealed in November.
  • Faber's authorised McGahern biography is forthcoming this October.

Galway news

  • The 14 October national public sector strike hit Galway, with non-emergency procedures cancelled at University Hospital Galway and Portiuncula.
  • Psychiatric nurses are set to resume industrial action in Galway, and the Health Minister says he is concerned after Galway nurse jobs were withdrawn.
  • City councillors rejected a proposed 10% increase in the local property tax, and Starbucks was refused a drive-thru at Galway Shopping Centre.
  • Galway United's 2-0 win over Shamrock Rovers on Friday was the city's sporting highlight of the week.
