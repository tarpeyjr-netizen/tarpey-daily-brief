August 7, 2026 Update

Roster Players

Jalen Brunson — New York Knicks

Brunson, the reigning 2026 Finals MVP, continues to headline the champion Knicks' offseason. His debut children's book "Jalen Plays It All" had its release date bumped up to November 24, 2026 to capitalize on his ring-year momentum. The 2024 team-friendly extension that left north of $100M on the table remains the load-bearing wall of the roster — it's what let New York absorb Karl-Anthony Towns and Mikal Bridges under the second apron. Coverage this window has focused on how much of a lift he'll be asked to carry after Mitchell Robinson's departure; no new injury or contract news in the past 15 days.

Josh Hart — New York Knicks

Hart becomes extension-eligible on August 10, and reporting through this window says he's expected to follow the Brunson/Bridges playbook and take a team-friendly four-year number to keep the champion core intact under the second apron. On August 4 he was announced as a Scouting America Ambassador — a nod to his Eagle Scout background. He's also been active on social giving a lighthearted reaction to training camp being two months out. Coming off 12.0 / 7.4 / 4.8 on 50.8% shooting in 66 games, he's positioned as the glue signing of the summer.

Mikal Bridges — New York Knicks

Bridges agreed to a four-year, $150M extension with a 2029-30 player option and a trade kicker, taking a slight discount to help the front office keep the title roster together. It's the direct sequel to his Game 5 Finals contribution (14 points in the 94-90 clincher over San Antonio on July 2) and cements the Knicks' return on the five-first-rounder trade — which looks even better after the second-rounder they received routed back into the Jose Alvarado deal. No injury notes; healthy and locked in through at least 2028-29.

Donte DiVincenzo — Minnesota Timberwolves

Rough stretch. DiVincenzo underwent surgery on his ruptured right Achilles (torn in Minnesota's Game 4 win over Denver) with Dr. Martin O'Malley — the same surgeon who repaired Jayson Tatum's Achilles in 2025. Reporting now leans toward him missing the entire 2026-27 season, with any return unlikely before late in the regular season. Compounding that, he was cleared to resume light training on July 21 but flared up his old turf toe within days, which scrapped his planned Italy trip. Wolves are proceeding as if he's out for the year.

Collin Gillespie — Phoenix Suns

Gillespie cashed in on his breakout year, re-signing with Phoenix on a four-year, $48M deal (announced alongside Jordan Goodwin's three-year pact). Coming off 12.7 / 4.1 / 4.6 on 40.1% from three across 80 games, the undrafted Villanova product is now firmly in the Suns' backcourt plans. With Royce O'Neale and Grayson Allen shipped to Charlotte in the Miles Bridges deal and Luke Kennard added for spacing, Gillespie's shot-making is expected to carry more offensive load in 2026-27.

Cam Whitmore — Washington Wizards

Biggest roster-mover of the window: Whitmore was traded from Houston to Washington in July for two second-round picks, giving the Wizards another young DMV-area piece to pair with Sarr, Coulibaly, Carrington, Johnson and George. Washington also exercised his 2026-27 rookie option. Health remains the caveat — he's still working back from the deep vein thrombosis / venous condition in his right shoulder that ended his 2025-26 season. No timetable for a return to full basketball activities has surfaced in the past 15 days.

Saddiq Bey — New Orleans Pelicans

Bey became extension-eligible on July 12 and both sides have publicly signaled interest in getting a deal done before he'd hit free agency. Coming off a career year (17.7 / 5.6 / 2.5 on 45.1/36.7/84.1 splits with five 30-point games including a 42-burger) after missing all of 2024-25 with a torn ACL, he's earned the raise. No signed number yet in this window, but talks are described as active.

Jermaine Samuels — Houston Rockets

Quiet offseason. Samuels finished 2025-26 on a two-way with Houston, appearing in 11 NBA games (4.8 mpg, 1.6 ppg) around a G-League All-Star selection with the Rio Grande Valley Vipers. No signing, workout, or Summer League news has surfaced in the past 15 days; his roster status for 2026-27 remains up in the air.

Jeremiah Robinson-Earl — Texas Legends (G-League)

No fresh news in the past 15 days. Last season's headline arc still frames him: 18.7 / 12.2 / 2.8 on 55.0% shooting with Texas, a 29-point career-high game in January, 10-day NBA call-ups with both Indiana and Dallas, and a February selection to USA Basketball's Men's World Cup Qualifying Team. He remains one of the more obvious two-way / hardship candidates whenever a big man goes down.

Eric Dixon — Memphis Hustle (G-League)

Nothing new in this window. Dixon — Villanova's all-time leading scorer and the 2024-25 NCAA scoring leader who went undrafted in 2025 — is coming off a strong first pro season with Memphis after being acquired from Greensboro in February, capped by a 48-point / 45-efficiency career night vs. Mexico City in March. No Summer League or two-way news has landed yet; watch for training-camp invite reporting in the next update.

July 25, 2026 Update

Roster Players

Jalen Brunson — New York Knicks

Riding high off the championship, Brunson swept individual hardware at the 2026 ESPYs (July 15–16), winning Best Championship Performance and Best Athlete — Men's Sports on the strength of his Finals run (32.6 ppg, 4.6 apg, 4.2 rpg, 2.2 spg). He's recovering from left wrist tendon surgery undergone after the Finals but made a WWE Saturday Night's Main Event appearance alongside Karl-Anthony Towns. He'll join Variety's new Sports & Entertainment Studio in New York, and told reporters (July 21) the team hasn't discussed a White House visit. On the business side, reporting this week floats Brunson eyeing a $300M max extension next summer after taking a team-friendly deal last time around.

Josh Hart — New York Knicks

Still enjoying the title run, Hart becomes extension-eligible on August 10 and is reportedly expected to take another team-friendly deal to help the champs run it back, following Brunson and Bridges' lead. He's been active and funny on social media about the short offseason ("let me put the wine down and pick up a ball") and about New York's chaotic offseason moves. No injury concerns.

Mikal Bridges — New York Knicks

Bridges signed a 4-year, $150M extension with the Knicks, taking a discount to help keep the championship roster intact; the deal includes a 2029-30 player option and a trade kicker. He was a key piece of New York's title run (their first since 1973) and the Knicks are bringing the group back largely intact aside from Mitchell Robinson (traded to Boston) and Ariel Hukporti (signed with Philadelphia).

Donte DiVincenzo — Minnesota Timberwolves

Still recovering from the torn right Achilles suffered in Game 4 of the first-round series against Denver in late April, which ended a career-best season (12.2 ppg, 4.1 rpg, 3.8 apg, 37.9% from three, all 82 games played). Minnesota has said it prefers to keep him out of offseason business while he rehabs and acquired Josh Green (via the LaMelo Ball trade) as a stopgap rather than moving DiVincenzo. No timetable given yet for his return.

Collin Gillespie — Phoenix Suns

Re-signed with Phoenix on a 4-year, $48M deal announced July 7. Gillespie, who turned 27 in June, is coming off a breakout year (12.7 ppg, 4.1 rpg, 4.6 apg, 40.1% from three over 80 games) and Suns GM Brian Gregory praised him as embodying the team's culture after an expanded role last season.

Cam Whitmore — Washington Wizards

No major new updates in the past 15 days. Whitmore remains in recovery from the deep vein thrombosis (upper-extremity, right shoulder) that ended his 2025-26 season in January after he averaged 9.2 ppg and 2.8 rpg over 21 games. He's expressed optimism about returning for next season, and Washington exercised his rookie option, signaling continued commitment. No specific July update on his recovery timeline was found.

Saddiq Bey — New Orleans Pelicans

Became extension-eligible July 10, and reporting indicates both Bey and the Pelicans are interested in getting a deal done after his breakout, career-best season (17.7 ppg, 5.6 rpg, 2.5 apg on 45.1/36.7/84.1 shooting), including a 42-point game and five 30-point outings. He'd missed all of 2024-25 with a torn ACL before arriving in New Orleans as a throw-in on the Jordan Poole trade.

Jermaine Samuels — Houston Rockets

Samuels has left the Rockets organization, signing with Spanish club San Pablo Burgos for the coming season after two years on a two-way deal with Houston and its G League affiliate, the Rio Grande Valley Vipers (where he earned a G League All-Star nod). No indication of an NBA return in the near term.

Jeremiah Robinson-Earl — Texas Legends (G-League)

No new news in the past 15 days beyond his established 2025-26 track record: he averaged 18.7 ppg, 12.2 rpg and 2.8 apg on 55.0% shooting across 17 G League games with the Legends, earned 10-day NBA hardship deals with both the Pacers and Mavericks, and was named to the USA Basketball Men's World Cup Qualifying roster in February. Currently between NBA opportunities.

Eric Dixon — Memphis Hustle (G-League)

Dixon, acquired by Memphis from the Greensboro Swarm in a February trade, has been a standout since the move — averaging 22.5 ppg, 6.5 rpg and 1.8 apg with the Hustle, including a 48-point outburst (12-of-21 FG, 6-of-12 from three, 12-of-12 FT) in his most recent notable performance. The former Villanova scoring leader and Third Team All-American continues to build a case for an NBA opportunity after going undrafted in 2025.
