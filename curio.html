Wikipedia Article of the Day

Tabancale language — Tabancale is an extinct language formerly spoken in the village of Aconipa, located along the Chinchipe River in the Department of Cajamarca, Peru, as well as in neighboring Ecuador, by the Tabancale people.

Interesting fact: When Paul Rivet and Čestmír Loukotka classified South America’s languages in 1952, they listed “Akonipa” as one of their 109 independent language families — a whole family resting on just five recorded words. The linguist Matthias Urban has since linked one of them, yema (“water”), to Chicham yumi.
