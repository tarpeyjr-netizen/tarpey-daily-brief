SUNDAY, AUGUST 16, 2026

🏗️ Developer Pulls Southeast Raleigh Data Center Plan

The developer behind a proposed data center in southeast Raleigh has withdrawn the plans, Raleigh's mayor said, ending a fight that had drawn unusually heavy neighborhood turnout. The project would have placed a nearly 100,000-square-foot facility on a 14-acre site along Jones Sausage Road, in a part of the city residents argued has long absorbed more than its share of industrial development. Dozens of people had signed up to speak against it before the withdrawal. The reversal lands as data center siting becomes a statewide flashpoint — Greensboro, Alamance County and Yadkin County are all weighing moratoriums on new facilities. Expect the Triangle's next proposal to face the same organized scrutiny.

🚨 Wake Coach Killed; Durham Sees Deadly Weekend

Raleigh police found 57-year-old Sheremy Gwenette Dillard, a Wake County Schools girls' basketball coach, dead at her Whistling Straits Way home Thursday afternoon after family members said they hadn't heard from her; her boyfriend, Rickey Harper, was charged with murder and arrested in Plymouth, Pennsylvania. Neighbors and former players described her as a fixture in the community, and the news rippled across Triangle school communities as the year began. In Durham, officers found a man shot to death on the 2500 block of Lane Street, days after a separate Middle Street shooting that ended with a suspect in custody. Wake Forest police, meanwhile, are still searching for a Durham man after a pursuit ended in a crash.

🌡️ Dangerous Heat, Storm Risk, and a Deepening Drought

Heat advisories remain in force across the Triangle, with advisories continuing into Monday alongside an evening severe-storm risk. Afternoon highs are pushing the mid-90s with heat indices as high as 110, and Monday is forecast to be the hottest day of the stretch — close to the RDU record of 99 degrees set in 1997. The heat is landing on already parched ground: despite scattered showers, Wake, Durham and Orange counties remain in severe drought or worse, and forecasters expect rain to stay spotty. It fits a longer pattern — July was the hottest month on record nationally, and Carolinas summers are warming measurably. In Orange County, a boil water advisory remains in effect for some Hillsborough customers after a main leak dropped system pressure.

🏛️ After the Takeovers, Raleigh Asks Where Teens Can Go

In the wake of the so-called "teen takeovers" that led to recent shootings in Raleigh, WUNC reported this week on a quieter structural problem: teenagers in Wake County have almost nowhere to gather without direct adult supervision. Malls have added parental escort policies, arcades have closed, and third spaces built for young people have thinned out over two decades. Experts told the station that the vacuum has contributed to both the violence and the crowd dynamics that produced it, and that curfews address the symptom rather than the cause. The question now facing Raleigh officials is whether enforcement or investment does more to break the cycle heading into the school year.

🏘️ North Hills Growth Squeezes Out Affordable Apartments

Kane Realty's planned 28-acre expansion of the North Hills Innovation District is forcing the closure of The Pointe at Midtowne on Navaho Drive, one of the few naturally affordable apartment communities left in midtown Raleigh. Residents there are being displaced in a submarket where comparable rents are scarce, a direct illustration of the trade-off behind the Triangle's development boom. It's a live tension elsewhere in the region too — Wake school leaders are pitching school-site and public developments to build affordable housing for teachers, while the broader debate over who should raise Wake teacher pay continues. Downtown Raleigh, for its part, still has roughly $1.5 billion in projects under construction.

🏒 Canes Sell Out, UNC Floats an Athletics LLC, Cary Turns 50

The Carolina Hurricanes have sold out season tickets ahead of the coming campaign, leaving single-game buyers to fight over what's left — a marker of how far the franchise's local footprint has grown. In the same roundup, WUNC reported that UNC is proposing a limited liability company to manage and maximize its athletics revenue, a structural move that would put Chapel Hill among the schools formally corporatizing their sports operations in the revenue-sharing era. On the field, No. 8 UNC women's soccer opened the season with a 4-0 win over Fairfield. And in Cary, more than 250 artists are set for the 50th Lazy Daze festival, the town's longest-running street fair.

FRIDAY, AUGUST 14, 2026

🚨 Woman killed in overnight East Durham shooting

Durham police are investigating a homicide after a shooting in the 900 block of East Main Street early Friday left a woman dead and a man seriously injured. Officers responded around 12:38 a.m. to a report of a gunshot wound and found both victims with life-threatening injuries; the woman later died at the hospital, while the man's condition has not been released. The shooting happened across from the Durham Hosiery Mill Apartments, where detectives spent much of Friday morning processing the scene behind crime tape and working out of a mobile command center. Police have not released the victims' identities or named a suspect. Anyone with information is asked to call Durham CrimeStoppers at 919-683-1200, which is offering up to $2,000 for tips leading to a felony arrest.

📊 Jackson says Duke Energy understated its rate hike

North Carolina Attorney General Jeff Jackson is publicly disputing Duke Energy's math on a proposed electricity rate increase, saying the company misrepresented under oath what residential customers would actually pay. Duke told the Utilities Commission on Tuesday that a settlement agreement would raise rates 6.8% over two years; Jackson contends the true figure for households is 9.3% and said flatly that "Duke got it wrong under oath." Climate group NC WARN, which noted Duke reported roughly $5 billion in profit last year, argued even 9.3% is too steep given that much of the money funds new plants serving data centers. Duke defended the deal as a compromise reached with the Public Staff and other stakeholders. The settlement still needs Utilities Commission approval; if cleared, the higher rates would take effect Jan. 1 across the Triangle and much of the state.

🌡️ Rain refills Falls Lake, but drought hangs on

Recent rainfall has pushed Falls Lake back to 89% — comfortably above the 75% trigger level that prompted Raleigh's now-lifted Stage 1 water restrictions — but drought still grips much of central North Carolina. The U.S. Drought Monitor has Wake County in moderate drought, northern Durham and Orange counties in severe drought, and swaths of Granville, Person, Caswell and Rockingham counties in extreme drought. Durham remains under Stage 2 Water Shortage restrictions as officials watch Lake Michie. NC State beef extension specialist Matt Poore warned that scarce hay will have to be trucked in from other states at high fuel prices, pushing costs onto small farms and horse owners, though soybeans, peanuts, sweet potatoes and cotton have rebounded since late-July rain. Boaters are returning to Falls Lake, where one visitor described banks exposed 40 to 50 feet back as "frightening."

🎓 Triple-digit heat index greets NC State's Class of 2030

Families hauling bins and bedding into North Carolina State residence halls Thursday battled feels-like temperatures in the triple digits as the Class of 2030 arrived in Raleigh. Incoming freshman Grace Gordon summed up the day between elevator trips: "My face is red, sweat stripping, but it's almost over." Wilmington families said they were "heat acclimated," while others juggled the physical grind of move-in with the emotional weight of goodbyes. The heat is not letting up — ABC11 has declared a First Alert Day for Friday with a heat index approaching 110 and a threat of severe storms. Move-ins continue across Triangle campuses through the weekend ahead of the fall semester.

🚨 SBI joins probe of Durham foundation's leaders

The State Bureau of Investigation has confirmed it is now investigating possible financial crimes involving Courtney Jordan Foundation founder Courtney Jordan and CFO Nicholas Gallardo, the Durham nonprofit also known as CJF America. The agency stepped in at the request of the Person County Sheriff's Office, whose investigators located the pair at a Timberlake home after a high-speed chase; search warrants say deputies recovered cell phones, suspected narcotics, laptops, cash, notebooks with financial account information and identification documents belonging to other people. A new civil lawsuit filed this week alleges the organization and its leaders stiffed an employee out of more than $56,000, adding to earlier suits from former workers who say they were never paid for last summer's work. The SBI expects the review to take considerable time given the volume of seized material. Jordan and Gallardo are due in Person County court Monday and back in Durham later this month on fraud charges.

🏗️ Adventure Landing closing after 38 years on Capital

Adventure Landing on Capital Boulevard will permanently close Sunday, ending a 38-year run as one of Raleigh's go-to spots for go-karts, mini golf, arcade games and birthday parties. Owner Hank Woodburn announced the closure on Facebook Thursday, thanking the guests, schools, churches and community groups that filled the park over the decades and singling out its annual Easter egg hunts on the mini golf course. The company says it will auction off much of the park's equipment and will share a link to the auction site soon. Guests have until Sunday to redeem outstanding arcade tokens and tickets, gift certificates, Groupons and other unused offers.

Tuesday, August 11, 2026

🚨 Durham assistant police chief cited after hit-and-run on I-540

Durham Assistant Police Chief Raul Garcia has been placed on paid administrative leave after allegedly striking another vehicle on Interstate 540 last week and driving away from the scene. The North Carolina Highway Patrol tracked Garcia to his home in Apex and cited him with hit-and-run, leaving the scene, failing to report the crash, and failing to maintain lane control. No serious injuries were reported, but the vehicle involved was a City of Durham-owned car. The Highway Patrol is continuing its investigation as the department weighs next steps for Garcia.

🏗️ FAA officials tour RDU to review Transform RDU expansion

Federal Aviation Administration officials visited Raleigh-Durham International Airport Monday to review progress on the Transform RDU project, which includes a runway replacement and major expansion of Terminal 2. The FAA is forecasting more than 50% growth in aircraft traffic at RDU over the next two decades, underscoring the urgency behind the buildout. Airport officials used the visit to press the case for continued federal support as the region's air travel demand climbs alongside its population and business growth.

🌡️ Raleigh lifts water restrictions as Durham, Chatham stay under Stage 2

Recent rainfall has eased drought conditions enough for Raleigh to lift its water restrictions, but Durham and parts of Chatham County remain under Stage 2 limits because reservoir levels there haven't recovered as quickly. Falls Lake had climbed to roughly 84% capacity after the recent rain, helping Raleigh's case, while Durham officials say they need to see sustained improvement before easing rules for residents. The split leaves Triangle neighbors under different watering and irrigation rules for now.

🚨 Serial peeping suspect faces new charges across Raleigh, Durham

Kevin McDonald Henderson, 44, is facing new felony secret peeping charges in both Wake and Durham counties after police say he took photographs of women inside stores. Henderson has a documented history of similar charges, and investigators say the latest cases span multiple retail locations in the two counties. The pattern has prompted renewed warnings from local police departments urging shoppers to report suspicious behavior in changing areas and store aisles.

🎓 Durant Road Elementary reopens after structural repairs

Emergency structural repairs at Durant Road Elementary School in Raleigh are now complete, clearing the way for students to return to their home building. The repair work forced students to attend Abbotts Creek Elementary temporarily at the start of the school year while crews addressed the structural issues. Wake County Public School System officials say the fix restores normal operations for the Durant Road community after weeks of disruption.

Monday, August 10, 2026

🚨 Downtown Raleigh nightclub stabbings mark violent weekend

Raleigh police responded to two separate nightclub violence incidents on Fayetteville Street over the weekend. Early Sunday, an argument at Alive Bar and Lounge escalated into a stabbing that left two people injured after the suspect fled before officers arrived; both victims are expected to recover. Days earlier, a fight inside another downtown club spilled onto the street and ended with a man stabbed in the arm, who then drove himself to the hospital with non-life-threatening injuries. Neither incident has led to an arrest so far. Separately, a man with a prior secret-peeping conviction faces new felony charges in Wake and Durham counties after allegedly photographing women in dressing rooms at stores near Triangle Town Center and a Durham thrift shop.

🌡️ Drought eases in Raleigh, but Durham and Chatham stay restricted

Raleigh has officially lifted its water restrictions after a record-wet July pushed Falls Lake up to 84% capacity, but the relief hasn't spread evenly across the Triangle. Durham and Chatham County remain under mandatory water conservation measures, with Durham's reservoirs sitting at about 55% capacity — enough for roughly 144 days of supply but still short of the threshold needed to ease Stage 2 rules banning spray irrigation and at-home car washing. Meanwhile, a stretch of mid-90s heat with humidity is settling back over the region this week, with heat index values expected to top 100 degrees and advisories possible by midweek. Forecasters say isolated afternoon storms are possible Wednesday and Friday, though most areas will stay dry.

🏈 UNC defensive coordinator Steve Belichick on indefinite medical leave

Bill Belichick announced that his son and UNC defensive coordinator, Steve Belichick is on medical leave ahead of the Tar Heels' season opener against TCU in Dublin on Aug. 29. The elder Belichick declined to share details, calling it "a medical situation" he couldn't comment further on, and said the team hopes to have the 39-year-old back "as soon as possible." Rather than naming an interim coordinator, UNC says it will coach the defense collectively as a staff during preseason practice while Steve Belichick, entering his second season in Chapel Hill, remains away from the team.

🤝 Duke basketball player helps give away backpacks to Triangle students

With the school year approaching, Duke basketball player Deron Rippey Jr. joined a community backpack giveaway in the area, handing out school supplies to local students ahead of the new academic year. The event adds to a busy week of back-to-school preparation across the Triangle as families gear up for the first bell. It's the kind of low-key community turnout that's become a late-summer staple for area college athletes as Duke and UNC both open fall camps in the coming weeks.
