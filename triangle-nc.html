🚨 $350K Burglary Spree Hits Three Raleigh Beltline Neighborhoods

Raleigh police say at least 10 of roughly 20 home burglaries reported over the past 90 days across three inside-the-Beltline neighborhoods appear connected, with more than $350,000 in cash, jewelry and other valuables stolen, WRAL reported. The break-ins have hit multimillion-dollar homes in Coley Forest, Sunset Hills and Budleigh along the Oberlin Road and Glenwood Avenue corridor, leaving residents in some of Raleigh's wealthiest neighborhoods on edge despite high-end security systems, according to CBS17. In one Coley Forest case, a homeowner said burglars used a signal jammer to disable an armed alarm, broke in and stole belongings, then switched the alarm back on before leaving, Hoodline reported. Police spokesperson LaToya Hughes said the investigation's slow pace reflects the work needed to verify tips and obtain outside records, and detectives have not said publicly how the connected cases are linked or identified a suspect.

🚨 Two Unrelated Shootings Kill Man, Young Girl in Durham

Durham endured two unrelated fatal shootings within minutes of each other Tuesday evening, leaving a man dead on Eva Street and a young girl dead on North Duke Street, ABC11 reported. Officers responding to the 900 block of Eva Street around 6:48 p.m. found a man with multiple gunshot wounds; despite efforts by officers and EMS, he died at the scene, and police called it an isolated incident posing no danger to the public, according to WRAL. Minutes later and about two miles away, officers called to the 700 block of North Duke Street just before 7 p.m. learned that two children had been playing with a gun when it discharged, fatally wounding the girl, WRAL reported. Durham police have not released either victim's identity and are asking anyone with information on the North Duke Street shooting to call Investigator G. Silla, per CBS17.

🏒 Hurricanes Rally Past Panthers in Preseason Shootout Thriller

The Carolina Hurricanes opened their home preseason slate with a 3-2 shootout win over the Florida Panthers at Lenovo Center on Tuesday night, a lineup heavy on prospects outlasting Florida's veterans, per ESPN. Florida's Brady Tkachuk opened the scoring 4:31 into the first period before Bradly Nadeau answered for Carolina less than three minutes later, and Aleksander Barkov restored the Panthers' lead on a breakaway wrist shot past goaltender Brandon Bussi, Florida Hockey Now reported. Justin Robidas tied it for Carolina midway through the second period, and neither team found a winner in regulation or overtime before the Hurricanes prevailed in the shootout, according to Sportsnaut. The result came a day after Florida beat Carolina 6-3 in the teams' first preseason meeting in Sunrise, as the defending Stanley Cup champions continue working younger players into the lineup ahead of next month's regular-season opener.

🏛 Affordability Concerns Linger a Day After WakeMed-Atrium Vote

A day after Wake County commissioners voted 5-2 to clear the way for WakeMed's combination with Atrium Health, questions about whether the deal will actually hold down health care costs are already dogging the agreement, CBS17 reported. A Brown University School of Public Health researcher who studies hospital mergers said the acquisition follows a pattern that typically brings price increases of 10% to 20%, while the advocacy group Patients Union contends Atrium already charges 15% to 40% more than WakeMed for comparable services. State Treasurer Brad Briner has also pushed back on the merger, arguing that hospital consolidation "leads to less competition and higher prices," even as WakeMed leaders maintain the partnership will improve affordability by expanding financial-assistance eligibility from 300% to 400% of the federal poverty level. The deal still needs approval from the Federal Trade Commission before Atrium's pledged $2 billion investment and other commitments can take effect.

🌟 Flash Flooding Snarls Roads Across the Triangle

Slow-moving storms dumped one to three inches of rain across the Triangle Tuesday evening, triggering flash flooding that closed roads in both Wake and Durham counties, CBS17 reported. In Raleigh, Glenwood Avenue at Ebenezer Church Road was shut down after floodwaters overtook the roadway, while in Durham a stretch of Blackwell Street and Jackie Robinson Drive near the Durham Bulls Athletic Park also flooded. Forecasters had declared it a WRAL Weather Alert Day ahead of the evening commute, warning the storms could turn severe and produce localized flash flooding in low-lying and poor-drainage areas, according to WRAL. The rain followed Monday's cold front and marked one of the first bouts of true fall weather for the region, with drier, cooler conditions expected to return by midweek.

TUESDAY, September 22, 2026

🚨 Teen Fires Shots at Durham Officers During Struggle

A confrontation between Durham police and a teenage robbery suspect turned into gunfire Monday afternoon after the boy pulled a handgun and fired twice while struggling with officers, WRAL reported. Officers had responded around 4:25 p.m. to a GoDurham bus in the 4000 block of Durham-Chapel Hill Boulevard after reports of an attempted larceny and located the juvenile male suspect nearby before the scuffle began, according to ABC11. No one was struck by gunfire, but one officer suffered a minor hand injury and was taken to a hospital for evaluation after colleagues wrestled the gun away and took the teen into custody, CBS17 reported. Durham police say the juvenile now faces charges tied to both the attempted larceny and firing at officers, adding another tense episode to a year that has repeatedly tested relations between the department and the public it serves.

🏛 Wake Commissioners Approve WakeMed-Atrium Merger, 5-2

Wake County commissioners voted 5-2 Monday evening to approve the legal changes clearing the way for WakeMed to combine with Charlotte-based Atrium Health, ending months of public fighting over the deal, WUNC reported. The vote amends WakeMed's articles of incorporation and transfer agreement, shifting oversight to a new 14-member board on which county commissioners retain the right to appoint eight members; in exchange, Atrium has pledged $2 billion to modernize and expand WakeMed facilities, a minimum of 8% charity and low-income care, and a five-year cap on price increases, according to CBS17. Commissioners Vickie Adamson and Tara Waters cast the two dissenting votes, with Waters faulting what she called a rushed process and Atrium's aggressive debt-collection track record, and Adamson arguing the board should have sent the agreement back for more due diligence, The Assembly reported. The deal now heads to the Federal Trade Commission for approval before it can be finalized.

🏒 Hurricanes Host Panthers Tonight in Preseason Opener

The defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes host the Florida Panthers tonight at Lenovo Center in their lone home exhibition game of the preseason, with puck drop set for 7 p.m., according to Florida Hockey Now. Carolina is dressing much the same lineup that opened the preseason Sunday in Sunrise, with just two skaters — a newly signed forward and 2025 seventh-round draft pick Filip Ekberg — appearing in their first game action as Hurricanes, per NHL.com. Either Brandon Bussi or Cayden Primeau will start in goal for Carolina, while Florida counters with Aleksander Barkov centering Brady Tkachuk and Sam Reinhart, WRAL reported. It's the first of two preseason meetings between the clubs before the regular season opens, as Carolina looks to defend the title it won over Vegas in June.

🤝 Fire Guts Beloved Chapel Hill Cafe and Bakery

A fire tore through Tonya's Café & Bakeshop in the early hours of Saturday, gutting the longtime Chapel Hill restaurant and bakery and forcing it to close for the foreseeable future, Chapelboro reported. Owner Tonya Council and her staff are safe and no injuries were reported, though the blaze left fire, smoke and water damage throughout the conjoined units in the Galleria shopping center on South Elliott Road, according to Hoodline. The cause of the fire has not yet been determined. Council says she plans to rebuild and is focused on retaining her staff and honoring existing catering and bakery commitments, and is exploring a pop-up or temporary location while repairs are underway, the News & Observer reported.

🌟 Cold Front Ends Heat Wave, Ushers In Fall

A cold front sweeping through central North Carolina overnight is expected to bring gusty storms and heavy downpours through Tuesday afternoon and evening, though forecasters say the severe-weather threat will be far lower than Monday's, when highs near 90 degrees and heat indices near 100 fueled stronger storms, according to CBS17. Behind the front, the Triangle should see a sharp cooldown, with highs settling into the mid-to-upper 70s and lows dipping into the upper 50s and low 60s for the rest of the week. The timing lines up with the calendar: astronomical fall officially arrives at 8:05 p.m. Tuesday in Raleigh, capping one of the hottest late-September stretches the region has seen in years. Forecasters expect a milder, drier pattern to settle in by Wednesday as the front clears the area.

MONDAY, September 21, 2026

🏛 Raleigh Abandons Youth Curfew Push After Feedback

Raleigh City Council members have quietly dropped their pursuit of a citywide youth curfew, with council member Christina Jones telling the News & Observer the body reached unanimous agreement to shelve the idea, WRAL reported. Mayor Janet Cowell and other leaders had floated a curfew after thousands of teenagers converged on Brier Creek and Glenwood South over Fourth of July weekend in so-called "teen takeovers" that ended in shootings and dozens of arrests, but the council reviewed data from other cities and found most of those arrested were over 18 and often not even from Raleigh, undercutting the rationale for restricting minors. Numerous residents also warned a curfew would unfairly punish the city's youth without addressing the underlying causes, according to the News & Observer. The Oct. 12 community meeting originally billed to gather input on curfew rules will instead focus on youth crime prevention and expanding safe spaces for teens.

🚨 600 Cars Swarm Durham Street for Reckless Stunt Meetup

An estimated 600 vehicles packed the 2000 block of Avondale Drive in Durham on Saturday night for an impromptu "car meet" that police say featured numerous careless and reckless driving stunts, CBS17 reported. Officers responded to the gathering near Foushee Street and the U.S. 501 Business split just before 9:40 p.m. after a noise complaint, with Durham police assisted by the Durham County Sheriff's Office and the North Carolina State Highway Patrol, according to WRAL. Despite the size of the crowd and the stunts described by officers, enforcement was minimal: police cited and towed just one driver for careless and reckless driving, made no arrests and reported no injuries, per Hoodline. The gathering is the latest in a string of large, informal car meets and street takeovers that have periodically drawn law enforcement attention across the Triangle this year.

🚨 Man Killed in Shooting at North Raleigh Apartment Complex

A 27-year-old man, identified by police as Basadar Lawya St. Phard, died after being shot Friday afternoon at The Park at Northridge Apartments in the 7300 block of Brookbank Lane, WRAL reported. Officers responded to a reported shooting around 4:55 p.m. and found St. Phard with gunshot wounds; he was taken to a hospital, where he later died, according to ABC11. An eyewitness told ABC11 they saw an altercation between a man and several women in the parking lot just before multiple gunshots rang out, and officers found shell casings and a handgun at the scene. One person was detained and treated for minor injuries, but after consulting with the Wake County District Attorney's Office, Raleigh police released that person without charges, and the case remains under investigation, per CBS17.

🏛 Wake Commissioners Set to Vote Today on WakeMed-Atrium Deal

Wake County commissioners are scheduled to vote this afternoon at 2 p.m. on amendments to WakeMed's articles of incorporation that would clear the way for the hospital system to combine with Charlotte-based Atrium Health, capping months of public fights over the proposal, WRAL reported. Opponents, led by the newly formed Patients Union and the North Carolina Justice Center, planned a "Stop the Atrium Takeover" rally at Nash Square at 12:30 p.m. ahead of the vote, arguing the deal will raise prices and cut services; Commissioner Vickie Adamson has already said she intends to vote no on the transfer agreement. WakeMed and Atrium counter that the revised deal includes a $2 billion investment pledge, more than 3,300 new jobs and $150 million in cash for the county, along with added county protections and control over indigent care, according to the Wake Weekly. Under the amended articles, Wake commissioners would still appoint eight of WakeMed's 14 board members, with Atrium naming the remaining six.

🤝 Durham Launches Program to Keep Small Businesses Locally Owned

The City of Durham, together with the North Carolina Employee Ownership Center and the national Democracy at Work Institute, officially unveiled the "Keep It Durham-Owned" initiative at a press conference at Provident1898, ABC11 reported. The yearlong program offers small-business owners free, confidential counseling on succession planning and employee-ownership models — including employee stock ownership plans, ownership trusts and worker cooperatives — meant to keep businesses and wealth in local hands when an owner retires or sells. City officials said the effort responds to a real gap: Durham County is home to more than 6,500 employer firms, roughly 34% of them owned by people 55 or older, while nationally only about 30% of business owners have a formal succession plan in place. The launch marks Durham's entry into DAWI's nationwide Employee Ownership Cities network, joining a growing list of municipalities promoting worker ownership as a business-continuity strategy, with the core program running through the end of 2027.

🏈 Belichick Resigns as UNC Probe Concludes; Tar Heels Fall to Clemson

UNC's seven-week internal investigation into its football program ended Thursday with defensive coordinator Steve Belichick's resignation, after the school found his conduct showed "a disregard for Carolina's values and standards of conduct," even as it deemed the actions "isolated in scope and not a systemic failure," ESPN reported. Investigators conducted more than 60 interviews, including one with head coach Bill Belichick, examining allegations that Steve Belichick used or supplied drugs to a player and a possible relationship between a program employee and a player; his resignation takes effect in late October when his medical leave ends, and follows the earlier departure of general manager Michael Lombardi, according to WRAL. Bill Belichick declined to discuss the now-closed probe Friday night at Clemson, where his Tar Heels squandered a 17-3 first-half lead and lost 28-20 after a nearly two-and-a-half-hour weather delay, with Gideon Davidson's go-ahead touchdown run putting the Tigers in front with five minutes left, WRAL's game recap said. The loss, UNC's eighth straight at Clemson, leaves the Tar Heels searching for stability just as the program tries to move past a scandal that has now claimed two senior staffers.

🎓 CHCCS Votes to Dissolve Glenwood's Mandarin World Language Track

The Chapel Hill-Carrboro City Schools board voted 5-1 Thursday night to dissolve Glenwood Elementary's Mandarin World Language pathway, the 45-minute-a-week program that enrolls roughly half the school's students, and will reassign those children to their neighborhood schools, according to WUNC. The vote leaves untouched Glenwood's separate Mandarin Dual Language immersion track, which will move intact to Rashkis Elementary when Glenwood closes in 2027, Chapelboro reported. The superintendent recommended the change as part of Glenwood's broader closure, a move the district projects will save about $2 million annually amid declining enrollment districtwide. Several parents who addressed the board said the World Language track was many students' only exposure to Mandarin and argued they were given too little notice before the option was eliminated, per WRAL.

🚨 11-Year-Old Killed in Hit-and-Run Near Zebulon; Driver Held on $1M Bond

An 11-year-old boy died early Saturday after the SUV he was riding in left N.C. 96 near Doyle Road north of Zebulon and struck a tree, with the driver fleeing the scene, Hoodline reported. Troopers say 40-year-old Correll Jermaine Deans of Spring Hope was found and arrested hours later; he faces three felony hit-and-run charges, misdemeanor death by motor vehicle and two counts of failing to properly secure passengers under 16, since two other children in the vehicle were also hurt, according to CBS17. A Wake County magistrate set Deans' bond at $1 million, and he also faces an outstanding Nash County warrant for felony heroin possession, WRAL reported. Deans remains in the Wake County Detention Center ahead of a Sept. 28 court date.

🌡 Severe Storms Spark Cary House Fire, Displace Family of Five

A suspected lightning strike touched off a two-alarm fire that tore through a home in the 900 block of Strathorn Drive in Cary on Saturday afternoon as severe thunderstorms carrying 60 mph wind gusts and hail moved through Wake County, Hoodline reported. Firefighters responded around 4 p.m. while the National Weather Service had the county under a severe thunderstorm watch, and crews confirmed significant structural damage but no injuries, per CBS17. The blaze displaced five residents, and neighbors quickly organized a GoFundMe for the family, with children from the neighborhood running a lemonade and snack stand Sunday to raise additional support, according to WRAL. The fire's cause remains under investigation, and no other homes were damaged.

🏈 Duke Stays Unbeaten, NC State Falls Late to Vanderbilt

Duke extended its winning streak to seven games Saturday, routing Stanford 35-7 at Wallace Wade Stadium behind Nate Sheppard's 154 rushing yards and two touchdowns plus a receiving score, with quarterback Walker Eget throwing for 202 yards and a touchdown, according to Duke Athletics. It marked the Blue Devils' fifth straight win in an ACC opener. NC State's day ended far less happily in Nashville, where Vanderbilt scored the winning touchdown with one second left to beat the Wolfpack 35-31 at FirstBank Stadium, per NC State Athletics. The loss snaps a hot start for the Wolfpack, who had opened the season with a 73-0 rout of Richmond the previous week, and drops them to 2-1 heading into conference play.
