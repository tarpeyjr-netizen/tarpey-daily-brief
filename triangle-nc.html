THURSDAY, AUGUST 20, 2026

🚨 Suspect in Coach's Killing Arrested After Pennsylvania Standoff

The man charged with killing Wake County teacher and girls' basketball coach Sheremy Gwenette Dillard was taken into custody in Pennsylvania after a brief standoff with officers, police said this week. Rickey Andra Harper, 60, faces a first-degree murder charge in the death of the 57-year-old educator, whose body was found with multiple stab wounds inside her Raleigh home shortly after noon last Thursday. Investigators say Harper was located hundreds of miles from the Triangle before surrendering to authorities. Dillard had spent years in Wake County classrooms and on the sidelines, and colleagues have described the loss as devastating to her school community. Extradition proceedings are expected to return Harper to North Carolina to face the charge.

🚨 Moore Square Sweep Yields 130-Plus Felony Charges

A summer-long enforcement operation centered on Raleigh's Moore Square has ended with 35 people facing more than 130 felony charges, according to North Carolina Alcohol Law Enforcement. ALE agents worked jointly with the Raleigh Police Department on the effort, which targeted persistent criminal activity in and around the downtown park and its surrounding blocks. The square sits at the center of Raleigh's warehouse and hospitality district and has drawn repeated attention from city officials weighing how to balance public safety with the park's role as a downtown gathering space. The announcement lands as the City Council prepares to take up a proposed teen curfew in September, a measure floated after a July shooting downtown.

🎓 Wake Board Again Divided Over Teacher's Hiring

For the second time this month, more than a dozen parents and residents packed a Wake County school board meeting to argue over the hiring of a teacher whom some speakers described as nonbinary or transgender. Board member Lynn Edmonds said the hire violated no district rules and that Wake County cannot legally discriminate against transgender applicants in its hiring decisions. Speakers on the other side pressed the board to revisit the decision, while supporters urged members to stand behind the employee. The dispute arrives days before traditional-calendar students return to class on Aug. 24, and alongside a separate board debate over budget trims needed to absorb state-mandated pay increases.

🏗️ Durham Weighs Affordable Housing at Old Police HQ

Durham's long-stalled plan to redevelop its former police headquarters downtown came back before the City Council on Monday, with a proposal to hand Charlotte-based Laurel Street a 99-year ground lease and more than $6 million in gap financing to build 80 affordable apartments. Twenty of the units would serve households earning below 30% of area median income, with 12 more below 50% and 48 below 60%. The housing would occupy roughly a quarter of the site, leaving the rest of the parcel's future unsettled. The package also gives Preservation North Carolina a 12-month window to find a buyer for the modernist Milton Small building before the council takes up a sale that could clear the way for demolition.

🏀 UNC and NC State to Meet Twice Under New Greensboro Deal

North Carolina and NC State have agreed to a two-year non-conference series in Greensboro, restoring a second annual meeting between the rivals after ACC scheduling changes ended their traditional home-and-home guarantee. The inaugural Tobacco Road Classic is set for Dec. 15 at the Greensboro Coliseum, with tickets going on sale Sept. 10 through the box office and Ticketmaster. The neutral-site arrangement guarantees Wolfpack and Tar Heel fans two regular-season matchups in 2026-27 and again the following year. It also softens a scheduling quirk that leaves NC State without a home game against either UNC or Duke for the first time in more than a century.

🌡️ Heat Advisory Today, Storms and Relief by Friday

The Triangle is under a heat advisory from 11 a.m. to 8 p.m. Thursday, with highs in the mid 90s and heat index values climbing toward 103 degrees. Forecasters have flagged another alert day before a cold front arrives to break the pattern. Highs drop into the 80s by Friday as scattered storms become likely, and additional storm chances linger into Saturday and Sunday. Officials continue to urge residents to limit afternoon exertion, check on older neighbors and keep pets indoors during peak heat.

TUESDAY, August 18, 2026

🏛️ Raleigh Sets September Vote on Teen Curfew

Raleigh city leaders moved a step closer Tuesday to adopting an 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. curfew for anyone under 18, though no vote was taken at the City Hall work session. Police Chief Rico Boyce told councilmembers that crimes with juvenile suspects are up 9% year over year — 894 so far in 2026 against 821 at this point last year — and pointed to a jump in rounds fired into vehicles and dwellings, from 15 incidents in 2025 to 190 this year. The proposal follows the July 4 Brier Creek shooting and the Glenwood South "teen takeover", and would mirror curfews already in place in Charlotte, Fayetteville, Rocky Mount and Winston-Salem. Critics including EmancipateNC's Dawn Blagrove warned the ordinance would fall disproportionately on Black children and called for community-based alternatives, while Boyce conceded a curfew "is not a fix, it is a tool." Two public hearings are planned next month ahead of a council vote expected Sept. 15.

🎓 Wake Schools Weigh Cuts to Cover State Pay Mandates

The board of North Carolina's largest school district took up budget reductions Tuesday night to close a gap opened by the new state budget's salary requirements. Wake County says staff bonuses require an additional $3.1 million in local funding while mandated raises for certified staff add $13.7 million, against a state mandate of an average 8% increase for teachers and instructional support and 3% for central office and noncertified employees. A board committee spent hours Tuesday afternoon debating options including lowering teacher supplements and scaling back the district's ReStart program. Board Chair Tyler Swanson called it "a tough budget" and said next year's will be tighter, adding he has "a lot of concerns about what do we do to ensure that there is not a lot of harm taking place in the classroom." State law requires the bonuses be distributed by October, which is driving the timeline.

🌡️ Another Weather Alert Day as Drought Keeps Easing

Tuesday was a WRAL Weather Alert Day under a heat advisory running 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., with humidity pushing the feels-like temperature past 100 — Raleigh's 17th heat alert of the season, roughly double the seasonal average. Highs stay in the 90s through Thursday before a slow-moving front brings scattered storms Friday and knocks weekend highs back into the 80s. The rain would land on ground that has already recovered considerably: the Aug. 13 drought report showed severe drought statewide down to 23% from 82% in July, and extreme drought down to 4% from 50%. Part of Chatham County is out of drought entirely, and forecasters expect most of the state — including the Triangle — could clear it by month's end. Forecasters note the weather looks favorable for bus rides when many area students return Monday.

🚨 Wake County Coach Killed; Boyfriend Charged With Murder

Tributes continued through the weekend for Sheremy Dillard, a women's basketball coach and recovery teacher at Felton Grove High School in Apex who was found stabbed to death in her Raleigh home Thursday. Dillard had previously spent years coaching and teaching at Carrboro High School, and friends described a mentor known for an infectious smile and a strong but comforting coaching style. Raleigh police have charged her boyfriend, Rickey Harper, with first-degree murder; investigators say he left North Carolina after the killing and was located outside Philadelphia on Friday with help from Flock cameras. The case has drawn renewed attention to domestic violence warning signs across Triangle communities. Anyone experiencing or concerned about a domestic violence situation can reach the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-SAFE.

⚖️ Courtney Jordan Foundation Leaders Face Judge

Two leaders of the Durham-based Courtney Jordan Foundation appeared in Person County court Monday on charges stemming from a high-speed chase that ended in a crash. Founder Courtney Jordan faces felony fleeing to elude arrest, injury to personal property and second-degree trespassing; CFO Nicholas Gallardo is charged with felony conspiracy, second-degree trespassing and simple possession of a controlled substance. Both told the judge they had retained counsel and entered no plea. The pair are also the subject of an ABC11 I-Team investigation into allegations the foundation failed to pay dozens of former employees, and the SBI has confirmed it is investigating possible financial crimes. CEO Kristen Picot, who attended in support, said the foundation is still active and working with the labor board but gave no timeline for paying workers. Separate fraud charges are pending in Durham County, with a court date later this month.

🏗️ Two Triangle Mainstays Close Their Doors

A pair of longtime local businesses shut down over the weekend. Adventure Landing on Capital Boulevard closed Sunday after 38 years of go-karts, mini golf and birthday parties, with owner Hank Woodburn saying it "has truly been a privilege to bring fun, laughter, and lifelong memories to so many families"; the company said its property lease was not renewed, following rumors in March that it denied at the time. Separately, Flavor Hills closed its downtown Raleigh and downtown Durham locations effective immediately on Saturday. The veteran- and Black-owned Southern comfort food restaurant, which opened in Raleigh in January 2024 as an offshoot of its original Jacksonville location, said in a Facebook statement that it "represented more than a restaurant. Flavor Hills represented hope." The owners plan to continue as the Flavor Hills Experience, focused on catering and private events.

MONDAY, AUGUST 17, 2026

🏛️ Developer Pulls Southeast Raleigh Data Center

Beacon Partners has withdrawn its request to annex land for a 100,000-square-foot data center in southeast Raleigh, the mayor said, ending a fight that had been building for weeks. The project was slated for a 13-acre parcel about a third of a mile from the Jones Sausage Road and Rock Quarry Road intersection, on land already zoned industrial mixed use. Opposition was overwhelming: the sign-up list for the public hearing filled to capacity within hours, and of the 32 residents expected to speak, 30 planned to oppose. Neighbors raised concerns about water and power demands and about the secrecy surrounding the project's end user. With the request pulled from the council agenda, both the comment period and the annexation vote scheduled for August 18 were cancelled.

🚨 Raleigh Police Address Downtown Death Rumors

Raleigh Police Chief Rico Boyce held a Monday afternoon news conference to confirm that a 32-year-old man found dead in downtown Raleigh over the weekend died by suicide. Officers and medics responded to the 300 block of South McDowell Street shortly after 3 p.m. Saturday after a report of a person hanging from a tree; the man was removed and pronounced dead despite life-saving efforts. Boyce walked through a timeline assembled from traffic and surveillance cameras showing the man walking alone toward the tree carrying an electrical cord, and said investigators found no evidence anyone else was involved. The department took the unusual step of going public after what it described as unverified information about the death spread widely online over the weekend.

🌡️ Durham Keeps Water Restrictions as Triangle Eases

Durham is holding firm on its Stage 2 water restrictions even as other Triangle systems relax theirs, with officials saying relief is not close. The city's reservoirs sit at roughly 55% capacity — about 144 days of supply — and the drought remains entrenched in the northern part of the county where Durham's watershed sits. Stage 2, in effect since June 15, bans spray irrigation, at-home car washing, and limits power washing, though hand watering, drip irrigation, and tree bags remain allowed. Raleigh has already lifted its restrictions, and voluntary limits were dropped in Siler City and southwest Chatham County, leaving Durham and Chatham as the holdouts. Recent rainfall has not moved the needle enough to change the city's posture.

🏗️ Adventure Landing Closes After 38 Years

Adventure Landing, a fixture on Capital Boulevard for 38 years, shut its doors to the public on Sunday. The park's miniature golf course, go-kart track, and arcade were a standing weekend destination for generations of Raleigh families. The business said its property lease was not renewed, ending the run at a site that has grown increasingly valuable as the Capital Boulevard corridor redevelops. No replacement location has been announced.

🎓 Students Return as Fall Semester Begins

The Triangle's campuses filled back up over the weekend as NC State opened its fall term today, the first day of classes for both the full semester and eight-week Session I. In Chapel Hill, UNC wrapped up move-in as students returned ahead of the start of classes, closing out the summer downtown lull on Franklin Street. The return was not without incident: NC State police stopped a man carrying a pistol and a rifle strapped to his body on Western Boulevard at Varsity Drive on Sunday after Raleigh police received a report of an armed person walking near campus. The man was later released from custody.

🏛️ Council Weighs Teen Curfew After July 4 Shooting

Raleigh City Council members are weighing whether to enact a teen curfew in response to violence over the Fourth of July weekend. The push follows a shooting in Glenwood South in which a 16-year-old was charged. Council discussion this month centered on how such an ordinance would be scoped and enforced, and whether it would meaningfully reduce late-night incidents downtown. Curfew proposals have drawn scrutiny in other North Carolina cities over enforcement equity, and Raleigh leaders have not settled on a final approach.

SUNDAY, AUGUST 16, 2026

🏗️ Developer Pulls Southeast Raleigh Data Center Plan

The developer behind a proposed data center in southeast Raleigh has withdrawn the plans, Raleigh's mayor said, ending a fight that had drawn unusually heavy neighborhood turnout. The project would have placed a nearly 100,000-square-foot facility on a 14-acre site along Jones Sausage Road, in a part of the city residents argued has long absorbed more than its share of industrial development. Dozens of people had signed up to speak against it before the withdrawal. The reversal lands as data center siting becomes a statewide flashpoint — Greensboro, Alamance County and Yadkin County are all weighing moratoriums on new facilities. Expect the Triangle's next proposal to face the same organized scrutiny.

🚨 Wake Coach Killed; Durham Sees Deadly Weekend

Raleigh police found 57-year-old Sheremy Gwenette Dillard, a Wake County Schools girls' basketball coach, dead at her Whistling Straits Way home Thursday afternoon after family members said they hadn't heard from her; her boyfriend, Rickey Harper, was charged with murder and arrested in Plymouth, Pennsylvania. Neighbors and former players described her as a fixture in the community, and the news rippled across Triangle school communities as the year began. In Durham, officers found a man shot to death on the 2500 block of Lane Street, days after a separate Middle Street shooting that ended with a suspect in custody. Wake Forest police, meanwhile, are still searching for a Durham man after a pursuit ended in a crash.

🌡️ Dangerous Heat, Storm Risk, and a Deepening Drought

Heat advisories remain in force across the Triangle, with advisories continuing into Monday alongside an evening severe-storm risk. Afternoon highs are pushing the mid-90s with heat indices as high as 110, and Monday is forecast to be the hottest day of the stretch — close to the RDU record of 99 degrees set in 1997. The heat is landing on already parched ground: despite scattered showers, Wake, Durham and Orange counties remain in severe drought or worse, and forecasters expect rain to stay spotty. It fits a longer pattern — July was the hottest month on record nationally, and Carolinas summers are warming measurably. In Orange County, a boil water advisory remains in effect for some Hillsborough customers after a main leak dropped system pressure.

🏛️ After the Takeovers, Raleigh Asks Where Teens Can Go

In the wake of the so-called "teen takeovers" that led to recent shootings in Raleigh, WUNC reported this week on a quieter structural problem: teenagers in Wake County have almost nowhere to gather without direct adult supervision. Malls have added parental escort policies, arcades have closed, and third spaces built for young people have thinned out over two decades. Experts told the station that the vacuum has contributed to both the violence and the crowd dynamics that produced it, and that curfews address the symptom rather than the cause. The question now facing Raleigh officials is whether enforcement or investment does more to break the cycle heading into the school year.

🏘️ North Hills Growth Squeezes Out Affordable Apartments

Kane Realty's planned 28-acre expansion of the North Hills Innovation District is forcing the closure of The Pointe at Midtowne on Navaho Drive, one of the few naturally affordable apartment communities left in midtown Raleigh. Residents there are being displaced in a submarket where comparable rents are scarce, a direct illustration of the trade-off behind the Triangle's development boom. It's a live tension elsewhere in the region too — Wake school leaders are pitching school-site and public developments to build affordable housing for teachers, while the broader debate over who should raise Wake teacher pay continues. Downtown Raleigh, for its part, still has roughly $1.5 billion in projects under construction.

🏒 Canes Sell Out, UNC Floats an Athletics LLC, Cary Turns 50

The Carolina Hurricanes have sold out season tickets ahead of the coming campaign, leaving single-game buyers to fight over what's left — a marker of how far the franchise's local footprint has grown. In the same roundup, WUNC reported that UNC is proposing a limited liability company to manage and maximize its athletics revenue, a structural move that would put Chapel Hill among the schools formally corporatizing their sports operations in the revenue-sharing era. On the field, No. 8 UNC women's soccer opened the season with a 4-0 win over Fairfield. And in Cary, more than 250 artists are set for the 50th Lazy Daze festival, the town's longest-running street fair.
