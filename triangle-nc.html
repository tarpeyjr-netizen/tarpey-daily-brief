🏈 Philip Rivers Formally Introduced as NC State Coach

Former Wolfpack quarterback Philip Rivers was formally introduced as NC State's head football coach on Monday, three days after the Board of Trustees approved a five-year deal running through 2031. According to a contract copy obtained by ABC11, Rivers will earn a $2.437 million base salary that rises $100,000 a year starting in 2028, plus supplemental pay starting at $812,500, which comes to roughly half of what Dave Doeren earned this season. Rivers, 44, comes from six seasons at St. Michael Catholic High in Fairhope, Alabama, and has never coached in college or the NFL. "By all means, I know I've got a lot to learn," he said. He had landed at RDU on Sunday with athletics director Boo Corrigan and met with players that night, and he named former Alabama recruiting director Jatavis Sanders as general manager.

🏗 Report Credits Raleigh Zoning Reforms for Housing Boom

A new Pew Charitable Trusts report finds that Raleigh's zoning and building-code changes since 2021 sharply increased housing production, including "missing middle" types like duplexes, townhouses and accessory dwelling units. Annual permits rose 74%, from an average of 3,301 units in 2017–2020 to 5,731 in 2021–2024, compared with 29% growth statewide. The city removed density limits in non-single-family districts in 2021 and 2022 and abolished citywide parking minimums in 2022, which the report says lowered building costs. Inflation-adjusted rents, which climbed 38% from 2017 to 2022, then fell 15% through 2025, with the steepest declines in older apartments that tend to house lower-income tenants.

🚨 Three Monday Night Shootings in Durham Leave One Dead

Durham police are investigating three separate shootings Monday evening that left one person dead and two men injured. Around 6:15 p.m., a man standing outside a business in the 400 block of Old Oxford Road was shot by someone in a car that fled, and about an hour later another man was shot outside a business at Linwood Avenue and South Alston Avenue; both had non-life-threatening injuries. Around 8:45 p.m., one person was killed in the 1000 block of Allgood Street in the Bragtown area. Police say the shootings do not appear to be connected, no arrests have been made, and tips can go to Durham CrimeStoppers at (919) 683-1200. Earlier Monday, a pedestrian was also struck and killed near Northpointe Drive and Strebor Street in a crash where the driver stayed at the scene.

🌡 Late First Freeze Arrives for Central NC Farmers

Central North Carolina farmers woke Sunday to frost on their crops for the first time this fall, nearly a week later than Raleigh's average first-freeze date of around Nov. 9. That average has already shifted about three weeks later since 1970. Garrett Strickland of Moore's Produce said hot peppers and eggplant were still available in mid-November but will be gone within about a month, while Kate Wolschon of Parsons Farm said greens sales rose because many customers wait for the first frost to buy collards. Crews spent the weekend insulating pipes and checking pumps and other equipment while still harvesting and packing produce.

🏈 ACC Suspends, Fines UNC and Louisville Over Brawl

The ACC on Monday suspended one player from each team and fined both schools after the fight that ended Louisville's 16-14 win over North Carolina at Kenan Stadium on Saturday night. UNC defensive back Greg Smith III and Louisville linebacker Stanquan Clark will each sit out the first half of this week's game. Both programs also received public reprimands. The league said the teams violated its sportsmanship policy and called the behavior "unacceptable," but it did not disclose the fine amounts.

🏒 Ehlers Named NHL Third Star After Helsinki Sweep

Hurricanes forward Nikolaj Ehlers was named the NHL's Third Star of the Week after posting five points (one goal, four assists) in Carolina's two Global Series wins over Seattle in Helsinki. He had two points in the opener and three assists in the finale, which the Canes won behind their power play on Saturday. Ehlers is riding a three-game assist streak and has 15 points in his last 11 games. In a roster move over the weekend, defenseman Mike Reilly was claimed off waivers by Colorado.

🚨 Wake Grand Jury Indicts Ex-Rep. Jason Saine

Former state Rep. Jason Saine, a Lincolnton Republican, and David Coble, treasurer of the Mooresville nonprofit Greater Carolina, have been indicted by a Wake County grand jury, Wake County District Attorney Lorrin Freeman announced Sunday. Each faces two counts of obtaining property by false pretenses and two counts of embezzlement of more than $100,000, and prosecutors allege Saine aided and abetted Coble. Prosecutors say Greater Carolina credit cards were used for personal expenses that had nothing to do with the group's economic development mission. Earlier search warrants showed about $30,000 in such charges on Coble's cards, which he said were inadvertent and later repaid. Saine denies wrongdoing, saying he looks forward to clearing his name, and both men are due in court Dec. 7.

🏒 NHL-Best Canes Return Home From Finland Sweep

The Hurricanes came back from the NHL Global Series in Finland at 16-3-2, first in the league standings, after outscoring the Seattle Kraken 9-2 over the two-game sweep in Helsinki. That +7 goal differential ties for the best ever by a team on a single overseas trip. Carolina also leads the NHL with 87 goals scored and has allowed the fewest, 46. In a roster move Sunday, defenseman Mike Reilly was claimed off waivers by Colorado. The Canes return to Lenovo Center Thursday at 7 p.m. against the Montreal Canadiens.

⚽ UNC Sweeps Both ACC Soccer Titles in Cary

North Carolina's men's and women's soccer teams both won ACC Tournament championships Sunday at WakeMed Soccer Park in Cary. The No. 2 women beat No. 10 Virginia 2-0 behind goals from Bella Devey and Amari Manning, earning the program's 23rd ACC title and its first since 2019, and Devey was named tournament MVP. The No. 12 men stunned No. 1 SMU 3-0, handing the Mustangs their first loss of 2026 and holding them without a shot on goal. Joschi Schelb opened the scoring with his 18th goal of the season, Ryan Zellefrow added two in the second half, and the margin tied the largest ever in an ACC men's title game.

🏈 Duke Falls at No. 5 Miami, 36-25

Duke's football team lost 36-25 at No. 5 Miami Saturday in Miami Gardens and dropped to 7-3 overall and 5-3 in the ACC. Nate Sheppard did a bit of everything for the Blue Devils, with 240 total yards, two rushing touchdowns and a pair of 47-yard runs. Quarterback Walker Eget went 26 of 42 for 195 yards with a touchdown and an interception. Miami's Darian Mensah threw for 382 yards, and the Hurricanes need just one more win to clinch a spot in the Dec. 5 ACC title game in Charlotte. Duke closes its home schedule Friday against Clemson.

🏗 Raleigh Opens $10M Blue Ridge Road Bike-Ped Corridor

Raleigh cut the ribbon on the Big Red Bridge Saturday, finishing the $10 million Blue Ridge Road Bike-Ped Improvement Project. The project adds a multi-use path for people walking and biking and better sidewalks along Blue Ridge Road, from north of Trinity Road to Reedy Creek Road. It also includes the city's first protected intersection, at Blue Ridge and Reedy Creek roads. The city teamed up with the West Raleigh Alliance on a Multimodal Celebration Day centered at the NC Museum of Art Visitor Center.

🤝 Durham Crash Victims' Families Rally for Safer Streets

Families of people killed by drivers, crash survivors and safety advocates gathered at CCB Plaza in downtown Durham Sunday for a Bike Durham vigil, part of a worldwide day honoring victims of traffic violence. Rich Davis marked one year since his 18-year-old son Iazias, a Hillside High senior, was struck and killed on Guess Road. Duke student Frank Cao described surviving a June bike crash with a traumatic brain injury and 16 fractures. Advocates asked Durham leaders to fund two Vision Zero coordinator positions, create an advisory group for victims' families, and update the city and county bike and pedestrian plan.

🏒 Canes Sweep Kraken in Helsinki, Welcome Back Staal

The Hurricanes beat Seattle 5-1 Saturday at Veikkaus Arena to sweep their two-game NHL Global Series in Helsinki, scoring three of their goals on the power play. Mark Jankowski and Andrei Svechnikov each scored twice and Jackson Blake added a power-play goal, while Nikolaj Ehlers assisted on three goals and Pyotr Kochetkov made 12 saves to improve to 12-1. Carolina is now 16-3-2, first in the league, and is the first NHL team to win each of its first four games played outside North America. Before the game the team activated captain Jordan Staal from injured reserve after he missed time with a calf injury suffered in warm-ups Oct. 10, and placed defenseman Mike Reilly on waivers to make room. The Canes fly back to Raleigh on Sunday and host Montreal on Thursday at 7 p.m.

🏈 UNC Falls to Louisville in Game Ending in Brawl

North Carolina lost 16-14 to Louisville Saturday night in Chapel Hill when Jabari Mack intercepted a fourth-down pass with 58 seconds left. The ball bounced off receiver Jordan Shipp, and Louisville kneeled out the win. Starting quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. left in the second half after a hard hit on a sack, and backup Travis Burgess struggled to sustain drives, though Benjamin Hall's second rushing touchdown with 3:43 left cut the deficit to two. Players from both teams shoved and threw apparent punches after the final snap before officials separated them, and Bill Belichick was seen trying to break it up. The Tar Heels fall to 5-5 and 2-4 in the ACC, still one win short of bowl eligibility, with a trip to Virginia next Saturday.

🏈 NC State Blows Late Lead in Senior Day Loss

NC State lost 34-31 to Syracuse on Senior Day at its final home game of the season, giving up 14 straight points in the fourth quarter after leading 28-20 entering the period. Quarterback CJ Bailey threw an interception that Tunmise Adeleye returned 18 yards for a touchdown with about five minutes left, and Syracuse took the lead on a 7-yard touchdown pass and two-point conversion with 48 seconds remaining. Bailey then fumbled on the final drive and Syracuse recovered with seven seconds left. The Wolfpack fall to 4-6 one day after trustees approved Philip Rivers' contract as the program's next head coach.

🚨 RDU Travelers Still Recovering From FAA Ground Stop

Raleigh-Durham International Airport still had 115 delays and 108 cancellations as of late Saturday night, a day after a nationwide FAA ground stop grounded about 3,500 planes. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said an old disconnected wire flooded an air traffic control router during a copper-to-fiber upgrade and disrupted radar feeds. Officials do not believe a cyberattack was involved, and investigators are still trying to find the exact fault. At RDU, some passengers waited three to four hours for their bags while airline employees sorted luggage from canceled flights. The airport is telling travelers to check flight status with their airlines, which must offer free rebooking or a refund on canceled flights.

🏛 Durham County Approves $49.5M Emergency Operations Center

Durham County commissioners approved a revised $49.5 million budget for a new Emergency Operations Center and backup 911 call center, down from an earlier estimate of $55.2 million. The two-story building will go up on the site of the current EOC, which dates to the 1940s and will be demolished once the new one is finished. It will also house a backup sheriff's communications center, and the backup 911 center will take calls for both the county and the City of Durham if the primary centers go down. Emergency management chief Elizabeth Schroeder called the current building "antiquated." Crews are expected to mobilize this week, and phase one is scheduled to finish in 2028.

🎓 Wake Schools Pledge Transparency on $680M Bond

The Wake County Public School System says it is changing how it tells families about repairs as it prepares to spend the $680 million school bond voters approved this month. The bond sets aside $98.7 million for infrastructure replacements, about half for HVAC work, compared with $23.7 million in the 2022 bond. About two dozen schools, including Lynn Road Elementary, are expected to get new HVAC systems next year. Superintendent Robert Taylor acknowledged a large deferred-maintenance backlog the bond cannot cover. Parent Lisa Rozier said she wants clearer timelines and priorities: "I do not feel that full transparency has been delivered."

🏈 Rivers Signs Five-Year, $18.25M Deal at NC State

NC State's Board of Trustees on Friday approved a five-year contract for Philip Rivers, the former Wolfpack quarterback, to take over as head football coach. The deal runs through 2031 and is worth $18.25 million in salary and supplemental pay, starting at $3.25 million in 2027 with $200,000 in annual raises, plus up to $2.2 million a year in academic and on-field bonuses. If Rivers leaves early he owes a buyout that falls from $5 million before the end of 2027 to $1 million before the end of 2030. Rivers, 44, has no college coaching experience. Before this job he played 18 NFL seasons and coached six years at St. Michael Catholic High in Alabama. His pay is about half of what Dave Doeren earned before NC State fired him Oct. 24, and the school still owes Doeren a buyout of about $10 million. Rivers will be introduced at a news conference Monday.

🎓 NC State Trustees Approve Another 3% Tuition Increase

Incoming in-state undergraduates at NC State will pay 3% more in tuition in fall 2027, or $202, after trustees approved the increase Friday. The board also approved higher fees for student media, the student center, dining and housing. Together the changes are projected to bring in a little over $4.5 million, which will go toward the educational experience and faculty promotional raises. It is the second year in a row of increases, after this fall's increase ended an in-state freeze across the UNC System that dated to 2017. A few protesters held signs asking for higher graduate stipends, and the NC State Campus Workers Union said graduate students pay the system's highest combined tuition and fees. The UNC Board of Governors still has to approve the increases.

🏒 Canes Face Kraken Again in Helsinki Today

The Hurricanes play the Seattle Kraken again at 1 p.m. ET Saturday at Veikkaus Arena, the second of their two NHL Global Series games in Helsinki. A win would make Carolina the first NHL team to go 4-0 in international games. Carolina is 15-3-2 and leads the Metropolitan Division after beating Seattle 4-1 on Thursday. Pyotr Kochetkov is expected to start in goal, and captain Jordan Staal could return from the torn calf he suffered Oct. 11. Sebastian Aho has 28 points in 20 games, his best start to a season, and Jackson Blake brings a career-best 10-game point streak. The Canes return home Thursday to face Montreal.

🚨 SBI Investigating Inmate Death at Durham County Jail

The State Bureau of Investigation is looking into the death of an inmate at the Durham County jail. Detention officers found the person in distress around 9:30 p.m. Thursday and began medical aid, but the inmate died at the scene after EMS was called. The Durham County Sheriff's Office has not released the inmate's name while it notifies next of kin, and it has not given a cause of death. The SBI was called in under standard protocol, and the sheriff's office is also running an internal review.

🎓 Judge Puts Restructuring Officer in Charge at St. Aug's

A bankruptcy judge has approved Malcomb Coley as chief restructuring officer of Raleigh's Saint Augustine's University. Coley now makes the decisions for the school during its Chapter 11 reorganization, a power the board previously held. He is a former St. Aug's professor and an accounting professional with bankruptcy experience, and he spent eight years as a trustee at Queens University in Charlotte. The university lost its accreditation at the end of last school year and is not holding classes this fall. It has until Jan. 8, 2027 to file a reorganization plan, after missing its first deadline and getting a 60-day extension. The bankruptcy administrator and lender Self-Help Ventures Fund both called the appointment a positive step.

🎓 Durham Educators Push for Extra-Duty and Hazard Pay

The Durham Association of Educators has set six priorities for its monthly meet-and-confer sessions with Durham Public Schools. North Carolina bars public employees from negotiating contracts, so the group uses these sessions instead. Among the priorities are protected planning time on required workdays and stipends that are the same at every school for extra duties and for covering vacant positions. The group also wants time-and-a-half hazard pay for the few staff who go into buildings during closures. More than half of the district's teachers and support staff belong to the group. President Mika Hunter Twietmeyer said the district has suggested it may need to close schools to fill its budget gap, and she pushed back: "we don't think that we're going to shrink our way into greatness."
