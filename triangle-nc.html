THURSDAY, September 3, 2026

🌡️ Broken A/C sends Triangle students home again

Air conditioning failures forced early dismissals across Triangle schools this week as temperatures inside classrooms climbed into the upper 90s. Students at Hillside High School in Durham were sent home shortly after 1 p.m. Wednesday when the building's cooling system stopped working, and Durham Public Schools said Thursday's schedule would depend on whether repairs held. A day earlier, students at Green Level High School in Cary had to be relocated for the same reason. Wake County Superintendent Robert Taylor said more than a third of the district's schools had an HVAC problem at the time of inspection, noting the district maintains 5,700 pieces of equipment across 204 buildings. WRAL has asked leaders in both counties why the failures keep recurring and what is being done to prevent them.

🎓 Wake test scores climb past pre-pandemic levels

New state testing data released Wednesday shows Wake County students posting their strongest results in years, with two-thirds to three-quarters passing end-of-grade and end-of-course exams. Reading proficiency hit 65.7% — the highest since at least 2018 — while math reached 68.8%, more than three points above pre-pandemic marks. Biology saw the sharpest jump, climbing from 60.9% to 68.9% in a single year, and English II rose five points to 75.3%. Knightdale Elementary, a historically low-performing school, saw reading proficiency leap from 42.8% to 56.9% after a new principal restructured schedules around small-group instruction. Statewide scores also rose, though some subjects still trail 2019 levels.

🚨 Eight charged in Garner apartment shootout

Court documents filed this week reveal that a juvenile and seven adults now face charges in an August 11 shooting at Timber Creek Apartments on Evolve Drive in Garner. Search warrants returned September 2 describe a shootout between two groups police said had "beef" following an earlier Raleigh shooting involving family members on both sides. Gunfire struck three vehicles and pierced the wall of an occupied apartment, though no one was injured. Charges range from felony conspiracy to five counts of assault with a deadly weapon with intent to kill. Separately, Wake County deputies ended a three-and-a-half-hour standoff at a Floyd Drive home in Raleigh early Thursday, arresting a man after a reported domestic assault.

🤝 Chapel Hill mourns UNC historian lost at Eastwood Lake

The widow of longtime UNC history professor Konrad Jarausch spoke publicly this week about the swim at Eastwood Lake that ended in his death. Hannelore Jarausch said the couple often swam together near Lakeshore Drive, but that Tuesday morning she surfaced from a longer lap and could not find him. With no phone and no one else at the lake, she went to a neighboring house for help; search teams recovered his body around 2:30 p.m. Jarausch taught for decades in Hamilton Hall and wrote more than 50 books, leaving behind two sons and four grandchildren. Chapel Hill police said they had no new updates on the investigation, and the Lake Forest Association describes Eastwood as a "swim at your own risk" lake.

🏒 Stanley Cup and Canes headline Cary pickleball night

The Stanley Cup comes to Cary Tennis Park on Thursday evening as Carolina Hurricanes players trade sticks for paddles at the Veolia Pickleball National Championships. Sebastian Aho, Andrei Svechnikov, Cam Ward and Justin Williams are all slated to play against some of the country's top pickleball athletes. The night opens at 6 p.m. with a Stormy meet-and-greet and photo opportunities with the Cup, followed by the exhibition match at 7 p.m. A portion of ticket sales benefits the Carolina Hurricanes Foundation. The championships run at the Cary Tennis Park through September 6.

🏈 Duke opens title defense Saturday at Wallace Wade

Defending ACC champion Duke begins its season Saturday at 3:30 p.m. against Tulane at Wallace Wade Stadium, with the Blue Devils installed as roughly a 9.5-point favorite over a Green Wave team returning 68 players. Duke arrives with far more turnover — quarterback Darian Mensah, who beat his former Tulane team last season before leading Duke to the ACC title and a Sun Bowl win, has transferred to Miami along with his top three receivers. UNC, fresh off an upset win over TCU in Ireland, sits idle this weekend before opening at home against East Tennessee State on September 12. The Tar Heels and Blue Devils meet October 17.

WEDNESDAY, September 2, 2026

🎓 UNC students harassed with racial slurs at campus watch party

A man unaffiliated with UNC-Chapel Hill is in custody after university police say he forced his way into Greenlaw Hall on Monday night and yelled racial slurs at Black students gathered for a watch party. Students told WRAL they dropped to the floor when the man reached toward his waistband, fearing a weapon, before realizing he was carrying an aerosol can and a lighter. UNC police said officers took 17 minutes to reach the scene after the first 911 call and that no Alert Carolina was issued because there was "no ongoing threat to campus safety" — a decision students are now questioning, along with how a locked building was breached. The African Students Association, Black Student Movement and the campus chapter of the National Society of Black Engineers said in Instagram statements that the incident undermined spaces built for students to gather safely. Police plan to charge the man with disorderly conduct, vandalism, property damage and aggravated assault once he is medically cleared, and the Orange County District Attorney will decide whether hate crime charges apply.

🌡️ Near-record heat collides with Wake County's HVAC backlog

Green Level High School in Cary told families Tuesday that both chillers cooling the building are failing — one needs full replacement, the other an invasive repair that will take it offline for a day or more — just as the Triangle enters a stretch of unseasonable heat with highs near 100 degrees and heat indices of 103 to 108 through Friday. Administrators are running fans, relocating classes to cooler wings and adding water breaks while keeping the regular schedule, though parents may check students out early. Green Level joins at least seven other Wake schools with confirmed outages this year, including Millbrook and South Garner high schools and Lynn Road, Banks Road, Wendell, Douglas and Penny Road elementaries. A WRAL Investigates analysis of inspection records found more than a third of Wake County schools had an HVAC issue at the time of inspection. The district employs 13 HVAC technicians, and leaders say repairs account for roughly a quarter of an $800 million deferred maintenance backlog.

🤝 Wake at Home moves 33 people from a Raleigh encampment into housing

Thirty-three people who had been living at a Raleigh encampment are moving into their own homes through Wake County's Wake at Home initiative, and the campsite has since closed. Eileen Rosa, director of the county's Continuum of Care lead agency, said the result followed eight weeks of intensive outreach, with teams visiting early mornings and evenings because some residents were working. Those housed include veterans, couples, older adults and a pregnant family placed before the baby arrived; each receives a year-long rental subsidy plus case management. The City of Raleigh, Oak City Cares, Triangle Family Services, Alliance Health and WakeMed's HEART team all took part. The first phase of Wake at Home aims to move 400 people directly into housing, with organizers pledging to line up resources before any further encampment closures.

🚨 Rabid goats from traveling petting zoo spark Durham County warnings

State health officials say three goats from the mobile petting zoo Farm Adventures tested positive for rabies and a fourth is presumed infected, with possible human exposures across seven North Carolina counties dating to July 28. Two of the events tied to the exposures were in Durham County, where fewer than 60 people have reported potential contact with the baby goats. Investigators believe a skunk entered the goats' pen in late July; the animals were euthanized to stop further spread. Veterinarian Michael Martin urged anyone who touched the goats to call their local health department, noting that while he has never heard of a human contracting rabies from a goat, livestock transmission is possible and there is no labeled rabies vaccine for the species. North Carolina's permitting and sanitation rules for animal-contact exhibits largely apply only to sanctioned agricultural fairs, a gap parents affected by the scare want closed.

🚨 Body of UNC professor emeritus recovered from Eastwood Lake

Search crews recovered the body of 85-year-old Konrad Jarausch from Eastwood Lake in Chapel Hill on Tuesday afternoon. Jarausch, a professor emeritus of history at UNC-Chapel Hill, had been swimming near Lakeshore Drive in a residential area Tuesday morning when he went missing. Chapel Hill police and fire crews were called to the lake around 11 a.m., and emergency teams from Orange, Chatham, Durham and Alamance counties joined the search. His body was found around 2:30 p.m.

THURSDAY, AUGUST 20, 2026

🏗️ Durham weighs $450M-plus reset of NC 147 through Hayti

Durham City Council members reviewed three options this week for reimagining the Durham Freeway, the highway whose construction displaced thousands of residents and gutted Hayti, once one of North Carolina's most prosperous Black communities. The choices on the table are replacing sections of NC 147 with an urban boulevard at roughly $500 million, capping the freeway with a land bridge for about $450 million, or simply modernizing the existing roadway for a similar $450 million. Mayor Leo Williams said the freeway cap is his preferred path because it would reconnect Hayti to downtown, framing the project as a chance to "repair the harms of the past." Eighty-seven-year-old Hayti native Melvin Speight, who watched the original construction erase blocks of homes and businesses, told ABC11 the neighborhood's hope "can be reestablished." Durham's Transportation Department has asked NCDOT for a condition assessment of bridges along the corridor, which will help determine which option advances.

🚨 Raleigh closing downtown bus station tunnel over safety concerns

Raleigh city leaders announced Wednesday that the pedestrian tunnel linking Wilmington Street to the GoRaleigh bus station will be sealed next week, with work starting Tuesday and wrapping by Wednesday. Assistant City Manager Evan Raleigh said the decision followed sustained complaints from downtown businesses and visitors, adding that "this is ultimately about safety." Raleigh police Capt. Matt Chestnut said closing the tunnel lets officers concentrate patrols around the station and Moore Square and push out loitering, drug sales and alcohol use, with more foot, bike and UTV patrols expected in the area. Riders will need to reach the station via Hargett or Martin streets once the closure is complete. City officials described the move as a first step in a wider conversation about downtown safety that also includes Glenwood South.

🎓 Wake schools drop color codes as 160,000 students return Monday

With more than 160,000 students heading back to class Monday, Wake County Public Schools is retiring its red-yellow-green emergency codes in favor of plain-language commands such as secure, evacuate and shelter. Senior director of security Kendrick Scott said the change removes a layer of translation at the worst possible moment: "In a time of panic, you may not be able to articulate yourself." The new protocol also adds a "hold" status that keeps a school contained while administrators investigate, filling the gap between a full lockdown and the old code yellow. Wake is separately rolling out mandatory safety-awareness videos for middle and high schoolers during the first week, covering inappropriate behavior and adult misconduct as required by a 2023 state law. Year-round schools are already using the system, with drills planned for traditional-calendar campuses; Durham and Chapel Hill-Carrboro also return Monday, Aug. 24.

📊 Wake County tourism hits record $3.4 billion in visitor spending

Visit Raleigh reported Thursday that 19 million visitors came to Wake County in 2025, spending more than $3.4 billion and generating over $343 million in local and state tax revenue — both records. President and CEO Dennis Edwards said that tax haul flows back into schools, public safety and roads, calling it a reinvestment in the community rather than a windfall for the hospitality sector alone. The organization credited growth in restaurants, attractions and tourism jobs, and pointed to the Omni Hotel and the new Red Hat Amphitheater as drivers of further gains. Edwards also argued the Carolina Hurricanes' Stanley Cup run raised Raleigh's national profile enough to help land a Major League Baseball team. Notably, the 2025 figures predate this year's championship, and Visit Raleigh says 2026 is already tracking ahead with four months left.

🏒 Lenovo Center's $310M overhaul races toward banner night

ABC11 toured Lenovo Center on Thursday as crews push to finish a summer of renovations before the Hurricanes' season opener. Lower Bowl aisles have been narrowed to squeeze in additional seating, new stairs and railings are in, and entire sections are being renumbered — meaning longtime season ticket holders will find themselves in unfamiliar seat assignments. New boards were going in this week, with officials aiming to have the arena ready by Sept. 15 for a scheduled event. Other completed work includes a refreshed third-level concourse, all-new bathrooms and a second grab-and-go concession stand, with a second phase of exterior changes still in planning. The full project carries a price tag of nearly $310 million, which fan George Hogshead called "overwhelming" but worthwhile given how far the market has come. Season ticket plans have sold out ahead of the banner raising in late September.

🌡️ Raleigh has gained 40 warm summer nights since the 1970s

A new Climate Central analysis found that Raleigh now experiences roughly 40 more warmer-than-normal summer nights than it did in the early 1970s, with about 71% of summer nights now running above the historical average. Morning temperatures at RDU have averaged above normal every month this summer, one to six degrees warmer than usual, making this the fifth-warmest summer on record at the airport. Climate Central meteorologist Shel Winkley said nighttime temperatures across North America are warming nearly twice as fast as daytime highs, with Southeast humidity trapping heat after sunset. Duke geriatrician Dr. Cathleen Colón-Emeric warned that without cool nights, the body loses its chance to recover from daytime heat stress, a risk that falls hardest on outdoor workers, older adults and households that cannot afford to run air conditioning around the clock. Cooling demand has risen in 97% of the major U.S. cities analyzed.

TUESDAY, August 18, 2026

🏛️ Raleigh Sets September Vote on Teen Curfew

Raleigh city leaders moved a step closer Tuesday to adopting an 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. curfew for anyone under 18, though no vote was taken at the City Hall work session. Police Chief Rico Boyce told councilmembers that crimes with juvenile suspects are up 9% year over year — 894 so far in 2026 against 821 at this point last year — and pointed to a jump in rounds fired into vehicles and dwellings, from 15 incidents in 2025 to 190 this year. The proposal follows the July 4 Brier Creek shooting and the Glenwood South "teen takeover", and would mirror curfews already in place in Charlotte, Fayetteville, Rocky Mount and Winston-Salem. Critics including EmancipateNC's Dawn Blagrove warned the ordinance would fall disproportionately on Black children and called for community-based alternatives, while Boyce conceded a curfew "is not a fix, it is a tool." Two public hearings are planned next month ahead of a council vote expected Sept. 15.

🎓 Wake Schools Weigh Cuts to Cover State Pay Mandates

The board of North Carolina's largest school district took up budget reductions Tuesday night to close a gap opened by the new state budget's salary requirements. Wake County says staff bonuses require an additional $3.1 million in local funding while mandated raises for certified staff add $13.7 million, against a state mandate of an average 8% increase for teachers and instructional support and 3% for central office and noncertified employees. A board committee spent hours Tuesday afternoon debating options including lowering teacher supplements and scaling back the district's ReStart program. Board Chair Tyler Swanson called it "a tough budget" and said next year's will be tighter, adding he has "a lot of concerns about what do we do to ensure that there is not a lot of harm taking place in the classroom." State law requires the bonuses be distributed by October, which is driving the timeline.

🌡️ Another Weather Alert Day as Drought Keeps Easing

Tuesday was a WRAL Weather Alert Day under a heat advisory running 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., with humidity pushing the feels-like temperature past 100 — Raleigh's 17th heat alert of the season, roughly double the seasonal average. Highs stay in the 90s through Thursday before a slow-moving front brings scattered storms Friday and knocks weekend highs back into the 80s. The rain would land on ground that has already recovered considerably: the Aug. 13 drought report showed severe drought statewide down to 23% from 82% in July, and extreme drought down to 4% from 50%. Part of Chatham County is out of drought entirely, and forecasters expect most of the state — including the Triangle — could clear it by month's end. Forecasters note the weather looks favorable for bus rides when many area students return Monday.

🚨 Wake County Coach Killed; Boyfriend Charged With Murder

Tributes continued through the weekend for Sheremy Dillard, a women's basketball coach and recovery teacher at Felton Grove High School in Apex who was found stabbed to death in her Raleigh home Thursday. Dillard had previously spent years coaching and teaching at Carrboro High School, and friends described a mentor known for an infectious smile and a strong but comforting coaching style. Raleigh police have charged her boyfriend, Rickey Harper, with first-degree murder; investigators say he left North Carolina after the killing and was located outside Philadelphia on Friday with help from Flock cameras. The case has drawn renewed attention to domestic violence warning signs across Triangle communities. Anyone experiencing or concerned about a domestic violence situation can reach the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-SAFE.

⚖️ Courtney Jordan Foundation Leaders Face Judge

Two leaders of the Durham-based Courtney Jordan Foundation appeared in Person County court Monday on charges stemming from a high-speed chase that ended in a crash. Founder Courtney Jordan faces felony fleeing to elude arrest, injury to personal property and second-degree trespassing; CFO Nicholas Gallardo is charged with felony conspiracy, second-degree trespassing and simple possession of a controlled substance. Both told the judge they had retained counsel and entered no plea. The pair are also the subject of an ABC11 I-Team investigation into allegations the foundation failed to pay dozens of former employees, and the SBI has confirmed it is investigating possible financial crimes. CEO Kristen Picot, who attended in support, said the foundation is still active and working with the labor board but gave no timeline for paying workers. Separate fraud charges are pending in Durham County, with a court date later this month.

🏗️ Two Triangle Mainstays Close Their Doors

A pair of longtime local businesses shut down over the weekend. Adventure Landing on Capital Boulevard closed Sunday after 38 years of go-karts, mini golf and birthday parties, with owner Hank Woodburn saying it "has truly been a privilege to bring fun, laughter, and lifelong memories to so many families"; the company said its property lease was not renewed, following rumors in March that it denied at the time. Separately, Flavor Hills closed its downtown Raleigh and downtown Durham locations effective immediately on Saturday. The veteran- and Black-owned Southern comfort food restaurant, which opened in Raleigh in January 2024 as an offshoot of its original Jacksonville location, said in a Facebook statement that it "represented more than a restaurant. Flavor Hills represented hope." The owners plan to continue as the Flavor Hills Experience, focused on catering and private events.
