SATURDAY, September 26, 2026

🚨 Durham Violent Crime Climbs 5.5% as Advocates Push Council

New Durham Police Chief Stephanie Mardis told a City Council work session Thursday that violent crime rose 5.5% in the first half of 2026, with 800 offenses reported from January through June compared with 758 over the same span last year, WRAL reported. Aggravated assaults climbed nearly 12% to 509 cases and reported rapes jumped almost 25% to 96, even as homicides, robberies and burglaries all declined and motor vehicle thefts fell 26%, according to CBS17. The presentation, Mardis's first crime-data briefing since being sworn in, came just two days after a pair of unrelated fatal shootings on Eva Street and North Duke Street, and city commissioners heard from residents and advocacy groups pressing for more investment in violence-interruption and youth programs, ABC11 reported. Mardis also flagged that 63% of the 170 firearms reported stolen in the second quarter were taken from unlocked vehicles, underscoring a recurring driver of gun violence in the city.

🏖 Raleigh Launches Affordable Housing Dashboard for Residents

The City of Raleigh rolled out a new Housing Production Dashboard this week, giving residents a single online hub to search affordable housing developments and track the city's progress on housing goals, the city announced. City staff said the tool highlights more than 5,900 affordable units the city has helped bring online, spanning projects built by both municipal and private partners, ABC11 reported. Officials framed the dashboard as part of a broader affordability push that includes increasing the supply of subsidized units, improving overall housing affordability and directing targeted investment toward ending homelessness. The launch comes as Wake County's growth keeps housing costs near the top of residents' concerns at council meetings across the region.

🏖 Former Hurricane Seeks 40-Story Tower on Glenwood South Bars

Former Carolina Hurricanes forward Bates Battaglia and his brother Anthony are moving to rezone the Glenwood Avenue lot beneath their bars, Teets and Lucky B's, to allow a 40-story mixed-use tower, up from the current seven-story limit, WRAL reported. The brothers' representatives say they envision a residential-and-office high-rise built above a rebuilt version of Teets at 425 Glenwood Ave. and 609 Tucker St., preserving the bar that has long been a gathering spot for Canes fans and players. Their attorneys at Morningstar Law Group have already held a community meeting and notified neighbors of the coming request, though as of Thursday evening the formal rezoning case had not yet been filed with the city. The move follows a familiar Glenwood South pattern of property owners entitling land for greater density before a sale to developers.

🌟 Sunshine Returns for Warm, Dry Fall Weekend

After Wednesday's chilly, rain-soaked high of just 64 degrees at RDU, the Triangle is drying out and warming back up for the weekend, with sunny skies and highs in the mid-70s Saturday climbing to near 80 on Sunday, Hoodline reported. Forecasters expect the warming trend to continue into next week, with highs reaching the low 80s Monday and mid-80s by Wednesday, even as overnight lows stay crisp in the 40s and 50s, according to WRAL. The mild, dry stretch marks a return to classic fall weather after a brief cold snap brought the coolest temperatures the region has seen since May. No rain is in the forecast through the weekend, setting up ideal conditions for outdoor plans across Raleigh, Durham, Chapel Hill and Cary.

🤝 Raleigh Market Closes for Final Weekend Before State Fair

This weekend marks the last chance to visit the Raleigh Market at the State Fairgrounds before it shuts down to make way for the 2026 N.C. State Fair, which runs Oct. 15-25, the N.C. Department of Agriculture announced. Vendors selling produce, crafts and prepared food fill the fairgrounds most Saturdays and Sundays, but the market will go dark starting next week so crews can convert the grounds for the fair's rides, exhibits and livestock barns. The State Fair remains one of the region's biggest annual draws, with organizers anticipating attendance approaching a million visitors over its 11-day run, according to Visit Raleigh. Shoppers looking for a final weekend at the open-air market are being encouraged to arrive early, as parking and vendor space are expected to be tight.

FRIDAY, September 25, 2026

🚨 Ex-N.C. State Trainer Arrested on 32 Sex Abuse Charges

Robert Murphy Jr., who ran N.C. State's sports medicine program from 2012 until resigning in 2022 amid an internal investigation, was arrested Wednesday night in Ocala, Florida, days after a Wake County grand jury indicted him, ESPN reported. The 32-count indictment accuses Murphy of sexually touching student-athletes under the pretext of medical treatment, comprising nine felony counts of sexual contact or penetration and 23 misdemeanor counts of sexual battery spread across 14 victims, with allegations dating from October 2016 to 2021, according to WRAL. More than 30 former Wolfpack athletes have accused Murphy of abuse over the years, and he is charged under a 2019 North Carolina law that made abuse committed under the guise of medical treatment a felony, ABC11 reported. Murphy, 54, appeared before a Florida judge Thursday and waived extradition, clearing the way for his return to Wake County to face the charges.

🚨 Man Stabbed at Southeast Raleigh Apartment Complex

Raleigh police are investigating after a man was hospitalized with stab wounds when officers responded to an apartment complex on Thoroughbred Lane near Sunnybrook Road around 1:40 a.m. Thursday, WRAL reported. The victim's injuries were described as non-life-threatening, and investigators have identified a person of interest but made no arrests as of Thursday morning, according to Hoodline. Police have not released the victim's name, age or a possible motive, and no weapon has been recovered or confirmed publicly. The attack is the latest in a run of overnight violence to hit the capital region this week, days after a stabbing outside a Garner elementary school.

🏒 Hurricanes Erase Four-Goal Deficit to Stun Predators

The defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes rallied from a 5-1 hole to beat the Nashville Predators 6-5 at First Horizon Coliseum in Greensboro on Thursday night, capping the franchise's celebrated return to the city for the first time since 1999, Yahoo Sports reported. Logan Stankoven scored twice and Jesperi Kotkaniemi added a goal and an assist as Carolina reeled off five unanswered goals, with Felix Unger Sorum burying the game-winner off a snap shot just three seconds from time, according to NHL.com. Vitali Pinchuk, Cole O'Hara, Jonathan Marchessault, Adam Edstrom and Jack Drury had all scored for Nashville before the collapse, while Taylor Hall chipped in two assists for the Canes. The win pushed Carolina to 2-1-0 in the preseason with one exhibition game left before the Hurricanes open their title defense next month.

🏈 NC State Hosts App State in First Meeting Since 2006

NC State hosts Appalachian State on Saturday night at Carter-Finley Stadium in the programs' first meeting in 20 years and just the seventh all-time, with the Wolfpack (1-2) still reeling from a gutting 35-31 loss at Vanderbilt in which they led for 59 minutes and 59 seconds before fumbling in the end zone as time expired, WRAL reported. App State arrives at 3-0 and riding its reputation as a giant-killer, and is seeking its first-ever win over the Wolfpack after six previous losses, all in Raleigh, according to App State Athletics. Kickoff is set for 7:30 p.m. on ESPNU, with the Mountaineers installed as 13.5-point underdogs despite their unbeaten record. The teams last met in 2006, when NC State beat App State 23-10 in the only blemish on the Mountaineers' 14-1 FCS championship season.

🏛 Chapel Hill Council Accepts $10M Gift, OKs Library Fee

The Chapel Hill Town Council voted unanimously at its Sept. 23 meeting to accept a $10 million gift from the Bill & Peggy Britt Foundation and name new park facilities after the longtime local business owners, Chapelboro reported. Of the donation, $5.8 million will build the Bill and Peggy Britt Building, an indoor pavilion for wellness activities and recreation programs at Cedar Falls Park on Weaver Dairy Road, while the remaining $4.2 million will extend the Campus Recreation Trail toward the N.C. Botanical Garden and the Pritchard Branch Trail connecting West Cameron Avenue to Morgan Creek, according to the Town of Chapel Hill. At the same meeting, the council also voted unanimously to institute a $65 library card fee for residents living outside Chapel Hill, a response to Orange County shifting county library funding toward the new Carrboro branch earlier this year, Chapelboro reported. Together, the two actions reflect a council balancing a rare philanthropic windfall against a real funding squeeze on town services.

🚨 Stabbing Outside Garner Elementary School Injures One

Garner police are investigating a stabbing that left one person injured on the sidewalk directly in front of East Garner Elementary School early Tuesday morning, WRAL reported. Officers, the Wake County Sheriff's Office and forensic crews responded to the 5500 block of Jones Sausage Road around 2:20 a.m. after radio traffic described a stabbing near the school, and a woman at the scene said her son was the victim, according to Hoodline. Investigators taped off the sidewalk and found a car idling nearby with two people standing in front of it who may be connected to the incident, though police have not said whether they were witnesses or suspects. No arrests had been announced as the investigation continued.

🏖 Raleigh's Growth Boom Strains Neighborhoods, Tests City Leaders

Raleigh City Council members are grappling with a steady stream of rezoning requests as the city works to balance rapid growth against longtime residents' fears that new development will erase their neighborhoods' character, WRAL reported. About 70% of current rezoning requests target already-built-up parts of the city rather than the outskirts, a shift Mayor Janet Cowell says reflects deliberate infill planning around growth nodes such as Lenovo Center, North Hills and downtown rather than sprawl into undeveloped land. The pressure is acute in Northeast Raleigh, where Litchford Point LLC's twice-revised plan for 47 townhomes on a 3.8-acre Litchford Road lot has drawn organized opposition from neighbors worried about traffic at an already strained intersection with Old Wake Forest Road. Council member Mitchell Silver, who represents that district, says it fields the fewest development requests citywide yet still faces eight active proposals, underscoring how widespread the growth pressure has become even in Raleigh's quieter corners.

🎓 Durham Schools Post Historic Gains as Superintendent Touts Turnaround

Durham Public Schools Superintendent Anthony Lewis used his annual State of the Schools address this week to highlight what he called historic academic gains, including proficiency improvements in reading, math and science across grade levels, ABC11 reported. For the first time since 2014, no Durham Public Schools campus received an F grade on the state's school report card, a milestone Lewis attributed to years of sustained focus on classroom instruction following pandemic-era setbacks. The district also begins this school year with just 58 teacher vacancies, which Lewis described as a record low even as he said his ultimate goal remains zero openings. The gains come with a caveat, however: districtwide graduation rates have slipped to a 10-year low of 80.3%, a gap officials say they still need to close even as test scores climb.

🏒 Stanley Cup Champion Hurricanes Return to Greensboro for First Time Since 1999

The defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes play the Nashville Predators tonight at First Horizon Coliseum in Greensboro, marking the franchise's first game in the city since it called the arena home during its inaugural 1997-98 and 1998-99 seasons, Spectrum News reported. Puck drop is set for 7 p.m. at the arena, now home to the Hurricanes' ECHL affiliate, the Greensboro Gargoyles, according to NHL.com. Tickets for the exhibition matchup ranged from $34 for upper-level seats to $181 for prime ice-level views, reflecting strong regional interest in seeing the champions up close, WRAL reported. The trip continues Carolina's tradition of taking preseason hockey on the road across the state as it prepares to defend the title it won over Vegas in June.

🌟 Coolest Day Since May Gives Way to Sunny, Dry Fall Weekend

Central North Carolina logged its coolest day since May on Wednesday, with RDU Airport topping out at just 64 degrees under clouds and steady rain as a cold front and an offshore area of low pressure moved through, CBS17 reported. Thursday is bringing more of the same, with lingering clouds, light rain and drizzle keeping highs in the 60s across the Triangle, according to WRAL. Forecasters say the pattern flips by Friday, when sunshine and warmer air return with highs back in the 70s, setting up a dry, mild final weekend of September with highs in the mid-70s both days. Meteorologists are also tracking Fay, a tropical depression roughly 2,000 miles off the North Carolina coast, but say it poses no threat to the East Coast.

🚨 $350K Burglary Spree Hits Three Raleigh Beltline Neighborhoods

Raleigh police say at least 10 of roughly 20 home burglaries reported over the past 90 days across three inside-the-Beltline neighborhoods appear connected, with more than $350,000 in cash, jewelry and other valuables stolen, WRAL reported. The break-ins have hit multimillion-dollar homes in Coley Forest, Sunset Hills and Budleigh along the Oberlin Road and Glenwood Avenue corridor, leaving residents in some of Raleigh's wealthiest neighborhoods on edge despite high-end security systems, according to CBS17. In one Coley Forest case, a homeowner said burglars used a signal jammer to disable an armed alarm, broke in and stole belongings, then switched the alarm back on before leaving, Hoodline reported. Police spokesperson LaToya Hughes said the investigation's slow pace reflects the work needed to verify tips and obtain outside records, and detectives have not said publicly how the connected cases are linked or identified a suspect.

🚨 Two Unrelated Shootings Kill Man, Young Girl in Durham

Durham endured two unrelated fatal shootings within minutes of each other Tuesday evening, leaving a man dead on Eva Street and a young girl dead on North Duke Street, ABC11 reported. Officers responding to the 900 block of Eva Street around 6:48 p.m. found a man with multiple gunshot wounds; despite efforts by officers and EMS, he died at the scene, and police called it an isolated incident posing no danger to the public, according to WRAL. Minutes later and about two miles away, officers called to the 700 block of North Duke Street just before 7 p.m. learned that two children had been playing with a gun when it discharged, fatally wounding the girl, WRAL reported. Durham police have not released either victim's identity and are asking anyone with information on the North Duke Street shooting to call Investigator G. Silla, per CBS17.

🏒 Hurricanes Rally Past Panthers in Preseason Shootout Thriller

The Carolina Hurricanes opened their home preseason slate with a 3-2 shootout win over the Florida Panthers at Lenovo Center on Tuesday night, a lineup heavy on prospects outlasting Florida's veterans, per ESPN. Florida's Brady Tkachuk opened the scoring 4:31 into the first period before Bradly Nadeau answered for Carolina less than three minutes later, and Aleksander Barkov restored the Panthers' lead on a breakaway wrist shot past goaltender Brandon Bussi, Florida Hockey Now reported. Justin Robidas tied it for Carolina midway through the second period, and neither team found a winner in regulation or overtime before the Hurricanes prevailed in the shootout, according to Sportsnaut. The result came a day after Florida beat Carolina 6-3 in the teams' first preseason meeting in Sunrise, as the defending Stanley Cup champions continue working younger players into the lineup ahead of next month's regular-season opener.

🏛 Affordability Concerns Linger a Day After WakeMed-Atrium Vote

A day after Wake County commissioners voted 5-2 to clear the way for WakeMed's combination with Atrium Health, questions about whether the deal will actually hold down health care costs are already dogging the agreement, CBS17 reported. A Brown University School of Public Health researcher who studies hospital mergers said the acquisition follows a pattern that typically brings price increases of 10% to 20%, while the advocacy group Patients Union contends Atrium already charges 15% to 40% more than WakeMed for comparable services. State Treasurer Brad Briner has also pushed back on the merger, arguing that hospital consolidation "leads to less competition and higher prices," even as WakeMed leaders maintain the partnership will improve affordability by expanding financial-assistance eligibility from 300% to 400% of the federal poverty level. The deal still needs approval from the Federal Trade Commission before Atrium's pledged $2 billion investment and other commitments can take effect.

🌟 Flash Flooding Snarls Roads Across the Triangle

Slow-moving storms dumped one to three inches of rain across the Triangle Tuesday evening, triggering flash flooding that closed roads in both Wake and Durham counties, CBS17 reported. In Raleigh, Glenwood Avenue at Ebenezer Church Road was shut down after floodwaters overtook the roadway, while in Durham a stretch of Blackwell Street and Jackie Robinson Drive near the Durham Bulls Athletic Park also flooded. Forecasters had declared it a WRAL Weather Alert Day ahead of the evening commute, warning the storms could turn severe and produce localized flash flooding in low-lying and poor-drainage areas, according to WRAL. The rain followed Monday's cold front and marked one of the first bouts of true fall weather for the region, with drier, cooler conditions expected to return by midweek.
