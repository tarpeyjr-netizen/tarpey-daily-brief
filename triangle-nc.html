TUESDAY, September 8, 2026

🚨 Labor Day Drowning Ends Falls Lake Search

A holiday afternoon at Falls Lake turned to tragedy Monday when a 16-year-old boy went under near the Sandling Beach recreation area off Creedmoor Road, and his body was recovered just before 8 p.m. after an hours-long search. Wake County deputies were called around 2:20 p.m., and Wake County EMS, the Northern Wake Fire Department and the Wake Forest Fire Department joined the effort, with the county's Mobile Command Unit, drones and rescue boats deployed across the water. Before first responders arrived, park rangers asked adult swimmers on the beach to form a line and wade through the shallows searching by hand. "What if it was, like, my sibling?" volunteer Jose Hernandez told ABC11, describing the emotional toll on the crowd that gathered along the shore. Authorities have not said whether the teen was wearing a life vest or what caused him to go under.

🎓 Drive-By Shooting Wounds Two NCCU Students

Durham police are investigating a late-night drive-by shooting that wounded two North Carolina Central University students just before 11 p.m. Sunday. A man and a woman were walking near Concord and Dupree streets, not far from East Lawson Street and the NCCU campus, when someone fired from a passing car; both suffered injuries believed to be non-life-threatening and have since been released to their families. NCCU Police Chief Robert Gaddy told the campus he has directed increased patrols on and around campus and urged students to travel in groups and use the free Eagle After Dark shuttle. A neighbor playing video games at home said his car's back window was blown out by the gunfire. No arrests have been made; Durham Crime Stoppers is taking tips at (919) 683-1200.

🏈 Belichick Deflects As UNC Probe Widens

Bill Belichick held his first news conference since general manager Michael Lombardi resigned, declining to discuss the university's ongoing investigation into alleged off-the-field misconduct and steering every question back to Saturday's home opener against East Tennessee State. Lombardi — the highest-paid GM in college football at more than $1.5 million a year — stepped down 38 days after being placed on paid administrative leave following an HR complaint from a former football staffer, and UNC has not disclosed what the complaint alleged. Belichick acknowledged that NCAA rules make head coaches responsible for compliance, saying "everyone's responsible, including me, for doing things properly." The scrutiny, compounded by son Steve Belichick's medical leave, has divided a fan base coming off a 4-8 season. "It's going to be a question of what did he know and when did he know it," longtime Tar Heel fan Wes Johnson said. Carolina opened the year with a 15-10 win over TCU in Dublin.

🌡️ Raleigh Rebounds While Durham Rations Water

The Triangle's drought is easing unevenly, and Labor Day boaters found sharply different conditions from one lake to the next. Falls Lake, Raleigh's primary drinking water source, has climbed back to 84% of its water supply pool — comfortably above the 75% threshold that triggers conservation — allowing the city to lift restrictions last month, though secondary source Lake Benson sits at just 53%. Durham has no such reprieve: the city remains under a Stage 2 Water Shortage Response, its lakes at 49% capacity and its days of supply falling from 147 in mid-August to 113, with officials calling demand "unusually high" despite the rules already in place. To avoid a Stage 3 escalation, Durham is buying more Jordan Lake water through the Town of Cary and continuing to pump from Teer Quarry. The U.S. Drought Monitor still has all of Wake County in moderate drought, with severe conditions in northern Durham and Orange counties and extreme drought in Person and Caswell.

🤝 Raleigh Church Claims Long-Delayed Federal Marker

Saint Paul AME Church in downtown Raleigh unveiled a National Register marker from the U.S. Department of the Interior this weekend, closing out a recognition effort that began nearly four decades ago. The congregation earned its National Register listing in 1987, but the federal government stopped issuing physical markers and left it to honorees to fund their own — a gap Historical Commission chair Florence Avery said the church finally closed. The designation honors a site central to the first Freedmen's Conventions, where freedmen gathered at Saint Paul in 1865 and 1866 to press for voting rights and the right to testify in court. "We are indeed grateful to stand as a place of hope, restoration, healing," said pastor Dr. Steve Lyons. Church leaders say their next push is for grants to restore parts of the 178-year-old building.

🚨 Labor Day Drowning Claims Teen at Falls Lake

🌡️ Rain Lifts Raleigh's Lakes While Durham Stays Rationed

The Triangle's drought is easing unevenly, and nowhere is the split clearer than between Raleigh and Durham. Falls Lake, Raleigh's primary drinking water source, sits at 84% of its water supply pool — comfortably above the 75% threshold that triggers conservation — and the city lifted its restrictions last month, while secondary supply Lake Benson holds at 53%. Durham, by contrast, remains under a Stage 2 Water Shortage Response, with its reservoirs at 49% capacity and days-of-supply falling from 147 in mid-August to 113 as of last week, a drawdown officials called "unusually high" given restrictions already in place. To avoid escalating to Stage 3, the city is buying more Jordan Lake water through the Town of Cary and continuing to pump from Teer Quarry. All of Wake County remains in moderate drought per the U.S. Drought Monitor, with severe conditions in northern Durham and Orange counties and extreme drought lingering in Person, Caswell and Rockingham. Boaters at Falls Lake on Monday reported visibly better levels but warned of stumps and rocks still sitting just inches below the surface.

🤝 Raleigh Church Gets National Marker 178 Years On

Saint Paul AME Church unveiled a National Register marker from the U.S. Department of the Interior this weekend, capping a recognition effort that began nearly four decades ago. The Raleigh congregation, founded 178 years ago, hosted the first Freedmen's Conventions in 1865 and 1866, gatherings that pressed for voting rights, the right to testify in court and other civil protections for newly freed Black North Carolinians. Historical Commission chair Florence Avery explained the long delay: the church earned its national listing in 1987, but the register stopped issuing physical markers, leaving honorees to fund their own. "It's been a long journey, you know, from '87 to now," she told ABC11. Pastor Dr. Steve Lyons said the marker lets "the world see that Saint Paul is here," and church leaders say their next push is grant funding to restore parts of the building.

SATURDAY, September 5, 2026

🌡️ Severe Storms Batter Central NC, Cut Power to Thousands

A line of severe storms tore across the Triangle and surrounding counties Saturday afternoon, leaving a trail of damage and power outages through Wake, Durham, Moore and Cumberland counties. Downed trees crushed vehicles and blocked roads, and thousands of Duke Energy customers in Wake County were still without electricity as the system pushed east. The storms capped a punishing stretch of weather that began with a heat advisory over the Labor Day weekend, when forecasters warned that older adults and outdoor workers faced dangerous conditions. It was the second major outage event of the week for Wake County — on Wednesday, a helicopter crew doing vegetation management struck a transmission line, knocking out service to thousands before crews restored it. Forecasters expect slightly cooler air Sunday but another round of storm chances.

🎓 G20 Innovation Summit Draws World Leaders and Protesters to Chapel Hill

Chapel Hill spent the week at the center of global technology policy as the G20 Innovation Ministerial brought delegations from 19 countries to UNC, with OpenAI's Sam Altman and Nvidia's chief executive among the headline names. On the summit's second day, policymakers and industry executives debated artificial intelligence's growth and its pressure on the workforce — a conversation with unusual local weight given the Triangle's concentration of research and tech employers. Not everyone was welcoming: about 150 demonstrators gathered outside the Carolina Inn to voice alarm over rapidly expanding technology and who benefits from it. Organizers framed the ministerial as a milestone for North Carolina's standing in the innovation economy.

🏈 UNC Football GM Resigns as Investigation Presses On

Michael Lombardi has resigned as general manager of UNC football amid an ongoing investigation, an abrupt exit from one of the most closely watched front-office experiments in college athletics. University officials confirmed that the inquiry continues despite the resignation, leaving open questions about what it will ultimately find. Students on campus reacted with a mix of surprise and frustration, with the program still early in its high-profile rebuild. The timing lands squarely in the opening weeks of the season, when roster and personnel stability matter most.

🚨 Deadly Shooting in North Raleigh Caps Violent Week

A man died after a shooting at a North Raleigh apartment complex, the most serious in a string of violent incidents across Wake County this week. On Thursday afternoon, a 16-year-old was shot outside a Subway in a Poole Road shopping center; Raleigh police have made no arrests in that case. Separately, court documents show Cary police charged a trainer at a Wake County YMCA with rape of a child following an Aug. 27 arrest. In a lighter counterpoint, families turned out Saturday for Cary's Public Safety Day, meeting first responders and touring emergency equipment.

🏗️ Durham Puts $10K Behind Fayetteville Street Businesses

Durham is offering $10,000 grants to Black-owned businesses along the historic Fayetteville Street corridor, an effort to shore up a commercial strip that has struggled to attract capital. Owners welcomed the money but told the city it falls well short of what the corridor needs, pointing to vacant storefronts, deferred infrastructure and years of investment flowing elsewhere in Durham. The program arrives as the city weighs how to spread the benefits of its downtown boom into neighborhoods that have not shared in it. Fayetteville Street runs through the heart of the Hayti community, once one of the most prosperous Black business districts in the American South.

🏛️ Elections Board Tightens Absentee Ballot Rules Before Midterms

The North Carolina State Board of Elections has approved changes to how county boards handle absentee ballots, adjusting procedures just as the 2026 midterm cycle gets underway. The revisions land alongside the start of absentee voting logistics statewide, with ballots going out to voters who have already requested them. Wake and Durham counties process among the largest absentee volumes in the state, so procedural shifts at the board level translate quickly into local workload. Election officials say the goal is consistency across all 100 counties heading into November.

FRIDAY, September 4, 2026

🏛️ Hundreds protest AI summit at the Carolina Inn

The G20 Innovation Ministerial wrapped a two-day run at UNC's Carolina Inn on Wednesday with roughly 150 demonstrators massed outside the historic hotel while Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Nvidia's Jensen Huang and OpenAI's Sam Altman met inside, and Elon Musk joined virtually. The march began at the Old Well around 10 a.m. and moved down Cameron Avenue with banners, chants and rubber chickens, protesters deliberately generating as much noise as they could to evoke the hum of an industrial-scale data center. A coalition including Sunrise UNC, TransparUNCy, Siembra UNC, the Black Student Movement and Students for Justice in Palestine organized the action, pressing the university and the visiting executives on data-center water and power demands and on what accountability looks like when a public campus hosts a private-sector AI agenda. Chapel Hill police kept the demonstration separated from the venue and reported no arrests. Mayor Jess Anderson has since begun a public debrief on what hosting the ministerial cost and gained the town.

🏛️ Orange County crisis center named for Jamezetta Bedford

Orange County commissioners voted unanimously at their September 1 meeting to name the county's forthcoming behavioral health facility the Jamezetta Bedford Crisis Care Center, with Bedford's family in the room as the resolution was read aloud. Bedford, a sitting commissioner, died June 21 at 67 from complications following a medical procedure, and had been an original member of the crisis diversion subcommittee that spent years pushing the project from concept toward construction. The same subcommittee brought the naming request forward, citing her lifelong advocacy for people with developmental disabilities and behavioral health needs. The Hillsborough facility is meant to give law enforcement and families an alternative to the emergency room or the jail for people in psychiatric crisis. Construction is targeted for completion next winter, with the center expected to open in spring 2028.

🚨 Three-and-a-half-hour Raleigh standoff ends in arrest

Wake County deputies were called to a home on Floyd Drive in Raleigh early Thursday morning on a report of a domestic assault, and the man inside refused to come out. Negotiators worked the scene for about three and a half hours before deputies entered the house and took him into custody. No injuries to deputies were reported and the neighborhood was reopened by mid-morning. It is the second barricade situation in the county in as many weeks, and comes as Durham prepares to swear in new police chief Stephanie Mardis, who starts Monday after 24 years in the Greensboro department.

🌡️ Summer heat lingers as Durham's taps stay restricted

Central North Carolina is still running well above normal for early September, and health officials say the toll is larger than the raw numbers suggest — the state logged 5,861 heat-related emergency department visits between May 1 and September 2. First responders are warning that late-season heat catches people off guard precisely because the calendar says fall. The dry stretch behind that heat is also why Durham is not loosening its water rules: despite recent rain, the city's water efficiency manager said Durham has not hit the trigger to leave Stage 2, which has banned spray irrigation since June 15. As of September 1, Lake Michie and the Little River Reservoir were about 49 percent full, roughly 115 days of supply. Hand watering, drip lines and tree bags remain allowed.

🏗️ New I-40 on-ramp opens on Chapel Hill's Highway 86

NCDOT activated the rebuilt westbound on-ramp from Highway 86 onto Interstate 40 over the weekend, moving it to a new location across from Whitfield Road and retiring the old ramp entirely. The change is one visible piece of an 11-mile I-40 widening through Orange County that has blown past its original summer 2026 completion estimate. With the on-ramp live, crews can now build the replacement westbound off-ramp, which will exit after the Highway 86 bridge and curve down to the Whitfield Road signal instead of dumping traffic before the overpass. Consolidating both movements at one signal should let NCDOT remove the older light and smooth flow through the interchange; eastbound ramps are unchanged, though a new crosswalk and traffic island are planned near Eubanks Road. Drivers are being told to expect an unfamiliar pattern for a while.

🏗️ A funeral home, an art gallery, and a hardware store's last sale

Hillsborough-based Endswell Funeral Home is taking 6,000 square feet on North Merritt Mill Road in Chapel Hill for its administrative operations and for memorial services, built around a curated gallery it is calling AfterLight — abstract work with a soft end-of-life theme, local artists, a grand opening planned for this fall. The bet is that families want a room that does not look like a funeral parlor. Elsewhere in the county the churn runs the other direction: Dual Supply hardware in Hillsborough has entered its final sale ahead of closing, and in Pittsboro, Cafe Root Cellar is shutting down as its chef scales up nonprofit work. And a Chatham County educator claimed a $10 million lottery prize this week, the largest in the county's history.
