🏈 Rivers Signs Five-Year, $18.25M Deal at NC State

NC State's Board of Trustees on Friday approved a five-year contract for Philip Rivers, the former Wolfpack quarterback, to take over as head football coach. The deal runs through 2031 and is worth $18.25 million in salary and supplemental pay, starting at $3.25 million in 2027 with $200,000 in annual raises, plus up to $2.2 million a year in academic and on-field bonuses. If Rivers leaves early he owes a buyout that falls from $5 million before the end of 2027 to $1 million before the end of 2030. Rivers, 44, has no college coaching experience. Before this job he played 18 NFL seasons and coached six years at St. Michael Catholic High in Alabama. His pay is about half of what Dave Doeren earned before NC State fired him Oct. 24, and the school still owes Doeren a buyout of about $10 million. Rivers will be introduced at a news conference Monday.

🎓 NC State Trustees Approve Another 3% Tuition Increase

Incoming in-state undergraduates at NC State will pay 3% more in tuition in fall 2027, or $202, after trustees approved the increase Friday. The board also approved higher fees for student media, the student center, dining and housing. Together the changes are projected to bring in a little over $4.5 million, which will go toward the educational experience and faculty promotional raises. It is the second year in a row of increases, after this fall's increase ended an in-state freeze across the UNC System that dated to 2017. A few protesters held signs asking for higher graduate stipends, and the NC State Campus Workers Union said graduate students pay the system's highest combined tuition and fees. The UNC Board of Governors still has to approve the increases.

🏒 Canes Face Kraken Again in Helsinki Today

The Hurricanes play the Seattle Kraken again at 1 p.m. ET Saturday at Veikkaus Arena, the second of their two NHL Global Series games in Helsinki. A win would make Carolina the first NHL team to go 4-0 in international games. Carolina is 15-3-2 and leads the Metropolitan Division after beating Seattle 4-1 on Thursday. Pyotr Kochetkov is expected to start in goal, and captain Jordan Staal could return from the torn calf he suffered Oct. 11. Sebastian Aho has 28 points in 20 games, his best start to a season, and Jackson Blake brings a career-best 10-game point streak. The Canes return home Thursday to face Montreal.

🚨 SBI Investigating Inmate Death at Durham County Jail

The State Bureau of Investigation is looking into the death of an inmate at the Durham County jail. Detention officers found the person in distress around 9:30 p.m. Thursday and began medical aid, but the inmate died at the scene after EMS was called. The Durham County Sheriff's Office has not released the inmate's name while it notifies next of kin, and it has not given a cause of death. The SBI was called in under standard protocol, and the sheriff's office is also running an internal review.

🎓 Judge Puts Restructuring Officer in Charge at St. Aug's

A bankruptcy judge has approved Malcomb Coley as chief restructuring officer of Raleigh's Saint Augustine's University. Coley now makes the decisions for the school during its Chapter 11 reorganization, a power the board previously held. He is a former St. Aug's professor and an accounting professional with bankruptcy experience, and he spent eight years as a trustee at Queens University in Charlotte. The university lost its accreditation at the end of last school year and is not holding classes this fall. It has until Jan. 8, 2027 to file a reorganization plan, after missing its first deadline and getting a 60-day extension. The bankruptcy administrator and lender Self-Help Ventures Fund both called the appointment a positive step.

🎓 Durham Educators Push for Extra-Duty and Hazard Pay

The Durham Association of Educators has set six priorities for its monthly meet-and-confer sessions with Durham Public Schools. North Carolina bars public employees from negotiating contracts, so the group uses these sessions instead. Among the priorities are protected planning time on required workdays and stipends that are the same at every school for extra duties and for covering vacant positions. The group also wants time-and-a-half hazard pay for the few staff who go into buildings during closures. More than half of the district's teachers and support staff belong to the group. President Mika Hunter Twietmeyer said the district has suggested it may need to close schools to fill its budget gap, and she pushed back: "we don't think that we're going to shrink our way into greatness."

🏒 Canes Win Global Series Opener in Helsinki

The Hurricanes beat the Seattle Kraken 4-1 Thursday at Veikkaus Arena in Helsinki, Carolina's first game in the NHL Global Series. Finnish players did most of the scoring: Sebastian Aho scored twice, one on the power play, and Juuso Välimäki added a goal in the second period, after Nikolaj Ehlers opened the scoring in the first. Brandon Bussi stopped 12 of 13 shots for his second straight win, and Carolina improved to 15-3-2. The two teams play again in Helsinki on Saturday at 1 p.m. ET.

🏛 Wake County Freezes Hiring Amid Budget Gap

Wake County has paused hiring for most open positions through June, and County Manager David Ellis is projecting a $20 million to $60 million deficit for next fiscal year. Ellis called it "one of the most challenging budget cycles in decades," citing rising costs, a difficult economy, and state and federal factors. Every department has been told to find possible cuts and review its capital improvement plan. Critical and emergency roles are exempt, as are jobs needed to meet state and federal mandates. That includes the sheriff's office, EMS, public health and social services, which have the most openings.

🌡 Durham Weighs Stage 3 Water Restrictions

Durham may move to Stage 3 water restrictions as the drought worsens. The city has about 78 days of accessible water if no more rain falls, and its rainfall deficit is 20 inches. Durham has been under Stage 2 since June 15. Stage 3 would largely end outdoor water use, though hand watering and drip irrigation would still be allowed. To stretch supplies, the city is buying about 9 million gallons a day from Cary, which draws from Jordan Lake, plus about 2 million from OWASA, and it is pumping about 4 million from Teer Quarry. The shoreline at Lake Michie is now dry.

🏗 Duke Health Files Plans for $575M RTP Hospital

Duke Health has asked state regulators to approve Duke Health at RTP, a five-story, roughly $575 million medical center on Page Road in southern Durham County, across the street from its Arringdon facility. The plan adds no new beds and would move 50 from Duke University Hospital. Duke points to a nearly 19% rise in inpatient days at Duke Regional Hospital since 2019. If approved, construction would start in 2029 and the hospital would open in 2031, at first without surgery. NCDHHS will hold a public hearing Dec. 14 at Southwest Regional Library. UNC Health is building its own hospital about four miles away.

📊 State Fair Food Revenue Triples Under New Fee

The N.C. State Fair took in just over $3.1 million from food and drink sales this year, up from just under $980,000 last year. The jump followed a switch from a flat $180 per foot of frontage to 15% of each vendor's gross sales, with vendors required to use the Clover point-of-sale system. Some of the 214 vendors called the change unfair, and a glazed-nut vendor said his company lost money and wants a lower rate for North Carolina businesses. Fair director Kent Yelverson said the fair expects to keep the model and will put some of the money toward improvements before the fairgrounds' 100th anniversary in 2028.

🚨 Raleigh Firefighter's Death Spurs Cancer Prevention Push

The 2023 death of Raleigh Fire Captain Nathan Burgess, a veteran of more than 21 years, is drawing new attention to occupational cancer among firefighters. He died about 18 months after his diagnosis. His wife, Christy Burgess, founded The Phoenix Project 780, a nonprofit that teaches firefighters about on-the-job exposures and donates extractors that clean carcinogens from gear. A third extractor has been installed and a fourth is planned. Raleigh Fire also runs Rehab 12, a mobile unit that decontaminates firefighters and their equipment at scenes.

🏈 Philip Rivers Deal With NC State Set for Friday

Philip Rivers is expected to become NC State’s next head football coach, with trustees set to finalize his contract at a meeting Friday, ABC11 reported, citing ESPN sources. The 44-year-old would replace Dave Doeren, who was fired October 24, and has no college coaching experience. He is in his sixth season leading St. Michael Catholic High School in Fairhope, Alabama, and reportedly asked to finish that season, which ended last Friday. Rivers was the Wolfpack’s quarterback from 2000 to 2003, setting school and ACC records with 13,484 passing yards and 95 touchdowns, and his son Gunner committed to NC State in February.

🤝 Dreamville Organizers Apply for 2027 Dix Park Festival

Organizers behind the Dreamville Festival have filed for a permit to stage a new festival at Raleigh’s Dorothea Dix Park on the first weekend of April 2027, a city spokesperson confirmed Wednesday, WUNC reports. The application was filed in October, but the city has not made a decision, and a permit filed last year never led to an event. The 2025 festival was billed as the last, though City Manager Marchell Adams-David later said Dix Park would host future music festivals likely focused on hip-hop with J. Cole involved. The new event’s name has not been made public; the 2024 festival drew more than 100,000 people and an estimated $146 million in economic impact.

🎓 UNC Breaks Ground on First New Dorms in Decades

UNC-Chapel Hill broke ground Tuesday on its first new residence halls in about two decades, naming one Jackson Hall in honor of trailblazing faculty members Blyden and Roberta Jackson, WUNC reports. Blyden Jackson became the university’s first tenured Black faculty member in 1969, and Roberta Jackson, tenured in 1974, was the School of Education’s first Black tenure-track faculty member. Chancellor Lee Roberts said the project will add 730 beds, with the halls slated to open in fall 2028 on the former site of the undergraduate admissions office. The construction is part of a 10-stage plan to grow enrollment, which Roberts said has risen by about 1,000 students over two years.

🎓 UNC Weighs 10% Out-of-State Tuition Hike

UNC-Chapel Hill trustees are considering a 10% tuition increase for out-of-state undergraduates in 2027-28, adding more than $4,700 a year and pushing total tuition and fees above $54,000, ABC11 reports. In-state tuition would stay flat, though a proposed $55 fee increase would help fund a new Recreation and Wellness Center. Trustees discussed the plan Wednesday with talks continuing Thursday, while NC State’s board will review a proposed 3% increase for both in-state and out-of-state undergraduates. The UNC System Board of Governors has final say on tuition and mandatory fee changes.

🚨 Carrboro Residents Push Back on Police Merger

More than 20 speakers urged Carrboro’s town council to reject a proposed merger of its police department with Chapel Hill’s at a meeting that stretched to nearly 2 a.m. Wednesday, WRAL reports; only two spoke in favor. Under the plan, Carrboro’s 28 officers would move under Chapel Hill Police in phases over five years, with projected savings, a single Orange County-run 911 system and expanded crisis co-responder programs. Opponents raised concerns about transparency, public safety and the loss of local culture. No vote was taken; Chapel Hill’s council is expected to discuss the idea November 18 and Carrboro will revisit it December 1.

🏛 Lame-Duck Legislative Session Returns to Raleigh Monday

The General Assembly is scheduled to reconvene in Raleigh on November 16 for a lame-duck session whose agenda remains unknown even to key lawmakers, WUNC reports. House Rules Chair John Bell said he had seen no plan, while Senate Minority Leader Sydney Batch of Wake County said she had heard Republicans could move to shift public corruption prosecutions away from the Wake County DA’s office. Five Stein vetoes await possible House override votes, and lawmakers may also take up property tax limits following voter approval of a constitutional amendment. The session could run, with a Thanksgiving break, until adjournment by December 18.

🚨 Suspect Faces 40 Felonies in Raleigh, Greensboro Burglaries

A 52-year-old man faces 40 felony charges tied to a string of home break-ins in Raleigh and the Greensboro area, ABC11 reports. Police say Stephen Neal Trotter targeted homes in Raleigh’s southwest and north districts starting in mid-May, prying open side or back doors between 11 p.m. and 4 a.m. while residents were out of town. Jewelry, watches, safes, firearms and silver kitchenware worth more than $380,000 were taken. Greensboro police shared surveillance video that investigators say matched footage from the Raleigh cases, and Trotter, who was supposed to be on house arrest on earlier felony charges, is now held without bond in the Forsyth County Jail.

🏛 House Democrats Put Affordability at Top of Agenda

Incoming House Speaker Robert Reives said Democrats will focus on lowering costs for families when they take control of the state House in January, ABC11 reports. The party will hold its largest House majority in 30 years, and its broader agenda includes cheaper health care, education changes and decriminalizing marijuana. “If Republicans have some ideas about what we can do to make life more affordable, they will be heard,” Reives said. WUNC reports that Reives also wants to change how the legislature operates.

🤝 Cyclists Push for Safer Roads After Deadly Crash

Triangle cyclists are calling for more driver awareness after Saturday’s crash in southern Wake County killed 63-year-old Patrick Turner of Cary and injured 20 others, WRAL reports. Rider Alejandro Sandoval said drivers and cyclists “both belong in the roads,” and noted that state law allows bicycles on public roads. He said some riders are afraid to return, though many are still riding, and that online comments blaming cyclists have upset the community. The 76-year-old driver, Donald Robert Morris-Jones of Apex, faces felony charges including hit-and-run and DWI.

🏗 Raleigh Dive Bar Sadlack’s Plans a Comeback

Sadlack’s, the Hillsborough Street dive bar that closed in 2013 to make way for the Aloft Hotel, is coming back under new ownership at 913 W. Morgan St., WUNC reports. The project is led by Henry Goodell, a longtime regular who called the revival “definitely an homage.” The bar opened in 1974 and was a hub of Raleigh’s 1990s alt-country scene, with bands such as Whiskeytown and 6 String Drag. The new version will serve drinks but no food, hold 80 people indoors with live music and later add an outdoor stage, and it is still several months from opening.

🏛 Union Square Still Lacks Monument to Black North Carolinians

Eight years after a state Historical Commission committee urged leaders to build a monument to African Americans on the old Capitol grounds in Raleigh “without delay,” none exists, WUNC reports. Funding was dropped in a 2019 budget fight and pulled again in 2021, and Gov. Josh Stein’s 2025 proposal for $5 million went nowhere in the legislature. The state budget passed in July 2026 funded Union Square monuments to Billy Graham and the Revolutionary War but nothing for Black North Carolinians. Dawn Blagrove of Emancipate NC said lawmakers are not prioritizing the project, even though the state has the money.

🚨 Arrest Made in Franklin Street Raising Cane’s Fire

Chapel Hill police arrested 45-year-old John Long after a fire late Sunday at a Franklin Street building that houses a Raising Cane’s and a vacant former Starbucks, WRAL reports. Long is charged with breaking and entering and burning a commercial structure. Firefighters responded at 11:52 p.m. and contained the fire within 15 minutes, and no one was hurt. The restaurant reopened early Monday morning while officials continued assessing the damage.
