🏒 Hurricanes Sweep Weekend Against Oilers and Rangers

The Carolina Hurricanes took two wins in two days, rallying from a 2-0 hole to beat Edmonton 7-4 at Lenovo Center on Saturday afternoon behind a five-goal second period. Sebastian Aho scored his 323rd career goal, passing Eric Staal for the most by a Hurricane since relocation, while Jordan Staal recorded his 500th point with the club and Taylor Hall was honored for his 1,000th NHL game. On Sunday at Madison Square Garden, Brandon Bussi made 25 saves in a 5-2 win over the Rangers, with goals from Eric Robinson (shorthanded), Jackson Blake, Alexander Nikishin, Sean Walker and Jordan Martinook. Carolina outshot New York 41-27, extending its run of outshooting opponents to 23 straight games, and became the first NHL team to reach 100 goals this season. The Canes play next on Wednesday at Tampa Bay.

🏈 Wolfpack Win, Tar Heels Fall Before Rivalry Finale

NC State snapped a two-game skid with a 28-10 win at Florida State on Saturday, as CJ Bailey threw for 292 yards and three touchdowns and the defense forced two turnovers on downs and two interceptions after allowing an opening-drive score. The game matched two interim coaches, Todd Goebbel for the Wolfpack and Rick Stockstill for the Seminoles. North Carolina, meanwhile, lost 41-24 at Virginia, where freshman Travis Burgess, starting for the injured Billy Edwards Jr., threw for 354 yards and ran for 106 but the Tar Heels were hurt by two turnovers, a missed field goal and a blocked kick. Both teams now sit at 5-6 heading into Saturday's regular-season finale against each other, and UNC needs the win to become bowl eligible.

🌡 Durham Edges Toward Stage 3 Water Restrictions

Durham has logged 562 reports of water-restriction violations but issued only 23 warnings and no fines, a WRAL investigation found, as the city's drought drags on. Conservation program manager James Lim said staff follow up on every report, but a warning requires an employee to witness the violation, which is difficult when reports arrive days later. The city has been under Stage 2 restrictions since June, banning spray irrigation and most car washing, and as of Nov. 17 had about 76 days of easily accessible water left. Durham Water Management's Joe Lunne said the city is "getting close to Stage 3," which would ban outdoor use of city water and require large users to cut consumption by half, while Durham has already spent more than $4 million buying water from neighboring communities.

🚨 Pedestrian Killed in South Saunders Street Hit-and-Run

Raleigh police are searching for a driver who struck and killed a pedestrian on South Saunders Street around 2 a.m. Sunday and left the scene. Police said the victim was crossing outside a crosswalk in heavy rain and dark clothing when a Jeep's side mirror knocked the person to the ground; that driver made a U-turn to help and is not facing charges. A second vehicle, described only as red, then hit the pedestrian and kept going. The victim died at the scene and has not been identified.

🤝 Food Bank Reports Generational Hunger Need Before Thanksgiving

The Food Bank of Central & Eastern North Carolina says more than 600,000 people across its 34-county service area face food insecurity heading into Thanksgiving, roughly one in seven residents and one in five children. President and CEO Amy Beros said rising costs are pushing families who have never needed help before to seek it, describing households where "the math doesn't work at the end of the month." The food bank distributes through more than 700 partner organizations and is running a matching campaign of up to $500,000, under which each dollar donated provides six meals.

🤝 Raleigh Parade Opens Holidays; Police Retire Last Crown Vic

Thousands lined downtown Raleigh streets Saturday for the annual Raleigh Christmas Parade, with marching bands, floats, balloons and Santa Claus marking the unofficial start of the season; WUNC posted a photo gallery of the parade. In a nod to an older era, the Raleigh Police Department has retired its final Ford Crown Victoria patrol car, a 2011 Police Interceptor. Rather than send it to auction, the department will keep the cruiser for community events after Officer J.C. Vucich, who started his career in a Crown Vic, asked to save it with Chief Rico Boyce's support.
