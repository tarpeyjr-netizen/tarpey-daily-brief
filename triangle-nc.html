Tuesday, July 28, 2026

🌡️ Severe storms, possible tornado threaten Triangle Tuesday evening

A First Alert Day is in effect across central North Carolina, with a level 2 of 5 risk of severe storms bringing damaging wind gusts, large hail, and isolated tornadoes through Tuesday evening. A separate severe thunderstorm warning hit Orange County earlier in the evening, prompting tornado warnings for Chapel Hill and Carrboro and alerts from both Orange County government and UNC's Alert Carolina system. Forecasters say the storm cell brought 60-mph gusts and nickel-size hail as it rolled through the Piedmont. Storms should clear most of the area by midnight, with a return to pleasant, drier conditions expected through the end of the month.

🌡️ Last week's soaking rain cuts Raleigh's drought deficit nearly in half

The Triangle's wettest rain event in more than a year delivered real relief to a region still gripped by extreme drought, according to the North Carolina State Climate Office. Raleigh picked up 3.21 inches during the multi-day soaking, cutting its year-to-date rainfall deficit nearly in half to 3.20 inches. Falls Lake, the primary drinking water source for more than 650,000 residents in Raleigh, Garner, Wake Forest and surrounding towns, jumped by more than two feet and is now a little over three feet below its guide curve. Officials caution the drought isn't over, with most of the state still carrying rainfall deficits of more than seven inches dating back to last August.

🎓 UNC football GM Michael Lombardi placed on paid administrative leave

The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill placed football general manager Michael Lombardi on paid administrative leave effective immediately, the school announced Monday. ESPN reported the move stems from a human resources complaint filed by a former UNC front-office employee, with an internal investigation ongoing. Lombardi, one of coach Bill Belichick's closest advisors since joining the program in December 2024, has faced scrutiny before, including comments last year that the Tar Heels "consider ourselves the 33rd NFL team." The leave isn't expected to affect the 2026 roster ahead of UNC's Aug. 29 season opener against TCU in Dublin, but could carry longer-term implications for recruiting and the direction of Belichick's second season in Chapel Hill.

🤝 Search continues for Cary mother missing in Grenada

Family and friends of Cary resident Elizabeth "Liz" Waddell are pleading for help as the search continues for the mother, who disappeared while swimming near Grand Anse Beach in Grenada. Waddell's family said she went missing after leaving her towel on a pier and going for a swim; Grenadian authorities are leading the search with support from the State Department. Friends describe Waddell as adventurous and deeply connected to her Cary community, and her husband Cailen has praised local authorities' efforts. Anyone with information has been asked to come forward as the search continues.

🚨 Pedestrian killed in hit-and-run on Blue Ridge Road in Raleigh

A person died Friday night after being struck by a car on Blue Ridge Road in Raleigh, police said. Witnesses told officers the person walked into the roadway around 10:30 p.m. and lay down in the northbound lane before a dark-colored SUV struck and killed them; the driver did not stop. Raleigh police are investigating and are asking anyone with information to contact the department or submit an anonymous tip through CrimeStoppers.

Monday, July 27, 2026

🌡️ A soggy week caps off with Monday morning storms and downed trees

The Triangle capped a stretch of drenching weather with another round of early Monday morning storms that toppled trees and knocked out power, closing a stretch of Lake Dam Road near Lake Johnson and cutting power to about 1,000 Duke Energy customers in northeast Chapel Hill. The damage capped a week of repeated flash flood warnings across Wake, Durham, Orange, Chatham and surrounding counties, with RDU setting a new daily rainfall record of 2.03 inches on Thursday. Forecasters say the saturated ground is making trees more prone to toppling even in ordinary storms. Tropical Storm Bertha, well off to the west, helped funnel extra tropical moisture into the region through the week.

🚨 Sixth juvenile charged in Raleigh's July 4 "teen takeover" violence

Raleigh police announced a sixth juvenile charged in connection with the July 4 weekend violence that left nine people shot across the city, after detectives obtained a secure custody order for a 15-year-old tied to the Brier Creek shooting. The teen was taken into custody in Durham County and transferred to a juvenile detention facility in Raleigh. It's the latest arrest in a monthslong investigation into the "teen takeovers" that erupted around Capital Boulevard, Glenwood South and Brier Creek over the holiday weekend, following a fifth juvenile charged just days earlier. City leaders have pointed to the string of arrests as they continue pushing a proposed teen curfew in response to the violence.

🌡️ Rain helps, but Durham and Orange counties are still in extreme drought

Despite this week's heavy rain, Durham and Orange counties remain in extreme drought, with Orange County still at 100% extreme drought and Durham at just over 82%. Farmers and backyard gardeners across the region say the dry stretch has damaged crops like tomatoes, forcing some growers to start over even as July shapes up as the wettest month the Triangle has seen since September 2024. No Raleigh-area counties remain in the most severe "exceptional" drought category anymore, a modest bit of relief, but experts say the region would still need 17 to 20 inches of rain in a single month to erase the deficit entirely. 2026 remains on pace to be North Carolina's driest year since 1895.

🤝 Noah Kahan's sold-out Raleigh stop gave a lift to local small businesses

Noah Kahan brought his sold-out Great Divide Tour to Carter-Finley Stadium Saturday night, and the stop rippled well beyond the stadium gates. Local spots including B&W Roasters and Little Blue Bakehouse rolled out special menu items tied to the show, while other businesses hosted themed pop-ups and a spin class benefiting The Busyhead Project, Kahan's mental health nonprofit. Openers Gigi Perez and Annabelle Dinda joined Kahan for a 26-song set. It's part of a pattern on this tour of Kahan's team partnering with small businesses in tour-stop cities to spread economic benefit beyond the venue itself.

🎓 Wake County's modified-calendar schools are back in session

About 40,000 Wake County students returned to class Monday as modified-calendar and year-round schools opened for the 2026-27 year, weeks ahead of the district's traditional-calendar schools, which don't start until Aug. 24. The district's Tools4Schools supply drive kicked off alongside the first day to help teachers offset classroom expenses. The early start for modified-calendar schools is part of Wake County Public School System's long-standing balanced-calendar approach, which staggers start dates across the district's schools.

Sunday, July 26, 2026

🌡️ Thursday storms flood Triangle, knock out power for thousands

Heavy rain swept across central North Carolina on Thursday, with forecasters warning of several inches of rainfall and flooding as the biggest risk to the region. More than 2,000 Duke Energy customers lost power in Durham and Chapel Hill, with additional outages reported in Garner, Chatham County and High Point, mostly from fallen trees damaging equipment. A flood advisory covered Wake, Durham, Chatham, Orange, Person and Granville counties through the morning as bands of rain slid southward, with areas southeast of the Triangle like Smithfield and Goldsboro facing the worst flooding risk.

🚨 Gas leak forces dozens from Durham homes near NCCU

A gas leak early Thursday morning prompted the evacuation of 30 people near the intersection of South Roxboro Street and Dunstan Avenue in Durham, just blocks from North Carolina Central University. Durham Fire Department and Enbridge Gas crews worked the scene to locate and fix the leak while police blocked off Dunstan Avenue. It was unclear how long repairs would take or when the road would reopen.

🚨 Deadly weekend on Triangle roads and streets

A string of violent incidents marked the end of the week across the Triangle. A 25-year-old Chapel Hill woman, Rebecca Claire Rains, was killed Thursday when she lost control of her car on I-40 in Pender County and crossed the median into a tractor-trailer; state troopers cited speed and wet roads as factors. In Raleigh, a pedestrian was struck and killed late Friday night at Blue Ridge Road and Macon Pond Drive by a driver who initially fled the scene before officers located the vehicle. And in Durham, a man was seriously injured in a Friday afternoon shooting on the 400 block of Formosa Avenue; police believe the suspect and victim knew each other and say there's no ongoing threat to the community.

🤝 Search continues for missing Cary woman vacationing in Grenada

Grenadian police are asking for the public's help locating 44-year-old Elizabeth "Liz" Waddell, a Cary resident last seen Wednesday at Grand Anse Beach. Her husband, Cailen Waddell, who manages Cary's Parks, Recreation and Cultural Resources department, flew to Grenada Friday to help with the search. Authorities are asking fishermen, boat operators and coastal residents to report any sightings, and no details on the circumstances of her disappearance have been released.

🤝 GalaxyCon wraps four-day run in downtown Raleigh

GalaxyCon Raleigh closes out Sunday after drawing an expected 50,000-plus fans to the Raleigh Convention Center over four days, with headliners including Star Wars actors Hayden Christensen and Rosario Dawson and members of the "Scream" cast marking that film's 30th anniversary. Greater Raleigh Convention & Visitors Bureau officials estimate the convention generates roughly $9.7 million annually for local hotels, restaurants and retailers. Organizers say the event has outgrown the convention center, with an expansion project expected to begin later this year to accommodate future growth.

Friday, July 24, 2026

🚨 Cyclospora outbreak tops 500 cases, Triangle schools pull produce

North Carolina's cyclosporiasis outbreak has surged past 500 cases since early May, with the bulk concentrated in Wake County, prompting Triangle school districts to strip produce from summer and back-to-school menus. Durham Public Schools is pulling all fresh produce from cafeteria lines until September, while Wake County has yanked salads, parsley and cilantro specifically. Chapel Hill-Carrboro schools, out for summer, haven't yet decided on a policy. State health officials and the FDA have traced likely sources to parsley, cilantro and lettuce but haven't pinned down the exact supply-chain culprit, and cases continue climbing statewide as the investigation continues.

🌡️ Durham taps 1-billion-gallon quarry as drought grinds on

Durham has begun pumping emergency water from Teer Quarry into the city's treatment system, drawing on roughly a billion gallons banked there to stretch Lake Michie and Little River Reservoir through the ongoing drought. Crews spent six weeks installing the temporary pumping system, with water reaching the Williams Water Treatment Plant this week. Residents have cut usage 12% so far, short of the city's 30% conservation target, and officials estimate under a month of quarry reserves remain at current draw rates. The push comes as recent rain has eased but not broken the statewide drought, with Raleigh-Durham International Airport logging well above-average July rainfall even as parts of the state sit in exceptional drought.

🏛️ Rani Dasi tapped to succeed the late Jamezetta Bedford on Orange County board

The Orange County Democratic Party's executive committee has selected Rani Dasi, a longtime Chapel Hill-Carrboro school board member, to fill the District 1 county commissioner seat left vacant after Jamezetta Bedford died June 21, shortly after winning her primary. Dasi won 66.9% of the committee vote over a field that included former Chapel Hill mayor Pam Hemminger and Bedford's primary opponent María Palmer. With no Republican challenger in the race, Dasi is positioned to win the seat outright this fall for a full four-year term.

🏈 UNC, Duke, NC State head into 2026 on very different trajectories

With fall camp approaching, North Carolina's three ACC football programs are telling very different stories heading into the season. NC State returns proven starter CJ Bailey at quarterback and enters as the presumptive favorite among the trio, though the Wolfpack lost transfer tackle Jai'Lun Hampton to a denied NCAA eligibility waiver. Duke is rebuilding at quarterback after Darian Mensah's offseason transfer to Miami, leaning on veteran transfers to replace one of the league's most productive passers. UNC, in Bill Belichick's second year after a 4-8 debut season, is leaning on the youngest roster in the ACC and a new-look offense built around transfer Billy Edwards Jr.

🚨 Chatham County woman charged after dozens of animals abandoned in Siler City

A Siler City woman faces two felony animal cruelty charges and 26 misdemeanor counts after deputies found dozens of abandoned animals at a Herb Beavers Road property, including chickens, turkeys, ducks, a rabbit, a dog and two pigs left without adequate food or water. Neighbors had already freed the trapped pigs before deputies arrived; the property's resident, Maggi Elizabeth Brady, 37, had reportedly moved out in June. The recovered animals — 22 chickens, two turkeys, a rabbit and a dog — are now up for adoption at the Chatham County Sheriff's Animal Resource Center in Pittsboro.
