Tuesday, November 10, 2026

🏈 Duke Routs NC State as Rivers Return Looms

Duke buried NC State 41-3 at Carter-Finley Stadium on Saturday, holding the Wolfpack without a touchdown for the first time since October 2023, according to the CBS Sports game tracker. Nate Sheppard ran for 217 yards and three touchdowns, and Andrew Pellicciotta returned a missed field goal 100 yards for a score just before halftime. The loss left NC State 4-5 under interim coach Todd Goebbel, who took over after Dave Doeren was fired October 24. ABC11 reports that Philip Rivers is expected to return to Raleigh as the next head coach, with an announcement expected in the coming days.

🚨 Apex Cyclist Crash Driver Held on $1M Bond

A Wake County judge on Monday kept a $1 million bond in place for the 76-year-old Apex man accused of driving into a group of Capital Cycling Club riders on Saturday, killing a 63-year-old Cary cyclist and injuring 20 others, WRAL reports. Donald Robert Morris-Jones faces felony death by vehicle, seven counts of felony serious injury by vehicle, eight counts of felony hit-and-run and DWI, and could face up to 112 years in prison. The crash on Optimist Farm Road killed Patrick Turner, and ABC11 notes the driver faces 19 charges in all. His next court date is November 30.

🎓 Sigma Nu Members Leave NC State House Amid Hazing Probe

NC State's Sigma Nu fraternity members faced a 4 p.m. Tuesday deadline to vacate their house, and by Monday WRAL reported that most had already moved out. The chapter will close indefinitely, and it could seek university recognition again in about four years. Law enforcement executed six search warrants tied to the fraternity in October, and Wake County District Attorney Lorrin Freeman said the hazing investigation could lead to criminal charges soon. Separately, NC State's trustees were set to consider another 3% in-state undergraduate tuition increase for fall 2027, while UNC-Chapel Hill is seeking a 10% hike only for out-of-state undergraduates.

🌡 Durham Reservoirs Hold 78 Days of Water

Durham's reservoirs held about 78 days of easily accessible water as of Monday, WRAL reports, though a city official stressed that does not mean the water would run out after that time. The city has been under Stage 2 restrictions since June and has issued 19 formal warnings. Stage 3 would be triggered by a 10% chance of reservoirs falling below 25% within eight weeks. A planned Jordan Lake water project is a longer-term fix that officials say will not help this year.

🏛 Wake Schools Extend Superintendent, Revisit Ethics Rules

The Wake County school board voted to extend the superintendent's contract, according to WRAL. The board is also revisiting its ethics policy and wants to add a censure process for members. Meanwhile, WUNC reports that Durham Public Schools delayed a vote on a $474,000 contract for a school "right-sizing" study.

Sunday, November 8, 2026

🚨 Driver Charged After Crash Into Wake County Cyclists

A 76-year-old Apex man is in custody on a $1 million bond after his vehicle struck a group of about 30 cyclists on Optimist Farm Road near Talicud Trail around 9:30 a.m. Saturday. Cyclist Patrick Turner was killed, and the injured were taken to WakeMed Raleigh, WakeMed Cary and UNC Health Holly Springs. Donald Robert Morris-Jones faces charges including driving while impaired, eight counts of felony hit-and-run involving serious injury or death, seven counts of felony serious injury by vehicle and one count of felony death by vehicle. The riders were part of the Capital Cycling Club, and authorities suspect impairment.

🏒 Hurricanes Rout Blackhawks 9-2 Before Finland Trip

Carolina matched its franchise record since relocating by scoring nine goals in a 9-2 win over Chicago at Lenovo Center on Saturday night. The Hurricanes led 7-0 after two periods, Jordan Martinook and Andrei Svechnikov each scored twice, and 15 skaters recorded a point, the second time that has happened in team history. Goalie Pyotr Kochetkov made 18 saves to improve to 11-1-0, becoming the 11th goaltender in NHL history to win 11 of his first 12 appearances in a season. It was Carolina’s second straight seven-goal win, and the team now heads to Finland for games against Seattle on Thursday and Saturday.

🏛 Raleigh Voters Back Incumbents and Two $101.5M Bonds

Raleigh voters returned Mayor Janet Cowell to office, and mayoral terms are now four years instead of two, so she serves until at least 2030. Sana Siddiqui won one of two at-large seats in a four-way race to become the council’s newest member, while incumbent Stormie Forte kept the other. Voters also passed two $101.5 million bonds, one for housing and one for transportation, which the city says will not cost taxpayers extra. Elsewhere in the county, Knightdale approved a $55 million bond for a new community center and park, and two incumbents held their Wake school board seats.

🎓 Durham Schools Delay $474K Right-Sizing Study Vote

The Durham school board tabled a decision Thursday on a $474,405 contract with Florida-based MGT Impact Solutions for a school consolidation study after a motion to approve it with added conditions failed 3-3. Critics questioned the cost and the company CEO’s ties to school privatization, and the board directed district attorneys to renegotiate terms before its Nov. 19 meeting. District planners project losing up to 8,000 more students by 2035, which would leave about half a million square feet unused, and a 2025 assessment found $965 million of failing infrastructure. Watts Elementary is among schools listed for possible closure in a draft capital plan.

🌡 Wake County Slides Back Into Extreme Drought

Wake County is back in extreme drought for the first time since July, with about 41% of the county affected in the Nov. 5 update. Extreme drought now covers 8% of the state, up from 3% a week earlier, and the Neuse and Cape Fear rivers hit daily record-low streamflows. The state has been dry since early October, and while Greensboro imposed mandatory water restrictions, scattered rain is possible next week.

🏈 NC State Eyes Philip Rivers as Next Football Coach

NC State moved on from Dave Doeren and is searching for a successor, and former NFL quarterback Philip Rivers is considered a leading candidate. Rivers, an NC State alumnus who coaches high school football in Fairhope, Alabama, met with school officials Wednesday and said nothing is imminent. His team lost in the state playoffs Friday, ending its season, and his son Gunner is committed to the Wolfpack.

🤝 Cary Police Chief Terry Sult to Retire in January

Cary Police Chief Terry Sult, 69, will retire effective January 15, 2027 after more than 50 years in law enforcement. His career spanned Charlotte-Mecklenburg, Gastonia, Sandy Springs, Georgia, and Hampton, Virginia, and he became Cary’s 14th chief in 2022 after serving as interim chief in 2021. No successor has been named, and the town manager plans a 2027 recruitment process.

Saturday, November 7, 2026

🏛 Cowell Wins Raleigh Mayor Race; Bonds Pass

Raleigh Mayor Janet Cowell won reelection over Ashleigh Health Armstrong, and voters approved two city bonds totaling $203 million for housing and transportation that the city says require no property tax increase, WRAL's Triangle election roundup reports. Wiley Nickel ran unopposed and will succeed Lorrin Freeman as Wake County district attorney, while Democrats Mona Singh and Christine Kushner took the two at-large seats on the Wake County commission. In Durham, District Attorney Satana Deberry and Sheriff Clarence Birkhead were both reelected unopposed. WRAL noted that many races, including the Wake County sheriff contest between incumbent Willie Rowe and Republican Kenny Blackwell, had not been called when the story was last updated.

🌡 Flash Flooding and Lightning Fire Hit Triangle

Severe storms brought heavy rain and flash flooding to the Triangle on Friday, with WRAL's Storm Tracker seeing high water on Walnut Street in Cary and West Millbrook Road in Raleigh. A flash flood warning was in effect and between 1 and 2 inches of rain had fallen at the time of the report, with more expected. Lightning also struck a tree beside a home on Lambeth Drive in North Raleigh, igniting an attic fire that left the house unsafe to occupy, according to ABC11. No one was hurt, no deadly impacts were reported in the Triangle, and Raleigh's First Friday Market and Movie was canceled.

🎓 NC State Closes Sigma Nu Chapter After Hazing Probe

NC State revoked recognition of its Sigma Nu chapter on Friday, and the fraternity's national organization suspended the charter, closing the university's first-ever chartered fraternity indefinitely, WRAL reports. The group will not be considered for reopening for at least four years. Police executed six search warrants in October, and videos on members' devices reportedly show drug use and members kicking what appeared to be pledges, after a Sept. 9 fire alarm response and five anonymous tips. Outgoing Wake County District Attorney Lorrin Freeman said charges could result, and incoming DA Wiley Nickel expects a briefing within two weeks.

🚨 Former Wake Deputy Charged With Tipping Off Suspects

A 29-year-old Wake County Sheriff's deputy, Anthony Garcia, was arrested Thursday and charged with felony obstruction of justice and accessing government computers for fraud, accused of warning suspects about arrest warrants, WRAL reported. Garcia, hired in August 2020, is no longer with the Sheriff's Office and was released on a $30,000 bond. He was scheduled to appear in court Friday at 1:30 p.m. Sheriff Willie Rowe said the office has safeguards and audits for its databases and will conduct a full internal review.

🏛 Wake School Board Extends Superintendent Taylor

The Wake County school board voted 7-1 on Wednesday to extend Superintendent Robert Taylor's contract by one year, through June 30, 2030, according to WRAL. Board member Toshiba Rice cast the lone no vote and declined to explain it, and member Chris Heagarty was absent. The board typically extends the contract each fall after a performance evaluation, up to the four-year maximum under state law, though state personnel-record protections kept this year's evaluation from being discussed publicly. Vice Chairman Sam Hershey called the extension deserved; Taylor, hired in 2023, earns more than $330,000 a year.

Tuesday, November 3, 2026

🚨 Man Fatally Shot in Northeast Raleigh Mobile Home Community

Raleigh police are searching for a suspect after a man was found fatally shot Monday night in a mobile home community off Buffaloe Road in northeast Raleigh. Officers responded shortly after 7 p.m. to the area of Beagle Run Drive and Foxhound Road, and ABC11 reports that K-9 units, drones and officers with long guns searched for about an hour. No arrests had been made late Monday, and the victim's identity has not been released. Police said a U.S. Postal truck near the evidence markers is unrelated, and they have not said whether a crashed car behind crime-scene tape is connected.

🚨 Four Teens Shot in North Durham, One Critically Hurt

Four people under 18 were shot Sunday night around 11:05 p.m. in the 700 block of Hedgerow Place in northern Durham, and police had announced no charges or motive as of Monday afternoon. ABC11 reports that officers found three young people wounded at the scene, one with life-threatening injuries, while a fourth arrived later at a hospital with a non-life-threatening wound. Neighbors described hearing gunfire and seeing a group of teenagers run from the area. Investigators spent much of Monday focused on the parking lot at Old Farm Road Park, and anyone with information is asked to call Officer J. Zemites at 919-560-4582 or Durham CrimeStoppers at 919-683-1200.

🏀 Triangle Men's Basketball Teams Open With Wins

All three Triangle men's programs opened the season with victories Monday night, led by No. 2 Duke's 84-50 rout of Army, in which freshman Dame Sarr scored 16 and 17-year-old Joaquim Boumtje Boumtje added 15 points, seven rebounds and three blocks in his debut. NC State beat Campbell 83-64 in Justin Gainey's first game as head coach, with Ven-Allen Lubin posting 20 points on 8-for-8 shooting and 10 rebounds. In Chapel Hill, UNC held off Western Carolina 80-72 in Michael Malone's debut, pulling away with a 13-2 run in the final six minutes despite shooting 9-of-30 from three. Duke hosts Coastal Carolina on Thursday, and UNC hosts Wofford on Friday.

🏛 Record 2.8 Million North Carolinians Voted Early

More than 2.8 million North Carolinians voted early in this midterm, the most ever for a midterm election in the state, WUNC reports. Early turnout ran about 30% above 2022 and 25% above 2018. Registered Republicans made up 26% of early voters, down from 31% four years ago, while unaffiliated voters rose to 35% from 30%. Polls are open today, Election Day, from 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m., with the U.S. Senate race between Roy Cooper and Michael Whatley and a closely watched 1st Congressional District rematch atop the ballot.

🏛 Knightdale Voters Decide $55 Million Parks Bond

Knightdale voters decide today whether to approve up to $55 million in general obligation bonds, chiefly to build a new community center on a 33-acre site at 2004 Marks Creek Road. WUNC reports the bonds would raise the property tax rate by 3.5 cents per $100 of assessed value, about $35 a year on a $100,000 property. The town is one of only two Wake County municipalities without a standalone town-run community center, and its current facility is attached to Forestville Elementary under a joint-use agreement. Knightdale's population has grown about 18% since 2020 to 23,144, and the center would open in roughly two years if the bond passes.

🎓 UNC Graduate Students Say Policy Changes Leave Them 'Discarded'

UNC-Chapel Hill graduate students and faculty say a series of funding and housing changes is making the university less competitive, WUNC reports. The Graduate School is more strictly enforcing a 10-semester funding limit for doctoral students, phased in over three years, while graduate housing is being removed to make room for more undergraduates and the minimum nine-month stipend is $21,592. Dean Beth Mayer-Davis says the university can no longer afford to fund students past five years as it works to cut more than $80 million from its budget. Sociology's newest PhD cohort is less than half the size the department wanted, and its director of graduate studies warns the university risks losing ambitious students to rivals.
