🌡 Thanksgiving Storm Knocks Out Power Across the Triangle

Duke Energy crews spent Thanksgiving restoring power after a storm system brought heavy rain and strong winds through the Triangle starting Wednesday night, ABC11 reports. A large tree that fell in a neighborhood off Avent Ferry Road in Raleigh cut power to more than 1,000 customers, and other trees and lines came down on Lake Dam Road and on Gorman Street near Kaplan Drive. By late Thursday most service was back, though several hundred Raleigh customers were still in the dark, and Duke Energy said it had restored more than 100,000 customers statewide. "Here in the Triangle, it was a hard-hit area," spokesperson Jeff Brooks said, warning that a cold front and another round of high winds could cause more outages. Crews can't work from bucket trucks once winds reach 30 mph, so stronger gusts could slow repairs.

🏒 Canes Rally Past Lightning for Historic 19th Win

The Carolina Hurricanes came from behind to beat the Tampa Bay Lightning 3-1 on Wednesday night, scoring twice in 97 seconds in the third period, according to the team's recap. Eric Robinson tied the game at 5:24 of the third, Jaccob Slavin scored the go-ahead goal (his first of the season), and Jackson Blake added an empty-netter. Brandon Bussi made a season-high 32 saves for his fourth straight win. The victory moved Carolina to 19-3-3, tying the 2001-02 Red Wings and 1983-84 Oilers for the most wins in NHL history before Thanksgiving. The Canes host Ottawa at Lenovo Center on Saturday at 7 p.m.

🚨 Two Killed in Head-On Crash on North Duke Street

Two Durham men died after a head-on collision in the 3400 block of N. Duke Street just before 5:45 p.m. Wednesday, ABC11 reports. Police said a 2010 Nissan Altima crossed the center line and hit a southbound 2015 Nissan Murano. The Altima's driver, 61-year-old Manuel Antonio Pacheco Paz, died at a hospital, and his passenger, 56-year-old Jorge Yobani Castro Lagos, died at the scene. The Murano's driver was not hurt. Durham police said speed and impairment appear to have been factors and the crash is still under investigation (WRAL).

🏛 Wake at Home Closes South Raleigh Encampment Before Freezing Nights

Wake County's Wake at Home initiative has closed a second homeless encampment, this one in South Raleigh near a busy road, and officials say 29 of its residents are now in safe housing or soon will be, WUNC reports. Each closure takes six to eight weeks of daily outreach, with the City of Raleigh's Care Navigation Team working alongside the Wake County Continuum of Care. The effort draws on $22 million in existing funding and a recently passed $12 million housing bond spread over four years. As temperatures dropped, the county activated its White Flag plan, which opens emergency shelters when the temperature with wind chill reaches 35 degrees or lower. Healing Transitions adds 50 drop-in beds at each of its shelters on those nights. By the end of Phase 1 in December 2027, the program aims to move 400 people from encampments into stable housing.

🤝 Durham Rescue Mission Serves Thousands Despite Rain

The Durham Rescue Mission held its 52nd annual Thanksgiving feast and giveaway on Thursday, and organizers said it served thousands of people even though rain fell all day, ABC11 reports. Volunteers prepared about 150 turkeys with traditional sides and handed out clothing and other essentials. "You can tell what people need most by what they run to," said the mission's Ernie Mills Jr. Among those helping was 29-year-old Koby Logner, who lives at the mission and says he is four months sober and rebuilding his life.
