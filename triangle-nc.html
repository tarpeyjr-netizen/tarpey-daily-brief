Thursday, October 8, 2026

🏛 Raleigh Pauses Data Center Development for Six Months

The Raleigh City Council voted Wednesday to pause large-scale data center development for six months while the city studies potential impacts. Activists who packed the debate argued the pause is too short and pressed for a longer moratorium. The vote comes as newcomers and seasoned campaigners compete to unseat incumbents in the November council elections, making growth and land use a live campaign issue.

🏛 Legislature Votes to Suspend NC Gas Tax Through Nov. 30

North Carolina lawmakers voted to drop the state gas tax until Nov. 30, sending the measure to Gov. Josh Stein. WRAL's coverage asks when prices could actually fall if the governor signs, and PolitiFact examined whether Senate leader Phil Berger reversed his earlier position on a suspension. The timing, weeks before the midterm elections, has not escaped notice.

🚨 Faith Hedgepeth Murder Trial Opens in Orange County

The trial of Miguel Salguero-Olivares, accused in the 2012 killing of UNC student Faith Hedgepeth, is underway after jurors were seated. Prosecutors opened with testimony from Hedgepeth’s former roommate, who described discovering the body in the apartment in September 2012. The case has been one of the Triangle’s best-known cold cases, and ABC11 is carrying live updates.

🏗 Cary BBQ Truck Drops Durham Restaurant Plan After Backlash

Dampf Good BBQ, the Michelin-recommended Texas-style food truck in Cary, will not open a downtown Durham restaurant. The decision followed criticism of social media posts by owner Nick Dampf that began circulating online. The reversal is a reminder of how quickly community reaction can reshape a high-profile opening in downtown Durham’s competitive food scene.

🌡 Drought Cuts Yields for NC Corn, Tobacco and Peanuts

Drought has diminished yields for corn, tobacco, peanuts and other North Carolina crops, with the damage tied to high temperatures and low rainfall in June. The hit to farmers lands as the Triangle’s forecast turns calmer: WRAL expects a pleasant midweek before rain chances rise this weekend.

Wednesday, October 7, 2026

🎓 WCPSS Tells All Schools to Open Campuses to Employee Groups

The Wake County school board on Tuesday revised its policy to require every school to let employee associations meet with staff on campus. The change targets uneven enforcement, with Superintendent Robert Taylor saying the district wants “a level playing field of access” rather than rules that vary by principal. The policy covers groups such as the North Carolina Association of Educators, and board member Chris Heagerty said it had long existed but needed clarifying after differing interpretations. Taylor added that explicitly extending access to broader employee groups like SEANC may require action from the state legislature.

🤝 Dampf Good BBQ Drops Downtown Durham Restaurant Plan

Michelin-recommended Cary food truck Dampf Good BBQ has decided against opening a brick-and-mortar restaurant in downtown Durham after community backlash. Criticism grew online over owner Nick Dampf’s past social media posts voicing conservative Christian views, including opposition to same-sex marriage, and was amplified when food journalist Matt Lardie questioned whether LGBTQ customers would feel welcome. Dampf says he does not discriminate and that customers of all backgrounds have eaten at his business. The planned Geer Street area location is in a neighborhood known for its LGBTQ community.

🎓 Michael Jordan Gives $10 Million to UNC Social Work

Michael Jordan has given $10 million to the UNC School of Social Work, the largest gift in the school’s history. The donation honors his mother, Deloris P. Jordan, who has been involved with the school for nearly four decades. The money will expand the Global Social Development Innovations Center, which will be renamed for her, support the Jordan Institute for Families, and fund scholarships for Master of Social Work students. Jordan said his mother has spent her life putting into action the belief in helping families and communities.

🏗 UNC Grad Students Fight Loss of Baity Hill Housing

UNC-Chapel Hill graduate students are pushing administrators to abandon a plan to convert Baity Hill, the university’s only affordable on-campus graduate housing, into undergraduate dorms. A petition signed by nearly half of residents argues there are no viable alternatives, especially for international scholars. Vice Chancellor Jonathan Sauls says the move follows a trustees directive to grow undergraduate enrollment and points to more than 2,000 nearby apartment vacancies. Future graduate housing at Carolina North could open around 2029, leaving a gap of two to three years for displaced students.

🌡 Roadless Rule Repeal Puts 144,000 NC Forest Acres at Risk

The federal repeal of the 2001 Roadless Rule would open about 144,000 acres of protected North Carolina forest to logging and development, according to environmental advocates. The rule barred road-building and timber harvests to protect drinking water, limit invasive species and preserve biodiversity. Roughly 18 percent of the state’s national forest land has old-growth characteristics, and advocates warn the change could also hurt the outdoor economy, including the trout fishing industry. The public comment period closes at 11:59 p.m. today, Wednesday, October 7.

🏛 NC Groups Prepare to Shield Immigrant Voters at the Polls

Advocacy groups including El Pueblo and N.C. Asian Americans Together are gearing up to protect immigrant voters in the November midterms. Their worries center on immigration enforcement near polling places, even though federal law bars ICE from polls. The groups are running multilingual hotlines in Spanish and nine other languages, placing nonpartisan poll monitors in multiple counties, and helping voters with vote-by-mail. Advocates say margins are slim in some races, so every vote counts.

🏛 Chapel Hill Weighs Fire Station and 105 Affordable Homes

The Chapel Hill Town Council meets at 6 p.m. Wednesday to consider a proposal for a new fire station paired with 105 affordable housing units on Weaver Dairy Road Extension, along with a plan to add 12 housing units at the Cedars in Meadowmont. Across the county line, Orange County commissioners meet tonight to hear final audit results on the contested property revaluation and an update on an ordinance to regulate data centers. Carrboro's council, also meeting tonight, will consider a CompostNow curbside composting service that could be the first town-wide program of its kind in North Carolina.

🏛 Raleigh Launches Housing Production Dashboard

Raleigh has put its new affordable housing dashboard online for residents after presenting it to the city council. The interactive map shows completed city-funded projects, pipeline developments, public housing and tax-credit sites, and tracks how 2020 Affordable Housing Bond money has been committed. The city says it has created or preserved roughly 5,800 units over the last decade, with drilldowns by council district and fiscal year.

🚨 Raleigh Police Chase Car Break-In Suspects; Club Alive Arrest

Raleigh police took one person into custody and are still looking for a second after reports of people breaking into vehicles around 2 a.m. Monday on Schenley Drive in south Raleigh, WRAL reported. Nearly a dozen police units responded, and one suspect was caught near Garner Road. Separately, detectives arrested 33-year-old Dontayvious Devonte Cotton on Oct. 1 in the Aug. 23 shooting at Club Alive on Fayetteville Street, and he faces four counts of assault with a deadly weapon with intent to kill inflicting serious injury. Police said a fight inside the club escalated before four people were shot, all of whom were expected to survive.

🏈 NC State Tops Louisville as Duke Rests at 4-0

NC State beat Louisville 31-28 at home to improve to 3-2 overall and 1-1 in the ACC, with three defensive turnovers helping quarterback CJ Bailey and the Wolfpack hold on. UNC fell to 2-2 after its 37-26 loss to No. 3 Notre Dame, and Duke, unbeaten at 4-0, had an open week before it heads to Georgia Tech on Saturday. The Tar Heels and Blue Devils then meet Oct. 17 in Durham, with kickoff set for 3:30 p.m. on ESPN2.

🏒 Hurricanes Visit Montreal After Overtime Win in Philadelphia

Carolina won 3-2 in overtime at Philadelphia on Saturday behind goaltender Pyotr Kochetkov, bouncing back from a 5-2 loss to Washington that coach Rod Brind'Amour said left his team outmatched. The Hurricanes sit at 1-1-1 and visit the Canadiens tonight, per the ESPN schedule. Forward Seth Jarvis remains out following offseason shoulder surgery, with a return projected between late October and late December.

Monday, October 5, 2026

🏒 Aho Sparks Hurricanes to First Win in Overtime

Sebastian Aho scored twice and set up Shayne Gostisbehere's power-play winner as the Hurricanes beat the Flyers 3-2 in overtime Saturday night for their first victory of the season. Aho scored on the opening shift of the game and again on the power play in the third period to tie it, and Gostisbehere ended it with 2:09 left in the extra session. The Stanley Cup champions had opened 2026-27 with an overtime loss to Florida and a 5-2 home loss to Washington on Friday. The win extended Carolina's streak of road-opener victories to nine seasons, while forward Seth Jarvis remains out with a shoulder injury expected to keep him sidelined until around December.

🏈 Notre Dame Outlasts UNC 37-26 in the Rain

Third-ranked Notre Dame scored 16 straight points across the third and fourth quarters to beat North Carolina 37-26 on Saturday in Chapel Hill. The Irish improved to 5-0 while the Tar Heels fell to 2-2. Next up for UNC is a home date with Duke on Oct. 17, the first of several Triangle rivalry games on the ACC calendar this month, according to CBS17.

🚨 Fatal Shooting and Highway Crash Mark Durham Sunday

One man died and another was wounded in a shooting around 12:30 a.m. Sunday in the 4400 block of Durham-Chapel Hill Boulevard, WRAL reported. The survivor's injuries were not life-threatening, no suspects have been arrested, and investigators are asking for tips through Durham Crimestoppers at 919-683-1200. Separately, a four-car wreck Sunday morning forced the closure of eastbound Interstate 40 at NC 55 in Durham and caused miles of gridlock before lanes reopened.

🚨 Arrest Follows Gunfire on West Franklin Street

Chapel Hill police arrested a suspect after shots were fired around 7 p.m. Friday in the 100 block of West Franklin Street, according to an Alert Carolina notice issued at 8:54 p.m. No injuries were reported, and the university said there was no ongoing threat to the campus community. The incident occurred off campus and the police investigation remained open at the time of the alert.

🏗 Cary's Veloxis Folded Into New Asahi Kasei Unit

Cary-based Veloxis Pharmaceuticals will be integrated into a new global commercial organization called Asahi Kasei Therapeutics, according to BioBuzz. Veloxis has grown about 20 percent a year since 2020 on its kidney transplant drug Envarsus XR, and parent company Asahi Kasei is positioning it as an anchor for western operations as it targets 300 billion yen in sales by 2030. In Durham, Humacyte added Scott Coward and Paul Kuznik to its board as it prepares to roll out its engineered vessel for vascular access, and Atsena Therapeutics reported that foveal schisis resolution persisted in seven of nine treated eyes in its gene therapy trial.
