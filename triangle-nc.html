Monday, August 10, 2026

🚨 Murder-suicide near Lake Royale leaves child safe, parents dead

A husband and wife, identified as Kevin and Kimberly Killworth, were found dead Friday morning in the front yard of their home on Sunlight Cove near Lake Royale in Franklin County, in what investigators are calling an apparent murder-suicide. A child under 10 was found safe inside the home after telling police he heard gunshots around midnight, hours before anyone called for help. The Franklin County Sheriff's Office and the SBI are continuing to investigate, and ABC11 reports the community near Louisburg has been shaken by the discovery. No further threat to the public is believed to exist.

🚨 Man shot in neck at northeast Raleigh apartment complex

A man was shot in the neck overnight Sunday at an apartment complex on Ashire Crossing near Old Wake Forest Road in northeast Raleigh, according to WRAL. Police responded around 2:45 a.m. and found the victim, who was taken to the hospital with injuries that do not appear life-threatening. No arrests have been announced, and Raleigh police are continuing to investigate what led to the shooting. It's the latest overnight incident police in the area have been tracking this summer.

🎓 UNC mourns Hinton James resident after weekend death

UNC-Chapel Hill police are investigating the death of a resident at Hinton James Residence Hall, after an Alert Carolina notice went out to the campus community late Saturday. University officials said the death is under police investigation, that no suspicious circumstances have been reported, and that there was at no point a danger to the residence hall or broader campus community. Counseling and Psychological Services staff have been made available to students, and Hinton James residents needing support were encouraged to reach out to their resident advisor or community director. The university has not released the student's name pending notification of family.

🌡️ Heat and humidity build across the Triangle this week

High pressure parked off the Southeast coast is locking in hot, muggy conditions typical of early August, with CBS17 forecasting afternoon highs climbing into the low-to-mid 90s across Raleigh, Durham, Chapel Hill, and Cary through the week. A tropical wave offshore will pump extra moisture into the region, raising the chance of scattered afternoon and evening downpours, some of them heavy. Lows will stay mild in the low 70s, offering little overnight relief. Forecasters advise limiting outdoor activity during peak afternoon heat as the heat index climbs.

Tuesday, August 4, 2026

🌡️ Raleigh Lifts Water Restrictions; Durham Holds at Stage 2

Raleigh officially lifted its Stage 1 water restrictions Monday after the wettest July on record since 1931 dropped more than a foot of rain on the region. The deluge raised Falls Lake from 66% to 84% of capacity, according to Ed Buchan, assistant director with Raleigh Water, prompting the city to roll back the limits enacted in April during the statewide drought — though officials still encourage voluntary conservation. Durham, however, is keeping its Stage 2 restrictions in place: the city's water supply sits at just 52%, with Lake Michie about 8 feet below full and Little River down 12 feet, and officials say levels haven't recovered enough to ease up.

🚨 Four Shot at House Party East of Raleigh

Wake County deputies are investigating a shooting that wounded four people Monday night in the 10100 block of Joe Leach Road, southeast of Raleigh. Deputies responding around 8:45 p.m. found two men and a 17-year-old with gunshot wounds, and a fourth victim later walked into a Johnston County hospital with serious injuries; all four are expected to survive. Neighbors described roughly 30 shots ringing out during a large gathering at a short-term rental home, with 10 to 15 cars speeding away afterward, and the homeowner told WRAL they found evidence of guns and drugs inside. No one is in custody, and the sheriff's office is asking anyone with information to call 919-856-6911.

🤝 Shake-Ups at Two Major Raleigh Arts Institutions

Raleigh's arts scene absorbed two significant pieces of news Tuesday. The Contemporary Art Museum of Raleigh is putting its Warehouse District building up for sale as it weighs its future, having stopped holding exhibitions last year amid difficulty raising enough money to cover operating costs. Meanwhile, North Carolina Museum of Art director Valerie Hillings is leaving the museum to become the first director of the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi, ending her tenure at one of the Triangle's flagship cultural institutions and kicking off a high-profile leadership search.

🤝 National Night Out Brings Block Parties Across the Triangle

Communities across the Triangle celebrated National Night Out on Tuesday, the annual first-Tuesday-in-August tradition that pairs neighborhoods with local law enforcement for block parties, games, and crime-prevention outreach. Raleigh hosted events at 21 locations from 3 p.m. to 8:30 p.m., including gatherings at North Hills, Oakwood Commons Park, and Renaissance Park, with the police department asking residents to leave porch lights on from 7 to 10 p.m. Chapel Hill moved its celebration inside the Hargraves Center gym due to threatening weather, while Carrboro filled Town Commons with food, a dunk tank, and safety exhibits. Durham and Wake Forest will hold their observances in October to take advantage of cooler temperatures.

Monday, August 3, 2026

🌡️ Raleigh Lifts Water Restrictions, But Durham Holds the Line

Thanks to a stretch of heavy rain, Raleigh and Fuquay-Varina lifted their Stage 1 water restrictions Monday, and Rocky Mount eased its rules from mandatory to voluntary over the weekend. Durham isn't following suit: the city says its reservoirs remain too low to exit Stage 2, with Lake Michie about 8 feet below full and Little River Reservoir 12 feet below, leaving overall water supply at roughly 52%. "The trigger is basically that our lakes are pretty close to full — we have to have a 95% chance of being at 95% full," said James Lim, Durham's water efficiency and conservation program manager, who added the city could be in Stage 2 "for a while" absent a tropical storm or other major rain event. Burlington is also staying put on its own Stage 1 restrictions for now.

🚨 Juvenile Shot at Raleigh Vape Shop

A juvenile was injured Saturday afternoon in a shooting at a vape shop in the 5100 block of Rock Quarry Road, according to the Raleigh Police Department. Officers responding to a call around 2:44 p.m. found the victim inside the shop with a gunshot wound to the arm, and they were taken to the hospital for treatment. Police have not released information about a motive or a suspect, and the investigation remains open.

🤝 Cary Family Returns Home as Search for Elizabeth Waddell Continues

The family of Elizabeth "Liz" Waddell, the Cary physical therapist who vanished July 22 while vacationing in Grenada, has returned to the United States even as the search for her continues. Waddell was last seen swimming near Grand Anse Beach, and surveillance footage released by investigators showed her entering the water but never coming back out. Her husband, Cailen Waddell, said that after consulting with family, local authorities and the U.S. Embassy, he does not believe foul play was involved, though Grenada's police commissioner says the case remains an active missing-person investigation and officials remain hopeful she will be located.

🏈 Belichick's Tar Heels Grind Through Fall Camp

UNC football is four days into fall training camp under Bill Belichick heading into his second season, with roughly 100 players on the field working through conditioning, assignments and communication ahead of the Aug. 29 season opener against TCU in Dublin, Ireland. The marquee storyline is an open quarterback competition, with transfers Billy Edwards Jr. and Miles O'Neill competing for the starting job alongside true freshman Travis Burgess and returning scholarship passer Au'Tori Newkirk. The camp opened just days after general manager Michael Lombardi was placed on paid administrative leave amid an HR-complaint investigation, and Belichick has largely declined to discuss the matter with reporters, saying his focus is on practice progress.

🏗️ CAM Raleigh Puts Its Downtown Building on the Market

The Contemporary Art Museum of Raleigh is selling its building at 409 W. Martin Street in the Warehouse District, where it has been housed since 2011. Commercial real estate firm CBRE will list the property, which carries an $11 million tax value, though no asking price has been set. Museum board chair Charman Driver framed the sale as a financial reset rather than a retreat, saying it "frees us to invest in artists, ideas, and experiences instead of a mortgage" as the foundation looks to design a leaner model after revenues fell from nearly $900,000 in 2018 to under $570,000 in 2025.

Sunday, August 2, 2026

🌡️ Raleigh to lift water restrictions Monday as Durham holds at Stage 2

The City of Raleigh announced it will lift Stage One water restrictions starting Monday, August 3, after recent rain pushed Falls Lake to 84% of its normal level, more than two feet higher than a week ago. The restrictions on sprinkler and irrigation use had been in place since April, and officials say the reservoir has recovered enough to meet the region's needs through the rest of the year, though they're still urging conservation. Durham isn't there yet: Mayor Leo Williams says the city is staying in Stage Two restrictions, noting Durham came dangerously close to escalating further before rain and the new Teer Quarry pumping project — which is trimming 3 to 4 million gallons a day off demand on Lake Michie and the Little River Reservoir — eased the pressure.

🏒 Hurricanes to raise Stanley Cup banner Sept. 29 against Panthers

The Carolina Hurricanes will hoist their second Stanley Cup championship banner at Lenovo Center on September 29, the franchise announced Wednesday, hosting the Florida Panthers in an ESPN tripleheader season opener with puck drop set for 5 p.m. It's Carolina's first title since 2006, capping a run that ended in June with a 4-2 series win over the Vegas Golden Knights in the final. Season-ticket holders and fans can expect a full pregame ceremony before the Canes open defense of their crown on home ice.

🎓 UNC Hussman journalism dean steps down, interim named

Harsha Gangadharbatla, the school's senior associate dean, will take over as interim dean of UNC-Chapel Hill's Hussman School of Journalism and Media effective August 1, stepping in after Raul Reis announced he is stepping down to return to research and teaching. Reis joined Hussman as dean in 2022 and leaves after a four-year run in which the school won the Hearst national championship in collegiate journalism — sometimes called the "Pulitzers of college journalism" — every single year of his tenure, an eighth consecutive title this spring. Reis will take research leave this fall before returning to the classroom in the spring.

🏛️ Downtown Raleigh parking rates jump for the first time since 2018

Drivers began paying more to park in downtown Raleigh on August 1, as the city's first parking rate hike in nearly seven years took effect. On-street parking rose to $1.50 an hour, city-owned garages like the Wilmington Street deck jumped from $2 to $3 an hour, and monthly permits climbed from $125 to $135. The free two-hour parking window at participating decks remains untouched, and city officials say the added revenue will go toward security, maintenance and upkeep of the parking system.

🤝 Wake County still housing foster teens in office building amid shortage

More than 100 Wake County foster children have spent nights in converted offices and meeting rooms at the Swinburne Building since the start of 2025, according to a new ABC11 report, with as many as nine kids sleeping there on a given night. The county's pool of licensed foster homes has been cut roughly in half since before the pandemic, leaving 539 children in care and just 76 licensed homes to place them in. An $8 million pilot program aimed at moving kids out of offices and into homes is underway, but officials say Wake County's roughly $235,000 share is still trickling in while the shortage keeps growing.
