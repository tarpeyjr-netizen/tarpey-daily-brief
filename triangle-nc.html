THURSDAY, September 17, 2026

🚨 Two Shootings Leave Teen, Man Dead in Durham

Two unrelated shootings tore through Durham within about two hours Tuesday night, killing a 17-year-old boy and a man and leaving three others hurt. Officers responded just before 10 p.m. near West Umstead and South streets, where they found the teen and a woman with gunshot wounds; the teen died at the hospital and the woman is expected to recover, according to WRAL. About two hours later, police found three men shot in the 400 block of West Club Boulevard, one of whom died at the scene while the other two were taken to the hospital with life-threatening injuries, CBS17 reported. Durham police have not said whether the two scenes are connected, and no arrests have been announced in either case. The violence, which brought the night's total to five people shot, adds to a summer and early fall that has repeatedly tested the city's public-safety response.

🏛 Raleigh Delays Teen Curfew Vote for More Input

Raleigh City Council pushed back an expected Sept. 15 vote on a citywide teen curfew, opting to gather more feedback before deciding on the measure, CBS17 reported. The proposed ordinance would restrict unsupervised minors during late-night hours and could penalize parents or businesses that allow violations; leaders began pursuing it after roughly 8,000 teenagers converged on Brier Creek and Glenwood South over Fourth of July weekend in "teen takeovers" that left nine people shot and led to 29 arrests. Council members said they specifically want to hear from teenagers themselves before finalizing the rules, according to ABC11. A community meeting where city leaders plan to hear directly from youth is set for Oct. 12 at the Five Point Center for Active Adults, with a new council vote expected sometime after that session.

🏗 Cary Water Main Break Knocks Out Service for 50,000

A 30-inch water main ruptured on N.C. 55 in the early hours of Sept. 14, cutting pressure to about 50,000 customers across Cary and Morrisville and forcing roughly 30 area schools to dismiss early, WRAL reported. The town closed N.C. 55 between the Jenks Road and Lewey Drive intersections while crews made repairs and issued a system pressure advisory urging customers to boil water for a full minute or use bottled water; several downtown Cary restaurants closed for the day as a precaution. No harmful bacteria was ultimately detected in the system, but the outage emptied store shelves of bottled water and left at least one daycare scrambling. Days later, Cary Utilities Director Jamie Revels defended the town's outreach at a council meeting, saying "we communicated on just about every forum that we could early and often," after residents and a town advisory board member complained they never got word of the advisory, according to WRAL.

🤝 Wake Libraries Start Lending Laptops, Hotspots

Wake County Public Libraries has begun lending out 100 Chromebooks and mobile hotspots to residents who lack reliable home internet, WUNC reported. The devices are available on a first-come, first-served basis for one-week checkouts at 13 branches spanning fast-growing suburbs and more rural corners of the county, including locations in Raleigh, Fuquay-Varina, Garner, Wendell and Zebulon, CBS17 reported. Since the grant-funded program quietly began rolling out in March, the library system says it has connected nearly 500 people to devices, and officials are seeking additional funding to expand it to more branches. The effort is aimed at closing the digital divide as Wake County's growth keeps pushing into areas broadband providers haven't fully reached.

🎓 UNC Probe Widens to Steve Belichick as Coach Stays Quiet

The university's investigation into UNC football has shifted focus toward defensive coordinator Steve Belichick, with lawyers questioning witnesses about whether he used or provided drugs to at least one player, and separately about a possible relationship between a program employee and a player, NBC Sports reported. Head coach Bill Belichick, Steve's father, told reporters this week that the program maintains "an environment of compliance" but declined to discuss specifics, with a team spokesman saying Belichick and other university employees "will not discuss the situation in any manner" while the inquiry continues, according to WRAL. The probe began after since-resigned general manager Michael Lombardi was placed on paid leave in late July, and it has now outlasted his departure by more than six weeks with no resolution announced. The uncertainty hangs over a Tar Heels program that is otherwise off to a 2-0 start under Belichick.

WEDNESDAY, September 16, 2026

🚨 Durham Man Charged With Murder in Ex-Girlfriend's Death

A Durham man is facing a first-degree murder charge after police say he blocked his ex-girlfriend's car on Revere Road near Sedwick Road Saturday afternoon, got out and opened fire while she was driving, with her two children inside. Officers responding around 1:30 p.m. found 43-year-old Victoria James dead at the scene; her adult daughter suffered non-life-threatening injuries and a 16-year-old passenger was uninjured, according to CBS17. Durham police say 38-year-old Lontrel Alston, who had previously dated James, is charged with first-degree murder, two counts of assault with a deadly weapon with intent to kill and discharging a weapon into an occupied vehicle, and is being held without bond. Police describe the shooting as domestic in nature. Loved ones told WRAL they are still struggling to process how a dispute between former partners turned deadly in broad daylight.

🏈 Triangle Programs Post Blowouts and a Comeback Win

College football's opening weekend delivered one of the more lopsided stretches in recent Triangle memory. NC State set school records with 734 total yards and 10 touchdowns in a 73-0 rout of Richmond at Carter-Finley — the Wolfpack's most points scored since 1936 — according to WRAL. Down the road at Kenan Stadium's 100th-anniversary opener, Bill Belichick's Tar Heels throttled East Tennessee State 35-3 behind three rushing touchdowns from Demon June and a defense that held the Buccaneers to 255 total yards, per WRAL's recap. Duke needed a bigger rally, erasing a 24-14 deficit to beat Illinois 31-27 on the road behind Nate Sheppard's three touchdowns and Walker Eget's three touchdown passes, extending the Blue Devils' winning streak to six games. All three Triangle programs enter this weekend undefeated or 2-0.

🏛 Raleigh Council Weighs Subdivision Rules, Water Funding

Raleigh City Council held public hearings Tuesday on Text Change TC-4-26, which would rewrite the city's development code to match new state rules on minor and exempt subdivisions and strip away some of the administrative cost of small-scale infill housing projects, according to the RaleighForward Roundup. The Planning Commission has already recommended approval, though council took no final vote at Tuesday's session. In the same meeting's work session, Raleigh Water staff briefed council on the city's drinking-water supply, drought recovery and options for expanding capacity as the city plans for growth. Council also heard a separate rezoning request for a roughly 0.93-acre parcel at Bragg and East streets that would loosen height limits to encourage more housing variety near downtown.

🏗 Chapel Hill Begins Draining Legion Pond for Housing Project

Crews have started removing the pond, dam and surrounding trees at Chapel Hill's Legion Road property, clearing the way for a future affordable-housing community and a new town park, the town told Chapelboro. The dam had been flagged as a high hazard for its structural condition, forcing the removal before any other site work can proceed; the town expects the draining and clearing phase to wrap by spring or summer 2027. The affordable-housing piece will front Legion Road on up to nine acres, while roughly 28 acres are set aside for the park and a connection to the adjacent Ephesus Park trail network. Housing construction itself won't start until 2028, and the constructed wetland meant to replace the pond will come only after those homes are built; in the meantime the site will stay fenced, though the dance studio and park trails remain open.

📊 Raleigh Chamber Touts Record Year of Business Recruitment

Business and civic leaders gathered in downtown Raleigh Tuesday for the Raleigh Chamber's annual economic development meeting, which organizers billed as capping an "unprecedented" year of growth, CBS17 reported. Since the chamber overhauled its business-recruitment strategy in 2019, Wake County Economic Development says it has helped land roughly 8,900 new jobs and more than $4 billion in capital investment. Outgoing board chair Kevin Howell, NC State's vice chancellor for external affairs, credited the region's talent pipeline and coordinated recruitment push for the momentum as he passed the gavel to new leadership. The meeting comes as national rankings, including CNBC's latest state business-climate survey, continue to place North Carolina among the top two states in the country.

TUESDAY, September 8, 2026

🚨 Labor Day Drowning Ends Falls Lake Search

A holiday afternoon at Falls Lake turned to tragedy Monday when a 16-year-old boy went under near the Sandling Beach recreation area off Creedmoor Road, and his body was recovered just before 8 p.m. after an hours-long search. Wake County deputies were called around 2:20 p.m., and Wake County EMS, the Northern Wake Fire Department and the Wake Forest Fire Department joined the effort, with the county's Mobile Command Unit, drones and rescue boats deployed across the water. Before first responders arrived, park rangers asked adult swimmers on the beach to form a line and wade through the shallows searching by hand. "What if it was, like, my sibling?" volunteer Jose Hernandez told ABC11, describing the emotional toll on the crowd that gathered along the shore. Authorities have not said whether the teen was wearing a life vest or what caused him to go under.

🎓 Drive-By Shooting Wounds Two NCCU Students

Durham police are investigating a late-night drive-by shooting that wounded two North Carolina Central University students just before 11 p.m. Sunday. A man and a woman were walking near Concord and Dupree streets, not far from East Lawson Street and the NCCU campus, when someone fired from a passing car; both suffered injuries believed to be non-life-threatening and have since been released to their families. NCCU Police Chief Robert Gaddy told the campus he has directed increased patrols on and around campus and urged students to travel in groups and use the free Eagle After Dark shuttle. A neighbor playing video games at home said his car's back window was blown out by the gunfire. No arrests have been made; Durham Crime Stoppers is taking tips at (919) 683-1200.

🏈 Belichick Deflects As UNC Probe Widens

Bill Belichick held his first news conference since general manager Michael Lombardi resigned, declining to discuss the university's ongoing investigation into alleged off-the-field misconduct and steering every question back to Saturday's home opener against East Tennessee State. Lombardi — the highest-paid GM in college football at more than $1.5 million a year — stepped down 38 days after being placed on paid administrative leave following an HR complaint from a former football staffer, and UNC has not disclosed what the complaint alleged. Belichick acknowledged that NCAA rules make head coaches responsible for compliance, saying "everyone's responsible, including me, for doing things properly." The scrutiny, compounded by son Steve Belichick's medical leave, has divided a fan base coming off a 4-8 season. "It's going to be a question of what did he know and when did he know it," longtime Tar Heel fan Wes Johnson said. Carolina opened the year with a 15-10 win over TCU in Dublin.

🌡️ Raleigh Rebounds While Durham Rations Water

The Triangle's drought is easing unevenly, and Labor Day boaters found sharply different conditions from one lake to the next. Falls Lake, Raleigh's primary drinking water source, has climbed back to 84% of its water supply pool — comfortably above the 75% threshold that triggers conservation — allowing the city to lift restrictions last month, though secondary source Lake Benson sits at just 53%. Durham has no such reprieve: the city remains under a Stage 2 Water Shortage Response, its lakes at 49% capacity and its days of supply falling from 147 in mid-August to 113, with officials calling demand "unusually high" despite the rules already in place. To avoid a Stage 3 escalation, Durham is buying more Jordan Lake water through the Town of Cary and continuing to pump from Teer Quarry. The U.S. Drought Monitor still has all of Wake County in moderate drought, with severe conditions in northern Durham and Orange counties and extreme drought in Person and Caswell.

🤝 Raleigh Church Claims Long-Delayed Federal Marker

Saint Paul AME Church in downtown Raleigh unveiled a National Register marker from the U.S. Department of the Interior this weekend, closing out a recognition effort that began nearly four decades ago. The congregation earned its National Register listing in 1987, but the federal government stopped issuing physical markers and left it to honorees to fund their own — a gap Historical Commission chair Florence Avery said the church finally closed. The designation honors a site central to the first Freedmen's Conventions, where freedmen gathered at Saint Paul in 1865 and 1866 to press for voting rights and the right to testify in court. "We are indeed grateful to stand as a place of hope, restoration, healing," said pastor Dr. Steve Lyons. Church leaders say their next push is for grants to restore parts of the 178-year-old building.

The Triangle's drought is easing unevenly, and nowhere is the split clearer than between Raleigh and Durham. Durham, by contrast, remains under a Stage 2 Water Shortage Response, with its reservoirs at 49% capacity and days-of-supply falling from 147 in mid-August to 113 as of last week, a drawdown officials called "unusually high" given restrictions already in place. 🤝 Raleigh Church Gets National Marker 178 Years On

Saint Paul AME Church unveiled a National Register marker from the U.S. Department of the Interior this weekend, capping a recognition effort that began nearly four decades ago. The Raleigh congregation, founded 178 years ago, hosted the first Freedmen's Conventions in 1865 and 1866, gatherings that pressed for voting rights, the right to testify in court and other civil protections for newly freed Black North Carolinians. Historical Commission chair Florence Avery explained the long delay: the church earned its national listing in 1987, but the register stopped issuing physical markers, leaving honorees to fund their own. "It's been a long journey, you know, from '87 to now," she told ABC11. Pastor Dr. Steve Lyons said the marker lets "the world see that Saint Paul is here," and church leaders say their next push is grant funding to restore parts of the building.
