🚨 Durham Unveils Violence Reduction Plan After Six Shootings

After six shootings in three days, Durham has a draft violence reduction plan built on a focused deterrence model, with a goal of cutting homicides, nonfatal shootings and shooting-related hospitalizations by 10% each year. Under the proposal, Durham police would review shootings weekly to find the people most at risk of violence. Outreach workers would respond to shootings, mediate conflicts and offer services such as 6 to 18 months of daily life coaching, job and education help, child care and emergency relocation. Mayor Leonardo Williams said people who keep committing violence would still face jail. The plan would cost about $450,000 through next June and about $4.6 million in its first full year, and WRAL has also covered the proposal. It is still a draft, a youth summit is planned for February, and the rollout is expected next year.

🤝 Raleigh Christmas Parade Follows Moore Square Tree Lighting

Downtown Raleigh started the holiday season Friday night with a tree lighting in Moore Square. The Raleigh Christmas Parade follows today. Organizers expect thousands of people downtown, and the parade will have more than 100 floats plus performances by local high school bands. Streets downtown will close during the parade, and organizers say parking will be available but advise arriving early. ABC11 has a guide to the parade for families planning to go.

🏈 Rolesville Edges Millbrook in Overtime Playoff Rematch

Rolesville beat Millbrook 21-18 in overtime Friday night in a Class 8A East Regional Semifinal. Nebraska commit Amir Brown scored the winning touchdown on a fourth-and-1 direct snap, answering a Millbrook field goal in overtime. The teams' first meeting in September was called off after several fights broke out among fans and the stands were evacuated. For the rematch, Wake County Schools capped attendance at 2,300 and added security, and no notable incidents were reported. Rolesville plays at Wilmington Hoggard next Friday in the 8A East final, with a trip to the state championship at stake.

🏒 Canes Host Oilers in Matinee Clash of Division Leaders

The Metropolitan-leading Hurricanes (16-3-3) host Edmonton at 1 p.m. today at Lenovo Center. Carolina is coming off Thursday's 2-1 overtime loss to Montreal, and the Pacific-leading Oilers (17-4-3) lost 3-1 at Tampa Bay the same night, which ended their six-game win streak. Taylor Hall will be honored before puck drop for reaching 1,000 NHL games, and Nikolaj Ehlers brings a four-game point streak into the game. The Oilers won the October meeting 6-5 in a shootout. The afternoon game opens a back-to-back that ends Sunday at Madison Square Garden against the Rangers.

🚨 AI-Made Nude Images Target Woods Charter Students, Staff

A juvenile is suspected of using AI to turn ordinary photos into nude images and videos of people at Woods Charter School in Chatham County, according to a letter from Principal Cotton Bryan. The letter says the material involved 47 current and former students and 14 current and former faculty members, and some of it was shared online. The Chatham County Sheriff's Office confirmed only that it is running a criminal investigation involving a juvenile, and the school said it expects charges soon. The school has brought in the Orange County Rape Crisis Center and Second Bloom of Chatham to support families. North Carolina changed its sexual extortion laws in 2024 to cover AI-generated pornographic material.

🎓 UNC Board Weighs Tougher Out-of-State Enrollment Penalties

A UNC Board of Governors committee has voted for tougher penalties for campuses that exceed their out-of-state enrollment caps. Under the change, schools would be fined every year they go over their cap, not only after two years in a row. UNC System President Peter Hans, rather than need-based aid programs, would decide where the penalty money goes. UNC-Chapel Hill and NC State are each capped at 18% nonresident students. Chapel Hill's incoming class this fall was about 20% out-of-state, and the university has already been fined $5 million and ordered to repay tuition for each extra student. The full board is expected to vote in January.

🌡 Extreme Drought Spreads as Durham Buys Water

Extreme drought now covers more than a quarter of North Carolina, including parts of Wake and Johnston counties, and Durham had just 76 days of easily accessible water left as of Wednesday, WUNC reports. Nearly 40 counties are in the worst category, up from 14 a week earlier, and Durham's reservoirs got less than a tenth of an inch from the latest cold front. To keep taps running, Durham has spent more than $4.1 million buying water from Cary and OWASA since declaring the drought on June 15, WRAL Investigates found, including $1.25 million to Cary in October alone. The city may move to Stage 3 restrictions, though officials say the purchases will not drive big rate increases. Forecasters see some rain this weekend and call an El Niño winter the state's best chance to recover.

🏒 Hutson Strikes Twice as Canes Fall in OT

The Hurricanes lost 2-1 in overtime to Montreal on Thursday night at Lenovo Center after Lane Hutson tied the game with 1:20 left in regulation and then won it 38 seconds into the extra period, according to the team's recap. Nikolaj Ehlers had put Carolina ahead with 6:19 left after Logan Stankoven's backcheck knocked the puck loose. Pyotr Kochetkov stopped 22 of 24 shots in only his second loss of any kind this season. 🎓 Wake School Board Rejects Brassfield Elementary Cell Tower

The Wake County school board voted 6-3 Tuesday night against leasing land beside Brassfield Road Elementary in North Raleigh for a 185-foot cell tower, WRAL reports. The deal with APC-EDGE would have paid the district $30,000 in its first year, rising 3% a year, for about $1.5 million over as long as 25 years. About a dozen parents and neighbors spoke against it, citing property values, safety near play areas and fears that families would leave the school. The board had delayed an October vote, and the developer had already cut the tower's height from 199 feet and moved it, but that did not win over the majority.

🏗 Publix Signs Lease for Third Durham Store

Publix has signed a lease for a third Durham grocery store, at Martin Luther King Jr. Parkway and Shannon Road in the southern part of the city, WRAL reports. The 50,239-square-foot Publix at University Crossing is expected to employ about 140 people. The chain already runs Durham stores at Marketplace at Ellis Crossing and Latta Park. No opening date has been set.

🏛 Neighbors Push Back on Fuquay-Varina Athletic Complex

Fuquay-Varina's plan for a youth athletic complex on 85 acres along Panther Lake Road in Willow Spring is drawing pushback from nearby homeowners, WRAL reports. The town paid $8 million for the land after looking at 10 sites, and says the complex will meet growing demand for youth sports. Neighbors including Stephanie Shattuck, who runs a photography business across the road, worry about traffic, noise and higher property taxes. The town has no final construction or financing plan yet and says traffic and infrastructure will be central to the next planning phase. Shattuck is set to meet Town Manager Adam Mitchell in December.

🤝 Triangle Cyclists Rally on Safety After Apex Crash

Dozens of Triangle cyclists met Thursday night to learn their rights on the road, nearly two weeks after a crash on Optimist Farm Road near Apex killed one rider and injured 20 others from a Capital Cycling Club group, WRAL reports. Oaks and Spokes and BikeWalk NC hosted the session, where attorney Ann Groninger covered how to stay safe, what to do after a collision and how insurance works. The event had been planned before the crash, and organizers opened it to a wider audience afterward. Registration fees will go to the Capital Cycling Club.

🏛 WakeMed Board Rejects Mandy Cohen as Nominee

WakeMed’s board turned down former state health director and CDC director Dr. Mandy Cohen as a board nominee, the only one of eight Wake County nominees it rejected, WUNC reported Wednesday. The board voted 5-1 to approve its slate without her, with Commissioner Tara Waters the lone dissent. Under new articles of incorporation tied to the WakeMed-Atrium Health partnership that commissioners approved in September, the county appoints eight of 14 board members only “with the consent” of the existing board, which in effect gives that board a veto. Commissioner Vickie Adamson, who recruited Cohen, said, “They don’t want true oversight; they don’t want accountability,” while State Treasurer Brad Briner and SEANC have warned the merger could raise costs for the State Health Plan’s 748,000 members.

🚨 Chapel Hill Pulls Carrboro Police Merger From Agenda

The Chapel Hill Town Council took a resolution off its Wednesday consent agenda that would have authorized talks with Carrboro on merging the two towns’ police departments, according to WRAL. The plan would move Carrboro’s smaller force into the Chapel Hill Police Department over five years, and the towns project savings by year two. At a Carrboro public hearing on Nov. 10 that ran until nearly 2 a.m., most of the more than two dozen speakers opposed the idea, citing transparency, public safety and the loss of Carrboro’s identity. Chapel Hill could take the item up as early as Dec. 9, though no date is set, and Carrboro’s council is expected to return to it Dec. 1.

🏗 250-Room Marriott Hotel Planned for North Hills

Kane Realty announced Wednesday that it will build a 250-room Autograph Collection hotel by Marriott at 500 Hardimont Road in Raleigh’s North Hills Innovation District, working with Raleigh-based CMC Hotels, WRAL reports. Plans include several restaurants, a speakeasy and a resort-style pool deck, and construction is set to start in the second half of 2027. The hotel joins a busy pipeline in the district: the 332-unit Tributary apartments broke ground in January, and the 20-story Vesper residential tower is scheduled to break ground in 2027. Kane is also planning another office tower, which would be the seventh at North Hills.

🚨 Labor Department Sues Durham Philanthropist Over Unpaid Wages

The N.C. Department of Labor has sued self-described philanthropist Courtney Jordan and three associates at CJF America, seeking more than $91,000 in unpaid wages for 13 people who worked at the organization’s summer camps, WUNC reports. Labor Commissioner Luke Farley announced the suit at a Raleigh press conference Wednesday and said the department would “pursue this case vigorously.” Durham police had been seeking Jordan and CFO Nick Gallardo since September 2025 on false-pretenses and conspiracy charges, and the two were arrested in March after a high-speed chase through Person, Durham and Orange counties. WRAL’s investigative team has also covered the case.

🌡 Strong El Niño Offers Best Hope Against Drought

A possibly record-strong El Niño this winter is North Carolina’s “best chance to really get some relief” from drought, a National Weather Service hydrologist in Raleigh told WUNC. Nearly 10% of the state, including large parts of Wake County, is in extreme drought, and Durham has 77 days of easily accessible water left. Assistant state climatologist Corey Davis said about three extra inches of rain a month from December through February would leave the state in much better shape by spring, though the drought won’t clear overnight. Rain is in the Triangle forecast for Sunday and Monday, and the State Climate Office expects a warm, wet winter for central North Carolina.

🎓 Wake School Board Approves 2027-28 Reassignment Plan

The Wake County Board of Education voted 8-1 on Tuesday to approve its 2027-28 student enrollment plan, which will move hundreds of students to new schools. The plan aims to relieve crowding, ease transportation problems, tighten feeder patterns and set attendance zones for the new Morrisville High School. Families in Wake Forest's Dansforth neighborhood will be moved from Rolesville Elementary to Forest Pines Elementary after the board turned down their request to go to Heritage Elementary instead. "I'm having a hard time believing that they're hearing our concerns," one Dansforth mother said. Under the plan, the Preston community will no longer be split between Green Hope and Morrisville high schools.

🚨 Durham Chief Urges Swift Action After Spate of Shootings

Durham Police Chief Stephanie Mardis told city and county leaders on Tuesday that she cannot wait for a long-term plan to take effect, after four shootings in less than 24 hours. Officials presented a strategy that would pair 12 life coaches with more than 100 of the highest-risk people and connect them, voluntarily, with jobs, education and mental health services, while focusing resources on higher-crime areas. The goal is a 10% drop in non-fatal shootings and homicides, and the plan would need $4.6 million in additional funding in 2028. Mayor Leonardo Williams warned that spreading the effort too thin could weaken work to stop the immediate violence, and he noted that a shooting happened during the previous night's council meeting.

🏛 Raleigh Teens Tell Council a Curfew Won't Work

Teenagers came to Tuesday's Raleigh City Council meeting to argue against a proposed youth curfew, which the city has been weighing since a violent Fourth of July weekend that included teen takeovers and shootings. A Community Engagement Department survey from a youth event last month found that 42% of teens opposed the curfew and only 10% supported it as written. Speakers asked for more places to gather, more programming and more mentorship instead, and Azucena Morales-Lopez said teens have "almost nowhere to just exist without being treated like a threat." Councilwoman Megan Patton said the feedback supports investing in youth programs. No vote was held, and no curfew proposal is on an upcoming agenda.

🚨 Ten Charges Dropped Against Former NC State Team Doctor

Wake County prosecutors have dismissed 10 misdemeanor sexual battery counts against Robert Murphy, NC State's former director of sports medicine, because they fell outside the statute of limitations. District Attorney Lorrin Freeman confirmed the dismissal after Murphy's attorney argued that the alleged incidents, from 2016, 2017 and 2019, predated a state law that extended the deadline for filing those charges. Murphy still faces nine felony counts of sexual contact or penetration under the pretext of medical treatment, plus 13 misdemeanor sexual battery counts involving former male athletes. He was arrested in September and remains held on a $2 million bond. A separate civil suit by 31 former athletes was dismissed and is now on appeal.

📊 Regulators Approve Duke Energy Rate Hikes, Data Center Rules

The North Carolina Utilities Commission has approved Duke Energy rate increases that will raise a typical Duke Energy Progress residential bill by $9.62 a month in January 2027 and by another $5.89 in January 2028. Duke Energy Progress serves much of the Triangle. Residential rates are projected to rise about 9% over two years, and the commission set Duke's return on equity at 9.8%, below the 10.95% the company had requested. The ruling also creates the state's first separate rate schedule for data centers of 50 megawatts or more, with long minimum contracts, upfront infrastructure payments and exit fees. Data centers make up 92% of the 6.7 gigawatts of new capacity Duke contracted between March 2024 and April 2026. Duke will put $20 million in shareholder money toward bill assistance and weatherization, while a $42 monthly credit for low-income customers expires at the end of December.
