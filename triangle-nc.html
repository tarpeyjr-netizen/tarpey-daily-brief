Saturday, October 24, 2026

🚨 Jury convicts Olivares in Faith Hedgepeth murder

A Durham jury found Miguel Salguero Olivares, 33, guilty Friday of first-degree murder and related charges in the 2012 killing of UNC-Chapel Hill sophomore Faith Hedgepeth, and he was sentenced to life in prison without parole, ABC11 reported. Jurors deliberated about six hours over two days after prosecutors tied Olivares to the scene through DNA, a fingerprint and a palm print on a wine bottle, and a blood-covered rum bottle identified as the weapon. The defense called no witnesses and argued the investigation had missed evidence and that earlier sexual contact may have been consensual. WUNC also covered the verdict in a case that went unresolved for roughly 14 years.

🏛 Cary hires Russ Overton as permanent town manager

Cary's town council voted 7-0 Thursday to name Russ Overton, interim manager since December, the permanent town manager, WRAL reported. He replaces Sean Stegall, who resigned in December 2025 after expenses came to light that much of the council said it did not know about. A 2,600-page State Auditor report released in July flagged questionable spending including car stipends, council sunglasses and $121,314 for retreat video production. The council also approved nine oversight recommendations, including a review of credit card procedures and reinstating the internal auditor position, and the town has already posted that job.

🏛 Wake teachers push $680 million school bond

Wake County voters will decide on a $680 million school repair bond this election, and local teachers say it is needed to fix broken HVAC, mold and pests, WUNC reported. For a median-value home the bond would add about $20 a year in property taxes, and 55 buildings are slated for major renovations if it passes. Fred A. Olds Elementary, built in 1927, is due for a new roof, intercom and HVAC after a classroom hit 86 degrees this fall. The teachers' association says the bond still falls short of the full need and is also campaigning against two proposed state constitutional amendments that would cap income and property taxes.

🏒 Canes earn a point in wild shootout loss to Edmonton

The Hurricanes fell to Edmonton in a shootout Thursday night after a high-scoring game in which Carolina scored five times in regulation, according to the team's recap. Seth Jarvis scored in his season debut after being activated Wednesday, joined by goals from Jackson Blake, Sebastian Aho, Logan Stankoven and a late Juuso Valimaki tally. Connor McDavid had a five-point night for the Oilers, and Ryan Nugent-Hopkins scored the only shootout goal. Carolina plays at St. Louis on Saturday, and a three-goal third period had powered a win in Vancouver earlier in the week.

🏗 Red Hat Amphitheater hosts final show before relocation

Downtown Raleigh's Red Hat Amphitheater held its last concert Friday after 16 years, with crews due to begin dismantling it Monday, ABC11 reported. The venue is moving one block south as part of a multiyear project that also includes a Raleigh Convention Center expansion and a new Omni Hotel. The new amphitheater is expected to open in June, the hotel in 2028 and the convention center expansion by the end of 2029. City leaders say the project is meant to boost tourism and draw larger events.

🤝 Orange County community rallies for teen detained by ICE

Isaac Gallegos, a 19-year-old Honduran asylum seeker and 2025 Orange High graduate from Hillsborough, has been held since Oct. 7 when ICE detained him at an asylum check-in in Charlotte, WUNC reported. He has no criminal record and is now at a detention center in Georgia, where DHS says he will stay until his immigration hearing. A GoFundMe has raised more than $36,000, and Durham churches have organized a letter-writing campaign for immigration court. Hillsborough Mayor Mark Bell and Rep. Valerie Foushee have both called for his release.

Friday, October 23, 2026

🚨 Faith Hedgepeth murder case goes to the jury

Jurors began deliberating Thursday in the trial of Miguel Salguero Olivares, charged in the 2012 killing of UNC-Chapel Hill sophomore Faith Hedgepeth, a case unresolved for about 14 years. Prosecutors cited DNA and latent fingerprint evidence and say he broke into her Chapel Hill apartment and killed her, while the defense argues the encounter was consensual and that investigators overlooked other leads. The jury was sent home around 5 p.m. Thursday and was due to resume Friday morning after asking for summaries of the fingerprint and DNA reports, and a legal expert expects deliberations could take considerable time.

🏛 Early voting surges across Wake County

More than 1.1 million ballots were cast statewide in the first week of early voting, roughly 38% ahead of the 2022 midterm pace, and Wake County leads the state with 137,030 votes through Wednesday. ABC11 reports turnout at NC State's relocated early-voting site is up more than 62% from the same period in 2022, though the data does not show how many of those voters are students. NC State and NC Central ranked second and third for same-day registrations entering Thursday, with the top sites all on or near college campuses.

🏛 Russ Overton sworn in as Cary's permanent town manager

Russ Overton was sworn in Thursday as Cary's permanent town manager after nearly a year as interim, with a contract starting immediately at $344,000 a year. He took over in December after Sean Stegall resigned while on leave amid financial problems and concerns about excessive spending. The council skipped a national search, and the town is now reviewing credit card purchases, restoring an internal auditor position and tightening financial oversight.

🏒 Hurricanes fall in a shootout in Edmonton

Carolina lost 6-5 in a shootout at Edmonton on Thursday night, a day after its 6-3 win in Vancouver on Tuesday. According to NHL schedule data, the Hurricanes sit first in their division at 8-2-2 with 18 points, and they are next scheduled to play at St. Louis on Saturday, October 24.

🌡 Fall weather stays pleasant but dry

ABC11's forecast calls it a fabulous fall, with overnight lows dropping into the 40s while the region remains abnormally dry, which is bad news for anyone trying to grow grass.

Thursday, October 22, 2026

🎓 UNC Settles With Justice Department, No Payment

UNC-Chapel Hill announced Monday a 10-page agreement with the U.S. Department of Justice that resolves 18 civil rights investigations in exchange for continued federal funding, according to WUNC. The deal requires antisemitism training for students, faculty and staff, specialized training for campus police, and a Title VI coordinator. Unlike settlements reached by Columbia and several other private universities, UNC agreed to make no monetary payments, and much of the agreement mirrors changes already made under UNC System mandates and state law. Chancellor Lee Roberts said it reflects a "commitment to the fair and equal treatment of everyone at Carolina," and the agreement runs a little over two years. ABC11 also covered the settlement.

🚨 Faith Hedgepeth Murder Trial Nears the Jury

The defense in the murder trial of Miguel Salguero-Olivares rested Wednesday without calling a witness or presenting evidence, and he told the judge he would not testify, ABC11 reported. Defense attorney James Rainsford moved to dismiss all charges, arguing the state had not met its burden of proof, but the judge denied the request. Salguero-Olivares is charged with first-degree murder in the 2012 death of UNC student Faith Hedgepeth, who was found in her Chapel Hill apartment. Prosecutors say DNA evidence links him to the crime, while the defense has challenged the investigation. Closing arguments are expected Thursday, after which the case goes to the jury.

🏗 Chapel Hill Weighs Rewrite of Development Rules

Chapel Hill leaders are considering a major overhaul of the town's land use management ordinance, the development rulebook dating to 2003, with a public hearing set for the Town Council's meeting Wednesday at 6 p.m., per ABC11. The draft would allow more housing types, including triplexes and fourplexes, encourage walkable districts and update environmental protections. A UNC graduate student argues the changes could ease a tight, expensive rental market, while UNC planning professor Andrew Whittemore supports more options but worries about strain on roads, schools, stormwater and sewers. The proposal is not final, and the council could vote as early as November 18.

🏛 Durham Joins Bloomberg Affordable Housing Pilot

Durham is one of 30 cities chosen for a Bloomberg Philanthropies affordable housing pilot that gives each city between $450,000 and $750,000 plus technical assistance, WUNC reported Monday. Housing director Sarah Viñas said Durham plans to expand accessory dwelling units and other housing types, and is interested in Austin's experience, where more than 2,800 ADU permits were issued from 2015 to 2024. Durham recently launched a pilot offering low-interest financing to 20 homeowners who build ADUs, and Raleigh has allowed ADUs since 2020. Mayor Leonardo Williams said the city is exploring options to meet residents' housing needs.

🎓 Wake Schools Revises Reassignment Plan for 2027-28

The Wake County school system revised its proposed 2027-28 student reassignment plan on Tuesday, dropping two contested moves but keeping most changes, WRAL reported. The plan's 21 proposals would move students out of 26 schools and into 24, aiming to ease overcrowding, reduce trailer classrooms and fill the new Morrisville High School. The district no longer proposes moving some Athens Drive Magnet students to Cary High or some Salem Elementary and Middle students, and it added a year-round calendar option. Many families asked to keep their current assignments, and board members were largely satisfied with the process. A final vote is tentatively planned for November 17.

🏈 NC State Mourns Former Coach Chuck Amato

Chuck Amato, who coached NC State football from 2000 to 2006, died early Monday at his Raleigh home at age 80 while under hospice care, WUNC reported. A former Wolfpack linebacker and ACC champion wrestler, he went 49-37 in Raleigh with five bowl wins, and his 2002 team won 11 games, the program's only double-digit-win season. His tenure included quarterback Philip Rivers and No. 1 overall NFL draft pick Mario Williams. Amato was fired after a 3-9 season in 2006 and later returned to Florida State, where he had been a longtime assistant under Bobby Bowden. Tributes came from Gov. Josh Stein, NC State coach Dave Doeren and Duke coach Manny Diaz.

🏒 Canes Rout Canucks 6-3 to Lead NHL

The Hurricanes beat Vancouver 6-3 on Tuesday night, and the AP recap on WRAL says the win gives Carolina the NHL lead in points at 8-2-1. Logan Stankoven and Nikolaj Ehlers each scored twice and added an assist, Nicolas Deslauriers had two goals, and Jackson Blake collected three assists. Pyotr Kochetkov stopped 14 shots in the win, while Vancouver, 2-8-1, lost its eighth straight. The Hurricanes next play Thursday night at Edmonton.

🎓 Wake Revises School Reassignment Plan

The Wake County school system on Tuesday revised its draft student reassignment plan for 2027-28, WRAL reported, reversing some changes but keeping most of them. The plan has 21 proposals that would move students out of 26 schools and into 24, easing overcrowding, cutting trailer classrooms and filling seats at the new Morrisville High School. Two proposals were dropped, including moving some Athens Drive Magnet students to Cary High, and the district added a year-round calendar option for some families. Green Hope and Panther Creek high schools stay on the plan despite family requests, and ABC11 covered the public hearing at which parents pushed for more changes. The school board tentatively plans to vote on the final plan Nov. 17.

🚨 Raleigh Advances Plan to Cut Deadly Crashes

Raleigh is advancing a Comprehensive Safety Action Plan aimed at cutting fatal and serious-injury crashes by 50% by 2040 and eliminating them by 2055, ABC11 reported Tuesday night. The city recorded 185 fatal crashes and 905 serious-injury crashes from 2019 to 2023, averaging about 40 traffic deaths a year, and a LendingTree report ranked its crash rate highest among the 50 largest U.S. cities. Capital, Glenwood, Western, New Bern and Saunders are named as high-injury corridors. Two raised crosswalks on Saint Mary's Street near Broughton High are in place, 13 more are planned downtown in early 2028, and the city has applied for a $12.6 million federal grant with a decision expected by the end of 2026.

🏛 Wake Sheriff Race Centers on Jail Crowding

Public safety and jail overcrowding are the focus of the Wake County sheriff's race between Democratic incumbent Willie Rowe and Republican Kenny Blackwell, WUNC reported. The county's two detention facilities hold 1,574 people combined, while Rowe says the average population hit 1,778 in July. Blackwell, a former deputy and U.S. marshal, campaigns on faster response times, fighting fentanyl and human trafficking, and higher pay for deputies. The district attorney race is uncontested, with Democrat Wiley Nickel set to succeed Lorrin Freeman. Early voting runs through Oct. 31 and Election Day is Nov. 3.

🎓 UNC Deal Largely Codifies Existing Policies

UNC-Chapel Hill's 10-page agreement with the Justice Department largely codifies changes the university has already made under UNC System mandates and state law, WUNC reported Tuesday. Unlike Columbia's $200 million settlement, the UNC deal carries no payment, and it ends 18 civil rights investigations and a compliance review. Terms include antisemitism training, a Title VI coordinator, quarterly complaint reports and a ban on race-conscious admissions practices. Chancellor Lee Roberts said it reflects fair and equal treatment for everyone at Carolina, while one Jewish student called the language too broad and described a chilling effect on protest. The same report notes the federal government has made similar admissions accusations against the Duke and East Carolina medical schools.
