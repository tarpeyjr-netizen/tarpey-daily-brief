Monday, September 28, 2026

🚨 Man killed in early-morning shooting outside downtown Durham bar

A man died early Friday morning after being shot near the intersection of Rigsbee Avenue and West Corporation Street in downtown Durham, close to the PickleBack 2 bar strip, according to WRAL. Durham police responded to a report of a gunshot wound just before 2 a.m. and found the victim with life-threatening injuries; he was pronounced dead shortly after arriving at the hospital. As of Friday, police had not released the victim's name or age, nor detailed a suspect description or motive, and no arrests had been announced, per CBS17. The shooting adds to a string of recent gunfire incidents in the popular nightlife corridor, and the investigation remains active.

🎓 UNC chancellor backs Belichick as football probe closes

UNC Chancellor Lee Roberts said Friday that head football coach Bill Belichick "continues to have our support" from the administration and Board of Trustees, following the conclusion of a roughly seven-week outside investigation into the program, ABC11 reports. The probe examined allegations involving drug use and relationships within the program, as well as its workplace culture, and found isolated conduct issues rather than a systemic failure. General manager Michael Lombardi and defensive coordinator Steve Belichick, Bill's son, both resigned amid the inquiry. Roberts added that continued support "comes with expectations around conduct and behavior," while UNC says it will add training, oversight and accountability measures going forward, according to WRAL.

🏈 Duke cracks Top 25, NC State survives App State scare

Duke improved to 4-0 with a 62-7 rout of William & Mary on Saturday at Wallace Wade Stadium, extending its winning streak to eight games — its longest since 2013 — and earning the Blue Devils their first US LBM Coaches Poll Top 25 ranking (No. 21) since 2023, per Duke Athletics. Quarterbacks Walker Eget and Dan Mahan combined for 308 passing yards and five touchdowns in the win. Across town at Carter-Finley Stadium, NC State watched a 21-0 lead nearly evaporate before holding off Appalachian State 41-31, according to ESPN. Both Triangle programs head into the final week of September with momentum, while UNC prepares for a marquee home matchup against No. 3 Notre Dame.

🏒 Hurricanes close preseason with rout, open regular season Tuesday

The Carolina Hurricanes closed out their exhibition slate with a 6-0 shutout of the Nashville Predators on Saturday in Nashville, capping a preseason that also included a wild 6-5 comeback win over Nashville in Greensboro after trailing 5-1, per NHL.com. The defending Stanley Cup champions now turn to the regular season, opening Tuesday, Sept. 29 at home against the Florida Panthers at Lenovo Center. Forward Seth Jarvis remains on a no-contact path back from offseason shoulder surgery and is not yet cleared for game action, per earlier WRAL reporting, but the young roster's preseason form has coaches optimistic heading into the opener.

🤝 North Raleigh welcomes new restaurants this weekend

Arepa Culture NC, a family-owned Venezuelan restaurant that started as a single food truck in 2014, opened its new storefront at Six Forks Station in North Raleigh on Saturday with live music, dessert and giveaways, according to Hoodline. Founder Pedro Rodriguez's business now serves Raleigh, Apex and Pittsboro and plans to add weekend brunch and a breakfast menu at the new location. The same weekend, the team behind Cary's Shinmai Shokudo and Shinmai Kumo opened their third concept, izakaya-style Shinmai Moku, at Raleigh's Quail Corners shopping center, part of a broader wave of Triangle restaurant openings this fall.

🏒 Hurricanes Lock Up Kochetkov, Trim Roster Before Opener

The defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes signed goaltender Pyotr Kochetkov to a two-year contract extension on Friday, keeping the 26-year-old in Raleigh through the 2028-29 season at an average annual value of $3.95 million. The move shores up Carolina's goaltending depth ahead of a title defense that opens Tuesday, Sept. 29, when the Hurricanes host the Florida Panthers at Lenovo Center. As of Saturday the team still carried 25 skaters and three goalies and must trim to the 23-man regular-season limit before puck drop, with The Hockey Writers reporting that prospects Filip Ekberg and Zachary Lansard were returned to their junior clubs in Ottawa and Regina as part of the crunch. The extension takes a pending 2027 unrestricted free agent off the market and locks in Carolina's crease behind starter Frederik Andersen.

🚨 Franklin County Deputy Fired Over Flock Camera Misuse

The Franklin County Sheriff's Office fired a detective and indefinitely suspended its entire Flock Safety camera network this week after an internal audit found she had repeatedly queried the system to track a private citizen with no legitimate law-enforcement purpose. Detective Janet Leonard was terminated Friday, and ABC11 reports the case has been referred to the Franklin County District Attorney's Office to decide whether criminal charges are warranted. The county's automated license-plate-reader network, part of a regional system that shares real-time vehicle data with departments across the Triangle, will stay offline while the sheriff's office reviews access controls and audit procedures. The episode adds fuel to a growing regional debate over oversight of Flock camera systems, which Wake, Durham and neighboring counties have expanded in recent years for public-safety use.

🚨 Teen Charged in Poole Road Shooting That Wounded 16-Year-Old

Raleigh police this week arrested 19-year-old Maziyah Rock'Keria Spencer in connection with a Sept. 3 shooting on Poole Road that left a 16-year-old boy wounded in the foot, according to CBS 17. Investigators say gunfire during the confrontation also struck the nearby Hibachi 88 restaurant twice while customers and staff were inside, and warrants allege Spencer tried to kill two other unidentified men during the incident. Spencer, who was already on probation following a July felony conviction for possession with intent to sell marijuana, now faces multiple charges including assault with a deadly weapon with intent to kill inflicting serious injury, discharging a firearm into occupied property and possession of a firearm by a felon. The arrest closes out one of several shootings that have drawn Raleigh police attention to the Poole Road corridor this year.

🎓 NC State Volleyball Sells Out Reynolds, Hosts Duke Today

NC State volleyball drew its first-ever sellout crowd at Reynolds Coliseum this week, only to fall to No. 24 North Carolina in a tight, historic ACC battle. The Wolfpack turns right back around to host Duke today, Sunday, Sept. 27, at 1 p.m. in a Triangle rivalry rematch. The sellout is a notable attendance milestone for a program usually overshadowed by NC State's basketball and football crowds. It also lands amid a reshuffling of Triangle rivalries: the newly released 2026-27 ACC men's basketball schedule means NC State won't host either UNC or Duke in Raleigh this season, the first time that's happened in more than a century, per CBS17.

🌡 Dry, Sunny Weekend as Nor'easter Stays Offshore

Central North Carolina is in for a stretch of clear, comfortable weather this weekend as a powerful nor'easter churns well offshore, pulling moisture away from the Triangle rather than into it, CBS 17 meteorologists say. Raleigh should see mostly sunny skies with highs near 78 degrees Saturday and around 80 today and Monday, a sharp contrast to the heavy rain, coastal flooding and damaging winds the same storm is dumping on the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic. Forecasters expect the dry pattern to hold through much of next week, with no meaningful rain chances until a frontal boundary approaches later in the period. The break in the weather gives the Triangle's packed slate of fall festivals and outdoor sports a clean window this weekend.

🤝 Durham Pride Week Kicks Off With Central Park Rodeo

Durham Pride Week launches today with a Pride-themed Vendor & Food Truck Rodeo at Durham Central Park, running noon to 4 p.m. in partnership with the LGBTQ Center of Durham. The event brings together more than 40 food trucks and 60 LGBTQ-affirming community vendors, plus a Drag Story Hour, opening a week of programming that runs through Saturday, Oct. 3. This year's theme, "Always Forward, Never Straight: Preserving History, Coming Home," ties the center's ongoing advocacy work to the celebration. The kickoff continues a decades-long Durham Pride tradition that draws visitors from across the Triangle each fall.

SATURDAY, September 26, 2026

🚨 Durham Violent Crime Climbs 5.5% as Advocates Push Council

New Durham Police Chief Stephanie Mardis told a City Council work session Thursday that violent crime rose 5.5% in the first half of 2026, with 800 offenses reported from January through June compared with 758 over the same span last year, WRAL reported. Aggravated assaults climbed nearly 12% to 509 cases and reported rapes jumped almost 25% to 96, even as homicides, robberies and burglaries all declined and motor vehicle thefts fell 26%, according to CBS17. The presentation, Mardis's first crime-data briefing since being sworn in, came just two days after a pair of unrelated fatal shootings on Eva Street and North Duke Street, and city commissioners heard from residents and advocacy groups pressing for more investment in violence-interruption and youth programs, ABC11 reported. Mardis also flagged that 63% of the 170 firearms reported stolen in the second quarter were taken from unlocked vehicles, underscoring a recurring driver of gun violence in the city.

🏖 Raleigh Launches Affordable Housing Dashboard for Residents

The City of Raleigh rolled out a new Housing Production Dashboard this week, giving residents a single online hub to search affordable housing developments and track the city's progress on housing goals, the city announced. City staff said the tool highlights more than 5,900 affordable units the city has helped bring online, spanning projects built by both municipal and private partners, ABC11 reported. Officials framed the dashboard as part of a broader affordability push that includes increasing the supply of subsidized units, improving overall housing affordability and directing targeted investment toward ending homelessness. The launch comes as Wake County's growth keeps housing costs near the top of residents' concerns at council meetings across the region.

🏖 Former Hurricane Seeks 40-Story Tower on Glenwood South Bars

Former Carolina Hurricanes forward Bates Battaglia and his brother Anthony are moving to rezone the Glenwood Avenue lot beneath their bars, Teets and Lucky B's, to allow a 40-story mixed-use tower, up from the current seven-story limit, WRAL reported. The brothers' representatives say they envision a residential-and-office high-rise built above a rebuilt version of Teets at 425 Glenwood Ave. and 609 Tucker St., preserving the bar that has long been a gathering spot for Canes fans and players. Their attorneys at Morningstar Law Group have already held a community meeting and notified neighbors of the coming request, though as of Thursday evening the formal rezoning case had not yet been filed with the city. The move follows a familiar Glenwood South pattern of property owners entitling land for greater density before a sale to developers.

🌟 Sunshine Returns for Warm, Dry Fall Weekend

After Wednesday's chilly, rain-soaked high of just 64 degrees at RDU, the Triangle is drying out and warming back up for the weekend, with sunny skies and highs in the mid-70s Saturday climbing to near 80 on Sunday, Hoodline reported. Forecasters expect the warming trend to continue into next week, with highs reaching the low 80s Monday and mid-80s by Wednesday, even as overnight lows stay crisp in the 40s and 50s, according to WRAL. The mild, dry stretch marks a return to classic fall weather after a brief cold snap brought the coolest temperatures the region has seen since May. No rain is in the forecast through the weekend, setting up ideal conditions for outdoor plans across Raleigh, Durham, Chapel Hill and Cary.

🤝 Raleigh Market Closes for Final Weekend Before State Fair

This weekend marks the last chance to visit the Raleigh Market at the State Fairgrounds before it shuts down to make way for the 2026 N.C. State Fair, which runs Oct. 15-25, the N.C. Department of Agriculture announced. Vendors selling produce, crafts and prepared food fill the fairgrounds most Saturdays and Sundays, but the market will go dark starting next week so crews can convert the grounds for the fair's rides, exhibits and livestock barns. The State Fair remains one of the region's biggest annual draws, with organizers anticipating attendance approaching a million visitors over its 11-day run, according to Visit Raleigh. Shoppers looking for a final weekend at the open-air market are being encouraged to arrive early, as parking and vendor space are expected to be tight.

🚨 Ex-N.C. State Trainer Arrested on 32 Sex Abuse Charges

Robert Murphy Jr., who ran N.C. State's sports medicine program from 2012 until resigning in 2022 amid an internal investigation, was arrested Wednesday night in Ocala, Florida, days after a Wake County grand jury indicted him, ESPN reported. The 32-count indictment accuses Murphy of sexually touching student-athletes under the pretext of medical treatment, comprising nine felony counts of sexual contact or penetration and 23 misdemeanor counts of sexual battery spread across 14 victims, with allegations dating from October 2016 to 2021, according to WRAL. More than 30 former Wolfpack athletes have accused Murphy of abuse over the years, and he is charged under a 2019 North Carolina law that made abuse committed under the guise of medical treatment a felony, ABC11 reported. Murphy, 54, appeared before a Florida judge Thursday and waived extradition, clearing the way for his return to Wake County to face the charges.

🚨 Man Stabbed at Southeast Raleigh Apartment Complex

Raleigh police are investigating after a man was hospitalized with stab wounds when officers responded to an apartment complex on Thoroughbred Lane near Sunnybrook Road around 1:40 a.m. Thursday, WRAL reported. The victim's injuries were described as non-life-threatening, and investigators have identified a person of interest but made no arrests as of Thursday morning, according to Hoodline. Police have not released the victim's name, age or a possible motive, and no weapon has been recovered or confirmed publicly. The attack is the latest in a run of overnight violence to hit the capital region this week, days after a stabbing outside a Garner elementary school.

🏒 Hurricanes Erase Four-Goal Deficit to Stun Predators

The defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes rallied from a 5-1 hole to beat the Nashville Predators 6-5 at First Horizon Coliseum in Greensboro on Thursday night, capping the franchise's celebrated return to the city for the first time since 1999, Yahoo Sports reported. Logan Stankoven scored twice and Jesperi Kotkaniemi added a goal and an assist as Carolina reeled off five unanswered goals, with Felix Unger Sorum burying the game-winner off a snap shot just three seconds from time, according to NHL.com. Vitali Pinchuk, Cole O'Hara, Jonathan Marchessault, Adam Edstrom and Jack Drury had all scored for Nashville before the collapse, while Taylor Hall chipped in two assists for the Canes. The win pushed Carolina to 2-1-0 in the preseason with one exhibition game left before the Hurricanes open their title defense next month.

🏈 NC State Hosts App State in First Meeting Since 2006

NC State hosts Appalachian State on Saturday night at Carter-Finley Stadium in the programs' first meeting in 20 years and just the seventh all-time, with the Wolfpack (1-2) still reeling from a gutting 35-31 loss at Vanderbilt in which they led for 59 minutes and 59 seconds before fumbling in the end zone as time expired, WRAL reported. App State arrives at 3-0 and riding its reputation as a giant-killer, and is seeking its first-ever win over the Wolfpack after six previous losses, all in Raleigh, according to App State Athletics. Kickoff is set for 7:30 p.m. on ESPNU, with the Mountaineers installed as 13.5-point underdogs despite their unbeaten record. The teams last met in 2006, when NC State beat App State 23-10 in the only blemish on the Mountaineers' 14-1 FCS championship season.

🏛 Chapel Hill Council Accepts $10M Gift, OKs Library Fee

The Chapel Hill Town Council voted unanimously at its Sept. 23 meeting to accept a $10 million gift from the Bill & Peggy Britt Foundation and name new park facilities after the longtime local business owners, Chapelboro reported. Of the donation, $5.8 million will build the Bill and Peggy Britt Building, an indoor pavilion for wellness activities and recreation programs at Cedar Falls Park on Weaver Dairy Road, while the remaining $4.2 million will extend the Campus Recreation Trail toward the N.C. Botanical Garden and the Pritchard Branch Trail connecting West Cameron Avenue to Morgan Creek, according to the Town of Chapel Hill. At the same meeting, the council also voted unanimously to institute a $65 library card fee for residents living outside Chapel Hill, a response to Orange County shifting county library funding toward the new Carrboro branch earlier this year, Chapelboro reported. Together, the two actions reflect a council balancing a rare philanthropic windfall against a real funding squeeze on town services.
