Saturday, August 1, 2026

🌡️ Storms knock out power to thousands across the Triangle

Powerful storms swept through the region this week, knocking out power to more than 6,000 customers overnight and downing trees that closed roads in several neighborhoods. Crews worked through the following day to restore service, and outage numbers have since improved significantly. The storms arrived on the heels of a wetter-than-normal July that has been chipping away at the region's long-running drought.

🌡️ Raleigh set to lift water restrictions as Durham holds the line

Raleigh announced it will lift its Stage One water restrictions on sprinklers and irrigation systems this Monday, after Falls Lake rose more than two feet in a week to 84% of normal levels. Durham isn't following suit just yet: city leaders say recent rain helped but not enough to ease Stage Two restrictions, though the newly online Teer Quarry pumping system is now trimming 3 to 4 million gallons a day off demand at Lake Michie and Little River Reservoir. Regionally, heavy rainfall over the past week improved drought categories by one to two levels across much of central North Carolina.

🏛️ Wake County commissioners adopt $2.28 billion budget

Wake County commissioners adopted a $2.28 billion budget for fiscal year 2027, which runs through June 2027. The spending plan absorbs rising costs tied to federal decisions, including tariffs and the conflict in Iran, and closes a roughly $35 million revenue shortfall from property tax appeals. Commissioners still found $8 million in new revenue for public safety, including reopening the county jail annex and adding EMTs to keep pace with growth.

🎓 UNC-Chapel Hill to pause four academic programs

UNC-Chapel Hill will pause four academic programs following an extensive review, with Executive Vice Chancellor and Provost Magnus Egerstedt presenting the findings to the Board of Trustees. Separately, the university's Ackland Art Museum is weighing its first major upgrade in decades, with plans that could more than double the museum's footprint.

🏗️ RTP development and a $35M gene therapy investment fuel growth

Research Triangle Park is moving closer to a mixed-use development after a key board vote, joining a wave of regional investment that includes JPI's planned $150 million multifamily complex in Durham and a data center developer eyeing a 300,000-square-foot facility near Raleigh. Gene therapy company ChromaGenix is investing $35 million to open a 6,500-square-foot office on NC State's Centennial Campus. The activity comes as U.S. News named Raleigh the fourth-best big city to live in for 2026-2027 and CNBC ranked North Carolina No. 2 among America's top states for business.

🚨 No injuries after shots fired at Raleigh Wingstop

Gunshots were fired at a Wingstop inside New Bern Plaza in east Raleigh, but no one was hurt. Police have not announced an arrest. The incident adds to an ongoing conversation at Raleigh City Hall about public safety and youth activity in commercial corridors.

Thursday, July 30, 2026

🌡️ Falls Lake rebounds, Raleigh to lift water restrictions Aug. 3

A soggy week has turned around the Triangle's drought picture: the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reported Falls Lake rose more than 2 feet over the past week, pushing storage from 66% to 84% full, its highest level since mid-April. Raleigh announced Wednesday it will lift its Stage 1 water restrictions on Monday, Aug. 3, ending limits on sprinkler and irrigation timing that had been in place for months. Not every reservoir has recovered evenly, though — Little River Reservoir and Lake Michie in northern Durham County remain 12.3 and 8.8 feet below normal, and Durham is keeping its own Stage 2 restrictions in place for now. State officials say streamflows are near normal across most of central and northeastern North Carolina after the rain, though Raleigh is still carrying a 16-inch rainfall deficit since last August.

🌩️ Man killed as falling tree hits Wake County home during Tuesday storms

A line of strong thunderstorms brought lightning, damaging wind gusts and heavy rain across the Triangle on Tuesday night, and a man was killed when a tree fell onto a home on Bartons Enclave Lane near Raleigh around 10 p.m. The same storm system contributed to flash flooding and power outages reported across the region as the front moved through. It's the latest reminder that even as the drought eases, the wetter pattern is bringing its own hazards to the area.

🚨 Pedestrian killed after being struck by multiple cars on I-885 in Durham

A pedestrian died late Monday night after being struck by at least three vehicles while crossing northbound I-885 in Durham, between the Holloway Street and Cheek Road exits, according to WRAL and CBS 17. The person was walking northwest across the lanes near mile marker 12 when they were hit in the middle lane by a northbound Nissan Versa; additional vehicles that struck the pedestrian did not remain at the scene. The northbound side of I-885 was closed for hours overnight, reopening around 4:30–4:40 a.m. Tuesday, and it remains unclear whether any driver will face charges.

🚨 Shots fired at Raleigh Wingstop, no injuries reported

Gunshots rang out Wednesday afternoon at a Wingstop restaurant inside New Bern Plaza in east Raleigh, WRAL reports, though no one was hurt in the incident, which happened shortly after noon along New Bern Avenue. Police have not released details on a suspect or motive. The shooting adds to a string of public-safety incidents the city has grappled with this summer, including the Fourth of July "teen takeover" violence that prompted Raleigh's mayor and police chief to back a proposed youth curfew.

Wednesday, July 29, 2026

🌡️ Severe storms kill Wake County man, cut power to thousands

Severe thunderstorms and brief tornado warnings tore through the Triangle Tuesday evening, killing a 71-year-old man in north Raleigh when a tree crashed into his home on Bartons Enclave Lane; emergency crews found him trapped inside and pronounced him dead at the scene. Roughly 13,000 Duke Energy customers lost power across Wake County as trees fell on Pinecrest Road, O'Neal Road, and Norwood Road, and one falling tree struck a fire truck responding to a call. The storms disrupted flights at RDU and caused flooding on roads including Lumley Road in Durham. Crews worked overnight to restore power in the hardest-hit corridor, stretching from south of I-440 near Lassiter Mill Road to north of I-540.

🚨 Pedestrian killed after being struck by multiple vehicles on I-885

A pedestrian died late Monday night after being struck by at least three vehicles while crossing northbound I-885 in Durham near mile marker 12, between the Holloway Street and Cheek Road exits. Durham police closed all northbound lanes for hours as investigators documented the scene, with traffic not resuming until roughly 4:30 a.m. Tuesday and the interstate fully reopened by 4:40 a.m. The victim's identity had not been released as of the latest update, and police have not said whether any drivers involved will face charges. The investigation into how the collision occurred remains open.

🌡️ Raleigh to lift water restrictions after Falls Lake rebounds

Raleigh will lift its Stage One water restrictions on Monday, August 3, after a stretch of near-record rainfall pushed Falls Lake's water-supply storage from 66% to 84% full in just over a week. The restrictions, which limited sprinkler and irrigation timing, had been in place since April 20 amid one of the region's most severe droughts in years. Central North Carolina remains in extreme drought overall, but the late-July rain marks the sharpest turnaround Falls Lake has seen since the dry spell began. City officials credited both the rainfall and resident compliance, even after inspectors found roughly 46% of checked irrigation meters in violation of conservation rules earlier this summer.

🏛️ Chapel Hill unveils long-awaited rewrite of development rules

Chapel Hill this week released a draft of its rewritten Land Use Management Ordinance, capping nearly a decade of work to overhaul development rules that haven't been updated since 2003. The draft targets five goals: simplifying the code, promoting housing affordability and density, expanding "missing middle" housing options between single-family homes and high-density apartments, strengthening climate resilience, and encouraging walkable mixed-use development. Town officials are collecting public feedback ahead of a Town Council vote scheduled for November, with in-person open houses planned at the Chapel Hill Public Library on August 17 and September 10. The full draft is posted online for residents to review ahead of the vote.

🚨 Three hospitalized after fight breaks out in Cary

Cary police are investigating a physical altercation near the Mobile Estates community off Southeast Maynard Road that sent three men to the hospital with non-life-threatening injuries. Officers responded around 11:50 p.m. Saturday to the intersection of Barnes and Reed streets, where they found the men had been involved in a fight with another person who left before officers arrived. Investigators are still working to identify that individual. No arrests had been announced as of the latest update.

Tuesday, July 28, 2026

🌡️ Severe storms, possible tornado threaten Triangle Tuesday evening

A First Alert Day is in effect across central North Carolina, with a level 2 of 5 risk of severe storms bringing damaging wind gusts, large hail, and isolated tornadoes through Tuesday evening. A separate severe thunderstorm warning hit Orange County earlier in the evening, prompting tornado warnings for Chapel Hill and Carrboro and alerts from both Orange County government and UNC's Alert Carolina system. Forecasters say the storm cell brought 60-mph gusts and nickel-size hail as it rolled through the Piedmont. Storms should clear most of the area by midnight, with a return to pleasant, drier conditions expected through the end of the month.

🌡️ Last week's soaking rain cuts Raleigh's drought deficit nearly in half

The Triangle's wettest rain event in more than a year delivered real relief to a region still gripped by extreme drought, according to the North Carolina State Climate Office. Raleigh picked up 3.21 inches during the multi-day soaking, cutting its year-to-date rainfall deficit nearly in half to 3.20 inches. Falls Lake, the primary drinking water source for more than 650,000 residents in Raleigh, Garner, Wake Forest and surrounding towns, jumped by more than two feet and is now a little over three feet below its guide curve. Officials caution the drought isn't over, with most of the state still carrying rainfall deficits of more than seven inches dating back to last August.

🎓 UNC football GM Michael Lombardi placed on paid administrative leave

The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill placed football general manager Michael Lombardi on paid administrative leave effective immediately, the school announced Monday. ESPN reported the move stems from a human resources complaint filed by a former UNC front-office employee, with an internal investigation ongoing. Lombardi, one of coach Bill Belichick's closest advisors since joining the program in December 2024, has faced scrutiny before, including comments last year that the Tar Heels "consider ourselves the 33rd NFL team." The leave isn't expected to affect the 2026 roster ahead of UNC's Aug. 29 season opener against TCU in Dublin, but could carry longer-term implications for recruiting and the direction of Belichick's second season in Chapel Hill.

🤝 Search continues for Cary mother missing in Grenada

Family and friends of Cary resident Elizabeth "Liz" Waddell are pleading for help as the search continues for the mother, who disappeared while swimming near Grand Anse Beach in Grenada. Waddell's family said she went missing after leaving her towel on a pier and going for a swim; Grenadian authorities are leading the search with support from the State Department. Friends describe Waddell as adventurous and deeply connected to her Cary community, and her husband Cailen has praised local authorities' efforts. Anyone with information has been asked to come forward as the search continues.

🚨 Pedestrian killed in hit-and-run on Blue Ridge Road in Raleigh

A person died Friday night after being struck by a car on Blue Ridge Road in Raleigh, police said. Witnesses told officers the person walked into the roadway around 10:30 p.m. and lay down in the northbound lane before a dark-colored SUV struck and killed them; the driver did not stop. Raleigh police are investigating and are asking anyone with information to contact the department or submit an anonymous tip through CrimeStoppers.
