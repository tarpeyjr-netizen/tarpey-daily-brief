Wednesday, October 14, 2026

🌡 Eight Tornadoes Confirmed Across Central NC

The National Weather Service has preliminarily rated eight tornadoes from Sunday's outbreak in central North Carolina, including EF-1 twisters in Wake County at the Perry Creek/Neuse Crossing neighborhood and in Carpenter. Other EF-1 touchdowns hit near Jordan Lake in Chatham County, Louisburg, Linden and Fayetteville, while Harrells and Clayton saw EF-0 tornadoes. Crews are still surveying, with more work set for Tuesday and Wednesday and final ratings pending. ABC11 also found the storm's emotional impact lingering in Raleigh as neighbors continue cleanup.

🏒 Hurricanes Win Sixth Straight, Lose Staal

The Hurricanes beat Washington 4-2 Tuesday night for their sixth straight win, with Andrei Svechnikov scoring twice and Pyotr Kochetkov stopping 20 shots to improve to 4-0-0. Mark Jankowski added an empty-netter, his fifth straight game with a goal, and Sebastian Aho extended his point streak to seven games. The good news came with a blow: captain Jordan Staal has a calf tear and is expected to miss roughly one to two months. Carolina will likely recall a forward from the Chicago Wolves, with Bradly Nadeau and Felix Unger Sorum the leading candidates.

🏛 Raleigh Council Hears Teens on Curfew Debate

Raleigh leaders met with teens Monday at the Five Points Center for Active Adults for a forum called "Not Just a Curfew," drawing more than 100 people, WUNC reported. The council considered a minors' curfew in September after summer takeovers on Glenwood South and at Brier Creek Commons but did not pass it after equity and enforcement objections. Downtown Raleigh Alliance chief Bill King said takeovers hurt businesses, while youth ambassador Desmond Jones said teens want a voice and less of a feeling of being policed. ABC11 found the city still divided on how to respond.

🏛 Wake Schools Seek $680M Bond for Growth

The Wake County school system is asking voters to approve a $680 million bond as part of a seven-year, $823 million capital program, ABC11 reported. The plan includes about $143 million for three new eastern Wake elementary schools, nearly $330 million to renovate or replace nine schools including Cary High and Athens Drive Magnet High, and roughly $359 million for districtwide needs such as HVAC, security and technology. Early voting begins Thursday in Wake County.

🏗 Prime Barbecue Owner Sued Over RTP Restaurant

Prime Barbecue owner Christopher Prieto is being sued over his planned Prime STQ restaurant at Hub RTP in Durham, WRAL reported. Fred Von Canon, who owns 48 percent of the company behind Prime Barbecue, filed the Wake County Superior Court suit Oct. 6, alleging Prieto improperly used the Prime name and recipes and moved the RTP project into a new company that excluded him. Hurricanes defenseman Jaccob Slavin is an investor in the planned spring 2027 restaurant. Prieto says he disputes the allegations and intends to enforce arbitration provisions in the parties' agreements.

🎡 State Fair Ride Inspections Near Finish

With the N.C. State Fair opening Thursday, inspectors had cleared more than 50 of the fair's 94 rides, including the new America 250 Wheel, ABC11 reported. Sixteen state Labor Department inspectors expect to log at least 1,000 hours checking bolts, restraints, welds and maintenance records. A Flip Side ride lost power during a media demonstration, leaving riders suspended for several minutes before everyone got off safely. The scrutiny follows a deadly roller coaster incident at Six Flags Over Georgia.

Tuesday, October 13, 2026

🌡 Tornadoes Strike Northeast Raleigh and Beyond

At least seven tornadoes touched down across central North Carolina on Sunday, with northeast Raleigh the hardest-hit area, according to WRAL. The storms were tied to the remnants of Isaias, and the National Weather Service said it was reasonably confident close to half a dozen caused damage, with more possible touchdowns awaiting confirmation. Neighborhoods along the Neuse River reported downed trees and damaged homes, while other twisters hit near Apex, Clayton, Louisburg and Fayetteville. No injuries had been reported, and about 2,000 Duke Energy customers lost power. Neighbors are now taking a hands-on approach to cleanup as survey teams continue assessing damage.

🚨 Durham Firefighter Andrew Brokes Escorted Home

A solemn procession carried the body of Durham firefighter Andrew Brokes, 24, from Raleigh to his hometown of Burlington on Monday, ABC11 reported. Brokes died Thursday when part of a structure collapsed during a fire on Marly Drive, and WRAL has covered the escort and the ongoing investigation. Visitation is Friday from 4 to 7 p.m. at Lamb's Chapel in Burlington, and the public is invited to the funeral Saturday at 4 p.m. Gov. Josh Stein ordered flags at state facilities lowered to half-staff through October 15.

🏒 Hurricanes Extend Winning Streak to Five

The Carolina Hurricanes beat the Philadelphia Flyers 4-1 on Sunday to run their winning streak to five games and improve to 5-1-1, per NHL.com. Sebastian Aho and Andrei Svechnikov each had a goal and an assist, and Pyotr Kochetkov stopped 18 shots. Captain Jordan Staal sat out with a lower-body injury, though coach Rod Brind'Amour expects the absence to be short. The loss was Philadelphia's seventh straight to open the season, a franchise record, and it followed a 5-2 win at Chicago on Saturday.

🏈 UNC Escapes Pitt as Duke, NC State Fall

North Carolina edged Pittsburgh 21-17 on Saturday, while the Triangle's other two ACC programs both lost. Duke fell 28-25 at Georgia Tech, and NC State dropped its homecoming game to Wake Forest 38-14, according to the teams' athletics schedules. UNC and Duke meet Saturday, Oct. 17, at 3:30 p.m. at Wallace Wade Stadium in Durham, so the Tar Heels arrive with momentum and the Blue Devils with a close defeat to shake off.

✈️ RDU Flights Delayed After Two Ground Stops

Hundreds of flights were delayed at Raleigh-Durham International Airport on Monday after two ground stops, WRAL reported. The first disruption came late in the morning when a fiber-optic line was cut during a nearby plumbing repair, and the FAA blamed the second stop, from 6:45 to 8 p.m., on an equipment outage. About 261 flights were delayed by Monday evening, according to FlightAware, and travelers reported diversions to Richmond and New Bern. RDU said repairs were ongoing and referred questions to the FAA.

Monday, October 12, 2026

🌡 Sunday Storms Damage Raleigh, Cary and Rolesville

Severe storms and possible tornadoes damaged parts of central North Carolina on Sunday, including Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Clayton, Rolesville and Fayetteville. In Raleigh, homes on Toyon Drive and near Blue Blossom and Groveshire drives were hit, and a vehicle was found in water near Glenwood Avenue. Chatham County dispatch confirmed a tornado touchdown about nine miles east of Pittsboro and Rolesville reported one as well, though the National Weather Service had not finished its damage survey. No serious injuries were reported in the central part of the state, while western North Carolina saw flash flooding, landslides and several rescues, including a Burke County couple and their dog.

🏛 Stein Signs Gas Tax Suspension Through November 30

Gov. Josh Stein on Friday signed a bill pausing North Carolina's 41-cent-per-gallon fuel tax until Nov. 30. The suspension is expected to cost about $360 million, covered from an inflation reserve, with the Department of Transportation and Department of Environmental Quality held harmless. The Legislature passed it in a nearly unanimous special-session vote, though Democrats argued the timing was political ahead of the midterms. Because retailers already paid the tax on existing inventory, drivers may wait to see savings, and an amendment to let the attorney general police compliance was rejected.

🎓 Michael Jordan Gives $10 Million to UNC Social Work

Michael Jordan gave $10 million to the UNC-Chapel Hill School of Social Work, the largest single gift in the school's history, in honor of his mother, Deloris P. Jordan. The money will expand the Global Social Development Innovations Center, which will be renamed for her, along with the Jordan Institute for Families. It also creates the Jordan Family Scholars program, whose ten inaugural Master of Social Work students receive $12,500 scholarships each. Chancellor Lee H. Roberts praised Deloris Jordan's lifelong dedication to children and families.

🤝 Orange High Graduate Detained by ICE in Charlotte

The family of Isaac Gallegos, a 2024 Orange High School graduate, is seeking his release after ICE detained him in Charlotte on Oct. 7 during a routine check on his asylum case. He came from Honduras with his mother in 2016, and his application has reportedly been pending since. His family says he has no criminal record and helped support the household after his father was seriously injured in a 2025 car crash. He is reportedly held at a detention center in Georgia, and DHS said a pending application does not confer legal status.

🤝 Durham Psychiatrist Returns From Congo Ebola Response

Chip Hunter, a Durham psychiatrist, has returned from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where he served with Doctors Without Borders during a major Ebola response. His team built and staffed a 350-bed hospital in roughly three to four weeks. Hunter focused on mental health support for patients, families and staff, many of them traumatized by the loss of young patients. He hopes to return before year's end and urges people from varied professions to volunteer.

Sunday, October 11, 2026

🎓 Michael Jordan Gives $10M to UNC Social Work

Michael Jordan has donated $10 million to the UNC-Chapel Hill School of Social Work, the largest single gift in the school's history, ABC11 reported Saturday. The gift honors his mother, Deloris P. Jordan, and the school's Global Social Development Innovations Center will now carry her name. Money will also support the Jordan Institute for Families and scholarships for Master of Social Work students, including a new Jordan Family Scholars program whose ten inaugural members each receive $12,500. The gift was celebrated at the center's Global Research Symposium at the Friday Center in Chapel Hill, where Chancellor Lee H. Roberts said the university "could not be more grateful." WUNC's weekly NC News Roundup also highlighted the donation.

🏛 Raleigh Voters to Decide $203M in Bonds

Raleigh voters will weigh two separate $101.5 million bonds in November, one for affordable housing and one for transportation, according to a WUNC explainer. The housing bond would put $57.6 million toward development and preservation, $21.4 million toward redeveloping city-owned sites, $12 million toward homelessness responses and $10.4 million toward homebuyer and senior assistance. The transportation bond would direct $51.5 million to three road projects, including Marsh Creek Road and Ebenezer Church Road, plus $40 million for citywide plans and $10 million for neighborhood traffic management. The city says it will finance the housing bond without raising property taxes, while the John Locke Foundation argues debt is not costless. Either measure can pass or fail independently, and both appear on the back of the ballot.

🚨 Faith Hedgepeth Murder Trial Under Way in Durham

Testimony is under way in Durham in the murder trial of Miguel Salguero Olivares, charged in the 2012 killing of UNC sophomore Faith Hedgepeth, WUNC reported. Hedgepeth, 19, was found beaten to death in her off-campus apartment, and Salguero Olivares was arrested in 2021 after a DNA match; he faces first-degree murder, rape and burglary charges. Prosecutors say his DNA was on the bloody rum bottle used as the weapon and on a note left at the scene. The defense contends police left out key details and lost evidence tied to possible blood in the bathroom, a theme WRAL covered when a witness testified about missing blood evidence.

🚨 Durham Firefighter Killed in Apartment Fire

Durham firefighter Andrew Brokes, 24, died battling a fire that destroyed the roof and second floor of a building where 14 people lived, WRAL reported. No residents were hurt, and one called Brokes "that angel" for saving lives. Flags across North Carolina were lowered to half-staff in his honor. The cause has not been determined, though residents described earlier electrical problems such as dimming lights. Online records indicate the building is not zoned for residential use, and tenants who paid about $500 a month are now displaced.

🎓 Ex-NC State Sports Medicine Chief Held on $2M Bond

A Wake County judge kept a $2 million bond in place for Robert Murphy, the former NC State director of sports medicine, at his first court appearance Friday, WRAL reported. Murphy faces 32 counts, nine felony counts of sexual contact under the guise of medical treatment and 23 misdemeanor sexual battery counts, involving 14 former athletes between 2016 and 2021. He was arrested in Florida on September 23 and extradited to Wake County this week, and his lawyers deny the charges. A civil suit by more than 30 former athletes was dismissed in June on procedural grounds and is being appealed. Murphy returns to court November 2.

🌡 Isaias Remnants Bring a Soggy Weekend to the Triangle

Remnants of Hurricane Isaias are bringing rain to central North Carolina this weekend after the storm made landfall on the Gulf Coast, ABC11 reported. State emergency officials are preparing for heavy rain and gusty winds, mostly in western and southeastern parts of the state, and have activated the state search and rescue program, though they do not expect a repeat of Helene. Triangle organizers adjusted too: the Capital City Classic soccer tournament in north Raleigh, with about 275 teams, moved to turf fields. Red Cross volunteers from North Carolina have deployed to the Gulf Coast, and WRAL looked at how Isaias could affect the state's ongoing drought.
