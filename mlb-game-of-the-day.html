Seattle — Walk-Off Win, Blown Six-Run Lead

Emerson's Walk-Off Single Survives Minnesota's Furious Rally

Seattle Mariners 7, Minnesota Twins 6 — Final

George Kirby carried a shutout into the eighth inning and Cal Raleigh's three-run blast had Seattle cruising, but the Twins erased a six-run deficit in the final two innings — including a game-tying homer off closer Andrés Muñoz — before Colt Emerson's walk-off single in the ninth spared the Mariners' bullpen from a full-blown collapse.

The Game

For seven innings, this looked like a laugher. Kirby was masterful, scattering five hits across seven scoreless frames, and Seattle's offense staked him to a comfortable cushion. Josh Naylor's two-run double in the first got the Mariners going, and Cal Raleigh turned a two-on jam in the seventh into a three-run homer off Kendry Rojas, stretching the lead to 6-0 and sending the T-Mobile Park crowd toward the exits early.

Minnesota had other plans. With Kirby out of the game, the Twins teed off on Seattle's relief corps in the eighth: Trevor Larnach opened the inning with a two-run shot off Eduard Bazardo, and two batters later Royce Lewis followed with a three-run homer off Jose A. Ferrer, suddenly cutting the deficit to 6-5 and silencing the ballpark.

The Twins weren't finished. In the ninth, with Andrés Muñoz on to close it out, Luke Keaschall turned around a first-pitch fastball for a solo homer that tied the game at 6-6 — the second blown lead of the inning span and a shocking turn for a Seattle bullpen that had barely been tested all afternoon.

The Mariners didn't need long to answer. In the bottom of the ninth, Josh Naylor drew a walk and was lifted for pinch-runner Stuart Fairchild. After a Victor Robles single moved the tying threat into scoring position, Colt Emerson lined a single up the middle to send Fairchild home with one out, sealing a 7-6 win that swung from laugher to letdown to walk-off in the span of three innings.

Kirby's final line got no decision, and Muñoz escaped with a win despite the blown save — his sixth of the season — a reminder of how single-handedly Minnesota's late-game power turned what should have been a routine Sunday getaway day into one of the wilder finishes of the summer.

Wild-Card Rivals Slug It Out — Arizona Survives Its Own Bullpen

D-backs Blow It Open, Nearly Blow It Back: Arizona Outlasts Guardians 12-8

Diamondbacks 12, Guardians 8 — Final

The Arizona Diamondbacks turned a tight pitchers' battle into a laugher with a seven-run seventh inning in Cleveland on Saturday, only to nearly hand it all back when reliever Paul Sewald allowed five runs without recording an out. Geraldo Perdomo and Corbin Carroll both went deep in the decisive frame, and Arizona held on for a 12-8 win that kept its wild-card push rolling.

The Game

Cleveland struck first when Rhys Hoskins turned on a Kohl Drake fastball in the first inning and drove it 424 feet to center for a two-run homer, scoring Chase DeLauter. The lead held up until the fifth, when Arizona tied it at 2-2 with an assist from a Guardians misplay: first baseman Hoskins made a diving stop on a Perdomo grounder, then had no one to throw to at the bag.

The game stayed knotted until the seventh, when Arizona sent the lineup around against Cleveland's bullpen. Perdomo turned on Hunter Gaddis's first pitch and lined it into the right-field seats for the go-ahead run, and Corbin Carroll followed with a three-run shot to center off Franco Aleman. James McCann added a two-run double and Ildemaro Vargas an RBI single, and by the time the inning ended, a 2-2 game had become a 9-2 rout.

Nolan Arenado tacked on a solo homer in the eighth to push the margin to 10-2 — and that's when things very nearly unraveled. Sewald came in to protect the eight-run cushion and gave up a home run to DeLauter on his first pitch of the inning, then allowed four straight singles before he was pulled without recording a single out. Kevin Ginkel entered with the bases loaded and nobody out and worked out of it, but not before Cleveland had clawed the deficit down to 10-7.

Arizona answered with two insurance runs in the ninth, and although the Guardians scratched across one more run in the bottom half off Jonathan Loaisiga, the Diamondbacks closed it out, 12-8.

The win was Arizona's fourth straight and kept the club in the second National League wild-card spot; the Diamondbacks are an NL-best 14-5 since July 9. Cleveland fell to .500 for the first time since May 10, though the Guardians remain tied with the Twins for the final American League wild-card spot.

Comeback Classic · Rookie Delivers Twice

Sal Stewart Ties It, Then Scores the Winner as Reds Outlast Pirates 8-7

Cincinnati Reds 8, Pittsburgh Pirates 7 — Final

Cincinnati blew two different leads and trailed by two runs in the eighth inning before rookie Sal Stewart tied the game with a two-run single and came around to score the go-ahead run on a Pittsburgh throwing error. The Reds held on behind closer Emilio Pagan for their second straight win over the Pirates.

The Game

This was not a crisp one. Cincinnati's defense unraveled in the first inning — a misplayed single, a bobbled double-play ball and a walk set up a two-run single that put Pittsburgh ahead before Hunter Greene retired a batter. The Reds answered immediately, tying it at 2 in the bottom half on a Tyler Stephenson double and a Nathaniel Lowe RBI single.

From there the lead changed hands like a hot potato. Jake Mangum's two-out solo homer put Pittsburgh back in front in the second, and Paul Skenes — working with diminished velocity that has become a season-long storyline — still carried a lead into the third before JJ Bleday deposited a fastball ten rows deep in right field to tie it again. Elly De La Cruz tripled and scored on a two-run homer by Stewart in the fourth to finally give Cincinnati some breathing room, only for Pittsburgh to trim it back to 5-4 in the fifth on a Brandon Lowe double and a sacrifice fly.

Cincinnati carried that one-run edge into the eighth, where it evaporated fast. Reliever Caleb Ferguson allowed back-to-back singles, and after a pitching change, Pierce Johnson surrendered a bloop single that tied the game and a pair of two-out knocks — one skipping just past a diving De La Cruz — that plated two more and handed Pittsburgh a 7-5 lead.

The Reds did not blink. Noelvi Marte and Matt McLain led off the bottom of the eighth with singles, and Stewart lined a two-run single into right-center to tie it at 7. He moved to third on a groundout, and when Pittsburgh third baseman Nick Gonzales fielded a routine grounder and fired to second for what should have been an inning-ending force, second baseman Brandon Lowe dropped the throw — an error that let Stewart trot home with the go-ahead run. Pagan needed just a scoreless ninth, surviving a hit batter and an appeal on a called third strike, to lock down Cincinnati's second straight win over Pittsburgh and pull the Reds to within a game and a half of the NL Central cellar exit, with the third NL Wild Card spot still very much in sight.

Ultimate Grand Slam — First in Twins History

Clemens' Two-Out Grand Slam Shocks Royals After Twins Were No-Hit for Six Innings

Minnesota Twins 4, Kansas City Royals 3 — Final

Down to their last strike, trailing 3-0 and having managed a single hit through eight innings against Kansas City left-hander Noah Cameron, the Minnesota Twins produced one of the most improbable finishes of their season Thursday at Target Field. With two outs and the bases loaded in the ninth, Kody Clemens crushed an 0-2 slider into the right-center field seats for a walk-off grand slam, turning a lifeless afternoon into a stunning 4-3 victory.

The Game

For eight innings, this looked like a laugher heading the other way. Cameron faced the minimum through five, and Jac Caglianone's first-pitch, two-run homer off Bailey Ober in the third and an unearned run set up by a misplayed relay throw in the fifth staked Kansas City to a 3-0 lead. Ryan Jeffers' leadoff single in the seventh was the only blemish on Cameron's line before he handed a shutout over to the Royals' bullpen after eight dominant innings.

Minnesota's defense and relief corps kept the deficit from growing in the meantime. Alex Jackson made a diving stop down the first-base line, Luke Keaschall tracked down a drive in center, and Andrew Morris induced a clean double play in the eighth — every out a small bet that the offense would eventually show up.

It finally did in the ninth. Trevor Larnach struck out and Victor Caratini lined out to put the Twins one out from defeat before Jeffers was hit by a pitch, Royce Lewis singled and Josh Bell drew a bases-loading walk against reliever Lucas Erceg. Royals manager Matt Quatraro turned to left-hander Matt Strahm to face Clemens, who fell behind 0-2 before getting a slider left over the plate and driving it 407 feet into the Minnesota night.

The blast was the 34th known "ultimate grand slam" in major league history — a walk-off slam with the batting team trailing by three runs — and the first in Twins franchise history. It was also the first walk-off slam with a team down to its final strike since the Cubs' David Bote hit one against Washington in 2018.

Yoendrys Gomez worked a scoreless top of the ninth to pick up the win, while Strahm took the loss. The result also happened to land on Twins manager Derek Shelton's 56th birthday, and it pushed Minnesota back to .500 on the season after a series win over Kansas City.

Walk-Off Win

Herrera's Walk-Off Double Erases Crow-Armstrong's Late Heroics as Cardinals Stun Cubs

St. Louis Cardinals 3, Chicago Cubs 2 — Final/10

Ivan Herrera lined a two-run double into the left-field corner with one out in the 10th inning, capping a three-run St. Louis rally that turned a Pete Crow-Armstrong masterpiece into a gut-punch defeat for the Chicago Cubs, 3-2, at Busch Stadium on Wednesday night. Crow-Armstrong's 424-foot, two-run homer had broken a scoreless tie in the top of the 10th, but the Cardinals answered immediately, loading the bases and cashing in twice more to keep their fading season alive.

The Game

For nine innings, this game belonged to the starting pitchers. Cubs lefty Matthew Boyd, working his way back into form since returning from the injured list, retired eight straight batters after allowing a first-inning double to Herrera and wriggled out of a bases-loaded jam in the sixth to finish seven scoreless innings on four hits and five strikeouts. Cardinals right-hander Dustin May matched him blow for blow, holding Chicago to a single hit — Alex Bregman's ground-rule double in the fourth — over five scoreless innings of his own.

The tension carried into the ninth. With two outs, the Cubs put a runner on second before Cardinals reliever Riley O'Brien intentionally walked Pedro Ramirez to face Dansby Swanson, who flied out to escape the jam. St. Louis had its own chance in the bottom half when Blaze Jordan doubled with two outs against Caleb Thielbar, but Nathan Church popped out to send the game to extra innings.

Crow-Armstrong broke the deadlock leading off the Cubs' half of the 10th, turning around a pitch from Luis Gastelum and driving it 424 feet over the right-center-field wall for his team-high 24th home run of the season. It looked like it might be enough to push Chicago to a season-high 16 games over .500.

It wasn't. St. Louis answered right away against Trent Thornton. Lars Nootbaar led off with a pinch-hit single and a second runner reached to put men on the corners, then Thornton walked JJ Wetherholt to load the bases with nobody out. Jordan Walker's groundout to third scored one run to trim the deficit to 2-1, and Herrera — who had also doubled off Boyd back in the first inning — lined his second career game-ending hit into the left-field corner, scoring the tying and winning runs and sending Busch Stadium into a frenzy.

The win snapped a three-game Cardinals losing streak in which they had been outscored 17-5 over the first two games of this series, and offered a jolt of life to a St. Louis team that entered the night at 53-54 and had dropped eight of its last ten. For the Cubs (61-46), still chasing the division-leading Brewers and holding onto a wild-card spot, it was a deflating way to see a dominant pitching effort go to waste.

Extra-Inning Classic — Blown Six-Run Lead

D-backs Survive Pirates' Six-Run Rally, Win It in the 12th

Diamondbacks 8, Pirates 7 — Final/12

Arizona built a six-run cushion behind seven scoreless innings from Brandon Pfaadt, then watched it evaporate in a furious two-inning Pittsburgh surge that included back-to-back game-tying home runs in the ninth. James McCann's RBI single in the 12th finally decided it, capping a four-hour, back-and-forth slugfest at PNC Park.

The Game

Arizona wasted no time building a cushion. Geraldo Perdomo tripled home a run in the first, and the Diamondbacks tacked on runs in each of the next three innings against Pirates rookie Bubba Chandler — an RBI fielder's choice and single in the second, an Arenado RBI single in the third, and a Brandon Lowe fielding error that let another run score in the fourth. A bases-loaded double-play ball in the sixth made it 6-0, and with Pfaadt cruising, the Diamondbacks looked in total control.

Then Pittsburgh turned it into a ballgame. The Pirates finally broke through against reliever Ryan Thompson in the eighth, stringing together three straight RBI hits from Ryan O'Hearn, Endy Rodríguez and Marcell Ozuna to cut the deficit to 6-3. It was only a prelude. In the ninth, Jake Mangum led off with a double, and Brandon Lowe and Bryan Reynolds followed with back-to-back home runs off closer Paul Sewald, completing the six-run comeback and tying the game at 6-6.

The drama didn't stop there. Arizona regained the lead in the 10th on an infield error that scored a run, only for Pittsburgh to answer immediately in the bottom half on another error, this one at first base, tying it again at 7-7. Both bullpens then settled in for two scoreless innings before McCann, who finished with four hits, delivered the decisive blow with an RBI single off Wilber Dotel in the 12th.

The result matters beyond the box score. Pittsburgh, fighting for a National League Wild Card spot, lost ground in the standings with the defeat, while Arizona improved to 56-52 and has now won 11 of its last 17.

Walk-Off Win Ends Franchise-Record Skid

Conine's Walk-Off Single Snaps Marlins' 12-Game Slide

Miami Marlins 8, Philadelphia Phillies 7 — Final

Griffin Conine lined a bases-loaded single into left field with two outs in the ninth inning Monday night, capping a stunning rally that erased a two-run, ninth-inning deficit and lifted the Miami Marlins past the Philadelphia Phillies 8-7. The win snapped a franchise-record 12-game losing streak that had buried Miami since July 9 — and did it against Philadelphia's own closer.

The Game

Bryce Harper staked Philadelphia to an early lead with a 419-foot, two-run homer off Marlins starter Tyler Phillips in the first — his 22nd of the season. Miami answered before the inning was over, plating a run on a throwing error and getting a solo shot from Heriberto Hernández, then broke the game open with three more in the second to seize a 5-2 cushion against Phillies ace Zack Wheeler.

Wheeler didn't make it out of the third, departing after allowing six hits and five runs; manager Rob Thomson later said the right-hander may have been feeling nauseous, with no structural injury involved. Tyler Phillips settled in after his rocky start, working into the sixth for Miami.

Philadelphia clawed all the way back in the sixth. Harper walked and scored on an Alec Bohm single, then J.T. Realmuto's fly ball bounced over the right-field wall for a ground-rule double that pulled the Phillies within one. Justin Crawford's single drove in Bryson Stott, and Realmuto came around on a throwing error by Hernández to hand Philadelphia a 7-5 lead.

That lead lasted until the ninth. Phillies closer Jhoan Duran opened the inning by loading the bases with no outs, then surrendered a two-run single to Hernández that tied it at 7. Two batters later, Conine lined his first career walk-off hit into left, sending loanDepot Park into a delayed, disbelieving roar — Miami's fifth walk-off win of the season, and its first win of any kind in nearly three weeks.

The Phillies, who have lost seven of their last ten since the All-Star break, will send Aaron Nola to the mound Tuesday against Miami's Sandy Alcantara as they try to avoid being swept up in the Marlins' sudden turnaround.

Miami — Franchise-Record Losing Streak

Machado, Merrill Erupt in the Eighth, Send Marlins to Record 12th Straight Loss

San Diego Padres 5, Miami Marlins 3 — Final

Manny Machado and Jackson Merrill went back-to-back in a four-run eighth inning Sunday, rallying the Padres from a 3-1 deficit to a 5-3 win over Miami at loanDepot Park. The loss dropped the Marlins to a franchise-record 12 straight defeats, eclipsing three previous 11-game skids in club history.

The Game

Miami looked to be cruising toward snapping its slide early. Padres starter Walker Buehler walked Liam Hicks and Griffin Conine in the second, and both scored — Hicks on a stolen base and a throwing error by catcher Luis Campusano, Conine on Graham Pauley's RBI single — to make it 2-0. Kyle Stowers stretched the lead to 3-0 with a leadoff homer off Buehler in the third.

Marlins starter Janson Junk was dealing, holding San Diego to a single run into the fifth before a scary moment ended his day: Ty France's comebacker deflected off reliever Michael Petersen's forearm after Junk departed, and Petersen left the game with a forearm contusion. Junk's final line was one run and seven hits allowed over 4 2/3 innings.

Miami still led 3-1 entering the eighth when reliever Lake Bachar came on to protect it. It fell apart in a hurry. Jake Cronenworth led off with a single, and two batters later Machado turned on a 3-2 fastball and drove it over the center-field fence to tie the game at 3-3. One out later, Bachar left a sweeper over the plate and Merrill pulled it out to right-center for a 4-3 Padres lead. Luis Rengifo doubled and scored on a Luis Campusano single to make it 5-3, capping a four-run inning that turned a two-run Miami lead into a two-run deficit in a matter of minutes.

San Diego's bullpen shut the door from there. Wandy Peralta worked around a hit for a scoreless inning to earn the win, and Mason Miller finished it for his 27th save, completing a three-game sweep at loanDepot Park in front of a crowd of 21,675 that jeered as the eighth inning ended.

The result was about more than one game for Miami. The Marlins had lost 11 in a row three times previously, most recently in June 2011, but had never dropped 12 straight. They'll try to end the skid Monday at home against Philadelphia.
