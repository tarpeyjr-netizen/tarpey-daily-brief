Win-or-Go-Home, Game 162

Wheeler Pitches Like It's Game 7, Phillies Survive Collapse to Clinch Wild Card

Philadelphia Phillies 7, Tampa Bay Rays 3 — Final

The Phillies erased a week of near-disaster in one afternoon, scoring early and often behind Zack Wheeler's ace-caliber start to beat the Rays 7-3 and claim the final National League Wild Card berth on the last day of the season. The win eliminated the Arizona Diamondbacks, who had clawed back from 6½ games out only to fall one win short.

The Game

A week earlier, none of this drama seemed possible. Philadelphia held a 6½-game lead over Arizona for the third NL Wild Card spot on September 1, but a September collapse — capped by a 12-1 blowout loss to Tampa Bay a day earlier, in which the Phillies scored only three runs across a four-game losing streak — turned Sunday's regular-season finale into a true win-or-go-home game. Interim manager Don Mattingly called it a "Game 7."

Philadelphia wasted no time answering the moment. Bryson Stott's RBI double in the first inning got the Phillies on the board against Rays starter Nick Martinez, and the floodgates opened in the second: J.T. Realmuto snapped an 0-for-34 slump with an RBI double, and Kyle Schwarber followed with a two-run double — punctuated by a belly-flop slide into second — to make it 4-0 and set Citizens Bank Park's rain-soaked crowd roaring.

Zack Wheeler was every bit the stopper his team needed, working around Tampa Bay's lineup for six innings and allowing only a fifth-inning solo homer to Jonny DeLuca. Philadelphia answered right back in the bottom of the fifth, as Bryce Harper crushed a solo shot and Edmundo Sosa followed with a two-run drive to push the lead to 7-1.

DeLuca, who already owned Tampa Bay's lone run, added a second solo homer in the seventh off reliever Alex McFarlane for the first multi-homer game of his career (his 11th of the season), but the Rays — already locked into the postseason as AL East champions and with little left to play for — could get no closer. Jesus Luzardo, activated off the injured list after a bout of shoulder inflammation, and Jose Alvarado combined to hold the line through the eighth before Orion Kerkering worked the final 1⅓ innings for his second save, surrendering one run in the ninth before closing it out.

Minutes later, word arrived from San Diego that the Diamondbacks had lost 9-4 to the Padres, officially eliminating Arizona and locking in Philadelphia's fifth straight postseason berth.

Kansas City · Bailey and Rocchio Homer Twice Apiece as Cleveland Clinches Third Straight Division Crown

Guardians Rout Royals 11-5 to Clinch Third Straight AL Central Title

Cleveland Guardians 11, Kansas City Royals 5 — Final

The Cleveland Guardians clinched their third consecutive AL Central title on the next-to-last day of the regular season, routing the Kansas City Royals 11-5 at Kauffman Stadium as Patrick Bailey and Brayan Rocchio each homered twice. Combined with Chicago's loss to Colorado, the win locked up the division for Cleveland and, with it, the American League's No. 2 seed and a first-round bye into the Division Series.

The Game

KANSAS CITY — Kansas City jumped on Cleveland starter Tanner Bibee for three runs in the first, with Carter Jensen doubling to open the game, advancing to third on a Bobby Witt Jr. bloop single and scoring on a throwing error by catcher Patrick Bailey as Witt attempted to steal second. Jac Caglianone followed with a run-scoring double of his own and eventually scored on a Michael Massey single to make it 3-0 before Bibee had recorded more than a handful of outs.

Cleveland answered with a four-run second that turned the game. A fielding error by Witt at shortstop opened the inning, and Bailey followed by hooking a two-run homer around the right-field foul pole. Brayan Rocchio doubled, and Steven Kwan's slicing liner eluded a diving Isaac Collins in left field for an RBI triple that put the Guardians ahead 4-3. Chase DeLauter, back in the lineup after missing four games with a right wrist sprain, lined Michael Wacha's second pitch of the third inning into the right-field seats for his 16th home run of the season, stretching the lead to 5-3.

Cleveland kept adding on: Travis Bazzana tripled in the sixth and scored on a Nathaniel Lowe sacrifice fly, and Rocchio's solo homer in the seventh, his 14th of the year, made it 7-3. Bibee worked six innings before handing off to the bullpen, allowing three runs — one unearned on the Bailey throwing error — on seven hits while striking out six. Wacha (9-10) took the loss, unable to slow Cleveland's attack after DeLauter's blast.

The Guardians put the game away with a four-run ninth, as Bailey and Rocchio connected for back-to-back home runs — their second of the night apiece — before Nathaniel Lowe capped the inning with a two-run single. Nine of Cleveland's 14 hits went for extra bases. Kansas City, already well out of postseason contention and losers of 11 of its last 13, added two runs late to reach the final margin.

The win, paired with Chicago's 9-6 loss to Colorado, gave Cleveland (85-76) a two-game lead over the White Sox (83-78) with one game left in the regular season, clinching the division outright and assuring the Guardians of the AL's No. 2 seed and a first-round bye into a best-of-five Division Series with home-field advantage against the winner of the Wild Card round.

The Bronx · Alonso Seizes Outright AL Home Run Lead as Eliminated Orioles Rout Yankees

Alonso's 471-Foot Blast Caps Two-Homer Day, Powers Orioles Past Yankees

Baltimore Orioles 10, New York Yankees 2 — Final (Game 1 of doubleheader)

Pete Alonso homered twice, including a 471-foot drive deep into the left-field bleachers, to take sole possession of the American League home run lead with 43, and the Baltimore Orioles routed the New York Yankees 10-2 in Friday's doubleheader opener at Yankee Stadium. Dylan Beavers added his first career grand slam in a five-run fourth inning and Jeremiah Jackson turned a ball off the center-field wall into an inside-the-park homer, giving Baltimore four home runs on the day against rookie right-hander Brendan Beck and New York's bullpen. The outburst came days after the Orioles had already been eliminated from postseason contention, turning the opener into a showcase for a lineup still swinging with real venom in the season's final week.

The Game

THE BRONX — Beck, up from Triple-A and making just his second big-league start, opened by striking out the side, but the good feeling did not last. In the third, Alonso jumped on Beck's first-pitch slider and drove it out for a two-run homer, part of a stretch in which he became the first Oriole since Mark Trumbo in 2016 to homer in seven straight games he'd gone deep. Trevor Rogers, working opposite Beck in a start that may have been his last with the club, held the Yankees to a single run over four innings, scattering five hits without issuing a walk.

Baltimore blew the game open in the fourth. With the bases loaded, Beavers turned on a 1-1 curveball and drove it into the right-field seats for his first career grand slam, the headline blow in a five-run inning that chased any thought of a competitive game. Beck was tagged for seven runs on six hits over five innings and fell to 0-2, with New York's bullpen inheriting a laugher.

The Orioles kept piling on. Jackson lifted a fly ball that eluded center fielder Spencer Jones at the warning track in front of the bullpen; the ball caromed away and Jackson raced around to beat right fielder Amed Rosario's throw home for an inside-the-park homer in the sixth, his 10th of the season and Baltimore's second of that rare variety in ten days. An inning later, Alonso sent a first-pitch changeup at least halfway up the left-field bleachers — the fourth-longest homer in the majors this year — to make it 9-1 and put him alone atop the American League home run race. An eighth-inning error by Jose Caballero on a Gunnar Henderson grounder let Christian Franklin score for a 10-1 cushion before New York scratched a run back on a Jones sacrifice fly in the ninth.

Albert Suarez followed Rogers with four scoreless innings out of the bullpen to lock down the win and improve to 3-0, while reliever Paul Blackburn absorbed the Orioles' late damage for New York. With Baltimore's season having no October implications left and the Yankees already locked into a wild-card berth, the opener carried little on the standings itself — New York would answer with a 6-3 win in the nightcap to salvage a doubleheader split — but Alonso's homer race and the rare-feat parade in the Bronx gave the day its story.

For a team already playing out the string, it was the kind of afternoon that stands out on its own merits: a milestone home run chase, a first career grand slam, and an inside-the-park homer, all in the same nine innings.

Kansas City · White Sox Clinch First Playoff Berth Since 2021 After Three Straight 100-Loss Seasons

Sandlin, White Sox Rout Royals to Punch Historic Playoff Ticket

Chicago White Sox 9, Kansas City Royals 1 — Final

David Sandlin allowed just one run over six innings and the Chicago White Sox jumped on Randy Dobnak for an eight-run first inning, routing the Kansas City Royals 9-1 on Thursday at Kauffman Stadium to clinch their first playoff berth since 2021. No team in major league history had ever reached the postseason immediately after three consecutive 100-loss seasons — until these White Sox, fresh off three straight 100-loss campaigns, pulled it off.

The Game

KANSAS CITY — The clincher was over almost before it started. Royals starter Randy Dobnak never made it out of the first inning, getting rocked for six hits and six runs (four earned) while recording just four outs; a throwing error on a pickoff attempt and a wild pitch compounded the damage as Chicago sent a parade of hitters to the plate and built an 8-0 lead before Kansas City had come to bat in the bottom of the frame.

With a laugher in hand, the White Sox handed the ball to rookie right-hander David Sandlin for the biggest start of his young career, and he did not blink. Sandlin worked around a fifth-inning run, scattering five hits over six innings without issuing a walk and striking out a career-high seven — a poised, efficient outing that turned what could have been a nervy afternoon into a formality.

Chicago tacked on more even as the outcome was no longer in doubt. Kyle Teel drove a fourth-inning pitch out to center field for his fifth home run of the season, and Miguel Vargas, the reigning American League Player of the Week, delivered two hits and drove in a pair of runs to extend the lead. Leadoff man Sam Antonacci reached base three times, drew a walk and came around to score three of Chicago's nine runs.

The result pushed the White Sox to 82-77 and, combined with results elsewhere, locked up no worse than an American League wild-card spot — with the division still mathematically alive, Chicago sits just a half-game behind the first-place Cleveland Guardians heading into the season's final days. Kansas City, meanwhile, fell to 68-90 and dropped its seventh straight game, a lost season now defined largely by playing spoiler.

What made the moment resonate was the road that led to it. The White Sox lost 101, 121 and 100 games over the previous three seasons — the 121-loss 2024 club set the modern major league record for futility — and Thursday's rout, punctuated by a clubhouse celebration at Kauffman Stadium, capped a rebuild that few outside the organization believed would turn around this quickly.

Seattle · Naylor's Walk-Off Snaps Hader's Save Streak, Muddies AL West Race

Naylor's 10th-Inning Single Sinks Astros, Keeps Mariners' Faint Hopes Alive

Seattle Mariners 6, Houston Astros 5 — Final/10

Josh Naylor lined a first-pitch single up the middle with the bases loaded in the bottom of the 10th, scoring Randy Arozarena and capping a wild 6-5 comeback win over the Houston Astros on Wednesday night at T-Mobile Park. The walk-off snapped Astros closer Josh Hader's streak of 26 consecutive converted save chances to open the season — an Astros record — and left the American League West unresolved with the regular season's final week at hand.

The Game

SEATTLE — Houston struck fast against George Kirby. With one out in the third, Allen doubled to left and Jose Altuve added a single down the left-field line that deflected in off third-base umpire Ryan Blakney's shin. Yordan Alvarez doubled to right to send Altuve to third, Isaac Paredes lifted a sacrifice fly to bring him home, and Yainer Díaz capped the four-run inning by lining his 14th home run of the season over the left-field wall. The Astros led 4-0 before Seattle had come to bat in the frame.

The Mariners answered immediately against Astros starter Ethan Pecko. Lazaro Montes drew a leadoff walk, J.P. Crawford singled to right, and both runners advanced on a wild pitch before Randy Arozarena walked to load the bases. Dominic Canzone lined a two-run single off the right-field wall to make it 4-2, and two batters later Julio Rodríguez doubled to left to plate Arozarena and trim Houston's lead to 4-3.

Seattle tied it in the fifth against rookie right-hander Josh Hendrickson, called up from Triple-A Sugar Land that same day and making his major league debut. Arozarena lined a one-out single to center, stole second and scored on another Canzone single to center, squaring the game at 4-4 — Canzone's third RBI of the night, extending his hitting streak to a career-best 12 games. Neither bullpen budged again through the ninth, sending the game to extra innings with the division still up for grabs.

Houston broke through first. With automatic runner Yordan Alvarez standing on second to open the 10th, Paredes tripled him home to make it 5-4 and brought Hader on to nail it down. Hader had been perfect in save chances all season, but he unraveled in the bottom half: Julio Rodríguez worked a full-count, bases-loaded walk to force in Crawford and tie it at 5-5, and Naylor lined Hader's first offering up the middle to send home automatic runner Arozarena with the winning run.

The result kept Seattle's postseason hopes on life support — the Mariners, now 74-84, sit four games back of the final American League wild-card spot with four to play, an outside chance that grew only marginally less remote. For Houston, the loss left the Astros (78-80) still tied atop the AL West with Texas, which has lost three straight games; Houston holds the season-series tiebreaker for now, but the division remains a coin flip heading into the last week of the regular season.

Bronx · Rasmussen's Near No-Hitter Clinches Rays' Fifth AL East Crown

Rasmussen Flirts With History as Rays Clinch AL East in Yankee Stadium Nightcap

Tampa Bay Rays 6, New York Yankees 1 — Final (Game 2 of doubleheader)

Drew Rasmussen carried a no-hit bid into the seventh inning and struck out 11, and Yandy Díaz drove in four runs with a pair of two-run singles, as the Tampa Bay Rays beat the New York Yankees 6-1 in the nightcap of a Tuesday doubleheader to clinch the American League East — their fifth division title and first since 2021. The victory also locked up home-field advantage throughout the AL playoffs for Tampa Bay, capping a day that began with the Yankees denying the Rays their first clinching chance in the doubleheader opener.

The Game

THE BRONX — The day-night doubleheader had two clinching scenarios in it for Tampa Bay, and the Yankees erased the first one emphatically. Carlos Rodón needed just three hits and 8 shutout innings to beat the Rays 2-0 in the opener, striking out eight without a walk while picking up his 100th career win and, along the way, his 1,500th career strikeout. Cody Bellinger's two-run homer in the fourth off Nick Martinez (15-5) was all the offense New York needed, and David Bednar closed it out for his 35th save, pushing the Rays' magic number back to two and setting up a winner-take-the-division nightcap.

Rasmussen made sure there would be no repeat. The right-hander was perfect through the storybook portions of his outing, and by the time he took the mound for the seventh he was six no-hit innings deep and nine outs from becoming the second pitcher in Rays history to throw one, joining Matt Garza. He got that far before Heliot Ramos lined a double to left on Rasmussen's 97th pitch to break it up. Rasmussen answered by retiring the next two batters before a full-count walk to Jazz Chisholm Jr. ended his night at a career-high 114 pitches — a final line of 6 2/3 innings, one hit, one walk and 11 strikeouts. Reliever Kevin Kelly inherited the runners and allowed George Lombard Jr.'s RBI single before getting Austin Wells to ground out, the Yankees' only run of the game.

Tampa Bay had already done its damage well before that. In the third, Taylor Walls dropped a safety-squeeze bunt that the Yankees botched by leaving first base uncovered, and Chandler Simpson walked home standing up for a 1-0 lead; two batters later, Ryan Vilade's groundout to second plated Walls himself to make it 2-0. Max Fried, working opposite Rasmussen, did not allow a ball out of the infield on either run but could not escape the small-ball squeeze, and he lasted 6 2/3 innings before Díaz delivered the blow that put the game away — a two-run single off reliever John Schreiber in the seventh that made it 4-0. Díaz added another two-run single in the ninth, his 175th hit of the season, tops in the American League, to finish 6-1.

The clinch is the earliest in Tampa Bay franchise history and comes with the Rays (96-61) having already wrapped up the AL's No. 1 playoff seed and home-field advantage throughout October. It is the franchise's fifth AL East title and first since the 100-win 2021 club, a validation of a roster built once again on pitching depth and situational hitting rather than star power. For the Yankees, the split doubleheader was mostly painless: at 90-66, New York is locked into a playoff spot and sits six games clear of Boston for the AL's top wild-card position, with the loss costing them only a chance to play spoiler against a division rival they will very likely see again in October.

Tampa Bay's clubhouse celebration spilled onto the Yankee Stadium infield once Wells grounded out to end it, champagne and cheap beer replacing the quiet, businesslike approach that has carried the Rays all season. It was a fitting image for a franchise that has now reached the postseason with a mix of unheralded arms and a lineup that manufactures runs as readily as it hits them out — and, on this night, very nearly did both at once.

Baltimore · Alonso's Milestone 40th Erases Three-Run Hole, Deepens Blue Jays' Wild-Card Trouble

Alonso's Two Homers, Including Milestone 40th, Rally Orioles Past Blue Jays

Baltimore Orioles 4, Toronto Blue Jays 3 — Final

Pete Alonso erased a three-run deficit almost single-handedly, homering in the first inning and then again with two outs in the eighth to reach 40 home runs for the fourth time in his career, lifting the Baltimore Orioles to a 4-3 comeback win over the Toronto Blue Jays on Monday night at Camden Yards. The loss, in the opener of a three-game series, dropped Toronto further off the pace in the American League wild-card race with less than two weeks left to play.

The Game

BALTIMORE — After a 47-minute rain delay pushed back the first pitch, Toronto wasted no time making its case. Orioles starter Shane Baz was called for a pitch-clock violation working a full count to Brett Bateman, then walked him anyway, and followed with another walk to Alejandro Kirk on a disputed checked-swing call. Nathan Lukes and George Springer each singled home a run, and a Sean Keys sacrifice fly capped a three-run first that put the Blue Jays up 3-0 before Baltimore had come to bat.

Alonso answered immediately. Facing Trey Yesavage — back on the mound for Toronto for the first time since knee inflammation sent him to the injured list in early August — Alonso drove a first-inning pitch out for a solo home run, his 39th of the season, to get the Orioles on the board. Yesavage's return was cut short at two innings, and the game settled into a quiet stretch as Baz worked around traffic to keep Toronto's lead at a single run.

Gunnar Henderson trimmed it further in the sixth, golfing a solo homer that was part of a 3-for-4 night that also included a single and a triple — missing the cycle by a double. Baz departed after 6 2/3 innings having allowed three runs on five hits with three walks and two strikeouts, never factoring into the decision, and both bullpens kept the score at 3-2 into the eighth.

That is when Toronto's night unraveled. Reliever Mason Fluharty walked the leadoff batter in the eighth, then recovered to get two outs and stood one away from stranding the inning. Instead, manager John Schneider turned to submarine right-hander Tyler Rogers specifically to face Alonso — and Alonso made him pay, driving a two-run homer, his 40th of the year, that turned a 3-2 deficit into a 4-3 Baltimore lead. It was only the second time in two seasons a right-handed hitter had gone deep against Rogers. Yennier Cano worked 1 1/3 shutout innings around the go-ahead blast to pick up the win, and Rico Garcia closed it out with a perfect ninth for his 10th save.

The result snapped a three-game Baltimore losing streak but did little to ease the Orioles' own postseason math, with the club still clinging to the faint edge of the AL wild-card picture. For Toronto, the defeat was more costly: the Blue Jays fell 3 1/2 games behind the idle Chicago White Sox for the final AL wild-card spot, their margin for error nearly gone with the regular season winding down.

Houston · Braves Complete Sweep, Clinch NL East Title

Yastrzemski's Three-Run Blast Caps Sweep, Crowns Braves NL East Champs

Atlanta Braves 4, Houston Astros 2 — Final

Mike Yastrzemski turned a three-game series into a coronation, driving a three-run homer in a four-run sixth inning that carried the Atlanta Braves to a 4-2 win over the Houston Astros on Sunday and clinched the National League East for the seventh time in nine years. The victory completed a three-game sweep at Daikin Park and delivered Atlanta's 24th division title in franchise history, tying the Los Angeles Dodgers for the most in major league history.

The Game

HOUSTON — Astros starter Hunter Brown was nearly untouchable for the first five innings, scattering three singles and two walks while striking out 10, and Jose Altuve made the early lead stand up with a solo home run in the fourth that put Houston ahead 1-0. Braves starter Martín Pérez matched Brown for stretches but worked into traffic repeatedly, departing after 4 1/3 innings having allowed the one run on six hits and two walks, with reliever Victor Mederos escaping a jam in the fifth to keep the deficit at a single run.

The turning point arrived in the top of the sixth. Bryan King relieved Brown to start the inning and immediately unraveled: Ozzie Albies lined an RBI single to tie it at 1-1, and two batters later Yastrzemski turned on a King fastball and drove it out for a three-run homer, his 12th of the season, that put Atlanta ahead 4-1 in a four-run frame. AJ Smith-Shawver, who had entered the game for the Braves that same half-inning, followed with a perfect bottom of the sixth, touching 100 mph and striking out two to lock in the lead and eventually pick up the win.

Houston would not go away entirely. Yainer Díaz golfed an opposite-field solo home run off reliever Ray Kerr in the eighth, his 13th of the year, trimming the margin to 4-2, but it was as close as the Astros would get. Dylan Dodd worked a spotless ninth, striking out the side for his second save, and the Braves poured out of their dugout at Daikin Park for a champagne celebration on their opponent's home field.

The clinch capped a three-game sweep that also carried heavy stakes on the preceding two nights — Atlanta locked up a playoff berth on Friday and Ronald Acuña Jr.'s go-ahead grand slam on Saturday knocked Houston out of first place in the AL West. Sunday's finale put a bow on it: the Braves' seventh NL East crown in nine seasons, their 24th division title overall (tying the Dodgers' all-time record) and a franchise-record 19th NL East championship, improving Atlanta to 92-64 with the pennant race now settled.

For Houston, the sweep was a gut punch to a fading postseason bid. The Astros have lost 10 of their last 14, were pushed behind the Texas Rangers in the AL West, and now trail the Chicago White Sox for the American League's final wild-card spot with the season's final week arriving.
