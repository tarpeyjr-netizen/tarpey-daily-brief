Walk-Off Win

Shut Out for Eight, Red Sox Erupt for Three in the Ninth — and Celebrate Twice

Boston Red Sox 3, San Francisco Giants 2 — Final

For eight innings at Fenway Park on Saturday night, the Red Sox had nothing: no runs, six hits, and a lineup that looked overmatched. Then, in the bottom of the ninth, they sent nine men to the plate against Sam Hentges, scored three times, and won it 3–2 when Eli White slid headfirst across the plate on a Wilyer Abreu grounder. Boston celebrated, waited out a replay review, and celebrated again.

The Game

San Francisco had the whole night mapped out early. Willy Adames turned on a Patrick Sandoval pitch with two outs in the first and dropped it over the Green Monster for his 21st homer. Two innings later, catcher Andrew Knizner — hitting .200 coming in — drove a solo shot of his own to make it 2–0. The Giants loaded the bases behind him and Sandoval wriggled free, which turned out to matter more than anyone knew at the time.

Blade Tidwell, coming off a start against Colorado in which he walked a career-high five, was the story for most of the evening. The right-hander gave up three hits over 5 2/3 scoreless innings and struck out a career-high seven, fanning Ceddanne Rafaela and Abreu in the first and punching out Abreu again to escape a first-and-second jam in the third. Boston hitters were consistently late all night.

The Red Sox had their chance in the eighth and blew it: Mickey Gasper doubled, Nick Sogard walked, and Rafaela singled to load the bases with nobody out. Dylan Smith struck out Abreu, then got Willson Contreras and a fly ball from Adley Rutschman to end it. Fenway went quiet.

The ninth started with a walk to Caleb Durbin and a hit batsman, Jahmai Jones. Andruw Monasterio moved Durbin to third on a fielder's choice. Pinch-hitter Eli White punched a single up the middle to break the shutout, and Sogard — who had just made a diving catch on Bryce Eldridge in the top half — ripped a double down the left-field line to tie it. San Francisco intentionally walked Rafaela to load the bases for Abreu, who was 0 for 4 with four strikeouts. He hit a hard grounder to the right side; Eldridge made a diving stop and threw home, but Knizner's foot came off the plate by inches as White dove in. Umpire James Hoye called him safe, the Giants challenged, and the call stood.

It was Boston's fourth walk-off win and 31st comeback victory of a remarkable summer — the Red Sox are 33–11 in their last 44 games and sit 70–59, six games behind Tampa Bay in the AL East. The Giants, at 52–77, have now lost 10 of 13.

Six-Run Ninth · Wild Finish

One Ball Off the Glove, and the Cardinals Are Off to the Races

St. Louis Cardinals 10, Cincinnati Reds 9 — Final

CINCINNATI — Two outs, nobody on the scoreboard for St. Louis since the third inning, and a routine fly ball drifting toward center field. Dane Myers dropped it, and the Cardinals kept running — six runs in the top of the ninth, then just enough left in the tank to survive a four-run Reds answer for a 10-9 win at Great American Ball Park on Thursday.

The Game

For six innings this looked like Jordan Walker's afternoon. He drove in a run with a first-inning groundout and then turned on a Brady Singer pitch in the third for a three-run homer, his 26th of the season and his second in as many games, staking St. Louis to a 4-0 lead. Michael McGreevy made it hold up, working through five clean innings on 90 pitches.

Then came the sixth. McGreevy gave up an RBI single and left with the bases loaded. Ryne Stanek came on and surrendered a run-scoring single to JJ Bleday before Matt McLain, hitting .200 on the morning, cleared the bases with the second grand slam of his career. Five runs, a 5-4 Cincinnati lead, and a ballpark that had flipped entirely.

The Reds' bullpen held the line into the ninth. Julian Garcia and Tejay Antone each worked a scoreless inning before Brock Burke took the ball for the save. He got two outs. He never got the third. Myers' error on a fly off the bat of JJ Wetherholt let Masyn Winn and Jose Fermin score for a 6-5 lead, and the inning unspooled from there: an Alec Burleson RBI single, a bases-loaded walk to Joshua Baez, and then Fermin — batting for the second time in the frame — lining a two-run single to make it 10-5. Six runs, none of them earned.

Cincinnati refused to go quietly. Eugenio Suarez, pinch-hitting, singled home Matt McLain for the 1,000th RBI of his career. Sal Stewart followed with a two-run single to cut it to 10-8 and push his own season total to 96, reclaiming the major league lead from Walker, who had briefly tied him at 94. With the bases loaded and two outs, Riley O'Brien walked Tyler Stephenson to force in Elly De La Cruz and make it a one-run game before McLain lined out to end it.

The win gave St. Louis the series 3-2 and moved the Cardinals (66-63) to within 2 1/2 games of the last wild card. The Reds (61-67) fell seven back.

Two-Hit Shutout · Crosstown Finale

Urquidy Silences Wrigley as White Sox Two-Hit the Cubs to Split the City

White Sox 3, Cubs 0 — Final

Two nights of Wrigley Field bedlam ended in silence. José Urquidy, a July trade pickup working as a bulk reliever behind an opener, retired 17 of the 19 Cubs he faced and struck out eight, leading a three-pitcher, two-hit shutout that gave the White Sox a 3-0 win Wednesday and salvaged the Crosstown Classic finale. It was the first time the South Siders blanked the Cubs at Wrigley since August 2021.

The Game

The Cubs had walked off the White Sox in each of the first two games of the series — Pete Crow-Armstrong's 10th-inning homer Monday, Alex Bregman's two-out single Tuesday, the 13th walk-off of a season that has turned the North Side into the sport's most reliable late-inning theater. Wednesday afternoon, with the wind blowing in off Lake Michigan, there was nothing to walk off.

Sean Newcomb opened with two hitless innings, ending a series-long habit of surrendering leadoff home runs to Crow-Armstrong. Urquidy took over in the third and made the game look easy: eight of his first nine outs came by strikeout, his changeup and curveball drawing swing after empty swing from baseball's second-highest-scoring offense.

Chicago's only crack of daylight came and went in the sixth. Tyrone Taylor led off with a double, then tagged late on Crow-Armstrong's line drive to right and was thrown out at third by a mile, Braden Montgomery's 95.1 mph throw registering as the fastest tracked assist by a White Sox outfielder since 2023. "It's a mental mistake," Taylor said afterward. "Kids watching this: No outs, when you're on second base, your job is to get to third on a fly ball and tag up. Don't do what I did."

The Sox had already scored by then, stringing back-to-back hits off Clay Holmes in the top of the sixth, with Andrew Benintendi's single bringing home Miguel Vargas for the game's only necessary run. Montgomery added the cushion in the eighth, lacing a two-run double that glanced off first baseman Michael Busch's glove. Sam Antonacci preserved the shutout an inning earlier, leaping into the ivy in left to pull back a Bregman drive that on a calmer day leaves the yard.

Grant Taylor worked a clean ninth for his seventh save, and the season series between the Chicago clubs finished tied 3-3 — the Sox having taken two of three at Rate Field in May, the Cubs two of three at Wrigley in August. The win was the White Sox's seventh in 11 games and sent them home holding a four-game lead in the American League Central.

Record-Tying Rout

Brewers Bury Mariners 22-0, Matching Baseball’s Most Lopsided Shutout Since 1901

Milwaukee Brewers 22, Seattle Mariners 0 — Final

MILWAUKEE — It began with Jake Bauers crawling home on a dropped tag and ended with a catcher on the mound throwing 70-mph fastballs. In between, the Milwaukee Brewers scored 21 runs over four innings to bury the Seattle Mariners 22-0 at American Family Field, matching the largest shutout margin in the major leagues since the American League was founded in 1901.

The Game

For four and a half innings this was a taut, ordinary Tuesday. Milwaukee’s only run came in the first, when Bauers doubled into the right-field corner and scored on a William Contreras bloop single — barely, and only because Cal Raleigh’s tag jarred the ball loose and Bauers scrambled back to the plate ahead of him. Kyle Harrison and Seattle’s Bryce Miller then traded zeros, Harrison retiring nine straight at one point and Miller nine of his own.

The dam broke in the fifth. David Hamilton beat out an infield single, stole second, then stole third alongside Brice Turang on a double steal that drew no throw at all. Jackson Chourio singled Hamilton home, Contreras’s sacrifice line drive brought in Turang, and with two outs Seattle manager Dan Wilson left Miller in to face Christian Yelich rather than go to either lefty warming in the bullpen. Yelich drove a three-run homer to right-center — his first since July 24 — and it was 6-0.

The sixth was the turning point that removed all doubt. Garrett Mitchell singled at 109.6 mph, Joey Ortiz walked, and Hamilton, a left-handed hitter facing lefty Josh Simpson, cleared the fence for a three-run shot and a 9-0 lead. Three batters later Bauers ambushed a hanging sweeper for his 22nd home run. Eleven to nothing, and the rout was on.

Seattle’s night curdled from there. Two more Milwaukee runs crossed in the seventh, one of them on a routine double-play throw that Josh Naylor simply dropped. By the eighth the Mariners had surrendered, sending utility infielder Leo Rivas to the mound at 37 mph. Rookie Luis Lara hit the first home run of his career off him, a three-run drive; nine runs scored in all, capped by the strangest sequence of the night — Yelich’s fly ball to center that Julio Rodríguez caught and then fired into the stands, believing it was the third out. It was the second. Two runners were awarded home, turning a sacrifice fly into a three-run play.

Catcher Gary Sánchez pitched a scoreless ninth to finish the five-hitter. The win pushed the majors-best Brewers to 78-48, 30 games over .500, and matched the franchise record for runs in a game — their largest output since a 22-2 win over Toronto in August 1992. Only Pittsburgh in 1975 and Cleveland in 2004 had previously won 22-0 in the modern era. For Seattle, it was the worst defeat in the 50-year history of the franchise.

Walk-Off Homer · 30-30 Milestone

Crow-Armstrong Bookends Crosstown Classic With Leadoff and Walk-Off Homers

Cubs 7, White Sox 5 — Final in 10 innings

CHICAGO — Pete Crow-Armstrong opened Monday night at Wrigley Field by driving Luis Castillo's first pitch of the game down the right-field line and out, and he closed it four hours later by launching a 2-1 slider into the right-field patio for the first walk-off home run of his career. In between came three lead changes, a blown save and a bases-loaded escape, and when the Cubs' 7-5, 10-inning win over the White Sox was finally over, Crow-Armstrong had become the first player in franchise history to post back-to-back 30-homer, 30-steal seasons.

The Game

The Cubs needed one pitch to take the lead. Crow-Armstrong's fifth leadoff homer of the season landed just inside the right-field foul pole, and four pitches later Seiya Suzuki drove one to deep left-center for back-to-back shots and a 2-0 Cubs lead before the first out of the night.

Shota Imanaga overpowered the South Siders otherwise — 10 strikeouts, one off his season high, and only two hits allowed — but the one mistake was expensive. In the fourth, Randal Grichuk turned on a pitch and sent a three-run homer to center field, scoring Miguel Vargas and Munetaka Murakami and flipping the game to 3-2 White Sox. It was the 29th home run Imanaga has surrendered this season, one shy of the major league lead.

The Cubs answered in the fifth, and not prettily. Nico Hoerner tapped a grounder that scored one run, and when Vargas' throw skipped away, Ian Happ raced all the way home from first for a second. Chicago led 4-3 until the eighth, when pinch-hitter Andrew Benintendi jumped on Ryan Zeferjahn's first offering and deposited it in the right-field basket for a two-run homer and a 5-4 White Sox lead.

Jordan Hicks could not finish it. Hoerner's RBI single in the bottom of the ninth tied the game at 5, and the Cubs loaded the bases with one out before stranding them. The White Sox then loaded the bases themselves in the 10th against Jacob Webb, who pitched his way out without a run — his 22nd consecutive scoreless appearance — and handed the bottom of the inning to the top of the Cubs' order.

With the automatic runner Carson Kelly on second and first base open, White Sox manager Will Venable chose to pitch to Crow-Armstrong rather than walk him with Suzuki on deck. Hagen Smith's 2-1 slider never had a chance. The win snapped a two-game Cubs skid, ended a three-game White Sox winning streak, and pushed the Cubs to 73-53 against a 65-59 White Sox club still leading the AL Central chase — two Chicago contenders, for once, meeting in August with something real at stake.

One-Hitter — Anaheim, Calif.

Cameron Misses History by a Bloop, Blanks Angels on One Hit

Royals 3, Angels 0 — Final

Noah Cameron carried a perfect game into the sixth inning and a no-hitter into the seventh before Vaughn Grissom's soft single dropped in behind shortstop, and the Kansas City left-hander settled for the next-best thing: a complete-game, one-hit shutout in a 3-0 win over the Angels on Sunday at Angel Stadium. Cameron struck out eight, walked one and threw 115 pitches in the first complete game of his career. Michael Massey and Bobby Witt Jr. supplied the only offense he needed with back-to-back-inning home runs.

The Game

For five innings there was nothing to write down. Cameron retired the first 15 Angels he faced, mixing his changeup off a fastball the home lineup never squared up, and Kansas City's defense was never asked to do anything difficult. The first blemish came with none out in the sixth, when Oswald Peraza drew a leadoff walk — the only free pass Cameron would issue all afternoon.

The perfect game gone, Cameron simply pitched around it. Peraza moved up on Travis d'Arnaud's groundout, Josh Lowe struck out swinging and Zach Neto bounced weakly to second to end the threat. He opened the seventh by striking out Mike Trout, then lost the no-hitter one batter later when Grissom lifted a ball high over Witt's head and into shallow left-center for the Angels' only hit of the day.

Kansas City had already done its scoring by then, and it did not take much. Massey turned on a pitch in the fourth and drove it 384 feet into the front row of the right-field bleachers for his 11th home run and a 1-0 lead. Witt followed an inning later, lining his 14th down the left-field line to double the margin.

The Royals added an unearned insurance run in the seventh, with Carter Jensen's RBI single off reliever Blake Weiman finishing the scoring. Angels starter Ryan Johnson had actually pitched well enough to keep it close — 5 1/3 innings, five hits, two earned runs — but on this afternoon two runs was a mountain.

Cameron came back out for the ninth and finished it in a tidy 2 hours, 16 minutes before 28,036, closing out a series win for a Kansas City club that has gotten little national attention this summer. It was the kind of afternoon that reframes a pitcher's season: Cameron's record climbed to 7-8, a line that says almost nothing about how he threw the baseball on Sunday.

First in MLB History

Three Swings, Three Homers: Báez Rewrites the Record Book in His First Big-League Game

St. Louis Cardinals 8, Chicago Cubs 4 — Final

CHICAGO — Joshua Báez had never seen a major league pitch before Saturday afternoon. By the sixth inning he had hit three of them over the wall at Wrigley Field, becoming the first player in the history of the sport to homer in each of his first three big-league at-bats and carrying the Cardinals to an 8-4 win over the Cubs.

The Game

The 23-year-old outfielder, promoted from Triple-A Memphis less than 24 hours earlier, did not wait to get comfortable. Matthew Boyd had already walked one batter and plunked another in the first inning, and after Alec Burleson's groundout made it 1-0, Boyd challenged Báez with a 93.4 mph fastball up in the zone. Báez turned it around at 108.6 mph and sent it a Statcast-projected 449 feet to dead center — a two-run shot on the first pitch of his career, and a 3-0 St. Louis lead.

Chicago answered quickly. An error by Cardinals third baseman Blaze Jordan opened the bottom of the second, and Michael Conforto punished the extra out with a three-run homer off Michael McGreevy that tied it at 3. It was the only real crack in McGreevy's afternoon; none of the three runs he allowed were earned.

Báez untied it in the fourth, working Boyd to a full count and driving a changeup 382 feet down the left-field line for a solo homer and a 4-3 lead. That made him just the ninth player ever to homer twice in a regular-season debut, and the second since 1900 to do it in his first two at-bats, joining Bob Nieman in 1951.

The turning point came two innings later, when the Cubs sent Boyd back out for the sixth with Báez due up and a one-run deficit to protect. Báez lined a 99.8 mph shot 368 feet the other way for his third homer, and two batters later Jordan added a solo blast that ended Boyd's day at 7-3. Burleson tacked on a solo homer off Colin Rea in the eighth — the fifth Cubs home run allowed on the day — and Chicago scratched back a lone run on a double play in the bottom half.

The loss snapped a stretch in which the Cubs had won nine of 11, and it laid bare their season-long problem: a pitching staff that has now surrendered 187 home runs, more than any club in baseball but one. For St. Louis, it evened the series and delivered the kind of debut a franchise builds around. Báez, the Cardinals' second-round pick in 2021 and their No. 3 prospect, arrived leading all of Triple-A with 34 home runs. Roughly 30 family members had flown in from Boston, Orlando, Miami and the Dominican Republic to watch him play his first game.
