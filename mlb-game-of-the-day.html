St. Petersburg · World Series Walk-Off in Extra Innings

Rays Rally in the 12th to Stave Off Sweep, Beat Brewers 4-3

Tampa Bay 4, Milwaukee 3 — Final/12 innings (Brewers lead World Series 3-1)

Ben Williamson's single scored the winning run in the bottom of the 12th as the Rays erased a one-run deficit against Milwaukee's Shane Drohan and kept their season alive in World Series Game 4. Milwaukee had taken the lead on Cooper Pratt's solo homer in the top of the inning.

The Game

Tampa Bay struck first in the second, when Chandler Simpson doubled home Jonny DeLuca. Milwaukee answered in the fourth on Pratt's RBI double, but the Rays regained the lead in the bottom half when Junior Caminero scored on a fielder's choice that included a throwing error by Brewers third baseman David Hamilton. Hamilton made up for it in the fifth with a homer to right-center that tied it at 2-2.

Then came the bullpens. Milwaukee's relievers and Tampa Bay's combined to throw scoreless baseball from the sixth through the eleventh, as Aaron Ashby worked 2 1/3 hitless innings for Milwaukee and Tyler Wells and Cam Booser combined for 2 hitless innings for the Rays.

In the 12th, Pratt drove a Ian Seymour pitch to left-center, putting Milwaukee one win from a title with a 3-2 lead. Seymour got out of the inning after a Jackson Chourio double.

The Brewers could not hold it. Caminero reached on a throwing error by Joey Ortiz, Liam Hicks singled him to third, and DeLuca's single tied the game at 3-3. Simpson singled to put the winning run in scoring position, and Williamson lined a single to right off Drohan to bring home Nick Fortes. Drohan was charged with the blown save and the loss without recording an out, facing four batters who all reached.

The win trims Milwaukee's lead to 3-1, with the Brewers still needing one victory to close out the Series.

Game played Monday, October 26, 2026
St. Petersburg — Record Two-Out Rally and a Near-Perfect Start Put Milwaukee One Win From a Title

Brewers Bury Rays Under Record Eight-Run Fifth, Move One Win From First Title

Brewers 10, Rays 1 — Final (Milwaukee leads World Series 3-0)

Milwaukee sent seven straight batters to a hit with two outs in the fifth inning, scoring eight runs in a postseason-record rally, and rookie Robert Gasser held Tampa Bay to one hit over six innings in World Series Game 3 at Tropicana Field. The Brewers now lead the series 3-0 and can win their first championship in Game 4 on Tuesday night.

The Game

For two innings, nothing happened. The game was scoreless until Milwaukee struck first with two runs in the third, and Gasser, the rookie left-hander, was already in command. His only blemish was a leadoff line-drive single by Yandy Díaz in the first; after that he retired 14 straight batters before Victor Mesa Jr. walked to open the sixth, so Gasser faced the minimum 18 hitters through six innings.

The fifth turned the night into a rout. With two outs and Rays starter Freddy Peralta in trouble, a forceout at second base was overturned on replay review, keeping the inning alive. Jackson Chourio had singled and stolen second, Christian Yelich walked, and William Contreras beat out an infield single to load the bases. Bryce Turang then lined a ball past shortstop Taylor Walls into center field, scoring two.

Garrett Mitchell followed with an RBI single, Cooper Pratt lashed a two-run double down the left-field line, and Sal Frelick doubled in another run to make it 8-0. Peralta was charged with five runs on seven hits in 4 2/3 innings. Cole Sulser then allowed an RBI single to David Hamilton and an RBI double to Chourio, and the Brewers led 10-0. Tampa Bay broke the shutout with a single run later in the game.

The Brewers finished with 16 hits and no errors. Brandon Woodruff, back from a July injury, pitched the ninth. Milwaukee has now won eight straight postseason games, including four in a row on the road.

The history now leans heavily toward Milwaukee. Of 43 teams to fall behind 3-0 in a best-of-seven postseason series, only the 2004 Red Sox have come back to win, and 21 of 25 teams with a 3-0 World Series lead went on to sweep. Neither club has won a championship: the Brewers' only previous World Series trip ended in a Game 7 loss to St. Louis in 1982, and the Rays have never won one. Game 4 is Tuesday at 8 p.m. ET.

Game played Friday, October 23, 2026
Milwaukee · World Series Game 1 Opener

Brewers Survive Ninth-Inning Scare, Steal World Series Opener From Rays

Brewers 3, Rays 2 — Final

Milwaukee took Game 1 of the 2026 World Series at American Family Field, holding off Tampa Bay after the Rays loaded the bases in the ninth. Trevor Megill struck out Jonny DeLuca for the final out to earn the save.

The Game

Christian Yelich tripled home Jackson Chourio in the first inning, and William Contreras followed with an RBI to make it 2-0 before Tampa Bay settled in. Flamethrower Jacob Misiorowski, who opened the night with a 103.5 mph first pitch, went five innings and allowed one run on four hits.

The Rays got on the board in the fourth, when Junior Caminero doubled and Jonathan Aranda scored on a groundout to cut the lead to 2-1. Contreras then took over behind the plate, throwing out Victor Mesa Jr. at third in the fifth and catching Aranda stealing second in the sixth to end a Rays hit-and-run. Manager Pat Murphy called Contreras his player of the game.

Chourio's single in the seventh scored Cooper Pratt for an insurance run, and it proved necessary. In the ninth the Rays loaded the bases against Megill. Brice Turang began what looked like a game-ending 4-6-3 double play, but replay showed Pratt's foot was off the bag and the out was overturned. Megill rebounded to strike out DeLuca and end it.

Tampa Bay finished 0-for-6 with runners in scoring position. Milwaukee leads the series 1-0.
