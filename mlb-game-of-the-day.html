Extra-Inning Classic — Blown Six-Run Lead

D-backs Survive Pirates' Six-Run Rally, Win It in the 12th

Diamondbacks 8, Pirates 7 — Final/12

Arizona built a six-run cushion behind seven scoreless innings from Brandon Pfaadt, then watched it evaporate in a furious two-inning Pittsburgh surge that included back-to-back game-tying home runs in the ninth. James McCann's RBI single in the 12th finally decided it, capping a four-hour, back-and-forth slugfest at PNC Park.

The Game

Arizona wasted no time building a cushion. Geraldo Perdomo tripled home a run in the first, and the Diamondbacks tacked on runs in each of the next three innings against Pirates rookie Bubba Chandler — an RBI fielder's choice and single in the second, an Arenado RBI single in the third, and a Brandon Lowe fielding error that let another run score in the fourth. A bases-loaded double-play ball in the sixth made it 6-0, and with Pfaadt cruising, the Diamondbacks looked in total control.

Then Pittsburgh turned it into a ballgame. The Pirates finally broke through against reliever Ryan Thompson in the eighth, stringing together three straight RBI hits from Ryan O'Hearn, Endy Rodríguez and Marcell Ozuna to cut the deficit to 6-3. It was only a prelude. In the ninth, Jake Mangum led off with a double, and Brandon Lowe and Bryan Reynolds followed with back-to-back home runs off closer Paul Sewald, completing the six-run comeback and tying the game at 6-6.

The drama didn't stop there. Arizona regained the lead in the 10th on an infield error that scored a run, only for Pittsburgh to answer immediately in the bottom half on another error, this one at first base, tying it again at 7-7. Both bullpens then settled in for two scoreless innings before McCann, who finished with four hits, delivered the decisive blow with an RBI single off Wilber Dotel in the 12th.

The result matters beyond the box score. Pittsburgh, fighting for a National League Wild Card spot, lost ground in the standings with the defeat, while Arizona improved to 56-52 and has now won 11 of its last 17.

Walk-Off Win Ends Franchise-Record Skid

Conine's Walk-Off Single Snaps Marlins' 12-Game Slide

Miami Marlins 8, Philadelphia Phillies 7 — Final

Griffin Conine lined a bases-loaded single into left field with two outs in the ninth inning Monday night, capping a stunning rally that erased a two-run, ninth-inning deficit and lifted the Miami Marlins past the Philadelphia Phillies 8-7. The win snapped a franchise-record 12-game losing streak that had buried Miami since July 9 — and did it against Philadelphia's own closer.

The Game

Bryce Harper staked Philadelphia to an early lead with a 419-foot, two-run homer off Marlins starter Tyler Phillips in the first — his 22nd of the season. Miami answered before the inning was over, plating a run on a throwing error and getting a solo shot from Heriberto Hernández, then broke the game open with three more in the second to seize a 5-2 cushion against Phillies ace Zack Wheeler.

Wheeler didn't make it out of the third, departing after allowing six hits and five runs; manager Rob Thomson later said the right-hander may have been feeling nauseous, with no structural injury involved. Tyler Phillips settled in after his rocky start, working into the sixth for Miami.

Philadelphia clawed all the way back in the sixth. Harper walked and scored on an Alec Bohm single, then J.T. Realmuto's fly ball bounced over the right-field wall for a ground-rule double that pulled the Phillies within one. Justin Crawford's single drove in Bryson Stott, and Realmuto came around on a throwing error by Hernández to hand Philadelphia a 7-5 lead.

That lead lasted until the ninth. Phillies closer Jhoan Duran opened the inning by loading the bases with no outs, then surrendered a two-run single to Hernández that tied it at 7. Two batters later, Conine lined his first career walk-off hit into left, sending loanDepot Park into a delayed, disbelieving roar — Miami's fifth walk-off win of the season, and its first win of any kind in nearly three weeks.

The Phillies, who have lost seven of their last ten since the All-Star break, will send Aaron Nola to the mound Tuesday against Miami's Sandy Alcantara as they try to avoid being swept up in the Marlins' sudden turnaround.

Miami — Franchise-Record Losing Streak

Machado, Merrill Erupt in the Eighth, Send Marlins to Record 12th Straight Loss

San Diego Padres 5, Miami Marlins 3 — Final

Manny Machado and Jackson Merrill went back-to-back in a four-run eighth inning Sunday, rallying the Padres from a 3-1 deficit to a 5-3 win over Miami at loanDepot Park. The loss dropped the Marlins to a franchise-record 12 straight defeats, eclipsing three previous 11-game skids in club history.

The Game

Miami looked to be cruising toward snapping its slide early. Padres starter Walker Buehler walked Liam Hicks and Griffin Conine in the second, and both scored — Hicks on a stolen base and a throwing error by catcher Luis Campusano, Conine on Graham Pauley's RBI single — to make it 2-0. Kyle Stowers stretched the lead to 3-0 with a leadoff homer off Buehler in the third.

Marlins starter Janson Junk was dealing, holding San Diego to a single run into the fifth before a scary moment ended his day: Ty France's comebacker deflected off reliever Michael Petersen's forearm after Junk departed, and Petersen left the game with a forearm contusion. Junk's final line was one run and seven hits allowed over 4 2/3 innings.

Miami still led 3-1 entering the eighth when reliever Lake Bachar came on to protect it. It fell apart in a hurry. Jake Cronenworth led off with a single, and two batters later Machado turned on a 3-2 fastball and drove it over the center-field fence to tie the game at 3-3. One out later, Bachar left a sweeper over the plate and Merrill pulled it out to right-center for a 4-3 Padres lead. Luis Rengifo doubled and scored on a Luis Campusano single to make it 5-3, capping a four-run inning that turned a two-run Miami lead into a two-run deficit in a matter of minutes.

San Diego's bullpen shut the door from there. Wandy Peralta worked around a hit for a scoreless inning to earn the win, and Mason Miller finished it for his 27th save, completing a three-game sweep at loanDepot Park in front of a crowd of 21,675 that jeered as the eighth inning ended.

The result was about more than one game for Miami. The Marlins had lost 11 in a row three times previously, most recently in June 2011, but had never dropped 12 straight. They'll try to end the skid Monday at home against Philadelphia.

One-Hit Shutout

Cease Untouchable Into the Sixth, Fires One-Hit Gem as Blue Jays Blank Red Sox

Toronto Blue Jays 6, Boston Red Sox 0 — Final

Dylan Cease carried a no-hit bid into the sixth inning at Fenway Park on Saturday before finishing with a one-hit, complete-game shutout, striking out 12 as the Toronto Blue Jays blanked the Boston Red Sox 6-0. George Springer's three-run homer highlighted a five-run third that put the game away early, leaving the night to Cease.

The Game

Cease was in total command from the first pitch, mixing a sharp slider with his changeup and fastball and generating almost no hard contact through five shadow-and-sunshine innings at Fenway. The only baserunner he allowed before the sixth came on a four-pitch walk to Caleb Durbin in the fifth. He didn't surrender a hit until Andruw Monasterio lined a leadoff double down the left-field line to open the sixth — the last real threat the Red Sox would muster.

Toronto's offense did its work early. In a five-run third against Boston starter Sonny Gray, the Blue Jays sent hits raining around Fenway — six of Gray's 10 hits allowed came in that single inning. Springer's three-run shot was the headline blow, with Vladimir Guerrero Jr. and Ernie Clement each adding RBI hits to blow the game open before Boston had settled in.

From there it was simply about how long Cease could keep it going. He struck out the side in order to close the game, finishing with 12 strikeouts and three walks over 120 pitches — his fourth career complete game and shutout. It came almost exactly two years to the day after he threw a full no-hitter for the San Diego Padres at Washington on July 25, 2024, and less than three weeks after a no-hit bid of his own was broken up by a leadoff single in the ninth at San Francisco.

The outing made Cease just the 16th pitcher — and the first since Felix Hernandez in 2007 — to throw a one-hit shutout against the Red Sox at Fenway Park, and the first Blue Jays pitcher to do it since Brandon Morrow in August 2010.

For Boston, the loss was just its second in 18 games. The Red Sox had won 15 straight, matching an 80-year-old club record, before that streak was snapped Wednesday — making Cease's mastery an abrupt reminder that even a red-hot lineup can be shut down cold.

Extra-Inning Slugfest

Olson's 10th-Inning Blast Lifts Braves Past Orioles in Camden Yards Classic

Braves 7, Orioles 6 — Final/10

Matt Olson turned a tied ballgame into a laugher with one swing, only for Baltimore to make it interesting again. The Atlanta Braves outlasted the Orioles 7-6 in 10 innings at Camden Yards, surviving a wild extra-inning trade of home runs to extend their second-half surge atop the NL East.

The Game

Baltimore built an early cushion, scoring twice in the second and adding another run in the fourth to lead 3-2 after Atlanta had chipped in a pair of its own. The lead held until the eighth, when Drake Baldwin's solo homer off reliever Ri Garcia — his second blown save of the year — knotted the score at 3-3 and sent the game into the late innings deadlocked.

Neither side could push a run across in the ninth, even after Atlanta intentionally walked Gunnar Henderson to set up a force play that ended the inning on a Taylor Ward groundout. That set the stage for a frantic 10th.

With a runner already placed at second to open the frame, Atlanta strung together a leadoff single from Joey Bart, a run-scoring fielder's choice aided by a throwing error from second baseman Jackson Holliday, and then the decisive blow: Matt Olson's three-run homer to right-center off reliever Kittredge, a 416-foot shot that turned a one-run game into a 7-3 rout.

Baltimore refused to go quietly. Christian Encarnacion-Strand, who entered the game hitting .400, answered in the bottom of the 10th with a two-run homer off Dylan Lee to pull the Orioles within 7-6, and a sacrifice fly from pinch hitter Jackson brought the tying run to the plate. But Atlanta closed the door there, escaping Baltimore with its sixth win in seven games.

The victory pushed the Braves to 61-42 and first place in the NL East, while the Orioles slipped to 50-54 and fourth in the AL East.

Late-Inning Rally · Playoff Push

Outman's Blast, Dingler's Tiebreaker Lift Surging Tigers Past Royals

Detroit Tigers 4, Kansas City Royals 3 — Final

James Outman erased a two-run deficit with a three-run homer in the seventh inning and Dillon Dingler followed with a go-ahead double in the eighth, lifting the Detroit Tigers past the Kansas City Royals 4-3 on Thursday night at Comerica Park. Closer Kyle Finnegan needed a ninth-inning double play to slam the door, extending Detroit's charge toward the playoff race to 14 wins in its last 19 games.

The Game

It was a scoreless pitchers' duel for five innings, with Royals right-hander Randy Dobnak matching Tigers starter Troy Melton zero for zero. Kansas City finally broke through in the sixth when Isaac Collins lifted a two-run homer, and the Royals carried that 2-0 cushion into the seventh looking to hand the ball to their bullpen with a clean lead.

They nearly got there. Kansas City reliever John Schreiber was one out away from escaping the seventh unscathed when Outman turned on a 2-2 fastball and sent it just over the wall in right field for a three-run homer, flipping a two-run deficit into a 3-2 Tigers lead in a single swing.

Kansas City answered right back. Josh Rojas lined an RBI single off Drew Anderson in the eighth to tie it at 3-3, but Detroit reclaimed the lead in the bottom half when Dingler ripped a go-ahead double off Steven Cruz, plating the go-ahead run and handing Anderson the win.

The ninth still had drama left. Finnegan entered with two runners aboard and only one out, but induced a double play to end the threat and lock down his second save of the season.

The win keeps Detroit's late-summer surge alive and adds weight to a bigger storyline hanging over the club: ace Tarik Skubal, before the game, said his preference is to finish the season in a Tigers uniform and compete for a championship rather than be dealt before the Aug. 3 trade deadline. Skubal is slated to start Friday against the same Royals club — a start that, depending on what the front office decides, could be his last in a Detroit uniform.

Six-Run Rally, Five-Run Scare

Jarvis, Braves Erupt for Six in the Eighth, Hang On as Padres Nearly Complete Stunning Rally

Atlanta Braves 7, San Diego Padres 6 — Final

The Atlanta Braves turned a tense pitchers' duel into a laugher with a six-run eighth inning, then had to sweat out a five-run Padres rally in the ninth before Raisel Iglesias slammed the door for his 20th save. Rookie Jim Jarvis delivered the go-ahead double in the decisive frame, and San Diego came within a swing of an improbable comeback at Truist Park.

The Game

For six innings, this looked like a low-scoring grind. Braves left-hander Martín Pérez, activated off the injured list after taking a Juan Soto liner off his forearm on July 5, allowed just one hit over four scoreless innings in his return. Padres right-hander Michael King opened shaky, walking the bases loaded before Michael Harris II's groundout drove in the game's first run in the first inning, and Atlanta's 1-0 lead held deep into the night as King settled in, working six innings with seven strikeouts.

San Diego finally broke through in the seventh when Jackson Merrill tied it at 1-1 with a solo homer off reliever Didier Fuentes, and the game looked headed for a tight finish.

Instead, the eighth inning blew the game open. Mauricio Dubón singled to start the rally against left-hander Wandy Peralta, and Jarvis followed with a double into the right-field corner that scored Dubón all the way from first for a 2-1 lead. Brewer Hicklen doubled in Jarvis, and then Ozzie Albies, Matt Olson, Austin Riley and Dominic Smith strung together RBI singles off Randy Vásquez. The Braves collected seven of their 14 hits in the inning alone, turning a one-run game into a 7-1 laugher.

San Diego was not finished. Víctor Mederos allowed a run-scoring single to Gavin Sheets and a two-run triple to Fernando Tatis Jr. in the ninth, and Luis Rengifo followed with a two-run homer off Iglesias to cut the deficit to 7-6. But Iglesias steadied himself to retire the final two batters and preserve the win, pushing Atlanta to 59-42 while San Diego dropped to 50-52.

Dylan Lee, who worked a scoreless eighth, picked up his fifth win of the season without a loss. The Braves and Padres close out their four-game series Thursday, with Chris Sale (10-6, 2.06 ERA) opposing Griffin Canning.
