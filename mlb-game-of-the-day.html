Cleveland · Guardians Retake First Place in AL Central

Fill-In Petey Halpin's Homer Lifts Guardians Past White Sox, Into First Place

Cleveland Guardians 6, Chicago White Sox 3 — Final

Petey Halpin, drafted into the starting lineup only because of injuries, drove a Sean Newcomb sinker 393 feet into the right-field stands for a three-run homer in the sixth inning, breaking a tie and sending Cleveland to a 6-3 win over Chicago on Wednesday. The victory, capping a two-of-three series win at Progressive Field, pushed the Guardians into sole possession of first place in the AL Central for the first time since early July.

The Game

CLEVELAND — With the two rivals separated by a half-game and nine to play, Wednesday's rubber match had the feel of a de facto division decider. Chicago second baseman Chase Meidroth opened the door in the third inning when a routine tag attempt slipped out of his glove, extending the frame; Jo Adell made the White Sox pay two batters later with a two-run single off Anthony Kay, and Cleveland led 2-0.

Chicago answered in the top of the fifth. Tommy Pham, activated off the injured list just before first pitch, turned on a pitch from Cleveland rookie Parker Messick and drove it out to left-center for a three-run homer, giving the White Sox a 3-2 lead and a jolt of energy on a get-away afternoon.

The lead did not last. Reliever Hagen Smith issued two straight walks to open the Cleveland sixth, and José Ramírez lined an RBI double to retie it at 3-3. Two outs later, with runners on first and second, Sean Newcomb relieved Smith and threw Halpin a 3-1 sinker on the inner half of the plate. Halpin, a reserve outfielder pressed into the starting lineup by injuries elsewhere on the roster, crushed it 393 feet and 103.7 mph into the right-field seats for a three-run homer that turned a tie game into a 6-3 Cleveland lead.

Messick worked around Pham's homer to finish six innings with three runs allowed on seven hits and eight strikeouts, and the Guardians' bullpen protected the lead the rest of the way — even after Erik Sabrowski walked two batters in the eighth to bring Chicago's tying run to the plate. Cade Smith came on to get the final out of the eighth and worked a perfect ninth for his 37th save.

The result dropped the White Sox to 77-75 and ended a run that had them in first place in the AL Central dating back to early July. Cleveland improved to 78-75, taking two of three in the series and claiming sole possession of the division lead for the first time since July 3 as it chases a third straight AL Central title. Ramírez's second double of the game also tied Hall of Famer Nap Lajoie for second on the franchise's all-time list with 424, one more piece of history piling up in a career that keeps producing it even as Cleveland fights for October.

Game played Tuesday, September 15, 2026
Milestone Homer · Extra-Inning Theater

Alonso's 300th Ties It, Mayo's Inside-the-Parker Wins It for Baltimore

Baltimore Orioles 7, New York Mets 5 — Final/11

Pete Alonso turned his first game back at Citi Field into a piece of personal history, launching his 300th career home run to tie the score in the ninth inning. Two innings later, Coby Mayo delivered the finishing blow, circling the bases on an improbable three-run inside-the-park home run that carried Baltimore past New York, 7-5, in 11 innings on Tuesday night.

The Game

QUEENS, N.Y. — Alonso's return as a visitor drew standing ovations from the Citi Field crowd before each of his plate appearances, seven years of goodwill from his time as the Mets' franchise first baseman spilling out for the man now wearing an Orioles uniform. Christian Encarnacion-Strand's solo home run in the second inning staked Baltimore to an early lead, but New York answered and, behind starter Sean Manaea and a parade of relievers, carried a one-run edge into the ninth.

Then Alonso got hold of a pitch from Kodai Senga and sent it a projected 414 feet over the left-center-field wall, a game-tying solo shot that stood as the moment of the night before Mayo topped it. Alonso flipped his bat toward the Baltimore dugout, rounded the bases to a roar from the same fans who once chanted his name, and later took a curtain call at his old teammates' urging. According to the Elias Sports Bureau, he became just the 20th player in American or National League history to reach 300 home runs against a club he once called home.

The game stayed level into extra innings before Baltimore broke it open in the 11th. With two runners aboard and two outs, Mayo drove a ball to center field that eluded New York's outfielders, and the third baseman never stopped running, sliding across the plate with a three-run, inside-the-park home run off Jonathan Pintaro that turned a tight game into a four-run cushion.

New York would not go quietly. The Mets pushed a pair of runs across in the bottom of the 11th and put the tying run in scoring position before Andrew Kittredge forced the game's final out to seal his 10th save. Baltimore finished with only eight hits to New York's twelve, but its four home runs — to New York's one — made the difference.

The result had little bearing on the standings; the Mets were already eliminated from playoff contention a night earlier, while the Orioles continued to chip away at the final American League wild-card spot with just over two weeks left to play. For one night, though, the game belonged to Alonso's milestone and Mayo's sprint around the bases.
