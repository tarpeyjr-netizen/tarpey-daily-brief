Three-Homer Game

Crow-Armstrong Hits Three, and Wrigley’s 150th Party Turns Into an MVP Rally

Chicago Cubs 17, Cincinnati Reds 5 — Final

Pete Crow-Armstrong homered three times and drove in six runs Saturday afternoon at Wrigley Field, carrying the Cubs to a 17-5 demolition of the Cincinnati Reds. At 24 years and 157 days, he became the youngest player in Cubs history with a three-homer game — and he did it in front of a ballpark stuffed with franchise alumni for the club’s 150th anniversary celebration.

The Game

It took two pitches. Crow-Armstrong turned on Andrew Abbott’s offering to lead off the bottom of the first and sent it 435 feet into right-center, his eighth leadoff home run of the season and all of them coming this year. Two batters later Alex Bregman added a two-run shot, and the Cubs had a 3-0 lead before Abbott recorded three outs.

Cincinnati punched back. Eugenio Suárez, a familiar tormentor at Wrigley, hit a two-run homer off Kevin Gausman in the second, and rookie Sal Stewart followed with a two-run drive in the third — his 30th of the year, making him only the second rookie in Reds history to reach 30 homers, after Frank Robinson in 1956. For an inning or so, at 6-4, this looked like a game.

It was not. Chicago had already answered in the second, when Tyrone Taylor scored on an error and Crow-Armstrong followed with a two-run drive for a 6-2 cushion. Bregman singled home a run in the fourth. Carson Kelly’s three-run homer in the fifth pushed it to 10-5, and after JJ Bleday’s sacrifice fly in the top of the inning the Reds never scored again — the Cubs’ bullpen allowed one hit over the final four innings.

The seventh belonged to Crow-Armstrong. He drove a Tony Santillan slurve 444 feet to center for No. 36, and the MVP chants that have followed him all summer got their loudest airing yet. By the eighth, the Reds had run out of pitchers and sent catcher Jose Trevino to the mound; Kelly doubled home a run and Seiya Suzuki hit a two-run homer off him before Crow-Armstrong, chasing a fourth, bounced out to second and got a standing ovation anyway.

The rout snapped a three-game Chicago losing streak and a stretch of six losses in eight games. At 77-59, the Cubs remain eight games behind Milwaukee in the National League Central with a month to play; Cincinnati fell to 64-72 and to 3-5 on a nine-game trip.

Game played Wednesday, August 26, 2026
Seven-Run Ninth · First Since 1958

Reds Drop Seven on the Giants in the Ninth, Steal One from the Bay

Cincinnati Reds 10, San Francisco Giants 9 — Final

Down six runs with three outs to work with, the Cincinnati Reds stunned the San Francisco Giants at Oracle Park on Wednesday, scoring seven times in the ninth inning for a 10-9 victory. Hector Rodriguez dropped a home run into the bay to open the frame, Eugenio Suarez emptied the bases with a grand slam moments later, and Tyler Stephenson finished it with a go-ahead single. It was the first time since 1958 that Cincinnati won a game it entered the ninth trailing by six.

The Game

For eight innings this looked like a routine afternoon at Oracle Park. Logan Webb worked six innings of two-run, five-hit ball, the Giants chipped away with pairs of runs in the second, fifth and sixth, and a three-run eighth pushed the lead to 9-3. San Francisco was three outs from a three-game sweep of a Reds club that has spent the summer drifting out of the National League race.

Then the ninth happened. Rodriguez led off with his second home run of the game, a drive that carried over the right-field wall and into McCovey Cove. The Reds kept the line moving, and four batters later Suarez turned on a pitch from Dylan Smith and cleared the bases with a grand slam. Just like that it was 9-8, with nobody out and the Giants' bullpen unraveling in real time.

Stephenson delivered the blow that flipped the scoreboard, a single that scored the go-ahead run and capped a seven-run inning built without a single wasted at-bat. Cincinnati finished the day with 15 hits and no errors; San Francisco had 12 hits and two errors, and lost a game it had led by six with three outs remaining.

Emilio Pagan handled the bottom of the ninth cleanly for his 17th save, sealing a win that spared Cincinnati a sweep. Tony Santillan got the decision despite surrendering three runs in his lone inning of work — a reminder of how strange the afternoon was, since a pitcher who allowed a crooked number wound up on the right side of the ledger.

The result does little for the standings. The Reds, at 63-71, remain 19.5 games back of Milwaukee in the NL Central; the Giants fell to 54-79. But historically it was the day's most remarkable half-inning of baseball anywhere in the majors — a six-run ninth-inning deficit erased by a franchise that had not managed the feat in 68 years.

Walk-Off · Rookie History in the Desert

Waldschmidt Ambushes Cubs Again, Sinking Chicago With a Two-Out, Two-Run Walk-Off

Diamondbacks 5, Cubs 4 — Final

PHOENIX — Ryan Waldschmidt came off the Arizona bench with two outs in the ninth inning, one run down, and drove a hanging 2-1 slider 408 feet into the left-field seats to finish a stunning 5-4 comeback over the Chicago Cubs on Tuesday night. The Diamondbacks had been shut out through seven innings and trailed 4-0 entering the eighth. By the time Chase Field stopped shaking, the rookie had authored a piece of major league history.

The Game

For seven innings this was Clay Holmes's night. The Cubs right-hander gave up two singles, walked two and struck out five, never letting Arizona put a runner in scoring position, and handed the bullpen a 4-0 lead after 93 pitches. He has now allowed one earned run over his last 19 innings across three starts.

Chicago's runs came in bunches against Brandon Pfaadt, who was hitless through three before a single and a double set the table in the fourth for Ian Happ, whose 23rd home run cleared the wall in right for a 3-0 lead. Michael Busch tacked on an RBI single in the sixth. Pfaadt finished with four runs on six hits over 6 2/3 innings.

The eighth turned it. Daniel Palencia retired the first man he faced, then hit Jordan Lawlar in the head with a 98 mph fastball — Lawlar walked off under his own power — and walked Lars Nootbaar. With two outs, Gabriel Moreno's check-swing double hugged the first-base line, struck the bag and stayed fair for a run, and Geraldo Perdomo lined a two-run single to center. Jacob Webb came on and got Max Kepler to ground out, but the shutout was gone and so were the Cubs' 23 straight scoreless innings.

Justin Martinez, making only his third appearance of the season, worked a scoreless ninth. Then Webb walked Tim Tawa with one out, Ildemaro Vargas flied to right, and pinch-runner Jose Fernandez stole second with two down — putting the winning run in scoring position and bringing Waldschmidt to the plate. One slider later, it was over.

Per OptaSTATS, Waldschmidt became the first rookie in major league history with two walk-off homers while trailing in the same calendar month, having done the same to the Dodgers on Aug. 7. The win was Arizona's 40th comeback victory of the season, tied with Chicago for the most in the majors, and pulled the Diamondbacks (70-63) within one game of San Diego for the last National League wild card. The Cubs (76-57) remain three games clear of Philadelphia for the top wild card.

Three-Homer Night · Streak Snapped

Raleigh's Three Homers Bury Wheeler, End Phillies' Nine-Game Run

Mariners 9, Phillies 2 — Final, T-Mobile Park

Cal Raleigh hit three home runs into the right-field seats Monday night, all of them from the left side of the plate, and the Seattle Mariners hammered Zack Wheeler for eight runs in a 9-2 rout of Philadelphia. The loss ended the Phillies' season-best nine-game winning streak. It was the first three-homer game of Raleigh's career and the first by any Mariner at T-Mobile Park.

The Game

Philadelphia struck first and never got closer. The Phillies loaded the bases against Logan Gilbert in the opening inning and pushed across a run on Alec Bohm's groundout, but Gilbert escaped by striking out Bryson Stott, and the visitors would not score again after the second.

The bottom of the first turned the night on its head. Julio Rodríguez drove a Wheeler pitch into the Phillies' bullpen in left for a three-run homer, his 20th of the year, and Raleigh followed with a two-run drive to right to cap a five-run inning. J.T. Realmuto's solo shot off Gilbert in the second cut the margin to 5-2, and that was as tight as it got.

Wheeler unraveled from there. He walked in trouble in the third and threw a bases-loaded wild pitch that scored Rodríguez, then opened the fifth by surrendering back-to-back homers to Raleigh and rookie Cole Young. The right-hander was pulled after four-plus innings having allowed eight runs on nine hits and five walks — a season high in runs, and a career-high-matching four home runs.

Raleigh finished it in the seventh, lofting a solo shot off reliever Chase Shugart that a leaping Bryce Harper could not reach in right. It was his 17th homer of the season and the fifth Seattle home run of the night. The switch-hitting catcher, runner-up for AL MVP a year ago when he led the majors with 60 homers, had gone the entire 2026 season without a multi-homer game before Monday.

The context cuts both ways. Philadelphia (73-59) remains firmly in the National League wild-card picture and three games behind Atlanta in the NL East, but Wheeler's slide — no quality starts in his last five outings entering the night — is an unwelcome subplot heading into September. Seattle, at 63-69, stayed three games back in the AL West after Texas moved into sole possession of first place, and three behind Cleveland for the league's last wild-card spot.

Six-Run, Two-Out Walk-Off Ninth

Down to Their Last Strike, Phillies Erupt for Six in the Ninth as Schwarber Ends It

Phillies 6, Cardinals 4 — Final

For eight innings Sunday at Citizens Bank Park, the St. Louis Cardinals played a nearly flawless road game and led 4-0. Then Philadelphia sent 10 men to the plate in the bottom of the ninth, scored six runs with two outs, and Kyle Schwarber ended it with a 459-foot two-run homer over the batter's eye. The Phillies won 6-4 for their ninth straight victory and a third consecutive series sweep.

The Game

Nothing about the first eight innings suggested what was coming. JJ Wetherholt beat out an infield dribbler to open the game, stole second and came home on the first of Alec Burleson's two RBI singles. Burleson struck again in the fourth, and José Fermín led off the fifth with a solo home run. Wetherholt's two-out single in the seventh made it 4-0 and chased Cristopher Sánchez, the National League's leader in wins, who had allowed nine hits without walking a batter.

Cardinals starter Kyle Leahy was sharp and brief — four scoreless innings on 63 pitches, two hits, four strikeouts — as St. Louis continued to manage the converted reliever's workload even with Hunter Dobbins and Andre Pallante newly on the injured list. Four relievers followed him: Luis Gastelum, Justin Bruihl, Ryne Stanek and George Soriano combined for four scoreless innings on two hits.

Riley O'Brien came on for the ninth with a four-run cushion and 33 saves in 38 chances. Bryce Harper doubled to open the inning — his second double of the afternoon — but O'Brien retired the next two on flyballs and stood one out from a clean getaway. Then it unraveled. J.T. Realmuto singled. Justin Crawford walked to load the bases. Edmundo Sosa pulled a bases-clearing double down the third-base line to make it 4-3, and Bryan De La Cruz singled Sosa home to tie it.

That brought up Schwarber, who fell behind 0-2 and then 2-2 before turning on a 98-mph sinker and driving it over the center-field batter's eye. It was his major-league-leading 39th home run of the season and, per Statcast, traveled a projected 459 feet. Alex McFarlane, who had thrown a scoreless top of the ninth, got the win without recording an out in the bottom half.

The Phillies moved to 73-58, three games behind Atlanta in the National League East and comfortably inside the wild-card picture. The Cardinals fell to 66-66 and closed an 11-game road trip with a sweep in Philadelphia, sliding to 5½ games back of the final wild-card berth — a weekend that also cost them Dobbins, Pallante and outfielder Everson Pereira to injury, with Gold Glove shortstop Masyn Winn missing a fourth straight start with a thumb problem.

Walk-Off Win

Shut Out for Eight, Red Sox Erupt for Three in the Ninth — and Celebrate Twice

Boston Red Sox 3, San Francisco Giants 2 — Final

For eight innings at Fenway Park on Saturday night, the Red Sox had nothing: no runs, six hits, and a lineup that looked overmatched. Then, in the bottom of the ninth, they sent nine men to the plate against Sam Hentges, scored three times, and won it 3–2 when Eli White slid headfirst across the plate on a Wilyer Abreu grounder. Boston celebrated, waited out a replay review, and celebrated again.

The Game

San Francisco had the whole night mapped out early. Willy Adames turned on a Patrick Sandoval pitch with two outs in the first and dropped it over the Green Monster for his 21st homer. Two innings later, catcher Andrew Knizner — hitting .200 coming in — drove a solo shot of his own to make it 2–0. The Giants loaded the bases behind him and Sandoval wriggled free, which turned out to matter more than anyone knew at the time.

Blade Tidwell, coming off a start against Colorado in which he walked a career-high five, was the story for most of the evening. The right-hander gave up three hits over 5 2/3 scoreless innings and struck out a career-high seven, fanning Ceddanne Rafaela and Abreu in the first and punching out Abreu again to escape a first-and-second jam in the third. Boston hitters were consistently late all night.

The Red Sox had their chance in the eighth and blew it: Mickey Gasper doubled, Nick Sogard walked, and Rafaela singled to load the bases with nobody out. Dylan Smith struck out Abreu, then got Willson Contreras and a fly ball from Adley Rutschman to end it. Fenway went quiet.

The ninth started with a walk to Caleb Durbin and a hit batsman, Jahmai Jones. Andruw Monasterio moved Durbin to third on a fielder's choice. Pinch-hitter Eli White punched a single up the middle to break the shutout, and Sogard — who had just made a diving catch on Bryce Eldridge in the top half — ripped a double down the left-field line to tie it. San Francisco intentionally walked Rafaela to load the bases for Abreu, who was 0 for 4 with four strikeouts. He hit a hard grounder to the right side; Eldridge made a diving stop and threw home, but Knizner's foot came off the plate by inches as White dove in. Umpire James Hoye called him safe, the Giants challenged, and the call stood.

It was Boston's fourth walk-off win and 31st comeback victory of a remarkable summer — the Red Sox are 33–11 in their last 44 games and sit 70–59, six games behind Tampa Bay in the AL East. The Giants, at 52–77, have now lost 10 of 13.
