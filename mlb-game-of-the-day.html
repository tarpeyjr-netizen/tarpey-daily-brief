One-Hitter — Anaheim, Calif.

Cameron Misses History by a Bloop, Blanks Angels on One Hit

Royals 3, Angels 0 — Final

Noah Cameron carried a perfect game into the sixth inning and a no-hitter into the seventh before Vaughn Grissom's soft single dropped in behind shortstop, and the Kansas City left-hander settled for the next-best thing: a complete-game, one-hit shutout in a 3-0 win over the Angels on Sunday at Angel Stadium. Cameron struck out eight, walked one and threw 115 pitches in the first complete game of his career. Michael Massey and Bobby Witt Jr. supplied the only offense he needed with back-to-back-inning home runs.

The Game

For five innings there was nothing to write down. Cameron retired the first 15 Angels he faced, mixing his changeup off a fastball the home lineup never squared up, and Kansas City's defense was never asked to do anything difficult. The first blemish came with none out in the sixth, when Oswald Peraza drew a leadoff walk — the only free pass Cameron would issue all afternoon.

The perfect game gone, Cameron simply pitched around it. Peraza moved up on Travis d'Arnaud's groundout, Josh Lowe struck out swinging and Zach Neto bounced weakly to second to end the threat. He opened the seventh by striking out Mike Trout, then lost the no-hitter one batter later when Grissom lifted a ball high over Witt's head and into shallow left-center for the Angels' only hit of the day.

Kansas City had already done its scoring by then, and it did not take much. Massey turned on a pitch in the fourth and drove it 384 feet into the front row of the right-field bleachers for his 11th home run and a 1-0 lead. Witt followed an inning later, lining his 14th down the left-field line to double the margin.

The Royals added an unearned insurance run in the seventh, with Carter Jensen's RBI single off reliever Blake Weiman finishing the scoring. Angels starter Ryan Johnson had actually pitched well enough to keep it close — 5 1/3 innings, five hits, two earned runs — but on this afternoon two runs was a mountain.

Cameron came back out for the ninth and finished it in a tidy 2 hours, 16 minutes before 28,036, closing out a series win for a Kansas City club that has gotten little national attention this summer. It was the kind of afternoon that reframes a pitcher's season: Cameron's record climbed to 7-8, a line that says almost nothing about how he threw the baseball on Sunday.

Game played Saturday, August 15, 2026
First in MLB History

Three Swings, Three Homers: Báez Rewrites the Record Book in His First Big-League Game

St. Louis Cardinals 8, Chicago Cubs 4 — Final

CHICAGO — Joshua Báez had never seen a major league pitch before Saturday afternoon. By the sixth inning he had hit three of them over the wall at Wrigley Field, becoming the first player in the history of the sport to homer in each of his first three big-league at-bats and carrying the Cardinals to an 8-4 win over the Cubs.

The Game

The 23-year-old outfielder, promoted from Triple-A Memphis less than 24 hours earlier, did not wait to get comfortable. Matthew Boyd had already walked one batter and plunked another in the first inning, and after Alec Burleson's groundout made it 1-0, Boyd challenged Báez with a 93.4 mph fastball up in the zone. Báez turned it around at 108.6 mph and sent it a Statcast-projected 449 feet to dead center — a two-run shot on the first pitch of his career, and a 3-0 St. Louis lead.

Chicago answered quickly. An error by Cardinals third baseman Blaze Jordan opened the bottom of the second, and Michael Conforto punished the extra out with a three-run homer off Michael McGreevy that tied it at 3. It was the only real crack in McGreevy's afternoon; none of the three runs he allowed were earned.

Báez untied it in the fourth, working Boyd to a full count and driving a changeup 382 feet down the left-field line for a solo homer and a 4-3 lead. That made him just the ninth player ever to homer twice in a regular-season debut, and the second since 1900 to do it in his first two at-bats, joining Bob Nieman in 1951.

The turning point came two innings later, when the Cubs sent Boyd back out for the sixth with Báez due up and a one-run deficit to protect. Báez lined a 99.8 mph shot 368 feet the other way for his third homer, and two batters later Jordan added a solo blast that ended Boyd's day at 7-3. Burleson tacked on a solo homer off Colin Rea in the eighth — the fifth Cubs home run allowed on the day — and Chicago scratched back a lone run on a double play in the bottom half.

The loss snapped a stretch in which the Cubs had won nine of 11, and it laid bare their season-long problem: a pitching staff that has now surrendered 187 home runs, more than any club in baseball but one. For St. Louis, it evened the series and delivered the kind of debut a franchise builds around. Báez, the Cardinals' second-round pick in 2021 and their No. 3 prospect, arrived leading all of Triple-A with 34 home runs. Roughly 30 family members had flown in from Boston, Orlando, Miami and the Dominican Republic to watch him play his first game.

No-Hit Bid · One-Hitter

Cavalli Carries No-Hitter Into Seventh, Buries the Hottest Lineup in Baseball

Washington Nationals 7, Chicago Cubs 0 — Final

Cade Cavalli was three outs from the sixth and 20 batters into an afternoon that had Nationals Park leaning forward on every pitch when Michael Busch dropped a bloop single into shallow left field. That was all the Cubs got. Cavalli finished with a one-hit, 10-strikeout shutout across eight innings, and Washington salvaged the finale of the series with a 7–0 win on Thursday.

The Game

Cavalli was perfect through five, mixing a knuckle-curve and sweeper around a fastball that sat at 98 mph — 1.2 ticks above his season average — and touched 99.1. Dansby Swanson finally interrupted the run with a four-pitch walk to open the sixth, but Cavalli erased him immediately, coaxing an inning-ending double play from Michael Conforto before striking out Miguel Amaya.

The no-hit bid survived until two were out in the seventh. Busch, the only Cub to reach base by hit all day, flared an opposite-field single to left, and the crowd — a mix of Nationals fans mourning the near-miss and Cubs fans relieved by it — rose anyway. Cavalli got Alex Bregman to fly out to center to finish the inning, then came back out for the eighth and set the side down again, walking off at a career-high 103 pitches.

The Washington offense had already made the outcome academic. Rookie first baseman Abimelec Ortiz singled with one out in the fourth and scored on Dylan Crews' double to left-center. An inning later the Cubs' Kevin Gausman lost the plot entirely: a walk to Jorbit Vivas, a single by Andrés Chaparro, a sacrifice fly from Andrew Pinckney, and then Ortiz, ambushing a first-pitch fastball and driving it out to center for a three-run homer. Crews singled and stole second before Daylen Lile brought him home. Five runs, and Gausman was gone with two outs still to get.

Making only his second start since arriving from Toronto at the trade deadline, Gausman had retired the first 10 he faced while leaning on his splitter more than half the time. When Washington stopped chasing it, he went to the four-seamer — and six of the seven hits he allowed came off it. Aaron Civale cleaned up with 3 1/3 innings of long relief, surrendering only a Vivas RBI single in the eighth.

The loss was only Chicago's second in 10 games for a club that had averaged 7.1 runs over that stretch and scored 30 in the previous three games alone. Cole Hamels remains the last pitcher to no-hit the Cubs, on July 25, 2015; Cavalli is the first since to strike out 10 or more in at least eight scoreless innings against them while allowing no more than one hit. It was also his seventh consecutive quality start, the longest active run in the majors and the longest by a Nationals starter since Max Scherzer's 14 in 2019. Washington moved to 60–63; the Cubs fell to 71–51.

Game played Tuesday, August 11, 2026
Pitching Duel

Eury Pérez Outduels Reigning Cy Young Winner as Marlins Blank Pirates

Marlins 2, Pirates 0 — Final

On a night with no blowouts, milestones, or walk-offs anywhere on the slate, the best story in baseball was a mound matchup in Miami: Eury Pérez outpitched reigning NL Cy Young winner Paul Skenes over seven scoreless innings, and Esteury Ruiz supplied every run the Marlins would need. Pete Fairbanks closed it out for his 18th save as Miami blanked Pittsburgh 2-0.

The Game

Pérez set the tone immediately, striking out the side in order to open the game, and the 23-year-old right-hander never let the Pirates get comfortable. He worked around three singles — two in the third, one in the seventh — and finished with seven strikeouts over 100 pitches, extending a streak of seven straight starts without allowing a home run.

Skenes matched him for two innings before Ruiz broke through in the third, lifting a one-out solo shot to left for his sixth homer of the year and a 1-0 Miami lead. The reigning NL Cy Young winner, who entered with a 6.26 ERA over his previous eight starts, otherwise kept the Marlins in check, striking out Owen Caissie with runners on second and third to end the fourth.

The turning point came in the fifth. Ruiz was hit by a pitch, stole second on a delayed steal, and came around to score when Xavier Edwards' infield single skipped past shortstop Jacob Gonzalez for a throwing error, making it 2-0. Two batters later, Bryan Reynolds robbed Griffin Conine of a two-out homer in left field — a defensive gem for the losing side that kept the deficit at two.

Skenes exited after five innings and 65 pitches, having allowed two runs (one earned) on five hits with four strikeouts, and fell to 9-11. Reliever Yohan Ramírez kept it a two-run game with three scoreless innings, but Miami's bullpen didn't blink: Michael Petersen struck out two in the eighth, and Fairbanks worked around a leadoff walk in the ninth to nail down his 18th save in 21 chances.

The win was Miami's 12th shutout of the season and third straight victory following a four-game skid, pushing the Marlins to 61-59. Pittsburgh, which got a strong final line from its ace but nothing behind him, dropped to 58-63.

Game played Monday, August 10, 2026
San Francisco — Extra-Inning Comeback

Astros Erase Late Deficit, Bury Giants With Three-Run 10th

Houston Astros 6, San Francisco Giants 3 — Final/10

Jeremy Peña's go-ahead single and Isaac Paredes' two-run double turned a tense ninth-inning gut check into a laugher of a 10th, lifting the Houston Astros to a 6-3 win over the San Francisco Giants at Oracle Park. Houston trailed for most of the night before Cam Smith's two-out RBI single off closer Dylan Smith forced extra innings, and Josh Hader made sure the rally stuck.

The Game

San Francisco controlled the middle innings behind Blade Tidwell, who worked 5 2/3 sharp innings and struck out six. Jung Hoo Lee's solo homer in the fifth staked the Giants to a lead, and after Cam Smith tied it with a solo shot in the seventh, Drew Gilbert answered right back with a go-ahead home run of his own in the bottom half to put San Francisco up 3-2.

Houston starter Hunter Wesneski battled through seven innings, allowing both Giants homers among his three earned runs, and handed a one-run deficit to the bullpen. The Astros looked headed for a quiet road loss until the ninth, when Smith — the Astros' right fielder, no relation to San Francisco's closer — lined a two-out single off Dylan Smith to knot the score at 3-3 and send the game to extras.

The turning point came in the top of the 10th. With the automatic runner at second, LaMonte Wade Jr. and a wild pitch from Jason Foley advanced the runner to third, and Peña promptly lined a go-ahead single. Paredes followed with a two-run double to blow the game open, and Foley was charged with all three runs in the loss.

Josh Hader slammed the door with two perfect innings spanning the ninth and 10th, striking out two and improving to 4-1, sealing Houston's second straight extra-inning win of the week and its fourth win in five games.
