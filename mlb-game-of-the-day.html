Cleveland · Late Comeback in a Division Series Game

Montgomery's Sixth-Inning Double Lifts White Sox Past Guardians, Into 2-0 ALDS Lead

White Sox 4, Guardians 3 — Final

Braden Montgomery lined a two-run double down the right-field line in the sixth inning, and the Chicago White Sox rallied from an early deficit to beat the Cleveland Guardians 4-3 in Game 2 of the ALDS. The White Sox are 4-0 this postseason, all on the road, and one win from their first ALCS in 21 years.

The Game

Cleveland struck first, scoring twice in the bottom of the first on a throwing error and an RBI single. Then Guardians starter Gavin Williams took over, striking out 11 White Sox through five innings, one shy of Charles Nagy's franchise postseason record of 12.

Munetaka Murakami's RBI single in the fourth cut the lead to 2-1, but the game turned in the sixth. Williams left at 98 pitches with a runner aboard, and reliever Erik Sabrowski gave up Montgomery's double, putting Chicago ahead 3-2. A wild pitch from Sabrowski helped move runners, and Chase Meidroth added a bloop single to right to make it 4-2.

Sean Burke kept Cleveland quiet in relief, allowing one hit over 5 1/3 innings. Jo Adell tripled off the top of the left-center wall in the eighth, a 413-foot shot that scored Chase DeLauter and made it 4-3, but Grant Taylor closed the door with a scoreless ninth.

The series moves to Chicago for Game 3 on Wednesday, where the White Sox, a franchise that endured three straight 100-loss seasons, will have two chances to finish the sweep.

Milwaukee — Walk-Off Win Puts Brewers Up 2-0 in the NLDS

Chourio Walks It Off Against Miller, Brewers Take 2-0 NLDS Lead

Brewers 4, Padres 3 — Final

Jackson Chourio lined a two-out, two-strike fastball from Mason Miller up the middle for a two-run single, giving the Milwaukee Brewers a 4-3 walk-off win over the San Diego Padres in Game 2 of the NLDS. Milwaukee, down 3-2 entering the ninth, now holds a 2-0 series lead.

The Game

San Diego scored first when Ty France singled home Manny Machado in the opening inning. The Padres made it 2-0 in the fifth on a force out that scored Luis Campusano, though a throwing error by shortstop Cooper Pratt kept the inning alive. Milwaukee answered in the bottom half, as Sal Frelick singled home Christian Yelich to cut the lead to 2-1.

In the seventh, France singled in Fernando Tatis Jr., who had reached on an error, to push San Diego back ahead by two. The Brewers pulled one back immediately when a pair of Jake Cronenworth errors let Garrett Mitchell score, keeping it a 3-2 game. The game featured five combined errors, three by Milwaukee and two by San Diego.

Trevor Megill set the Padres down in order in the ninth. In the bottom half, against Miller, Yelich and Frelick drew walks around an Andrew Vaughn flyout and Gary Sánchez walked to load the bases. Chourio then ripped the single to center, scoring Yelich and Frelick to end it.

Milwaukee now leads the best-of-five series 2-0, while San Diego has yet to win.

St. Petersburg — Near No-Hitter in ALDS Game 1

Rasmussen Carries No-Hitter Into the Eighth as Rays Blank Yankees in ALDS Opener

Rays 1, Yankees 0 — Final

Drew Rasmussen struck out 10 and held the Yankees hitless until two outs in the eighth inning, and Jonathan Aranda's solo homer off Gerrit Cole was all Tampa Bay needed to take Game 1 of the AL Division Series. Bryan Baker closed it out with a perfect ninth.

The Game

The only run came in the third inning, when Aranda sent a Cole offering 417 feet to right-center at 107.1 mph. It was the first postseason hit of his career, and it stood up as the lone run of the night.

Rasmussen did the rest. He retired 15 batters in a row at one stretch, walked two, and threw 69 of his 101 pitches for strikes. His 10 strikeouts tied Tyler Glasnow's franchise record for a postseason game.

The no-hit bid ended with two outs in the eighth, when Austin Wells doubled down the right-field line. Wells was thrown out trying to stretch it to third, ending the inning and the Yankees' only real threat. Rasmussen's night was done after eight innings.

Baker took over in the ninth and struck out pinch-hitter Giancarlo Stanton, froze Ben Rice with a changeup for a called third strike, and got Cody Bellinger on a grounder for his 42nd save of the season. Cole went five innings, allowing one run on five hits, and Yankees relievers Weathers, Blackburn and Headrick combined for three scoreless innings. Tampa Bay leads the best-of-five series 1-0.

Game played Thursday, October 1, 2026
Atlanta — Winner-Take-All Wild Card Game 3

Harris's First-Inning Blast Sets the Tone as Braves Bury Phillies in Game 3

Braves 6, Phillies 2 — Final

Michael Harris II hit a three-run homer in the first inning, and Atlanta added two more long balls to eliminate Philadelphia in the decisive Game 3 of the NL Wild Card Series at Truist Park. The Braves, who advance to a Division Series against the Dodgers, won a postseason series for the first time since their 2021 World Series run.

The Game

The night was largely decided before the Phillies recorded their third out. With Ronald Acuña Jr. and Matt Olson aboard in the bottom of the first, Harris jumped on a 93.5-mph Aaron Nola sinker and sent it 386 feet to right-center for a 3-0 lead. Nola lasted 1 2/3 innings, charged with three runs on two hits and two walks.

Philadelphia turned to Zack Wheeler out of the bullpen, and he steadied things briefly. But in the fourth, with Austin Riley aboard, Ozzie Albies turned on a 96.5-mph Wheeler fastball and drove it 419 feet to right-center for a two-run homer and a 5-0 edge.

Meanwhile, Atlanta's Ray Kerr shut the door on the Phillies' lineup, throwing 3 1/3 scoreless innings on 47 pitches. He allowed one hit and one walk, both to Kyle Schwarber. The Phillies finally broke the shutout on a Schwarber single, a Bryce Harper double and an Alec Bohm RBI groundout, and Olson answered in the seventh with a solo homer off Jonathan Bowlan to restore the five-run margin at 6-1.

Derek Hill led off the eighth with a triple and scored on a Trea Turner sacrifice fly to make it 6-2. With two outs, manager Walt Weiss summoned Chris Sale, who struck out Harper on four pitches and then fanned Bryan De La Cruz and pinch-hitter Otto Kemp in the ninth. Sale, the Game 1 starter, threw 21 pitches and got the final four outs, ending the Phillies' season.

Atlanta — Extra-Innings Comeback With a Season on the Line

Bohm's Two-Out Homer in the 10th Keeps Phillies Alive, Forces Game 3

Phillies 4, Braves 3 — Final (10 innings)

Alec Bohm's two-out solo home run in the top of the 10th lifted the Philadelphia Phillies to a 4-3 win over the Atlanta Braves in Game 2 of the NL Wild Card Series. Back-to-back eighth-inning homers from Kyle Schwarber and Bryce Harper had erased a 3-1 deficit, and the win forces a decisive Game 3.

The Game

Both teams scored in the first, and then pitching took over. Tyler Mahle held Philadelphia to one run over seven innings with six strikeouts, and Cristopher Sánchez matched him for much of the night as the game stayed tied at 1-1.

The turning point looked like it came in the seventh. Drake Baldwin's drive to right glanced off the glove of Harper, who had run past the ball and tried to jump back to make the play. Sean Murphy scored on what was ruled an RBI double, and Atlanta went on to take a 3-1 lead.

Harper answered an inning later. Schwarber led off the eighth with a 447-foot homer off Braves setup man Dylan Lee, and Harper followed with a 418-foot shot to tie it at 3-3. They were the first two homers Lee had allowed to left-handed batters all season.

With two out in the 10th, Bohm drove a 357-foot homer to right off Didier Fuentes (0-1) for the go-ahead run. Andrew Painter, in his postseason debut, worked scoreless relief and ended the game with a 99-mph fastball that an automated ball-strike challenge confirmed as strike three. Philadelphia is 1-1 in the series, and Zack Wheeler is lined up for Game 3.

Atlanta — Late Comeback in a Wild Card Opener

Riley's Eighth-Inning Blast Off Duran Flips Wild Card Opener for Braves

Braves 5, Phillies 3 — Final

Austin Riley turned on a 99.9 mph fastball from All-Star closer Jhoan Duran and sent it over the right-center wall for a two-out, three-run homer in the eighth, lifting the Atlanta Braves past the Philadelphia Phillies 5-3 in Game 1 of the NL Wild Card Series. The Braves were one win from the Division Series after trailing 3-2 entering the inning.

The Game

Philadelphia struck first in the opening inning, when Kyle Schwarber walked and Alec Bohm doubled home a run with two outs. Atlanta answered on the very next pitch, a game-tying single up the middle by Mike Yastrzemski, and the game settled into a duel between Braves ace Chris Sale and Phillies starter Jesús Luzardo, who was making his first start in 22 days after a shoulder issue.

Luzardo got through five innings on 77 pitches, and Sale kept Philadelphia at bay, at one point touching 100 mph on Bryce Harper in the first. Ozzie Albies tripled home Michael Harris II in the sixth to give Atlanta a 2-1 lead, and Harper's long fly ball narrowly missed the foul pole in the sixth before Sale struck him out.

The Phillies took the lead in the seventh. With two outs, J.T. Realmuto lined a two-run triple into the right-field corner off reliever Didier Fuentes, making it 3-2. Interim manager Don Mattingly went to Duran, his closer, in the seventh inning with a one-run lead, and Duran retired the first two Braves in the eighth on three pitches.

Then it unraveled. Harris singled and Albies was hit by a pitch, and after a review delay Duran's first pitch was a 99.9 mph fastball in the middle of the plate. Riley launched it just over the fence for the go-ahead runs. Dylan Lee worked a scoreless eighth for the win and Raisel Iglesias pitched the ninth for the save, giving Atlanta a 1-0 lead in the best-of-three series.
