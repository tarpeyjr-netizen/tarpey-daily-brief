Los Angeles — Pennant Clinched, Comeback Sweep

Brewers Erase Ohtani's Four-Run Lead, Sweep Dodgers Into the World Series

Milwaukee Brewers 6, Los Angeles Dodgers 4 — Final (Brewers win NLCS, 4-0)

Milwaukee overcame a 4-0 deficit at Dodger Stadium, scoring in four different innings and sealing it with a ninth-inning home run from David Hamilton, to finish a four-game sweep of the Dodgers in the National League Championship Series. Aaron Ashby closed it out.

The Game

The Dodgers looked in command early. In the second, Los Angeles took a run on an Andy Pages single, and then Shohei Ohtani sent a fly ball to right-center for a three-run home run, making it 4-0. Brewers starter Shane Drohan went one inning, and Dustin May took the blow that frame.

Milwaukee answered in the fourth. Jake Bauers hit a ground-rule double that scored Brice Turang and William Contreras to cut the lead to 4-2. In the fifth, David Hamilton singled in Sal Frelick and Bauers drew a bases-loaded walk to force in another, tying the game at 4-4. Dodgers starter Tyler Glasnow struck out six but allowed four runs over 4.1 innings.

Garrett Mitchell then put the Brewers in front with a sixth-inning home run to center. Hamilton added insurance with a solo shot to right-center in the ninth, and Ashby shut the door for the save. Los Angeles did not score after the second inning; JoJo Romero earned the win and Tanner Scott took the loss.

The result completes a sweep of the NLCS: Milwaukee won 2-1, 7-4, 6-2 and 6-4, and advances to the World Series.

Game played Wednesday, October 14, 2026
Los Angeles · Back-to-Back Homers Put Brewers One Win From the World Series

Bauers, Mitchell Go Back-to-Back in the Seventh as Brewers Push Dodgers to the Brink

Brewers 6, Dodgers 2 — Final (Milwaukee leads NLCS 3-0)

Jake Bauers followed an 11-pitch at-bat with a go-ahead two-run homer off Blake Snell in the seventh, and Garrett Mitchell homered right behind him as the Milwaukee Brewers beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 6-2 in Game 3 of the NLCS. The Brewers now lead the series 3-0 and are one win from their first World Series since 1982.

The Game

Jackson Chourio set the tone on the game's first pitch, sending a leadoff home run off Snell for a 1-0 Milwaukee lead. From there it became a pitchers' duel. Snell retired 14 straight batters at one stretch, and Brewers starter Robert Gasser kept the Dodgers off the board through five innings.

Los Angeles broke through in the sixth. Gasser gave way to Abner Uribe, who walked three, and Will Smith doubled off the top of the left-center-field wall to score two and put the Dodgers up 2-1. DL Hall came on and stranded both runners.

The seventh turned it. Andrew Vaughn drew a leadoff walk, and Bauers worked an 11-pitch at-bat, fouling off several pitches before driving a fastball to right field for a 3-2 lead. Mitchell, also a left-handed hitter, followed with a solo shot to make it 4-2.

Milwaukee added to it in the eighth, when Brice Turang scored on a Justin Wrobleski wild pitch after an infield single and a William Contreras single. Joey Ortiz's two-strike RBI single in the ninth made it 6-2, and Trevor Megill recorded the final outs.

The Dodgers, who went 3-for-29 with runners in scoring position earlier this postseason, now face long odds: only the 2004 Red Sox have ever come back from 3-0 down in a best-of-seven series. Los Angeles swept Milwaukee in last year's NLCS, and a Brewers win in Game 4 Thursday (Tyler Glasnow starting for the Dodgers) would return the favor.

Game played Tuesday, October 13, 2026
St. Petersburg — Postseason Pitching Duel Evens the ALCS

Peralta Deals, Rays Pounce on One Guardians Error to Level ALCS

Rays 3, Guardians 1 — Final

Freddy Peralta worked six scoreless innings and Tampa Bay turned a fourth-inning Cleveland error into three runs, beating the Guardians 3-1 at Tropicana Field to even the American League Championship Series at one game apiece. It was the only game on the day's schedule, and a tight, low-scoring pitching contest was its best storyline.

The Game

Through three innings, ALCS Game 2 was a quiet affair, with Peralta and Cleveland left-hander Parker Messick trading zeroes. Tampa Bay broke through in the bottom of the fourth after Junior Caminero drew a walk and was erased on a force play, with the Rays unsuccessfully challenging the call at first base.

Two outs later, Chandler Simpson singled to put runners at the corners and Ben Williamson reached when third baseman José Ramírez booted his grounder, allowing Victor Mesa Jr. to score. Liam Hicks then lined a single to center that brought home Simpson and Williamson, and the Rays led 3-0. None of the three runs was earned.

Cleveland threatened late. Patrick Bailey walked on an overturned pitch challenge in the seventh and, with Kevin Kelly on the mound, came around to score on Chase DeLauter's double for the Guardians' only run. Ian Seymour then struck out Jo Adell to strand a runner on third.

From there, Tampa Bay's bullpen closed the door. Seymour worked into the eighth, Bryan Baker retired the Guardians in order in the ninth for the save, and Cleveland finished with nine men left on base. The series now stays in St. Petersburg with the teams tied 1-1.

Game played Monday, October 12, 2026
Milwaukee — Three Comebacks in One Night, and a 2-0 NLCS Lead

Contreras Rallies Brewers Three Times to Bury Dodgers in NLCS Game 2

Brewers 7, Dodgers 4 — Final

The Milwaukee Brewers overturned three separate deficits at American Family Field on Monday, beating the Los Angeles Dodgers 7-4 to take a 2-0 lead in the National League Championship Series. Catcher William Contreras delivered a game-tying homer and a go-ahead single, and a Brewers bullpen led by Aaron Ashby's five-out save did the rest.

The Game

Milwaukee chased Dodgers ace Yoshinobu Yamamoto after just eight outs, the shortest postseason start of his career. Jake Bauers worked a 12-pitch walk in the third, and Christian Yelich followed with a two-out, two-run single to give the Brewers their first lead.

Los Angeles did not stay down. In the fifth the Dodgers retook the lead, helped by a second throwing error from Brewers starter Logan Henderson, and Freddie Freeman's sacrifice fly off Dustin May gave them their third lead of the night.

Contreras answered each time. He tied the game with a 436-foot homer to straightaway center off Kris Bubic, then later put Milwaukee ahead for good with a single to left. In the eighth, Brice Turang and Bauers each drove in a run with a single to make it 7-4.

Four Brewers relievers, beginning with May, combined for five perfect innings, the most in a postseason game in history. Ashby recorded a five-out save, ending it by striking out Miguel Rojas. Milwaukee relievers have allowed one earned run across six postseason games. Teams leading a best-of-seven postseason series 2-0 have won it 79 of 95 times (83%). Game 3 is Wednesday in Los Angeles.

Game played Sunday, October 11, 2026
Milwaukee — NLCS Game 1, a One-Run Opener Settled by Small Ball

Turang Homer, Lara’s Go-Ahead Single and a Shutdown Bullpen Give Brewers NLCS Opener

Milwaukee Brewers 2, Los Angeles Dodgers 1 — Final (Brewers lead NLCS 1–0)

Brice Turang tied it with a solo homer in the fourth, and Luis Lara’s single in the fifth drove in the go-ahead run as Milwaukee took Game 1 of the NLCS over Los Angeles at American Family Field. Four Brewers relievers combined to hold the Dodgers scoreless over the final 4⅓ innings, with Trevor Megill closing it out.

The Game

It was the only completed MLB game on the Sunday slate, and a one-run postseason opener between the 103-win Brewers and the 100-win Dodgers was the clear pick. There is no bigger storyline to manufacture here: this was a tight game decided by a handful of plays.

Los Angeles struck first in the second. Shohei Ohtani singled and Teoscar Hernández moved him to second, and Andy Pages singled to right to bring Ohtani home for a 1–0 lead.

Turang answered in the bottom of the fourth, sending a Tarik Skubal pitch 384 feet to left-center to make it 1–1. In the fifth, Garrett Mitchell walked and was bunted to second by Cooper Pratt, and Lara singled to center to score him. Milwaukee loaded the pressure further with an infield single by Jackson Chourio and a walk to Turang, but Skubal got William Contreras to fly out to end the inning, and the Brewers led 2–1.

From there the bullpen took over. DL Hall relieved starter Jacob Misiorowski with a runner on in the fifth and got Freddie Freeman to ground into a double play. Abner Uribe, Aaron Ashby and Megill followed. Los Angeles put runners on in the sixth and again in the ninth, when Kyle Tucker and pinch-hitter Josué De Paula drew walks against Megill, but Mookie Betts flied out to center to end it.

The Dodgers outhit Milwaukee 7–6 and left several men on base, while the Brewers survived their own bases-loaded jam in the eighth, when Tanner Scott got Mitchell to ground out to short after two intentional walks.

Cleveland — Winner-Take-All Game 5, a Pitching Duel Decided by One Swing

Ramírez’s Fourth-Inning Homer and a Bullpen Wall Send Guardians Past White Sox

Cleveland Guardians 2, Chicago White Sox 0 — Final (Guardians win ALDS 3–2)

José Ramírez’s two-run homer in the fourth inning was all the offense Cleveland needed in a decisive ALDS Game 5 at Progressive Field. Gavin Williams struck out 12 over 5⅓ innings, and Cade Smith closed out the final 1⅔ innings as the Guardians advanced.

The Game

No game on the Saturday slate had more at stake: one game, one trip to the next round. There was no other completed MLB game on the day, and this winner-take-all finale was the clear pick. Both starters kept it scoreless through three innings.

The break came in the bottom of the fourth. Steven Kwan led off with a single to right, and Ramírez followed with a line drive to right field, 348 feet according to MLB’s tracking, that scored Kwan ahead of him and made it 2–0. Chase DeLauter flew out, Jo Adell singled, and Nathaniel Lowe and Travis Bazzana ended the inning, but the damage was done.

From there the Cleveland pitchers took over. Williams, working on 94 pitches, allowed one hit and walked two before giving way in the sixth. Erik Sabrowski recorded one out, and Hunter Gaddis covered 1⅓ innings that included a one-out single by Sam Antonacci in the eighth.

Cleveland had a chance to add on in the eighth. Petey Halpin doubled and Kwan walked, and Chicago intentionally walked Ramírez to load the bases, but a force at home on DeLauter’s grounder and an Adell groundout kept it at 2–0. It did not matter. Smith struck out Munetaka Murakami, Andrew Benintendi and Tristan Peters in the ninth, all swinging, to finish it.

The White Sox finished with three hits and stranded six, and Sean Burke took the loss after allowing both runs over five innings.

Six-Run Sixth · Series Tied 2–2

Guardians Hang Six in the Sixth to Force a Game 5 in Chicago

Guardians 9, White Sox 5 — Final

Cleveland trailed 4–3 entering the sixth inning of ALDS Game 4 at Rate Field, then sent 10 men to the plate, strung together a double and four straight singles, and scored six runs to even the best-of-five series at two games apiece. The decider is next.

The Game

Chicago opened with Hagen Smith, who walked three in two-thirds of an inning before Erick Fedde came on with two outs in the first and kept the Guardians quiet through the early innings. The White Sox broke through in the third on a bases-loaded walk to Munetaka Murakami and a sacrifice fly by Sam Antonacci to lead 2–0.

Cleveland answered in the fifth. Patrick Bailey homered on a line drive to right field, and one out later José Ramírez launched a two-run shot to right-center to put the Guardians up 3–2. Chicago immediately took the lead back in the bottom half, scoring on an Antonacci hit-by-pitch and a Brenton Doyle sacrifice fly to go up 4–3.

The sixth turned the game. Travis Bazzana doubled in the tying run, Bailey singled in the go-ahead run, Ramírez added an RBI single, Chase DeLauter followed with another, and Jo Adell’s two-run single made it 9–4. Chicago’s bullpen allowed six runs on seven hits in the inning.

Miguel Vargas homered in the ninth to make it 9–5, but Chicago stranded 12 runners over the game and never got closer. Cleveland used eight pitchers; Erik Sabrowski earned the win with a scoreless inning in relief after Tim Herrin was charged with a blown save, and Anthony Kay took the loss.
