San Francisco — Extra-Inning Comeback

Astros Erase Late Deficit, Bury Giants With Three-Run 10th

Houston Astros 6, San Francisco Giants 3 — Final/10

Jeremy Peña's go-ahead single and Isaac Paredes' two-run double turned a tense ninth-inning gut check into a laugher of a 10th, lifting the Houston Astros to a 6-3 win over the San Francisco Giants at Oracle Park. Houston trailed for most of the night before Cam Smith's two-out RBI single off closer Dylan Smith forced extra innings, and Josh Hader made sure the rally stuck.

The Game

San Francisco controlled the middle innings behind Blade Tidwell, who worked 5 2/3 sharp innings and struck out six. Jung Hoo Lee's solo homer in the fifth staked the Giants to a lead, and after Cam Smith tied it with a solo shot in the seventh, Drew Gilbert answered right back with a go-ahead home run of his own in the bottom half to put San Francisco up 3-2.

Houston starter Hunter Wesneski battled through seven innings, allowing both Giants homers among his three earned runs, and handed a one-run deficit to the bullpen. The Astros looked headed for a quiet road loss until the ninth, when Smith — the Astros' right fielder, no relation to San Francisco's closer — lined a two-out single off Dylan Smith to knot the score at 3-3 and send the game to extras.

The turning point came in the top of the 10th. With the automatic runner at second, LaMonte Wade Jr. and a wild pitch from Jason Foley advanced the runner to third, and Peña promptly lined a go-ahead single. Paredes followed with a two-run double to blow the game open, and Foley was charged with all three runs in the loss.

Josh Hader slammed the door with two perfect innings spanning the ninth and 10th, striking out two and improving to 4-1, sealing Houston's second straight extra-inning win of the week and its fourth win in five games.

Game played Wednesday, August 5, 2026
Milestone Win — Rookie's Near No-Hit Bid

Bratt's One-Hit Gem Downs the Ace Arizona Wanted

Diamondbacks 10, Padres 4 — Final

Mitch Bratt struck out nine and surrendered just one hit over a career-high seven innings Wednesday night, and the rookie left-hander's first career win couldn't have come against a more pointed opponent: Casey Mize, the very starter Arizona's front office chased at the trade deadline before he landed in San Diego instead. The Diamondbacks piled on for a 10-4 win at Chase Field, tightening their grip on the National League's second wild-card spot.

The Game

Making his sixth big league start, all of them this season, Bratt worked around three walks but never let a Padres hitter find any comfort. San Diego's only hit of the night stood alone against a career night that included personal bests in innings, strikeouts and, in the best possible sense, hits allowed. His ERA dipped to 3.90, and he strengthened his case in an ongoing battle with fellow rookie left-hander Kohl Drake for a rotation spot ahead of Michael Soroka's expected return this weekend.

Arizona's offense made sure Bratt had all the support he would need. Gabriel Moreno lifted a sacrifice fly in the third, and four pitches later Tim Tawa sent a 414-foot drive into the left-center bleachers for his sixth homer of the season. The floodgates opened in the fourth: a Tyler Locklear ground-rule double set up four consecutive singles, and trade-deadline pickup Lars Nootbaar delivered his first hit and RBI as a Diamondback in the middle of it. Tawa added a second sacrifice fly and Ildemaro Vargas ripped a two-run double before Moreno's bases-loaded walk finally chased Mize — a six-run inning that put Arizona up 8-0 and buried the Padres' new starter under a season-high eight earned runs.

Vargas wasn't finished. His two-run triple in the eighth pushed the lead to 10-0 before San Diego mounted token resistance in the ninth. Fernando Tatis Jr. led off with a solo home run, and Gage Workman followed with a three-run double off reliever Kevin Ginkel, who allowed two hits and two walks before Paul Sewald arrived to record the final out and preserve the 10-4 final.

The result carried extra weight beyond the box score. Arizona general manager Mike Hazen spent Monday's trade deadline trying and failing to land a front-line starter, missing out on names like Clay Holmes and Jose Soriano — and on Mize, who instead went to San Diego, Arizona's direct rival for the NL's final wild-card spot. That Bratt, a stopgap rotation piece by his own team's estimation, outpitched the arm Arizona had coveted only sharpened the storyline.

The win pushed the Diamondbacks to 16-7 over their last 23 games, gave them a 5-4 edge in this year's season series with the Padres, and restored a two-game cushion in the wild-card race — with just three head-to-head meetings left, all coming in the final series of the regular season in late September.

Game played Tuesday, August 4, 2026
Blockbuster Debut Spoiled

Hoerner, Cubs Crash Skubal's Dodgers Debut as Wrigley Boos the New Villains

Chicago Cubs 5, Los Angeles Dodgers 1 — Final

CHICAGO — Tarik Skubal's first night in Dodger blue had everything but a happy ending. Days after the biggest trade of the deadline sent the two-time AL Cy Young winner from Detroit to Los Angeles, the Cubs spoiled his debut 5-1 at Wrigley Field, handing the slumping Dodgers a fifth consecutive loss behind Nico Hoerner's go-ahead infield single and late solo homer.

The Game

The circus arrived before the first pitch. A plane circled over Wrigley trailing a banner that read "THE DODGERS RUINED BASEBALL" — apparently flown upside down — and a crowd of 40,584 booed Skubal at introductions and again when he took the mound. Kyle Tucker, jeered all night in his return to Chicago after signing a four-year, $240 million deal with Los Angeles, answered first, driving a 2-1 Javier Assad sinker to left-center in the second for his 10th homer and a 1-0 lead.

Skubal, meanwhile, looked every bit the ace the Dodgers traded for. He retired his first six hitters, three by strikeout, with his fastball averaging 97.3 mph and touching 99.3. But Dansby Swanson jumped on his first pitch of the third and sent it deep into the left-center bleachers to tie the game, and Wrigley roared back to life.

The game turned on a 90-foot dribbler. With two out and runners at the corners in the sixth, Hoerner chopped one high off the infield grass to the right of the mound; by the time the Dodgers corralled it, Alex Bregman had scampered home for a 2-1 Cubs lead. It was the only unearned mercy Skubal needed none of — both runs against him were earned on just four hits.

Chicago pulled away against the bullpen. Pete Crow-Armstrong and Seiya Suzuki lined RBI singles off Alex Vesia in a two-run seventh, and Hoerner capped the night with a solo homer off Edwin Díaz in the eighth. The Dodgers managed four hits in all, their fifth straight defeat — the franchise's longest skid since early September last year — even as they cling to the NL West lead at 69-45. The Cubs, second in the NL Central at 65-49, have won three of four.

Game played Monday, August 3, 2026
Three-Homer Night

Burleson's Three-Homer Blitz Powers Cardinals Past Rallying Yankees

Cardinals 13, Yankees 7 — Final

Alec Burleson launched three two-run home runs off three different Yankees pitchers, the last one capping a seven-run eighth inning, as the St. Louis Cardinals outslugged the New York Yankees 13-7 at Yankee Stadium on Monday night. The Cardinals blew a 6-0 lead before rallying past a Yankees squad that had briefly gone ahead 7-6.

The Game

The night began with Burleson learning his own fate. About an hour before first pitch, on the day the Cardinals traded outfielder Lars Nootbaar to Arizona and pitchers Dustin May and JoJo Romero to Milwaukee, manager Oliver Marmol called Burleson into his office, briefly let him think he'd been dealt too, then told him he was staying. Burleson responded with the best power night of his five-year career.

He wasted no time making it memorable, pulling a first-inning fastball from Yankees starter Cam Schlittler into the right-field seats for a two-run shot. St. Louis kept adding on — a José Fermín double and a Nathan Church single made it 4-0 — before Burleson turned on an 0-1 sinker from reliever Tim Hill in the fourth for his second two-run homer and a 6-0 Cardinals cushion.

New York clawed back on the strength of the long ball. Austin Wells and Trent Grisham went deep in the fifth to cut the deficit to 6-3, and the Yankees kept chipping away before newcomer Luis García Jr. — acquired from Washington that same afternoon — put New York in front 7-6 with a two-run homer in the seventh.

The lead didn't last. St. Louis erupted for seven runs in the eighth, all of it against reliever Angel Chivilli, as rookies Nathan Church and Jimmie Crooks each went deep before Burleson fouled off three straight pitches and then drove a 2-2 slider 418 feet into the second deck in right for his third homer of the night and a 13-6 lead. Gordon Graceffo shut the door from there, retiring New York's final six batters to secure the win.

The teams combined for 10 home runs, with 18 of the game's 20 runs scoring via the long ball. Burleson became the 15th player in Cardinals history with a three-homer game, the first Cardinals first baseman to do it since Yankees slugger Paul Goldschmidt in 2023, and just the eighth visiting player to do it at the current Yankee Stadium. St. Louis (56-57) won consecutive games for the first time since mid-July; New York (63-50) remained in second place in the AL East.
