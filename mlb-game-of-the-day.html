Milwaukee · World Series Game 1 Opener

Brewers Survive Ninth-Inning Scare, Steal World Series Opener From Rays

Brewers 3, Rays 2 — Final

Milwaukee took Game 1 of the 2026 World Series at American Family Field, holding off Tampa Bay after the Rays loaded the bases in the ninth. Trevor Megill struck out Jonny DeLuca for the final out to earn the save.

The Game

Christian Yelich tripled home Jackson Chourio in the first inning, and William Contreras followed with an RBI to make it 2-0 before Tampa Bay settled in. Flamethrower Jacob Misiorowski, who opened the night with a 103.5 mph first pitch, went five innings and allowed one run on four hits.

The Rays got on the board in the fourth, when Junior Caminero doubled and Jonathan Aranda scored on a groundout to cut the lead to 2-1. Contreras then took over behind the plate, throwing out Victor Mesa Jr. at third in the fifth and catching Aranda stealing second in the sixth to end a Rays hit-and-run. Manager Pat Murphy called Contreras his player of the game.

Chourio's single in the seventh scored Cooper Pratt for an insurance run, and it proved necessary. In the ninth the Rays loaded the bases against Megill. Brice Turang began what looked like a game-ending 4-6-3 double play, but replay showed Pratt's foot was off the bag and the out was overturned. Megill rebounded to strike out DeLuca and end it.

Tampa Bay finished 0-for-6 with runners in scoring position. Milwaukee leads the series 1-0.

Game played Sunday, October 18, 2026
Cleveland — ALCS Game 5: Rasmussen Stifles Guardians, Rays Take 3-2 Series Lead

Rasmussen Goes Seven Strong, Rays Build Early Lead and Move One Win From the World Series

Tampa Bay 3, Cleveland 1 — Final

Drew Rasmussen allowed one run over seven innings and Tampa Bay scored all three of its runs in the first three innings to beat Cleveland 3-1 at Progressive Field. The Rays now lead the American League Championship Series three games to two. It was the only MLB game completed Sunday, a makeup of the postponed Friday game.

The Game

Tampa Bay jumped on Guardians starter Parker Messick right away. In the first inning, Jonny DeLuca drew a bases-loaded walk — after Cleveland catcher Patrick Bailey challenged the ball-four call and the call was confirmed — to score Yandy Díaz. Jonathan Aranda singled in Ben Williamson in the second, and Liam Hicks singled in Chandler Simpson in the third to make it 3-0.

That was all the Rays needed. Messick was gone after two innings and 57 pitches, having allowed five hits, two earned runs and two walks, and Cleveland's relievers — Tim Herrin, Shawn Armstrong, Erik Sabrowski, Hunter Gaddis and Cade Smith — combined to allow one run over the final seven innings. Tampa Bay still stranded nine runners.

Cleveland got on the board in the sixth. Steven Kwan scored on a Chase DeLauter groundout, but the Guardians managed only five hits all night and left five men on base. After Rasmussen left, Cam Booser and Tyler Wells covered the eighth and Bryan Baker worked a scoreless ninth for the save, getting Nathaniel Lowe to pop out and Travis Bazzana to ground out to end it.

The Rays took the lead in a series that had been tied 2-2 after Saturday's Game 4, and now hold a 3-2 edge.

Game played Saturday, October 17, 2026
Cleveland — ALCS Game 4: Rays Rally Late to Even Series

Rays Break Open a Tight ALCS Game 4 in the Seventh, Then Diaz Seals It With a Ninth-Inning Homer

Tampa Bay 7, Cleveland 3 — Final

Tampa Bay erupted for three runs in the seventh inning, helped by a Chase DeLauter fielding error, and Yandy Díaz added a two-run homer in the ninth to beat Cleveland 7-3 at Progressive Field. The win squares the American League Championship Series at two games apiece.

The Game

Cleveland struck first when Steven Kwan scored on a first-inning single by DeLauter, but DeLauter was thrown out at second to end the inning. Tampa Bay answered in the third on Jonathan Aranda's single that scored Díaz, and Junior Caminero's sixth-inning double scored Aranda to put the Rays ahead 2-1.

The game turned in the seventh. Aranda singled to right, Richie Palacios and Taylor Walls scored, and DeLauter's fielding error let Aranda reach third. Chandler Simpson's sacrifice fly made it 5-1.

Cleveland fought back in the bottom half. Travis Bazzana homered to right, and Brayan Rocchio singled in Patrick Bailey to cut the deficit to 5-3. But the Rays' bullpen held from there, and Díaz's two-run shot in the ninth, which also scored Palacios, closed it out. Bryan Baker worked a scoreless ninth.

The series is now tied 2-2, with the winner of the next game one step from the World Series.
