Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · Europe

United Kingdom

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

Britain is under its newest prime minister: Andy Burnham took over from Keir Starmer on 20 July, promising a “new political and economic model.” His cabinet reshuffle removed Rachel Reeves and installed John Healey as Chancellor. One of the first moves reported was the scrapping of Starmer’s nationwide digital ID scheme, estimated to have cost £1.8 billion over three years. Last week’s Budget was Healey’s first, delivered on 28 October.

Economy & Business

Markets took Healey’s first Budget calmly: Reuters-based coverage reports help for households and drivers facing higher energy costs, higher taxes on banks, and £112 billion of borrowing this financial year, in line with forecasts, while a pre-statement leak of the bank-tax details drew a complaint from the Treasury Committee chair. Accounts differ on the size of the gilt move; one report has 10-year yields down as much as 4 basis points to 5.30%. Inflation is the bigger worry: CPI rose to 3.5% in September from 3.1%. The Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% in September on a 6–3 vote, is expected by most economists in a Reuters poll to raise it by 25 basis points on Thursday, 5 November.

Tourism

Bonfire Night on 5 November is the next big domestic draw, with large organised displays in London, Leeds, Edinburgh and Birmingham and the torch-lit processions of Lewes in East Sussex the best-known of all, per a visitor guide. The same guide notes that visa-free overseas visitors now need a UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (£20) before travelling, with enforcement in place since 25 February 2026; check GOV.UK for the official position.

Site to visit: Stonehenge in Wiltshire is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world’s best-known prehistoric monuments, a stone circle whose main phase dates to roughly 2500 BC. Timed tickets are booked in advance through English Heritage, and the site is reached by road from Salisbury or Amesbury; check the site for current prices and winter hours.

🔥 Trending

Indie band / music · United Kingdom

Risco first broke through with a run of viral TikTok covers that reached millions of listeners, then moved the momentum offline by touring the UK grassroots circuit. The band has since drawn backing from the PRS Foundation, and an EP is listed as due at the end of 2026. It fits a wider trend toward grassroots venues and promoters nurturing new acts, as described in a January Skiddle feature. (The band’s home city was not stated in the sources found.)

Monday, November 2, 2026 · South America

Argentina

South America · population ~46M

Politics & Government

Prosecutors have appealed the dismissal of the $LIBRA cryptocurrency case against President Javier Milei, his sister Karina Milei and nine others, arguing that fund tracing and communications evidence remain unexamined; the decision now sits with Federal Court No. 8. Relations with Brazil are strained after Milei stayed silent on Lula's re-election while other right-wing regional leaders offered congratulations. The friction deepened when the government confirmed that Flávio Bolsonaro crossed at Puerto Iguazú before flying to Miami, prompting opposition demands for a report to Congress. Abroad, Argentina was criticised for its stance at UN nature talks after voting against a General Assembly resolution condemning the US embargo on Cuba.

Economy & Business

The IMF urged Argentina to “stay the course” after staff reached agreement on the third review of the Extended Fund Facility, which would unlock about US$900 million if the board approves it in early December. The Fund cut its 2026 growth forecast from 3.5% to 2.1% while keeping a primary surplus target of 1.4% of GDP, and net reserves beat the June target by roughly US$4 billion. Four joint measures aim to turn stabilisation into growth, including deeper capital markets, labour flexibility and faster infrastructure concessions. Separately, Buenos Aires Times reports Argentina is eyeing the China swap line to cover 2027 financing needs.

Military & Security

Argentina's dispute with the United Kingdom over the Sea Lion oil project, about 200 km north of the Falklands, heads to the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea in Hamburg, with hearings set for November 10–11. Law of the sea expert Donald Rothwell warns the case could fail at the first hurdle because the tribunal cannot rule on sovereignty. At home, nearly 100 intellectuals, jurists and politicians signed a declaration rejecting the Falklands as a “national cause” that admits no dissent.

Society & Culture

Pope Leo XIV's South American tour begins in Montevideo on November 6, with an Argentine leg from November 8 to 11, the first papal visit since 1987. He is due to meet Milei at the Casa Rosada, visit the Devoto prison complex, travel to Córdoba and celebrate Mass at the Basilica of Our Lady of Luján, despite recovering from lung cancer surgery. The government has declared holidays tied to the visit, including a national holiday on Monday, November 9. A “Chalice of Peace” made from Malvinas War munitions is among the gifts being prepared.

Tourism

The papal visit is shaping travel across the country: reporting indicates a holiday on November 9, with November 10 observed in Córdoba and Buenos Aires City and November 11 in Buenos Aires Province, creating a long weekend. Lodging demand around the events is reported to be high, though no official occupancy figures have been published. Before the visit, inbound tourism was already growing, with foreign arrivals up 8.8% year on year in the first half of 2026.

Site to visit: Iguazú National Park in the north-east is a UNESCO World Heritage site where a network of some 275 waterfalls spills along the Argentina–Brazil border, including the thunderous Garganta del Diablo (Devil's Throat). Most visitors fly to Puerto Iguazú, and the park's walkways are best visited early in the day; foreign-visitor entry fees are set in pesos and change often, so check the park's current rate before going.

Science & Technology

At the COP17 biodiversity talks in Yerevan, diplomats accused Argentina, Russia and Saudi Arabia of resisting scientific assessments from the UN-backed IPBES linking biodiversity loss, climate change and economic drivers. A French delegate likened the dispute to earlier fights at the IPCC, and one developed-country negotiator described a clear alliance aimed at weakening the reports.

🔥 Trending
The “therian” youth movement
Youth subculture · Buenos Aires

Teenagers who say they identify with non-human animals have turned Buenos Aires plazas into weekend gatherings, with masks, tails and pack-style meetups organised through TikTok. AP reported in February that the #therian hashtag had passed 2 million posts, with Argentina leading Latin American engagement, and one teenage “pack” leader had more than 125,000 followers. Regional outlets have since reported calls for a first meetup in Córdoba, suggesting the scene is spreading beyond the capital. A Buenos Aires psychologist told AP the behaviour is largely symbolic but warned of risks if the identification becomes fully rooted.

Sunday, November 1, 2026 · North America

Canada

North America · population ~38M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted EU leaders in Montreal this week, saying Canada is seeking to become the bloc's first associate member, although a draft joint statement had dropped the words "associate membership". The summit ended with the announcement of an "Alliance for the Future" that is open to other like-minded countries. Carney also said Ottawa is finding "solutions" on a pending EU carbon tariff. Separately, he said Canada is not in a "position right now" to agree to a U.S. trade deal as talks intensify ahead of the U.S. midterms.

Economy & Business

The Bank of Canada raised its benchmark rate a quarter-point to 2.5 per cent on Wednesday, its first increase since July 2023, citing high energy prices; the bank expects inflation near 3.5 per cent by the end of 2026. Two days later, Statistics Canada reported that GDP was flat in August and likely fell 0.1 per cent in September, though the third-quarter flash estimate is still 1.3 per cent annualized growth. Ottawa also announced it will invest up to $1.6 billion in Toronto-based AI company Cohere, while Industry Minister Mélanie Joly says she will meet Stelco's U.S. owner only after seeing a concrete plan to bring workers back to the Hamilton steel plant.

Military & Security

Ottawa released its first national security strategy in more than two decades, which pledges to ramp up intelligence-gathering and to "prepare for all contingencies up to and including war"; fuller defence and foreign policies are due in the coming months. Defence spending is already rising toward more than $163 billion by the middle of the next decade. However, the Auditor General found that the strategic ammunition stockpile cannot support war readiness or NATO commitments, blaming slow procurement and inventory management, and saying fixes will take several years despite a $9.2-billion funding pledge.

Society & Culture

Ottawa tabled new medical assistance in dying legislation that excludes people suffering solely from mental illness. As clocks fell back this weekend, CBC reported that more provinces are ending time changes, prompting debate over which option is better for health. In Toronto, Olivia Chow was elected to a second term as mayor.

Tourism

National parks, a core draw for visitors, face cuts: Parks Canada is set to close warden detachments and cut law-enforcement positions in British Columbia, Alberta, Ontario, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Yukon and the Northwest Territories, with at least a dozen positions affected. The agency also quietly ended a search and rescue dog program dating to the early 1970s that was heavily used in the mountain parks. Meanwhile, Canada's winter-sports calendar is under way, with short track speed skating's World Tour stopping in Montreal this weekend.

Site to visit: Banff National Park in Alberta, part of the UNESCO-listed Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks, is Canada's oldest national park, known for turquoise lakes and mountain scenery. Entry is $12.25 per adult per day, or $83.50 for an annual Discovery Pass; the town of Banff is roughly a 90-minute drive from Calgary.

Science & Technology

Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk conducted his first spacewalk on Thursday to install solar arrays outside the International Space Station, and afterward said the seven-hour outing and seeing Earth "changed" him. Researchers in Alberta also reported that 29 of 37 documented cougar deaths in a west-central Alberta study were caused by people.

🔥 Trending
Ursa 150 all-mechanical tractor
Agriculture / consumer phenomenon · Bowden, Alberta

A small parts and servicing shop in Bowden, Alberta, began building the Ursa 150, an all-mechanical tractor with no computer codes and no wait for dealer diagnostics, after a client's son struggled to afford equipment for the family farm. After it was featured in farm publications this summer, owner Wilson says he is fielding a flood of calls, including from non-farmers and from as far as South Africa, with one unit shipped to Florida. Production is planned at about 50 units over the next year at a shop with 10 employees, and Wilson sees the interest as a reaction to increasingly high-tech, manufacturer-tied machinery.

Saturday, October 31, 2026 · Europe

Iceland

Europe · population ~0.37M

Politics & Government

On October 28 the government approved a bill setting an age limit for children on social media and sent it on to parliament. Abroad, Iceland's foreign minister met China's Wang Yi in Beijing to discuss trade, geothermal cooperation, tourism and the war in Ukraine. Back home, Iceland Review reports that the cabinet has also published a 2027–31 law enforcement plan. Iceland Review also reports that the government is under continuing pressure on spending and unemployment, themes picked up in the Reykjavik Grapevine's October 3 news roundup.

Economy & Business

The Central Bank of Iceland left its key rate unchanged at 8% in early October after a run of 25bp hikes, with headline inflation near a two-year high of 5.9% in September. The labour confederation ASÍ now forecasts just 1.1% growth in 2027 and elevated unemployment through 2028. In corporate news, Spanish firm Boluda Shipping agreed to acquire logistics company Samskip, with completion expected at the start of 2027, and the Blue Lagoon approved a public offering and Nasdaq Iceland listing on October 31.

Military & Security

UN Disarmament Chief Izumi Nakamitsu visited Reykjavik to discuss nuclear risks, the arms race and military AI with the foreign minister. Earlier in October, Iceland Review headlined remarks by US Secretary of State Rubio that any US military expansion in Iceland would require Iceland's consent, a sign that defence ties with Washington remain a live topic. Separately, an Icelandic hosting provider declined a request to remove data identifying US ICE staff.

Society & Culture

Iceland's main hospital says it has cut emergency admission waiting times by 43 percent. Translator John Swedenmark won the 2026 Vigdis Prize for literary translation. Iceland is also absent from the Eurovision 2027 participant list, with its broadcaster yet to confirm participation. Homeowners can now apply to put supplementary pension contributions toward mortgage principal, with a December deadline for 2026 retroactive use.

Tourism

Tourism is holding roughly flat: Islandsbanki now expects visitor numbers in 2026 to be virtually the same as in 2025, and Keflavik Airport's own forecast points to about 2.24 million foreign tourists versus 2.27 million last year. The Blue Lagoon, one of the country's best-known attractions, is heading for a stock-market listing, and the foreign minister's Beijing talks included tourism. Travellers should note that autumn storms continue: a storm closed part of the Ring Road between Markarfljot and Vik on October 14, and yellow wind warnings covered the south on October 29.

Site to visit: Þingvellir National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site where the site of Iceland's first parliament, founded around 930, sits in a rift valley between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates. Entry is free and the paths and viewpoints are open year-round; the practical cost is parking, about 1,000 ISK per day for a car, payable online or at machines. It is roughly an hour's drive from Reykjavik on the Golden Circle.

Science & Technology

Energy policy is in the news as Landsvirkjun's chief executive challenges a competition authority fine and questions whether Icelandic electricity is too cheap. The Iceland-China talks also covered geothermal cooperation, an area where Iceland is a world leader. On the safety side, the Icelandic Met Office remains the key source for volcanic and weather alerts after a stormy October.

🔥 Trending
Spider Network collective
Music · Reykjavik

A Reykjavik collective of alternative artists, including MC Myasnoi and ronja, is being singled out as the new vanguard of the city's underground scene. The Line of Best Fit's preview of homegrown acts making their first Iceland Airwaves appearances highlights the group ahead of the festival, which runs in downtown Reykjavik in early November. The festival's lineup also features Shygirl, The Streets and Daði Freyr.

Friday, October 30, 2026 · South America

Peru

South America · population ~33M

Politics & Government

President Keiko Fujimori spent the week in El Salvador, where she met President Nayib Bukele and the two leaders agreed to deepen cooperation against organized crime. At home, Congress approved a supplementary credit to address El Niño and other urgent needs, while Fujimori announced 6 billion soles for school construction in 2027.

Economy & Business

Trade officials say Peru wants to exceed $140 billion in exports by 2040, noting that January–August 2026 exports reached $74.1 billion, up 34.3% year on year. The government also plans to relaunch hydroelectric plant tenders to draw private investment, and is seeking a double taxation agreement with Australia. Separately, an Oxfam analysis found Peru's richest captured 42 percent of all new wealth.

Military & Security

Crime dominated the security agenda: Prime Minister Galarreta said the government is firm in its decision to confront crime, and Fujimori's visit to El Salvador's CECOT mega-prison was framed by allies as a signal to criminal gangs. Last month Peru joined the US-led Shield of the Americas security alliance.

Society & Culture

Preparations are underway for Pope Leo XIV's November visit, with the Inca fortress of Sacsayhuamán set to host 75,000 people in Cusco and state broadcaster IRTP premiering a flash mob in tribute. Peruvian athletes with transplanted organs won 12 medals at the Pan American Games in Uruguay.

Tourism

Puno opened its 358th-anniversary celebrations with a colorful identity parade and plans to stage the legend of Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo on Lake Titicaca with more than 500 actors. In Ayacucho, a "Charming Passport" was launched to promote Sarhua, and the government is transferring $2.76 million to improve visitor services at Choquequirao.

Site to visit: Choquequirao is an archaeological park in the Cusco region, often called a “sister of Machu Picchu” for its extensive Inca stone city set high above the Apurímac canyon. It is far quieter than Machu Picchu because it has traditionally been reached on foot, so check current access and services before planning a trip, as new funding is aimed at improving them.

Science & Technology

Researchers discovered a new orchid species in the cloud forests of northern Peru. Meanwhile, officials warned that Lake Titicaca's water level has dropped 62 cm and the trend is expected to continue.

🔥 Trending
Q-pop (Lenin Tamayo)
Music / social media · Peru

Singer Lenin Tamayo became a social media phenomenon by fusing Korean-pop style with songs in Quechua, an approach dubbed “Q-pop.” His rise was covered by international media, and it sits within a wider grassroots revival of Peruvian popular music, including chicha. Note: searches did not surface a clearly dated October 2026 viral item, so this is a longer-running grassroots phenomenon rather than a this-week spike.

Thursday, October 29, 2026 · Oceania

Australia

Oceania · population ~26M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese ended a difficult parliamentary week bogged down in questions over a $6 million invitation-only grant to the Marrickville Golf Club, which he had defended as funding to fix sewage overflow into a local river; officials told Senate questioners that sewerage works were not part of the project, and Albanese later conceded the link “was not correct”. The Coalition has seized on the saga while Opposition figure Angus Taylor released costings for spending cuts. Separately, One Nation has refused the prime minister's offer of an extra parliamentary staffer, with Pauline Hanson accusing him of meddling in staffing allocations. In Tasmania, a Greens bill banning conversion practices passed the lower house.

Economy & Business

Annual inflation rose to 4.3 per cent in September from 4 per cent in August, driven by housing (up 5.2 per cent) and a 9.4 per cent jump in fuel prices; markets put the chance of a 0.25-point Reserve Bank hike at about 85 per cent ahead of its meeting next week. Treasurer Jim Chalmers has lowered expectations for further fuel discounts. Elsewhere, the failed float of AI data-centre firm Firmus has cast doubt on billions of dollars in AI chip commitments, and the federal government conditionally committed $180 million to the Donald rare earths and mineral sands mine in western Victoria, which local farmers oppose.

Military & Security

Security attention this week centred on the alleged attack on independent MP Helen Haines, who was hit by a vehicle outside her Wangaratta electorate office on 19 October and lost both legs; she has since come out of an induced coma and is stable. The man accused, Brendan Nugent, faces charges including intentionally causing serious injury and harming a Commonwealth public official, and Victoria Police are leading the investigation. An initial prison assessment explored delusions and possible psychosis but found no sign of ideological extremism. He remains in custody ahead of a court date in February.

Society & Culture

Hundreds of nurses and prison guards walked off the job in Canberra in what is described as the largest coordinated ACT public-sector strike since self-government in 1989. Doctors working in Northern Territory prisons say limited access to detainees is hindering care, while experts report a “systemic” rise in Canberra children with complex developmental needs. In the arts, Biennale of Sydney chief Barbara Moore is being made a knight of France's Order of Arts and Letters.

Tourism

Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport officially opened to passengers on 25 October with a Jetstar flight to the Gold Coast, and its first international flight, from Auckland, landed the next day. The curfew-free airport is intended to operate around the clock, though the planned Metro rail link was not running at launch, so visitors should arrange ground transport in advance. At Uluru, rangers and tour operators warn that invasive buffel grass is spreading around the base of the rock, threatening the landscape's World Heritage values.

Site to visit: Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park in the Northern Territory is a UNESCO World Heritage site, jointly managed with its Anangu traditional owners, and home to the great sandstone monolith Uluru and the domed rocks of Kata Tjuta. A park pass is required (multi-day passes cost roughly A$38 for adults; check Parks Australia for current prices), and the cooler months of May to September are the most comfortable for walking; the park is a short drive from Ayers Rock (Connellan) Airport.

Science & Technology

Western Australia is poised to become the first Australian jurisdiction to legalise plug-in “balcony” solar panels and batteries, opening consultation on safety rules; the state estimates households could save about $240 a year, or up to $500 with a battery. In energy and research, internal messages suggest CSIRO worked to manage its public image while helping Santos investigate a long-undisclosed methane leak at Darwin LNG, and a leaked letter shows the national regulator raised concerns with Santos over its Barossa project.

🔥 Trending
Native-ingredient fine dining from the National Indigenous Culinary Institute
Food & culture movement · Sydney / nationwide

The National Indigenous Culinary Institute, set up in 2012 by restaurant critics and chefs including Neil Perry and Matt Moran, runs an apprenticeship that turns Indigenous cooks into qualified chefs. Its graduates are carrying bush flavours into mainstream kitchens, from finger lime salsa to strawberry gum macarons built on French patisserie technique. More than 100 chefs have completed the program, and the 2026 class of 11 is its largest yet, though only about a third of apprentices finish. Alumni such as Luke Bourke, head chef at Felons Seafood on Manly Wharf, are now earning industry recognition.
