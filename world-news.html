Friday, August 14, 2026 · Asia

Iran

Asia · population ~90M

Politics & Government

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei spent the week consolidating control over the security apparatus, ordering the merger of the Armed Forces General Staff with the Khatam ol-Anbia Central Headquarters and installing former IRGC commander Mohsen Rezaei as secretary of the Supreme National Security Council on August 9. The appointments have entrenched a maximalist line in Tehran — absolute Iranian control of the Strait of Hormuz, removal of US forces from the region, and continuation of the nuclear programme. That hardening has produced friction inside the system: a Paydari Front lawmaker confirmed that parliamentarians are preparing impeachment proceedings against Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi for failing to extract those demands from Washington. Externally, more than 30 governments including France, Britain and Canada issued a joint statement on August 13 condemning Iran's use of capital punishment, accusing the Islamic Republic of executing protesters to silence dissent.

Economy & Business

Iran's economy is in its worst condition since the revolution, with annual inflation running at 87.9% in July, barely down from 88.6% in June and far above the IMF's 68.9% forecast for the year. The rial has collapsed to roughly 1.86 million to the dollar on the open market — a currency that traded near 70 to the dollar in 1979 — while the IMF projects the economy will contract 6.1% in 2026. The binding constraint is trade rather than sanctions alone: the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the US naval blockade reimposed on July 14 have severed most of Iran's oil exports and international commerce, with officials privately conceding the blockade's impact is devastating. Domestic industry is operating on rationed power and imported inputs that no longer arrive.

Military & Security

The Strait of Hormuz has displaced the nuclear file as Tehran's central strategic preoccupation, with a US military intelligence assessment concluding that Iran's priority has shifted from weapons development to control of the waterway. President Trump claimed on August 11 that the United States holds "100% control" of the strait and demanded reparations as a condition of talks; a spokesman for Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters flatly rejected the claim, insisting no vessel transits without Iranian authorisation. Tehran's push to convert that leverage into permanent revenue — transit fees, mine-clearance terms, shipping controls — is the main obstacle to extending the fragile US–Iran memorandum, which expires Sunday. Traffic tells the story: roughly 10 to 12 vessels are crossing daily, against 130 to 140 before the war.

Society & Culture

Extreme summer heat has collided with a failing grid and a water system near collapse, producing hours-long daily power cuts and recurring water shortages across Khuzestan, Ilam, Lorestan, East Azarbaijan, Alborz and Tehran. Official figures put the aquifer deficit at 130 billion cubic metres, and Iran's own meteorological authorities now use the phrase "water day zero" for the point at which supply systems simply stop — a condition hydrologists describe as "water bankruptcy," irreversible on human timescales. The resulting protests differ in character from earlier cycles: environmental grievance has fused with economic collapse, and demonstrators are demanding liveability rather than any single policy reversal. Rural depopulation from dried farmland is adding pressure to cities already short of housing and power.

Tourism

Aviation has recovered only partially since the war. Iran reopened its airspace on April 25 after a near two-month closure, restarting six airports including Mehrabad, Mashhad, Birjand, Gorgan and Zahedan, with Imam Khomeini International resuming international service to Istanbul and Muscat and later Baku, Najaf and Baghdad. The restoration is running on a four-stage phased plan, and Mashhad's reopening is expected to revive religious tourism to the Imam Reza shrine, historically the country's single largest visitor draw. Western governments have not softened their guidance: the US maintains a Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory and urges American citizens to leave Iran now, and Canada's advice is comparable. Practical constraints — blackouts, banking isolation and intermittent internet — make independent travel difficult even where flights operate.

Site to visit: Naqsh-e Jahan Square in Isfahan is one of the largest city squares on earth at 89,600 square metres, laid out by Shah Abbas I in 1598 and inscribed by UNESCO in 1979. Its four sides frame the Masjed-e Shah, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, the Ali Qapu palace and the Qeysarieh bazaar portal — the definitive assembly of Safavid architecture. The square itself is free to walk at any hour and is at its best in the late afternoon, when the tilework warms and families gather; the individual monuments charge separate, modest admission.

🔥 Trending
Noghteha
Tech / Messaging App · Iran (diaspora-supported, Tehran user base)

When the January 8 blackout cut 85 million Iranians off mid-protest, the workaround that spread was Bluetooth. Iranians turned en masse to Bitchat, the serverless mesh messenger that relays messages phone-to-phone over Bluetooth Low Energy with no accounts or phone numbers, pushing downloads past 28,000 and nearly quadrupling the prior two months combined. What makes the story local rather than imported is Noghteha — a fork of Bitchat rebuilt by Iranian developers with full Persian and right-to-left support, a redesigned interface and features tuned to how the blackouts actually behave on the ground. It has become the version people share by word of mouth in Tehran, precisely because it was made by people living the problem, and it works best in exactly the dense urban blocks where crowds gather.

Wednesday, August 12, 2026 · Asia

Israel

Asia · population ~7M

Politics & Government

Israel is heading into an October 27 parliamentary election, and recent polling shows Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing and religious coalition falling short of the 61 seats needed for a majority, even as he remains the country's single most dominant political figure. A Times of Israel poll found 62% of Israelis believe Netanyahu's promised "total victory" will never come, while separate surveys show mounting anxiety about the vote itself, with roughly 70% fearing for election integrity and just 40% optimistic about democracy's future. The campaign has also been marked by defections — a fourth Blue and White MK has now jumped to a rival faction — and by a rare public clash after Defense Minister Israel Katz publicly denounced IDF Central Command chief Avi Bluth on live television, prompting Netanyahu to call the conduct "obscene."

Economy & Business

Israel's economy is proving resilient despite regional tensions, with the IMF trimming its 2026 growth forecast to 3.5% and Moody's cutting its own projection to 3.7% while affirming Israel's Baa1 sovereign rating with a stable outlook. Capital markets have outperformed expectations, with the Tel Aviv 35 index surging and the shekel strengthening against that backdrop. The tech sector continues to anchor growth, with cybersecurity, enterprise AI, and deeptech drawing the biggest startup checks, building on record foreign investment in the sector including Google's $32B acquisition of Wiz and Palo Alto Networks' $25B purchase of CyberArk. National carrier El Al reported a quarterly net profit of $132 million, double the prior year, even amid ongoing regional conflict.

Military & Security

Netanyahu has rejected the Trump-backed Board of Peace's 15-point Gaza plan, insisting the IDF will not withdraw until Hamas is fully disarmed, even as the IDF chief visited Gaza and vowed the army would keep operating proactively against the group. On the Lebanon front, two IDF reservists were killed and four seriously wounded by an IED blast in the south, prompting retaliatory strikes on Hezbollah for what Israel called a ceasefire violation. In the West Bank, Duvdevan commandos captured a Palestinian Islamic Jihad operative in Nablus following Shin Bet intelligence, while separately, U.S. lawmakers are weighing a phase-out of direct military funding to Israel while deepening ties through other channels.

Society & Culture

Public anxiety over political violence is rising ahead of the election, with commentators warning the bitter campaign risks tipping into unrest. In Jerusalem, weekly Shabbat protests outside a contested cafe have shown no sign of abating, drawing sustained local media attention. Military leadership has also faced scrutiny after the IDF chief reportedly slammed a "warped" Haredi draft law, accusing the government of ignoring the army's views on ultra-Orthodox conscription — a perennial fault line in Israeli society.

Tourism

Israel's tourism sector is rebounding sharply: 109,000 tourists entered the country in July 2026, a 28% jump from July 2025, bringing the year-to-date total to 539,000. Industry figures presented at the International Mediterranean Tourism Market in Tel Aviv point to roughly 1.3 million international visitors this year, a 37% increase, though still below pre-pandemic levels, with the U.S., France, and the U.K. as top source markets. More carriers, including Wizz Air, Delta, United, and American, have resumed or expanded routes to Ben Gurion Airport, and 83% of visitors surveyed said they'd recommend Israel as a destination.

Site to visit: Masada, the clifftop fortress-palace built by King Herod above the Dead Sea, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001 for its remarkably preserved Roman siege works — the most complete such system found anywhere in the world. About 750,000 visitors make the trip each year, either up the steep Snake Path or via cable car for sweeping desert views; go early to beat the heat, and carry at least two liters of water per person.

Science & Technology

Israel is working to consolidate its position in quantum computing, with the Israel Innovation Authority launching a NIS 100 million national R&D infrastructure program giving researchers and companies shared access to multiple quantum hardware platforms for benchmarking and algorithm development. The initiative follows Israel bringing its first domestically built quantum computer online and comes as local quantum startups keep attracting capital, including Q-Factor's recent $24 million seed round. Officials frame the shared infrastructure as a way to help processor, controller, and algorithm developers validate full technology stacks without each having to build one from scratch.

🔥 Trending
Temper City — "Self Aware"
Music · Israel

Formed by Israeli musicians Eytan Peled, Chen Kordova, and Aviv Barenholtz — who had spent years producing and songwriting for other Israeli and international acts before teaming up — alt-rock trio Temper City released their debut single "Self Aware" in February 2026. The moody, Cage the Elephant-indebted track took off almost entirely through organic TikTok spread rather than a label push, and by April it had crossed 434,000 video uses on the platform. That grassroots momentum carried the song onto the Billboard Hot 100 in April, making Temper City the first Israeli act ever to chart there, and it later hit No. 1 on the Alternative Airplay chart — a rare grassroots breakout for a debut single with no major-label machine behind it.

Tuesday, August 11, 2026 · Europe

Finland

Europe · population ~6M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Petteri Orpo's coalition—spanning the National Coalition Party, the Finns Party, the Swedish People's Party and the Christian Democrats—remains Finland's 77th government and has kept up a steady drumbeat of activity, with the Prime Minister's Office issuing multiple press releases in early August. Among recent legislative moves, Finland amended its Criminal Code to criminalise denial of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes. The government continues to govern amid a mix of routine administration and the broader European security backdrop shaping its agenda.

Economy & Business

Finland's economy is edging out of recession, though the Bank of Finland still expects only subdued 0.7% growth for 2026 as elevated energy prices tied to the Middle East conflict weigh on household purchasing power. Unemployment is forecast at 10.2% for the year, easing slightly to 9.7% in 2027 as the business cycle improves. On the fiscal side, Finland plans to trim its reduced VAT rate from 14% to 13.5% and lower the top marginal income tax rate toward roughly 52%. Meanwhile Helsinki's startup ecosystem, now valued near $77 billion across 1,359 active companies, keeps drawing investment into AI infrastructure, healthtech and space tech.

Military & Security

Finland's role as a NATO forward-defence hub deepened this summer: following the Ankara summit, France announced it will rotate ground forces, including a combat unit, into NATO's Forward Land Forces Finland, joining Denmark, Iceland, Italy, Norway and the UK in contributing to the battlegroup. Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen confirmed Finland will exceed NATO's 1.5% spending target, with total defence- and security-related spending reaching about 2.4% of GDP in 2026. On procurement, Finland is buying short-range ground-based air defence systems from Sweden, and the Finnish Defence Forces signed a deal with Insta and Lockheed Martin to open a maintenance and service centre in Tampere.

Society & Culture

Oulu's turn as European Capital of Culture 2026 is powering a month-long August Festivals programme of jazz, chamber music, literature and performance, capped by a "Delta Life" grand finale. In Helsinki, the Jazz-Espa concert series brought 17 ensembles to Esplanade Park in early August, while Erottaja Square hosted the Red Bull Dance Your Style street-dance battle. Tampere is also hosting the European Conference on Educational Research, its best-attended edition yet, and Finnish commercial radio's summer charts are dominated by homegrown acts, led by the band Kuumaa's "Ylivoimainen."

Tourism

Finland is having a record tourism year, with international arrivals up 7.1% and overnight hotel stays reaching 7.2 million, a 12% jump that outpaces the European average. The "coolcation" trend—travelers seeking cooler summer climates—is a major driver, alongside long-haul interest from Japan, China, India and the US. Lapland is leading regional growth on the strength of Arctic experiences, and in Helsinki the historic Hotel Kämp completed a major refurbishment, reopening with 22 new rooms, a spa and refreshed dining.

Site to visit: Suomenlinna, the UNESCO-listed 18th-century sea fortress spread across a cluster of islands off Helsinki, remains one of Finland's signature attractions and is free to wander—only its museums and church charge admission. Built by Sweden starting in 1748, it's reachable by a 15-minute public HSL ferry from Market Square (about €2.95 each way), making it an easy half-day trip; summer (June–August) is the busiest and most pleasant time to visit, with the church's bell tower open only during those months.

🔥 Trending
Pehmoaino
Music · Finland

At just 19, singer-songwriter Pehmoaino has become one of the country's fastest-growing grassroots music stories after her piano-led debut single "haluun takas mun perhoset" ("I want my butterflies back") caught fire organically, becoming one of the first Finnish songs to go viral on TikTok. The track's fragile, confessional take on childhood and loss resonated well beyond Finland's borders, part of a wider wave of homegrown alt-pop acts—alongside artists like OLGA and Malla—pushing Finnish music into new international territory without major-label backing.

Monday, August 10, 2026 · South America

Brazil

South America · population ~215M

Politics & Government

Brazil is barreling into Latin America's largest electoral cycle of the year, with party conventions running from July 20 through August 5 to finalize candidates for president, governor and legislative seats ahead of an October 4 election day. The marquee matchup pits President Lula against Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, though Governor Romeu Zema and ex-Governor Ronaldo Caiado are also drawing votes, and anti-establishment activist Renan Santos is gaining traction with young Brazilians according to the AS/COA poll tracker. All candidates must be registered by August 15, with official campaign advertising beginning August 16. A second-round runoff would follow on October 25 if no candidate clears 50% of the vote.

Economy & Business

Brazil's benchmark Selic rate remains parked at 14.25%, keeping mortgages and corporate credit expensive as the country enters the election season with a shaky fiscal outlook and mounting public debt. Despite the tight-money backdrop, Brazil's startup scene continues to post strong numbers, with the country's unicorn roster — QI Tech, Dock, Neon, Olist, Omie and Mercado Bitcoin among them — anchoring a market that pulled in $2.03 billion in 2025 venture funding across more than 5,000 ranked startups. Fintech remains the standout sector, with founders building around PIX, local credit and fraud infrastructure to serve Brazil's payments-heavy digital economy.

Military & Security

Brazil's 2026 budget proposal, submitted to Congress on August 29 and worth roughly BRL2.4 trillion, sets aside BRL141.9 billion (about $26.4 billion) for defence, a roughly 6% boost meant to modernize the armed forces and grow the domestic defence industry. The push builds on President Lula's broader rearmament drive, which included a 13% military spending increase in 2025 and $600 million funneled into SISFRON, Brazil's Elbit-drone-based border monitoring system. Separately, Brazil has moved national guard units toward its border with Venezuela amid heightened regional tension over U.S.-Venezuela relations.

Society & Culture

Brazil's culture is having an outsized global moment this year: the UK/Brazil Season of Culture is showcasing the country's art, music, film and design abroad, while Brazil has separately been named Travel + Leisure's 2026 Destination of the Year. At home, Indigenous artists are pushing visibility through performance — dancer Marciele Albuquerque Munduruku's turn at the 2026 Parintins Festival, blending synthesizers with the traditional maracá, was highlighted in a UN News feature on Indigenous youth using art as resistance. Underneath the celebration, commentators note the election season is also surfacing familiar culture-war fault lines around sexuality, gender, religion and national identity.

Tourism

Brazil is in the middle of a genuine tourism boom, with over 2.6 million international visitors arriving in just the first two months of 2026, driven partly by a surge in travelers from the United States and Canada. More international airlines are adding routes into Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo, and visa-free entry for Chinese passport holders (up to 30 days) took effect on May 11. São Paulo hosts the International Conference on Sustainable Tourism and Economic Development on August 14, part of a packed August calendar of South American festivals and events.

Site to visit: The Brazilian side of Iguazu Falls, a UNESCO World Heritage site shared with Argentina, offers sweeping panoramic views of the roughly 275 individual cascades from its walkway circuit. August sits in the shoulder season, with comfortable 20–30°C days, smaller crowds than the December–February peak, and hotel rates down as much as 40%; park entry on the Brazil side runs about $18, reachable via Foz do Iguaçu's international airport.

🔥 Trending
Belo Horizonte's "mandelão" funk wave (MC HK da 7, DJ Ws da Igrejinha)
Music · Belo Horizonte, Minas Gerais

A new generation of Brazilian funk producers out of Belo Horizonte is breaking into national consciousness through TikTok, with MC HK da 7 carrying the city's "mandelão" sound to wider audiences via viral, baile-ready tracks built for short-form video. DJ Ws da Igrejinha is pushing a related strand of the genre with breakout tracks like "Baile do Bruxo," layering heavy baile funk rhythms over sharp electronic textures. Neither artist has major label backing; the momentum is almost entirely organic, spreading through TikTok dance challenges and underground producer networks before crossing over into mainstream streaming charts and club rotations nationwide.

India

Asia · population ~1.44B

Politics & Government

Parliament's Monsoon Session has been repeatedly disrupted by a government-opposition standoff, with the Lok Sabha pushing through the Taxation (Amendment) Bill, 2026 without discussion amid the protests. The government is now weighing a three-day special session from August 16–18 to take up pending legislation including the Delimitation Bill and the Women's Reservation Bill. Separately, the Union Cabinet approved GOBARdhan, a national circular bioenergy scheme worth roughly Rs. 23,731 crore, and Prime Minister Narendra Modi marked seven years since the abrogation of Articles 370 and 35(A). Election-year positioning is also building in West Bengal and Tamil Nadu ahead of upcoming state contests.

Economy & Business

The OECD's August 2026 outlook confirms India remains the world's fastest-growing major economy, forecasting GDP growth of 7.6% for FY2025–26 before moderating toward 6.1% the following year. On trade, New Delhi is accelerating a free trade agreement with New Zealand and continuing bilateral negotiations with the United States, even as US tariff threats and the risk of sanctions tied to Russian oil imports cloud the outlook. Inflation is projected to climb to 4.8% in FY2026–27 on higher food, energy and fertiliser costs, and the current account deficit is expected to widen as energy import costs rise.

Military & Security

The Indian Navy is preparing to induct four Project 17A stealth frigates — Taragiri, Mahendragiri, Dunagiri and Vindhyagiri — by August–September, adding significant multi-domain maritime capability. The Air Force has meanwhile ordered 83 Tejas Mk1A jets from Hindustan Aeronautics Limited and is rolling out predictive-maintenance analytics to improve fleet readiness. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh has pressed for tighter integration between the ministry's military and civilian wings, while along the western border, Pakistan has reportedly bolstered its artillery with Chinese-made SH-15 self-propelled howitzers, keeping regional tensions elevated.

Society & Culture

Labour unrest has spread across several states: Maharashtra's resident doctors halted emergency services from August 6 over pay and working conditions, Telangana teachers have staged a maha dharna and Chalo Assembly march over unmet funding promises, and farmers in Odisha's Kandhamal district have protested a fertiliser shortage. These sit against the backdrop of India's wider 2026 Gen Z protest movement, which erupted over mismanagement of the NEET medical entrance exam, including an alleged question leak and the scrapping of results.

Tourism

India's travel and tourism sector grew 7.3% in 2026, with Air India now partnering with the Ministry of Tourism on international promotion and the "Modified Viksit UDAN" scheme aiming to add over 100 aerodromes for regional connectivity. Experiential travel is booming, with wellness, wildlife and adventure segments redefining how visitors explore the country, and domestic travelers increasingly favoring homegrown adventures over trips abroad. For late-monsoon travelers, the India Meteorological Department's forecast of slightly below-average rainfall means fewer landslide closures in the Western Ghats this season.

Site to visit: The Taj Mahal, the ivory-white Mughal mausoleum in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, remains India's most iconic UNESCO World Heritage Site and a global symbol of devotion, built by Emperor Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal. It's best visited at sunrise, when crowds thin and the marble glows gold; foreign visitors pay roughly ₹1,100 for general entry (plus ₹200 for the mausoleum itself), and the site is closed to general viewing on Fridays.

Science & Technology

India's Gaganyaan human spaceflight programme has cleared further milestones in validating its human-rating systems, moving the country closer to its first indigenous crewed launch. A new government R&D statistics report shows India now ranks third globally in scientific publications and sixth in resident patent filings, with gross R&D spending reaching 0.84% of GDP and private-sector investment climbing to 45.2% of the total. Separately, the National e-Governance Division launched "AAERI Verify," a digital document-verification platform built with the Association of Australian Education Representatives in India, while Prime Minister Modi highlighted growing domestic manufacturing in medical technology at IIT Delhi's 57th convocation.

Reble, a 24-year-old rapper from Meghalaya's West Jaintia Hills, has spent a decade building a following in India's underground Desi Hip Hop scene with raw, punk-inflected tracks like "Terror" and "New Riot" before this year's breakout. Friday, August 7, 2026 · Europe

Spain

Europe · population ~48M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is weathering a fresh political setback after his brother was found guilty of administrative misconduct, feeding a polling narrative that a large majority of Spaniards believe corruption has escalated under his tenure. On the international stage, Sánchez has kept Madrid firmly opposed to the ongoing 2026 Iran war, denying U.S. forces access to the joint bases at Rota and Morón and drawing threats of trade sanctions from President Trump. Meanwhile, opposition Partido Popular leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo is trying to seize the domestic agenda with a pledge to build one million homes if he wins the 2027 general election, framing Spain's housing squeeze as the country's central grievance.

Economy & Business

Spain has raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2.6%, with real output up 2.7% year-on-year in Q1 and Goldman Sachs highlighting that Spain is expanding roughly three times faster than the euro-area average. Job creation remains strong — social security affiliations rose 2.4% year-on-year in April even as headline unemployment sat at 10.3% — while inflation held at 3.2% in June for the third month running. To cushion households from Middle East–driven fuel spikes, the government has extended a temporary support package worth roughly €5 billion (0.3% of GDP).

Military & Security

Spain currently deploys more than 3,000 military personnel across 17 missions on four continents under UN, NATO and EU mandates, with the patrol boat Centinela continuing maritime security sweeps in the Alboran Sea and the Strait of Gibraltar. Defence contractor Indra announced on 30 July that it will build a second of three military vehicle plants in the northern corridor, and the U.S. State Department has cleared a possible $1.7 billion Foreign Military Sale to modernise Spain's F-100 frigates with upgraded AEGIS, sensors and combat systems. On the strategic ledger, Spain still spends only about 1.3% of GDP on defence, keeping it 17th globally — a figure allies continue to press Madrid to lift.

Society & Culture

Anti-tourism protests have intensified across the country this summer, with peaceful marches in Barcelona, Palma and the Canary Islands targeting housing costs, overcrowding of historic districts and mass-tourism's environmental footprint. In parallel, Health Minister Mónica García is trying to move her long-delayed Framework Statute for healthcare staff through Congress, but doctors and unions remain divided, demanding on-call hours count toward Social Security contributions and better compensation for night work. Transport disruption is also on the calendar, with rolling rail, bus and ferry strikes tracked into August putting further pressure on a system already strained by summer travel.

Tourism

Spain is on track to welcome close to 100 million foreign visitors in 2026, with official projections showing a cumulative 80 million arrivals by the end of August and the peak summer stretch alone forecast to generate a record €64 billion ($73 billion) in revenue. A growing share of that traffic is pivoting inland toward rural and cultural destinations as coastal hotspots push back against overcrowding. Attention next week will focus on Castilla y León, where roughly 500,000 visitors are expected for the total solar eclipse on 12 August, straining rural accommodation but offering a rare shoulder-season windfall.

Site to visit: The Alhambra in Granada — a sprawling Nasrid palace-fortress overlooking the city and one of the finest examples of Moorish architecture in the world — remains Spain's must-see UNESCO site. General adult admission is €21, but daily visitor caps mean tickets to the timed-entry Nasrid Palaces routinely sell out weeks in advance, so book online well before your travel dates.

Science & Technology

Spain is doubling down on non-dilutive startup funding, with the national Neotec Program offering up to €250,000 for young R&D-heavy tech companies and ICEX Innova Invest covering 25–50% of eligible costs — one reason August is peaking as Europe's busiest month for tech conferences, with more than 635 events concentrated in Madrid, Barcelona, Palma, Bilbao and Málaga. On the research front, Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya has reaffirmed itself as Spain's top university for EU Horizon Europe funding, having pulled in €103.4 million across 211 projects since 2021. Renewables continue to reshape the grid: Spain's expanding wind and solar base has helped keep electricity bills lower than most European peers even through the Middle East energy shock.
