Saturday, October 3, 2026 · Europe

United Kingdom

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

Westminster is in conference recess, with the Commons not due to sit again until Monday 12 October. In the meantime, committee work continues: MPs have invited Meta, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic to appear before the Business Committee amid growing AI safety concerns, and the Treasury Committee chair has written to HMRC about any tax implications of the Premier League's investigation into Manchester City. The Housing Committee chair has also responded to the prime minister's commitments on leasehold reform and the wider housing agenda.

Economy & Business

The Bank of England held interest rates for a sixth consecutive time on 17 September, keeping Bank Rate at 3.75%. Inflation rose to 3.1% in August from 2.9% in July, and the Bank's central forecast has it peaking around 3.75% in the final quarter of 2026. Analysts see little chance of cuts soon, with a softening labour market pulling against energy-driven price pressures, as the House of Commons Library has noted of an economy that has so far beaten forecasts.

Military & Security

The UK led a major Joint Expeditionary Force exercise as defence ministers met in Iceland on 2 October, while Royal Marines deployed to the Faroe Islands for the first time since the Second World War to train with Danish and Faroese forces around the Greenland-Iceland-UK Gap. The Royal Navy's sixth Astute-class attack submarine has left Barrow-in-Furness for sea trials, and the RAF expects to field thirteen Atlas transports a day by the end of 2027 under a £190m Airbus support deal. Britain also set out what it called the fundamental facts of Russia's war against Ukraine in a statement to the OSCE.

Society & Culture

Industrial relations in health remain uneasy even after resident doctors in England accepted a government offer in June, ending that dispute. In Northern Ireland, the union NIPSA has warned that health worker strike action could come as early as October if the pay award is not settled, describing a possible “winter of discontent”, though no dates are confirmed because a statutory ballot and formal notice must come first. Smaller trust-level walkouts by support and administrative staff are also running in Greater Manchester.

Tourism

The government is planning a charge on overnight visitors and is encouraging England's mayors to borrow against the income to reinvest in their regions. VisitBritain is hosting nine international influencers across northern England and Wales until 5 October to promote screen tourism, and is backing a North of England mayoral trade mission to India this month. The next official visitor estimates, IPS 2025, are due on 16 October.

Site to visit: Hadrian’s Wall is a UNESCO World Heritage Site stretching roughly 73 miles across northern England, built by Roman soldiers from AD 122 as the empire’s north-western frontier. Well-preserved forts such as Housesteads and Vindolanda are the highlights, and the AD122 bus service along the route, running in the warmer months, makes it easy to explore without a car; spring to early autumn is the best time to walk it.

Science & Technology

A UK science minister has said the country is “choking” on its failure to commercialise research, while advisers warn the government to address the London skew of venture capital funding. Universities are also struggling to vet Chinese funding after an MI5 warning, prompting calls for more intelligence sharing on research security. On the funding side, UKRI is offering up to £1.5m for critical-minerals manufacturing research and a £6m doctoral award in chip design.

Friday, October 2, 2026 · South America

Argentina

South America · population ~46M

Politics & Government

President Javier Milei opened Argentina Week in Paris on October 1, joined by 13 provincial governors at an investor event hosted at the OECD. At home, cabinet chief Diego Santilli met governors on the 2027 budget, and the government is pushing to suspend or scrap the PASO primaries in 2027 to cut spending and political risk. The opposition has called an October 15 session to reject Milei's Decree 70/2023, but is 14 deputies short of the 129-member quorum. Separately, Elisa Carrió filed a criminal complaint against Milei over posts insulting journalist Maru Duffard.

Economy & Business

The Paris event produced headline investment pledges: companies announced projects worth about US$62.55 billion, including the long-planned Argentina LNG gas-export venture. TotalEnergies presented a portfolio worth close to US$10 billion in oil and gas, while US$10.5 billion in new energy and mining pledges were tied to provinces. Toyota also joined the RIGI incentive regime, planning a US$1.341 billion plant in Zarate, Buenos Aires province. Pledges are announcements rather than spending, and delivery will depend on macroeconomic stability.

Tourism

Inbound travel is recovering: Argentina recorded 432.9 thousand inbound tourists in August, with inbound up 8.9% and outbound down 18.3%. In July, foreign arrivals rose 17.9%, led by Brazil and Uruguay, while resident outbound travel fell 21.2%. That is a reversal from 2025, when the country received 8.7 million visitors, down 19.7% from 2024. Authorities are also promoting nature tourism such as El Impenetrable National Park, with its wildlife and river experiences.

Site to visit: Iguazú National Park in Misiones is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and home to one of the world's largest waterfall systems, set in subtropical rainforest on the border with Brazil. It is a mainstay of Argentina's tourism offering. The nearest gateway is Puerto Iguazú, reached by direct flights from Buenos Aires, and the cooler, drier months make walking the park's catwalks more comfortable.

🔥 Trending

Music · Buenos Aires

Buenos Aires rappers Stiffy and AgusFortnite2008, who record as Swaggerboyz, have built a DIY scene around Spanish-language hypertrap alongside peers such as Joshu Joshu. Rolling Stone describes a generation of rappers who grew up online and learned their craft through freestyle culture. The scene is part of a wider wave of under-30 Argentine urban artists making their mark through streaming and live shows.

Thursday, October 1, 2026 · North America

Canada

North America · population ~38M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Mark Carney is rallying Liberal MPs ahead of what he calls a “crucial session” of Parliament, welcoming three members who won August byelections. He says he has a “very clear mandate” to push ahead with a bill limiting federal project reviews to one year and changes to the Labour Code, despite criticism. Carney was also expected to designate a West Coast oil pipeline a project of national interest under the Building Canada Act alongside Alberta Premier Danielle Smith. On trade, he has said Canada will resume talks “when the Americans are ready.”

Economy & Business

The Bank of Canada says global trade is changing how the Canadian economy works, with businesses adjusting supply chains and exploring new markets. Governor Tiff Macklem has warned that re-escalating trade tensions and high energy prices are shifting the risks for monetary policy. The labour market remains soft, with unemployment around 6.5% and subdued wage growth, according to the central bank’s September deliberations summary.

Military & Security

Canada’s first F-35A completed its maiden flight from Fort Worth on September 23, moving the Royal Canadian Air Force closer to a fifth-generation fighter fleet. Canadian and US forces also ran a joint Arctic naval exercise near Dutch Harbor from September 18 to 22. Meanwhile, CTV reports a growing military presence in the Arctic amid concerns over disputed waters.

Society & Culture

Labour tension is building in health care: 99 per cent of CUPE 5430 members voted for a strike in Saskatchewan, the largest strike vote in the province’s history, after years without a wage increase. In Quebec, students at all four medical schools voted for an unlimited strike mandate over a provincial law tying part of doctors’ pay to performance targets.

Tourism

Domestic travel is booming as Canadians redirect spending away from the US; one report says cross-border travel has reversed, with provinces capturing millions of additional domestic visits. Another notes record demand in hubs such as Ottawa and Halifax. In the industry, Intrepid Travel’s small-group trips are now bookable through Air Canada Vacations.

Site to visit: Banff National Park in Alberta is Canada’s oldest national park and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Canadian Rockies, known for turquoise lakes and mountain scenery. Autumn offers fewer crowds and golden larches, and a Parks Canada pass is required for entry.

Science & Technology

Ottawa announced about $550 million in research funding on September 2 for the Eddie Goldenberg Research Chairs and Canada Impact+ Emerging Leaders programs, reportedly including 48 recruits from US institutions such as Harvard and MIT. Canada also took part in the G20 Innovation Ministerial.

🔥 Trending
Eh!
Social network · Vancouver Island, British Columbia

Eh! is a Canadian-built social network pitched as “local social infrastructure” for creators and small businesses. It was built by TrailMix CEO Jessica Glowacki’s Vancouver Island startup, as MobileSyrup profiled. It arrives as Canadians increasingly favour domestic products amid trade tensions with the US.

Wednesday, September 30, 2026 · South America

Peru

South America · population ~33M

Politics & Government

President Keiko Fujimori, barely two months into her term, travelled to New York for the 81st UN General Assembly high-level week, pitching Peru as a partner for cooperation and investment. At home, attention is turning to the October 4 regional and municipal elections, in which voters will choose 25 regional governors, 196 provincial mayors and 1,695 district mayors, with security dominating the Lima mayoral race as the first national test of the new government. Prime Minister Luis Galarreta has said the executive will keep pushing its priority reforms, while a recent poll analysis reports that Fujimori's approval has fallen sharply in her first month.

Economy & Business

Peru's economy rebounded in July to top forecasts, with output up 3.56 percent year on year and 3.13 percent for January to July. High copper and gold prices continue to support the terms of trade, and BBVA Research projects growth of about 2.9 percent in 2026. The main drag is El Niño: the central bank now puts its cost at 0.9 points of 2026 growth, and fishing is forecast to shrink by roughly 30 percent this year.

Military & Security

Security forces killed "Comrade Richard," reportedly third in command of the Shining Path remnants, in the VRAEM just a day after Fujimori took office on July 28, according to Jamestown analysis. The government has extended the military intervention in the region to "consolidate" it, even though the group has largely become a narco-trafficking cartel rather than an ideological insurgency, as InSight Crime describes. Critics such as the World Socialist Web Site argue the killing is being used as a pretext for tougher security policies.

Tourism

The Culture Ministry and Sernanp reopened Inca Trail routes 1, 2 and 3 on September 5 after storms and mudslides closed them in August. On September 11 authorities announced improvements to the Machu Picchu and Inca Trail ticketing system aimed at more predictable reservations, and daily visitors are capped at 5,600 during the busy season through November 2. Operators are also courting high-end Chinese travellers with itineraries that go beyond Machu Picchu to Lake Titicaca and Andean cuisine.

Site to visit: Machu Picchu, the 15th-century Inca citadel above the Urubamba valley near Cusco, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most recognisable landmarks in the Americas. Entry is by timed ticket booked in advance through the official Culture Ministry system, and the dry season from May to September is the most reliable for clear weather, so book well ahead.

Science & Technology

The central bank has upgraded its assessment of the current El Niño from strong to extraordinary, warming coastal waters and pushing anchovies into deeper water away from coastal fleets. The anchovy fishery crisis has been called a systemic alert for the global fishmeal industry, with landings in the first half of the year far below last year's. Analysts also warn that the event could hit as much as half of Peru's food supply while remittances cool.

Lenin Tamayo is a Peruvian social media phenomenon who fuses Quechua-language music with K-pop style, a blend that has drawn large viral audiences online. His rise shows how Andean identity and global pop aesthetics can find a young audience without any major-label push. Read more on his profile. Separately, the #peruesclave hashtag spread after Lima's Vibra Festival, turning a concert into a wider conversation about labour and inequality.

Tuesday, September 29, 2026 · Oceania

Australia

Oceania · population ~26M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese spent the week on the international stage, opening Climate Week in New York with a keynote on the growing threat of climate change in the Indo-Pacific. He also visited Apple Park to discuss Australia's legislation on safer online environments for children with Tim Cook. During UN High-Level Week, the government announced a bid for a seat on the Security Council for the 2029-2030 term, and the Foreign Minister named four new Heads of Missions.

Economy & Business

The Reserve Bank raised the cash rate to 4.60%, the fourth increase of 2026, in a unanimous decision, and signalled that further tightening remains possible as firms lift prices and inflation expectations stay elevated. The board pointed to renewed disruption in global oil supply. Meanwhile, the RBA's August outlook had already flagged stronger-than-expected growth and inflation, with GDP up 0.4% in the June quarter and 2.1% over the year. Unemployment rose to 4.6% in August even as employment increased.

Military & Security

Defence Minister Richard Marles met US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth in Washington, where the two discussed US force posture in Australia, and Washington said it would accelerate its military presence in the country. Announcements on an expanded footprint are expected in coming months, on top of Marine rotations through Darwin. Arrangements for Submarine Rotational Force-West, due to be established in 2027, have been finalised under AUKUS.

Tourism

Australia recorded 9.07 million international arrivals in the year to July 2026, up 6.9%, although July alone saw 710,980 short-term visitor trips, down 4.3% on a year earlier. Tourism Australia is courting Indian travellers with Sara Tendulkar as ambassador for its "Come and Say G'Day" campaign, and industry groups are warning of a possible global travel slowdown.

Site to visit: Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park in the Northern Territory is a UNESCO World Heritage site, sacred to the Anangu people, whose towering sandstone monolith glows red at sunrise and sunset. A park pass is required, and the cooler months from May to September are the most comfortable for walking the base. Most visitors fly into Ayers Rock Airport, about 20 minutes from the park.

🔥 Trending
Build Australia
Volunteer movement · Australia-wide

Build Australia began when a group of founders, operators and creatives rallied after a viral tweet about the national mood. The volunteer-run organisation aims to shift the narrative toward optimism and ambition, and it has gained momentum within the local startup community, which is itself showing strength in deep tech.

Monday, September 28, 2026 · Asia

South Korea

Asia · population ~52M

Politics & Government

President Lee Jae Myung's approval rating rebounded for a second straight week, climbing to 37.9 percent after a bruising stretch, even as his government juggles a widening set of controversies. Seoul's presidential office demanded an apology from Kyiv after accusing Ukraine of leaking details of a North Korean POW transfer to the South, a claim Ukrainian officials have flatly denied. At home, confirmation hearings for justice minister nominee Kim Seung-won turned into a shouting match of accusations and insults over lobbying allegations, deepening opposition pressure on the cabinet. Lee also returns to a packed agenda that includes a Supreme Court appointment dispute alongside the DMZ fallout described below.

Economy & Business

South Korea's exports surged 78.3 percent in the first 20 days of September to a record $71.4 billion, an AI-driven boom that pushed semiconductor shipments to an all-time high of $34.1 billion, nearly 48 percent of total exports, with shipments to China and the United States both jumping sharply. Riding that momentum, President Lee is set to chair a meeting with Samsung and SK executives this week to check progress on the government's "three megaprojects" drive spanning semiconductors, physical AI and AI data centers, a plan that envisions roughly 4,755 trillion won in combined investment according to Seoul Economic Daily.

Military & Security

Three South Korean soldiers, including a battalion commander, were wounded on September 21 when a suspected landmine detonated inside the DMZ during a route-clearing operation, with investigators treating a North Korean-planted mine as the leading theory. The response has become a political flashpoint: opposition lawmakers accused the Lee administration of a "deliberately delayed" on-site probe that risked letting rain destroy key evidence, while military officials countered that residual blast risk made it unsafe to send inspection teams in sooner, with South Korean and UN Command personnel finally entering the site five days after the blast.

Society & Culture

South Koreans marked the three-day Chuseok harvest holiday from September 24 to 26, with families packing highways and KTX trains to reach ancestral hometowns while hospitals and pharmacies ran reduced holiday schedules searchable via the national E-Gen emergency service. In the workplace, unionized staff at Naver's metaverse affiliate Naver Z staged a full-day walkout on September 23, with roughly 70 percent of members joining to protest a wage freeze, per Digital Today, reflecting broader labor friction in Korea's tech sector.

Tourism

South Korea welcomed a record 15.05 million foreign visitors in the first eight months of 2026, up 21.6 percent year-on-year, with the tourism ministry eyeing October as the peak arrivals month. The autumn festival calendar is now in full swing, headlined by the Andong International Mask Dance Festival running September 25 to October 4 and the sprawling 56-day Seoul Autumn Festival spanning more than 200 venues across the capital.

Site to visit: Hahoe Folk Village, a UNESCO World Heritage-listed riverside settlement near Andong, preserves centuries-old tile-roofed and thatched hanok houses still inhabited by descendants of the Ryu clan and is the ancestral home of Korea's iconic hahoe talchum mask dance. It pairs naturally with the ongoing Andong Mask Dance Festival; entry costs about 5,000 won including a shuttle bus, and it's best reached by a roughly hour-long bus ride from Andong, itself about three hours from Seoul, with early morning or autumn weekday visits recommended to avoid festival crowds.

Science & Technology

Seoul is hosting AI4Sci Korea 2026, the country's first international "AI for Science" academic conference, running September 27 to October 1 at Yongsan-gu's Dragon City Hotel with roughly 600 researchers from 19 countries presenting about 220 papers, according to Digital Today. The gathering coincides with a broader government push into strategic technologies, including small modular reactors and fusion, and comes ahead of the planned October 7 launch of Korea's fifth Nuri rocket carrying a record 15 satellites, among them the country's first orbital satellite constellation.

🔥 Trending
HANRORO
Indie music · Seoul (Korea University scene)

HANRORO, a 26-year-old singer-songwriter and Korean literature major who writes and releases her own material outside the K-pop system, has become one of Korea's most talked-about grassroots music stories of the year. She built her following through self-penned tracks like "Landing in Love," which climbed to No. 2 on Melon's weekly chart, and by simultaneously dominating streaming charts and bestseller lists as an unsigned indie act, a rare feat that saw her three EPs sweep the top of Yes24's album sales chart. Momentum has only accelerated this month: she sold out her first overseas headline show in Tokyo on September 8 and, per Sports Khan, is now in the middle of a packed autumn university festival circuit across Korean campuses, culminating in an Inha University appearance on the 17th.
