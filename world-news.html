Sunday, September 6, 2026 · South America

Venezuela

South America · population ~29M

Politics & Government

Acting president Delcy Rodríguez, who was sworn in after U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in January and flew him to the United States on drug-trafficking charges, told reporters at Miraflores Palace this week that national elections will be held when the country is "ready" — declining again to name a date. Her caretaker government passed the 180-day limit the 1999 constitution sets for an acting presidency back in early July, and analysts have begun describing her approach as normalization without transition — restoring international business ties while deferring the vote. Washington-facilitated talks between the government and a segment of the opposition, which opened in August, are scheduled to resume in October, though María Corina Machado remains outside the process and Edmundo González Urrutia is still in exile. Pressure from Congress has grown in parallel, with senators publicly urging that elections proceed rapidly rather than being traded away for energy access.

Economy & Business

The economy is running two directions at once: annual inflation climbed to roughly 576% in July from 544% in June, still the highest rate in the world, even as forecasters project double-digit GDP growth for 2026 off an extraordinarily low base. The engine is oil — output has pushed back above one million barrels per day on the strength of joint ventures with Chevron, Repsol, Eni and Maurel & Prom, and crude shipments to the U.S. have averaged around 329,500 bpd, a roughly 192% jump over the 2025 average. U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright was in Caracas this week to sign fresh agreements with Chevron and Eni, and 2026 oil revenues are projected near $22 billion, the best showing since 2018. The structural problem is unchanged: hydrocarbons still supply more than 83% of hard-currency inflows, leaving household purchasing power hostage to a single commodity while GDP sits around $111 billion after an 80% collapse from its 2013 peak.

Military & Security

The Caribbean remains heavily militarized under Operation Southern Spear, the U.S. naval deployment framed as a counter-narcotics campaign against Latin American trafficking networks. Lethal strikes on vessels alleged to be running cocaine for Tren de Aragúa have continued through 2026, including the killing of a senior gang figure in a June strike. Legal scholars and several members of Congress continue to dispute the basis for the strikes, noting that maritime interdiction rules ordinarily permit lethal force only against an imminent threat, and that officials have not publicly explained how those aboard the targeted boats were identified as gang members. Domestically, the sequence of events since January — a decapitated leadership, an unelected caretaker and a foreign naval presence offshore — leaves the security picture unusually fluid.

Society & Culture

Recovery from the twin magnitude-7.2 and 7.5 earthquakes that struck north-central Venezuela on June 24 continues to dominate daily life. The quakes and more than thirty aftershocks devastated communities around Caracas and La Guaira; official tallies count thousands dead, some 16,700 injured, nearly 18,000 displaced and 38 health facilities damaged. PAHO reports the response has moved into an early recovery phase, shifting from emergency triage to restoring routine services and heading off disease outbreaks in crowded shelters. Aid agencies emphasise that the emergency window has closed but reconstruction will run for months and, in the worst-hit neighbourhoods, years — with durable housing for displaced families and rebuilding schools named as the central long-term tasks.

Tourism

Air links to Venezuela are reopening faster than at any point in a decade. Copa Airlines has confirmed a new Panama City–Porlamar service to Isla Margarita beginning November 26, running Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays and making the island Copa's sixth Venezuelan destination after Caracas, Valencia, Maracaibo, Barcelona and Barquisimeto — which puts Margarita within one connection of more than eighty cities across the Americas. American Airlines restored daily Miami–Caracas flights on April 30, restoring the most commercially important U.S. link. Operators are pitching a three-part product — Caribbean beaches on Margarita and Los Roques, tepui-and-waterfall adventure travel in the Gran Sabana, and heritage tours in Caracas — though prospective visitors should note that Western governments still maintain restrictive travel advisories and the earthquake zone around the capital remains in reconstruction.

Site to visit: Canaima National Park, a 30,000-square-kilometre UNESCO World Heritage Site in Bolívar state, is built around the flat-topped tepui mountains and Angel Falls, the tallest uninterrupted waterfall on earth at 979 metres. There is no road access — visitors fly into Canaima village from Caracas or Ciudad Bolívar, then continue by motorised curiara up the Carrao and Churun rivers. Practical detail: go during the June-to-December wet season, when the river is deep enough for boats to reach the falls and the cascade is at full volume; in the dry months it can thin to a trickle.

Science & Technology

The electricity grid remains the country's binding technical constraint. Monitors recorded at least 35 blackouts in the first quarter of 2026, with average outages lasting four hours and thirteen minutes and voltage instability rising nearly 300% year-on-year — failures attributed less to missing hardware than to deferred maintenance and disinvestment. Since March the electric energy ministry has been led by Rolando Alcalá, a Simón Bolívar University-trained electrical engineer who has prioritised the stalled Tocoma project, maintenance at the Guri hydroelectric complex, and re-engagement with Siemens and General Electric on thermoelectric plant recovery. Independent estimates put the cost of rebuilding the national grid at between $12 billion and $24 billion, a figure that dwarfs current public investment and that analysts argue must be resolved before any manufacturing revival is realistic.

🔥 Trending
Cashea
Fintech / Consumer · Caracas

Cashea is a Venezuelan buy-now-pay-later app that has quietly become one of the most widely used pieces of software in the country, and its rise is a direct consequence of a vanished banking system: consumer credit in Venezuela collapsed from roughly $15 billion to $1.7 billion as the economy contracted, and only about 7% of adults hold a credit card. Founded in Caracas to fill that vacuum, the app lets shoppers split purchases into interest-free instalments at partner stores, using in-app behaviour rather than a credit bureau to set limits — and it grew from 1.2 million users in its first year to more than 5 million largely through word of mouth. It now reports around 10 million accounts, reaching an estimated 35% of the adult population — more Venezuelans have a Cashea account than a credit card. In 2026 the company raised $100 million from international backers and says the entire sum will be deployed inside Venezuela.

SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 5, 2026 · AFRICA

Egypt

Africa · population ~106M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly has ordered ministries to redraw Egypt's planning scenarios through 2030, explicitly including a worst-case model that assumes prolonged regional war and instability — a rare public acknowledgment of how exposed Cairo feels to the conflicts on its borders. On the domestic front, Madbouly has been pressing implementation of President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi's directives to stabilise commodity supplies and expand discounted public markets nationwide, a politically sensitive file with inflation still in double digits. Diplomatically the government has leaned hard into the Beijing relationship, with the prime minister calling Xi Jinping's visit “historic” as it coincided with the 70th anniversary of Egypt–China ties. Rights groups meanwhile continue to document a tightly closed political space: Human Rights Watch notes that peaceful protest remains effectively banned and that journalists, rights defenders and opposition figures still face prosecution, even as Sisi has ordered work on a national human rights strategy.

Economy & Business

The pound has clawed back ground against the dollar, trading near 50.95 in early September after weakening past 52 in March, but the swing underlines that currency risk has not disappeared despite the IMF anchor. Annual urban inflation ticked up to 14.9% in July from 14.3% in June, and August's electricity tariff increase is expected to push household costs higher before any year-end easing arrives. The Fund has nonetheless turned more constructive, lifting Egypt's 2026 growth outlook to around 4.5–4.6% and projecting inflation easing into the low teens, with the July seventh review adding roughly $1.8 billion to disbursements under the extended fund facility. The other bright spot is the Suez Canal, where Q2 revenues climbed 13% quarter-on-quarter to about $1.26 billion as transits rose 7.7% to 3,580 vessels — still short of pre–Red Sea-crisis levels, but the clearest sign yet that shipping lines are returning.

Military & Security

Cairo remains the indispensable mediator on Gaza and is spending most of its diplomatic capital trying to keep the ceasefire from unravelling, having warned Israel that escalation threatens the entire framework and hosted urgent talks with a senior Hamas delegation. Egypt has drawn one hard red line throughout: it rejects any arrangement that channels Palestinians toward the Rafah crossing or pushes displacement into Sinai, framing that as complicity in ethnic cleansing. The Rafah crossing reopened for limited traffic earlier this year, initially permitting only around 50 people a day in each direction, mostly medical cases and returnees. Cairo has offered troops for a proposed international stabilisation force but has conditioned participation on Hamas agreeing to demobilise, while coordinating in parallel with Qatari, Turkish and American mediators.

Society & Culture

Egypt's schools return this month under a restructured calendar, with the education minister insisting on the September 6 start for international schools and September 12 for public schools, alongside a significant expansion of actual teaching days to 183 — a jump intended to bring Egypt closer to international norms. Sisi used an August meeting in Alamein to press for continued overhaul of the system, and a presidential decree has established a new non-profit “International University for Sciences, Arts, and Technology” in the New Administrative Capital as part of a push to raise Egyptian universities' global standing. The reforms land against a harder fiscal backdrop: Human Rights Watch argues that public spending on education and health has fallen below constitutional requirements, straining services just as households absorb sustained price increases. With poverty rates rising alongside inflation, the gap between headline reform announcements and classroom and clinic conditions remains the central social story.

Tourism

Tourism is Egypt's strongest-performing sector this year: the country welcomed 6.1 million visitors in the first four months of 2026, up 7% on the same period in 2025, and the government is targeting roughly 21 million arrivals for the full year. The Grand Egyptian Museum has been the engine, drawing more than three million visitors in its first seven months — about 60% of them international — and running at 7,000 to 10,000 admissions a day against a 15,000 daily cap. Officials now expect the museum to settle at six to eight million visitors annually, and it has already been ranked among the world's three most-visited museums. Red Sea resorts have absorbed much of the remaining growth, supporting a national ambition to double tourism receipts by 2030.

Site to visit: The Grand Egyptian Museum sits on the Giza plateau roughly two kilometres from the pyramids, and is the largest archaeological museum in the world devoted to a single civilisation — its centrepiece is the complete Tutankhamun collection, more than 5,000 objects displayed together for the first time. The vast glazed atrium frames a direct view of the pyramids, which is worth timing for. Tickets are sold online with timed entry and the daily cap is 15,000, so booking ahead is effectively mandatory in high season; late afternoon slots are the least crowded.

Science & Technology

Egypt's clean-energy ambitions are concentrated almost entirely in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, which has launched a National Clean Hydrogen Program with UNIDO to coordinate what has until now been a scattered pipeline of memoranda. The first genuinely operational milestone came in January, when a 100 MW Scatec-led facility began initial exports — small against the gigawatt-scale projects announced, but the first real proof of concept. Commercial momentum has picked up since, including an April joint venture with Poland's Hynfra for a green ammonia project and a memorandum with China's UEG for a Mediterranean hydrogen hub, part of a portfolio Cairo values at roughly $40 billion in prospective investment. The strategic logic is geographic rather than domestic: siting production beside the canal lets Egypt sell zero-carbon bunker fuel to transiting ships and export to Europe, though most headline projects have still not reached final investment decision.

🔥 Trending
Hamo El Morshdy
Music · Cairo

Mahraganat — the autotuned, working-class fusion of Egyptian shaabi wedding music with EDM and hip-hop that the state musicians' syndicate once tried to ban outright as a “virus” — has produced its biggest mainstream breakout yet. Hamo El Morshdy debuted on the charts in March and has climbed steadily since, reaching No. 4 on Billboard Arabia's Artist 100 on the back of tracks like “Ana Five Star Ala Kawkaby” and “El Wala Hamo Geh” — the highest any mahraganat performer has ever placed. His rise is the clearest evidence of a broader shift: the genre grew entirely outside the licensing system, distributed through YouTube and WhatsApp from Cairo's informal neighbourhoods, and now dominates streaming charts that were until recently the preserve of polished Gulf-financed pop. Nine mahraganat tracks landed among YouTube's most-streamed songs in Egypt last year, and TikTok keeps feeding the cycle — Ahmad Dogary's “Television Remote” went viral in early July on the back of a dance clip. What began as music the establishment refused to license has become, without ever being granted permission, the country's default popular sound.

FRIDAY, September 4, 2026 · ASIA

China

Asia · population ~1.41B

Politics & Government

Beijing has spent the week pushing an administrative agenda built around industrial policy and Party discipline, unveiling a five-year plan to promote small and medium-sized enterprise development and installing a new head of the country's top market regulator. Xi Jinping also published a fresh article on Party self-governance, continuing a drumbeat of anti-corruption messaging that has run alongside high-profile purges of senior military figures over the past year. Disaster response dominated the domestic headlines after a landslide struck a county in Chongqing Municipality, with Xi ordering search-and-rescue operations be carried out "in a scientific manner." Analysts tracking the year's priorities note that the 2026 policy mix announced in March leaned heavily on growth stabilisation and innovation, and this week's SME measures read as an incremental delivery on that framing rather than a new direction.

Economy & Business

The growth picture has softened noticeably: China's economy expanded 4.3% year-on-year in the second quarter, down from 5.0% in Q1 and the weakest annual reading since late 2022, as subdued private investment and the unresolved property downturn outweighed resilient AI-related exports. Trade has been the bright spot — the National Bureau of Statistics reported first-half goods trade of 25.47 trillion yuan, up 16.9% year on year, with mechanical and electrical products growing 20.1% and now accounting for 63.5% of total trade value. The dominant external variable remains the US-China tariff escalation, which has sharply contracted direct bilateral trade and pushed Chinese exporters toward Southeast Asian, European and Global South markets. Independent forecasters expect momentum to weaken further across the rest of the year, particularly if commodity prices stay elevated.

Military & Security

Taiwan's 2026 Military Power Report, submitted to its legislature this week, concluded that recent PLA exercises are increasingly oriented toward "joint blockade and control" of the island's external air and sea routes, combining maritime and air blockade drills with joint firepower strikes and amphibious landing rehearsals. Chinese Defence Minister Dong Jun said the PLA would serve as a "steadfast bulwark" for national reunification and would thwart any move toward Taiwan independence or external military interference. In the South China Sea, the Southern Theater Command ran joint maritime and air drills around Huangyan Dao involving Type 071 amphibious transport docks and H-6K bombers, framed as a response to Philippine patrol activity conducted with outside partners. Beijing separately urged foreign governments to view China-Russia military cooperation "objectively and rationally" following recent joint exercises and maritime patrols.

Society & Culture

China's demographic squeeze has moved from projection to arithmetic fact: the total fertility rate now sits near 1.0 children per woman — among the lowest ever recorded for a large country — with some demographers placing it closer to 0.95. Median age has climbed to roughly 40, up from 22 in 1980, and about 15% of the population is now 65 or older, a share projected to hit 30% by 2050. Policy responses have accelerated: couples are now eligible for full reimbursement of standard childbirth costs including prenatal checkups and delivery fees, and tuition waivers for the final kindergarten year in public preschools now reach roughly 12 million children. The countervailing pressure is youth unemployment near 20%, which continues to feed the "rùn" emigration discourse among graduates and complicates any straightforward pro-natalist message.

Tourism

Inbound tourism is the clearest post-pandemic recovery story in the Chinese economy, driven almost entirely by visa liberalisation. China recorded 22.91 million entries by foreign nationals in the first half of 2026, up 20.4% year on year, with roughly 17.8 million of those — 77.7% of the total — arriving without a traditional visa. The unilateral waiver programme now covers citizens of dozens of countries for stays of up to 30 days, including most EU member states, Singapore, Malaysia and the UAE, and runs through December 31, 2026, giving airlines a stable horizon for capacity planning. Holiday travel has surged alongside it: the May Day period saw 436,000 visa-free arrivals, a 14.7% increase, against an average of more than 2.26 million daily border crossings.

Site to visit: The Mogao Caves outside Dunhuang in Gansu province are a UNESCO World Heritage site of some 492 painted grottoes carved into a cliff face over roughly a thousand years, preserving the densest surviving record of Buddhist art along the Silk Road. Because the pigments are fragile, access is tightly rationed — only about 6,000 tickets are released per day, and the peak-season A-class ticket runs CNY 238 for digital films plus eight caves, dropping to CNY 140 in low season. Book up to 30 days ahead through the official site or WeChat mini-program, and aim for late spring or mid-autumn when desert temperatures are manageable.

Science & Technology

China's push toward semiconductor self-sufficiency produced several concrete milestones this year. Sugon Information Industry brought online the country's largest AI-for-science computing cluster, built entirely from 60,000 domestic chips and aimed at drug discovery, materials screening and large-scale simulation. More consequentially for the long game, Shanghai Yuliangsheng has begun production of deep ultraviolet lithography machines — precisely the equipment US export controls were designed to withhold. On the research side, Peking University teams published an all-optical interconnect linking conventional electronic chips that reported roughly hundredfold AI speed gains at a fraction of the usual compute draw. Industry forecasts now expect domestic chips to take a sharply larger share of enterprise computing procurement this year, with inference workloads the likeliest point of full substitution.

🔥 Trending
The "Handmade Economy" (手搓经济)
Tech / Consumer · Nationwide, concentrated in Hangzhou & Shenzhen

A genuinely bottom-up movement has taken hold across Chinese social platforms in which students, hobbyists and solo developers — not funded startups — ship small commercial software products largely by themselves. The enabling ingredient is a crop of cheap, capable homegrown language models, particularly Alibaba's Qwen3 and Zhipu AI's GLM series, which have built a devoted following among indie developers for their coding strength and low cost relative to foreign alternatives. What makes it resonate is the reversal of the usual hierarchy: a creator with sharp market insight but no engineering team can now go from idea to polished product in days, and a growing number are turning side projects into real income. The scale is not trivial — more than 16 million one-person companies were registered nationwide as of April 2026, and commentators have started framing the trend as evidence that small tools can carry big ideas in a job market where 20% youth unemployment has made self-employment less a choice than a necessity.

Thursday, September 3, 2026 · Europe

United Kingdom

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

Westminster returned from summer recess on Tuesday 1 September, and the week has been dominated by Andy Burnham's first full sitting weeks as prime minister following his arrival in Downing Street on 20 July. Burnham made his debut at the despatch box for Prime Minister's Questions on Wednesday, and his government has framed its programme around "rewiring the state" and pushing significantly greater powers out to local and regional authorities. The Hansard Society's parliamentary bulletin notes the Commons opened the term with a debate on strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs), while the Armed Forces Bill entered clause-by-clause scrutiny in Committee of the Whole House, with a second sitting due on 8 September. Fiscal detail remains the outstanding question: a budget statement is promised for October alongside a ten-year plan, and until then the legislative agenda is being read closely for signals about how the devolution push will be funded.

Economy & Business

Inflation is moving in the wrong direction as the new government settles in. CPI inflation reached 2.9% in July, up from 2.6% in June, and the Bank of England's central projection has it peaking near 3.2% in the fourth quarter, driven largely by energy costs linked to Middle East conflict. A second increase to the Ofgem household price cap is expected in October, which would add further pressure. The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75% at its July meeting, but the vote split three ways against, with a minority arguing for an immediate rise to 4.0% on fears that energy costs are feeding into broader prices. Both the August CPI release on 16 September and the MPC's decision the following day now sit as the month's key economic markers.

Military & Security

Counter-drone capability and allied technology partnerships have driven the week's defence news. Saab UK has delivered Giraffe 1X radar systems to the Royal Air Force for detecting and tracking uncrewed aerial systems on deployed operations, while Serco has taken a five-year Ministry of Defence contract to keep the early warning radar at RAF Fylingdales in North Yorkshire running. On the international side, the UK has signed an AI cooperation agreement with Ukraine covering joint development of future military capabilities, and Defence Readiness and Industry Minister Luke Pollard has opened an Indo-Pacific visit centred on radar technology cooperation with Australia. Separately, high-level talks have begun between British and Irish officials on Ireland joining the ten-nation Joint Expeditionary Force, and a review of the Stratus long-range missile programme run with France and Italy is due to report this month.

Society & Culture

Industrial action across the health service has escalated through the start of September. Estates workers at South London and Maudsley NHS Trust resumed an on-call ban on 1 September, with picket lines at Denmark Hill running 2–4 September after 97% of members backed the action in a pay dispute. Health visitors in Tower Hamlets closed their own strike ballot on 2 September, arguing over the retention of full NHS Agenda for Change pay and conditions. Hundreds of health workers also rallied outside Parliament against what union leaders characterised as a return to austerity in NHS funding. Elsewhere in the system, NHS leaders have warned that autism and ADHD assessment pathways are close to collapse under spiralling costs.

Tourism

VisitBritain's most recent published outlook forecasts 45.5 million inbound visits in 2026 spending £35.7 billion, growth of roughly 4% in volume and 7% in nominal spend over 2025, which would put visitor numbers at 105% of 2019 levels though real-terms spending still trails at about 96%. Long-haul markets were projected to grow fastest at 5% by volume and 8% by value, with European markets close behind. That forecast was compiled in January and explicitly excludes the effects of the Iran conflict; flight bookings have been tracking down year-on-year since February with sharp declines from Middle Eastern origin markets and softening demand from India, and VisitBritain has flagged that its mid-year update is likely to revise the figure downward. The Great Britain Tourism Survey covering January to June is the next official data point on domestic travel patterns.

Site to visit: The Giant's Causeway on the north Antrim coast is a UNESCO World Heritage Site made up of roughly 40,000 interlocking basalt columns formed by volcanic activity around 60 million years ago, and it remains Northern Ireland's most visited natural attraction. The stones themselves are free to walk on at any time of year; the charge applies only to the National Trust Visitor Experience. Adult admission runs about £13.50 and includes reserved parking, hourly guided tours, an audio guide in eleven languages and access to the exhibition, with online pre-booking recommended year-round and essential in summer.

Science & Technology

UK Research and Innovation opened funding calls on 1 September for collaborative projects integrating quantum computing into high-performance computational workflows, aimed at broadening practical user access rather than pure research. This sits inside UKRI's newly published five-year strategy, which commits to supporting more than 20,000 researchers across AI, quantum and other frontier technologies and to deploying the government's £38.6 billion research and innovation settlement. On the health research side, UK-run large-scale clinical trials have reported results for a single blood test capable of detecting around 50 cancer types before symptoms appear, one of the more consequential diagnostic developments of the year. Between the quantum funding round and the diagnostics work, the through-line this month is translation — moving lab results into deployable systems.

🔥 Trending
EsDeeKid & the “UK Ug” underground rap wave
Music · Liverpool

EsDeeKid is a Liverpool rapper who performs in a balaclava and has never publicly confirmed his name or age, and he has become the centre of gravity for what fans call UK Underground rap, or "UK Ug." His sound leans on cloud rap and trap textures — atmospheric lo-fi production, distorted 808s, futuristic synth lines — carried by a thick Scouse accent that anchors it firmly to the city. He now sits alongside fakemink as one of two acts pulling a raw, rowdy Scouse rap scene into wider view on both sides of the Atlantic.

WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 2, 2026 · SOUTH AMERICA

Argentina

South America · population ~46M

Politics & Government

President Javier Milei's legislative machine kept grinding through late August, with the Chamber of Deputies approving a new charter for the Central Bank on August 26 by 144 votes to 102, barring the monetary authority from financing the fiscal deficit through money creation and restoring currency-value preservation as its sole mandate; the bill now awaits an unscheduled Senate vote. Deputies also cleared accession to the Patent Cooperation Treaty on August 27, though a reservation attached to Chapter Two sends it back to the upper house. The momentum traces to La Libertad Avanza's landslide in the October 2025 midterms, which followed February's contentious labour reform package that cleared the Senate. Analysts nonetheless note softening approval numbers and increasingly public disputes inside the governing coalition, with speculation already circulating about alternative candidates on the right.

Economy & Business

The disinflation story has stalled rather than reversed: annual inflation ticked up to 33.8% in July from 33.5% in June, a vast improvement on the 211% peak but well short of the government's targets, with monthly readings stuck near 2.9%. Growth remains the brighter side of the ledger — the economy expanded 4.4% in 2025 and forecasters cluster around 3–4% for 2026, powered by energy and agribusiness, while poverty fell to 28.2% in the second half of 2025, its lowest since 2018. The central bank hit its US$10.1 billion dollar-accumulation goal in the first half and has loosened the peso's trading band to keep building reserves. The overhang is roughly US$20 billion in maturities falling due this year against uneven sectoral growth and minimal job creation.

Military & Security

Argentina is rebuilding an air-defence capability it effectively lost when the Mirage fleet retired in 2015. The first six operational F-16s arrived in December 2025 with US support, and six more are due each December through 2028 for an eventual fleet of 24. Flight operations began at Área Material Río IV while Tandil Air Base — the jets' future home — finishes modernisation; a dedicated maintenance and instruction centre opened at Tandil in March, and the 2026 budget carries a 892.5-billion-peso comprehensive security and defence system across both bases. The air force is targeting initial operational capability by year's end. Defence analysts read the purchase as much as a geopolitical signal of alignment with Washington as a hardware upgrade.

Society & Culture

Austerity is colliding hardest with the public sector's classrooms and clinics. Argentina's public higher-education system went dark for a 24-hour shutdown on August 12, called by the FEDUN and FATUN unions over salaries, budget shortfalls and the disputed University Financing Law, with a 48-hour escalation threatened for August 27–28 absent a deal. Researchers have mounted a fresh wave of protests at the Obelisk over unrestored scholarships and salaries the government was required to update under a 2025 law. On the health side, unions have marched against hospital service cuts, medicine shortages and vaccine disruptions, and Argentina's withdrawal from the WHO has drawn sustained criticism from public-health scholars.

Tourism

Inbound travel is running well ahead of last year, with Argentina receiving 4.79 million non-resident visitors in the first half of 2026, up 8.8% year on year and six consecutive months of growth. Brazil remains the dominant source market at roughly 30% of arrivals, followed by Chile, with North American and European numbers climbing on better air connectivity. Buenos Aires posted hotel occupancy between 62% and 75% over the half, helped by a premium-travel push built around its Michelin-recognised dining scene, while Mendoza wine country, Córdoba and Salta all logged gains. Patagonia is the standout: Los Glaciares National Park recorded its highest visitor numbers in years. The one sour note is the outbound imbalance — favourable rates abroad still send more Argentines out than foreigners in.

Site to visit: The Perito Moreno Glacier, in Los Glaciares National Park near El Calafate in Santa Cruz province, is a 250 km² ice mass roughly 70 metres tall at its face and one of the few advancing glaciers on earth, famous for the thunderous calving that drops apartment-sized blocks into Lago Argentino. A network of steel walkways puts visitors within a few hundred metres of the ice, and boat trips and ice trekking are sold separately. As of August 2026 park entry runs about ARS 50,000 for foreign visitors and ARS 25,000 for Argentines, bookable online up to a month ahead, with a 50% discount on a second visit within 72 hours.

Science & Technology

Resource technology is where Argentina's capital is going. Neuquén province has opened its largest Vaca Muerta acreage offering since 2016, putting 15 exploration blocks on the market at once to draw foreign operator expertise, with the shale play forecast to generate roughly US$18 billion in annual exports and a US$12.5 billion energy trade surplus. Vaca Muerta now funds about a third of Neuquén's wage bill. On the lithium side, IDB Invest has committed up to US$700 million to the Sal de Oro project in Salta, targeting some 48,000 tonnes of lithium products a year and around 620 permanent jobs, while Catamarca's Sal de Vida, Fénix and HMW phases advance under a new ten-year national lithium plan. Sustainability remains the open question, with outside researchers pressing water and emissions strategies on the basin's operators.

🔥 Trending
Marilina Bertoldi
Music · Rosario / Buenos Aires

Argentina's alt-rock scene has spent 2026 revolving around Marilina Bertoldi, the Rosario-born guitarist and songwriter who has turned five albums of confrontational, queer-forward rock into genuine mainstream pull. Her first US tour in March ran six dates through Los Angeles, Austin and New York — an outcome she admitted she hadn't imagined — and she has since anchored the country's two biggest rock festivals. At Cosquín Rock her set doubled as political theatre, with a pointed on-stage broadside against the labour reform and Milei himself, while her appearance at Rock en Baradero drew from a weekend crowd of more than 25,000. The appeal is partly that she keeps breaking the genre's boys'-club default without softening the guitars.

Monday, August 24, 2026 · South America

Chile

South America · population ~19M

Politics & Government

President José Antonio Kast's administration, now roughly five months into its term, remains dominated by the aftermath of the catastrophic July storms and by a hard-line security agenda. Sixty deputies have formally petitioned the president to grant individual pardons to Carabineros and armed-forces personnel convicted over conduct during the October 2019 protests, a request that revives one of the most divisive fault lines in Chilean politics. Kast has meanwhile pushed his security doctrine outward, arguing at regional forums that Latin American states must adopt life sentences for gang leaders and total isolation regimes in prisons to stop criminal organisations operating from behind bars. On the international stage, former president Michelle Bachelet placed fourth in the UN Security Council's first informal straw poll for the succession to Secretary-General António Guterres, keeping her candidacy alive but well behind the front-runners. Analysts continue to characterise 2026 as a pivot year for Chilean governance, with the balance between security, austerity and social spending the defining question of the term.

Economy & Business

Chile's Congress approved Kast's flagship economic package on 4 August, a sweeping bill of corporate tax cuts and investment incentives intended to revive growth, though Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz has signalled the rollout will be delayed while the government vetoes provisions added in debate and other measures go to constitutional review. Copper remains the fiscal engine: Cochilco has lifted its 2026 price forecast to US$5.95 a pound from US$5.55 in May, even as it cut the output projection to 5.27 million tonnes, 2.6% below 2025, on a weak first half at El Teniente and Quebrada Blanca. In a first for the state miner, the government on 10 August allowed Codelco to retain and capitalise the full US$2.42 billion of its 2025 profits rather than remit them to the treasury, easing pressure on a balance sheet carrying a US$21.65 billion project pipeline. Lithium is the other bright spot, with SQM posting record quarterly sales above 84,000 tonnes and Salar de Atacama expansion expected to lift national output to about 67,300 tonnes this year.

Military & Security

The centrepiece of Kast's security programme is the "Border Shield Plan", an effort to seal the roughly 1,000-kilometre northern frontier with Peru and Bolivia using physical barriers and surveillance technology; the government reports about US$18.5 million invested and an 89% increase in military and police presence, alongside a claimed 95% fall in clandestine entries in the first half of 2026. Chile has also deepened regional counter-crime architecture, leading the Santiago Regional Commitment signed in May with Argentina, Bolivia, Ecuador and Peru on intelligence sharing and joint judicial action, and sealing a bilateral "strategic security alliance" with Javier Milei's Argentina in April. In the south, the military continues to provide logistical and security support to civilian authorities in Araucanía and Biobío, where a state of emergency first declared in 2022 is still renewed on a rolling 30-day basis amid politically motivated violence. Foreign analysts note the country retains a modest force of roughly 77,000 troops on a US$5 billion budget, making the border build-out a significant reallocation of scarce capability.

Society & Culture

Chile's social temperature has stayed high since Kast's first State of the Nation address, when unions and student federations clashed with police in Valparaíso accusing the government of dismantling public healthcare, education and social rights. Student marches in Santiago drew thousands and ended with 35 arrests and injuries from water cannon and tear gas, with parallel rallies in Antofagasta and Concepción. The flashpoint was Kast's declaration that "no one who burns a bus, no one who destroys public property deserves free education", a line critics read as making schooling revocable as punishment, and his National Reconstruction bill of spending cuts now working through the Senate. Overlaying all of it is the humanitarian toll of July's storms, which killed at least 13 people, destroyed or badly damaged more than 1,100 homes and left around 150,000 without power across ten of Chile's sixteen regions.

Tourism

Tourism is one of the few unambiguously good news stories in Chile right now. Sernatur and the Undersecretariat of Tourism report more than 3.1 million international visitors in the first six months of 2026, putting the country on track to clear six million arrivals for the year after roughly 5.8 million in 2025. Long-haul demand is growing fastest: Australian arrivals jumped 80.1% year-on-year in the first half, driven almost entirely by Patagonia itineraries. Chile also took the World Travel Awards' Leading Nature Destination and Leading Adventure Tourism Destination titles for 2026, and authorities have launched a new National Tourism Activity Index to give operators and investors standardised demand-forecasting data. August falls in the southern winter low season, when the Atacama and the central ski resorts carry most of the traffic and Patagonian lodges run reduced operations.

Site to visit: Torres del Paine National Park in Magallanes is Chile's signature wilderness destination — a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve of granite spires, turquoise glacial lakes and the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, best known for the multi-day "W" and "O" circuit treks. Entry must be booked in advance through CONAF's pasesparques.cl portal rather than paid at the gate, and international visitors pay roughly US$35 in high season (1 November–31 March) and about US$18 in low season. Right now is deep low season with short daylight and many refugios closed, so the shoulder months of November and March generally offer the best balance of access and crowd levels.

Science & Technology

Chile's Atacama skies continue to anchor roughly 70% of global astronomy investment, and 2026 has been a heavy build year. The Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope now stands near the summit of Cerro Chajnantor after a 34-year development effort led from Cornell, and is expected to become the world's fastest mapping instrument at submillimetre wavelengths. At Paranal, a Chilean-led construction consortium has begun foundation work on the southern array of the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory, with the first gamma-ray telescopes due for deployment before the end of the year. On the energy-technology side, the government has walked back its earlier green hydrogen ambitions, opening public consultation on a revised 2026–2030 strategy that lowers production targets and reorients toward domestic demand and derivative products rather than bulk export.

🔥 Trending
AKRIILA
Music · Santiago

AKRIILA, born in 2003, has become the breakout face of neoperreo — a Chilean-incubated mutation of reggaeton built on heavier 808s, hyperpop textures, abrasive club production and unapologetically queer, feminist writing. Her debut album epistolares and its deluxe edition earned her a OnesToWatch Class of 2026 slot, and a 20-song Lollapalooza Chile set in March — which included unreleased material — turned domestic buzz into a genuine live reputation. What makes the rise notable is how little of it came through traditional Latin-music channels: the neoperreo scene grew out of Santiago's DIY club and internet underground, and a cluster of Chilean urban artists has ridden it into international attention. She is now touring across 14 countries with roughly two dozen dates booked, including London and Paris, making her one of the clearest signals that Santiago — not San Juan or Medellín — is where reggaeton's current experimental edge sits.
