Sunday, September 20, 2026 · Europe & Asia

Ukraine

Europe · population ~44M (pre-war estimate)

Politics & Government

US efforts to broker an end to the war regained momentum this month: special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held a three-hour Kremlin meeting with Vladimir Putin on 6 September before travelling on to Kyiv to press President Trump's latest proposal, only for the push to be undermined almost immediately when Russian missiles and drones resumed strikes after a brief lull, killing five people (Trump's new Ukraine push is already being undermined). President Volodymyr Zelensky has said elections are possible only once a ceasefire holds for several months (Zelenskyy on election timing), a question now entangled with the biggest corruption scandal of his presidency: polling suggests roughly 57% of Ukrainians now blame him for graft in state institutions, nearly double last year's figure, with several rival challengers projected to beat him head-to-head in a runoff (Is Ukraine Turning on Zelensky?). He has kept up a full diplomatic calendar regardless, signing a Ukraine-Canada "100 Year Partnership" declaration with Prime Minister Mark Carney and holding a run of bilateral meetings with European leaders this month (Office of the President of Ukraine, news).

Economy & Business

Ukraine's economy is under mounting strain: GDP grew just 0.4% year-on-year in the second quarter, one-and-a-half times weaker than first reported, as Russian strikes on energy infrastructure, a colder winter and rising energy prices weigh on output (Ukraine's economy hobbled by Russian strikes). The National Bank of Ukraine estimates Russia's blockade of Black Sea ports alone will cost roughly $2.5 billion in lost foreign-exchange earnings in the second half of the year, on top of new EU quotas on steel imports, and officials now warn full-year GDP could shrink if the shelling campaign continues into a "very difficult" winter (Analysts revise GDP forecasts for 2026). Kyiv's own economists have revised down their growth estimates to match, underscoring how directly the war's trajectory is now dictating the economic outlook (Ukraine revises down Q2 2026 GDP growth estimate).

Military & Security

On the ground, analysts say Russia's rate of advance has slowed markedly through 2026, with Ukrainian forces holding or regaining ground northwest of Hulyaipole even as Moscow expands drone strikes on rail hubs and border crossings to strangle grain exports and military supply lines (Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, September 6, 2026). The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant remained at the centre of the crisis, cut off from external power for more than a week before a locally brokered ceasefire let repair crews back in; Energoatom has logged more than 750 drone and artillery attacks on the site since late April, and Rosatom's chief met the IAEA's Rafael Grossi in Vienna on 15 September over its "sharply deteriorated" safety situation (Energoatom warns of critical safety situation, Rosatom, IAEA discuss plant safety in Vienna). Kyiv, meanwhile, is weighing a "radical" overhaul of mobilisation rules, including raising the draft's upper age limit to 50–55, as it looks to replenish depleted units without lowering the conscription floor below 25 (Ukraine weighing 'radical' mobilization changes).

Society & Culture

Ukraine's cultural institutions notched a rare wartime win this week: on 19 September the country signed the Council of Europe's convention on co-producing audiovisual works in series form, opening the door for Ukrainian producers to co-finance and distribute television series with European partners and reach new international markets (Ukraine signs Council of Europe convention). The signing came a week after heritage professionals gathered in Kyiv for a "Preserving Cultural Freedom in Ukraine" panel at the Fall 2026 Ukraine Action Summit, reflecting on efforts by museums, archives and cultural institutions to safeguard collections while continuing to operate under Russian bombardment (Preserving Ukraine's Cultural Freedom). The same week also carried Ukrainian Cinema Day and Physical Culture and Sports Day on 12 September, modest but pointed reminders that cultural life continues as the country enters its fifth wartime autumn.

Tourism

Tourism in Ukraine remains shaped by the war rather than paused by it: major governments continue to warn against non-essential travel and martial law persists nationwide, yet a blend of heritage travel, solidarity visits and domestic breaks has kept parts of the country, especially the relatively untouched west, busier than the headlines suggest (Ukraine's tourism resurgence amid conflict). Lviv has emerged as the clearest beneficiary, its old town now home to displaced Ukrainians alongside visitors, with bars and restaurants reportedly full most nights even as air-raid alerts and curfews remain part of daily routine elsewhere in the country (Tourism in Ukraine 2026: is it safe to travel?).

Site to visit: The Lviv Historic Centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose cobbled Market Square, Armenian and Latin cathedrals and fortification towers survived the Soviet era almost untouched, making it one of the best-preserved old towns in Eastern Europe. Most churches ask only a small donation to enter, the Tourist Information Centre inside Lviv's Town Hall is free to browse, and the historic core is easiest to reach on foot or by tram from the city's main railway station, which has direct rail connections from Kyiv, Warsaw and Kraków.

Science & Technology

Ukraine's drone industry keeps setting the pace for the war: independent estimates put 2026 domestic drone production at five to six million units, while roughly 280 companies now build more than 550 distinct uncrewed ground vehicle models, some logging over 10,000 logistics missions a month (Drone superpower Ukraine is now Europe's first line of defense). New AI-enabled systems are increasingly able to strike targets with greater autonomy while routing around Russian jamming, a capability credited with helping Ukraine blunt Moscow's numerical advantage on parts of the front (How Ukraine's drone innovation reversed Russia's momentum). The push now has formal European backing, too: the European Commission hosted the first meeting of the EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance in Brussels on 11 September, bringing together 19 defence contractors, including Ukraine's own Athlon Avia, to coordinate next-generation drone and counter-drone production (Commission hosts first meeting of EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance).

🔥 Trending
PlantIn
Consumer tech / plant-ID app · Kyiv

PlantIn, a plant-identification and care app built in Kyiv by brothers Mykhaylo and Dmytro Grynets, has quietly become one of Ukraine's biggest consumer tech exports without any celebrity backing or ad blitz. Since launching in 2020 the app has passed 35 million downloads and now counts more than 40 million users worldwide who point their phone camera at a leaf or flower to identify it against a database of over 24,000 species with a claimed 99% accuracy (PlantIn App Review 2026). Its rise has been driven almost entirely by word-of-mouth and App Store discovery rather than marketing spend: it has topped the global App Store rankings outright and become the No. 1 plant app in the US market, while collecting "App of the Day" features across the UK, Canada, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand (Ukrainian PlantIn hits 35 million downloads, PlantIn ranks first in global App Store). For a Ukrainian-founded product built and maintained through years of war, that kind of organic, borderless popularity is itself part of the story.

Turkey

Asia · population ~86M

Politics & Government

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's inner circle has now confirmed what had long been assumed: a senior adviser said on 17 September that the 72-year-old leader intends to run again in an early presidential election likely in April 2028, the first official word on his 2028 candidacy, with Justice Minister Akın Gürlek adding that "an election will be held to re-elect our esteemed president" (Erdogan to compete in 2028 elections, officials say). Triggering an early vote requires three-fifths of parliament, 360 of 600 seats, which Erdoğan's AKP and its allies do not currently hold with 326 seats, leaving the government roughly 34 votes short and fuelling speculation over which smaller parties might be courted to close the gap (Top adviser says Erdoğan will run again in 2028). The timeline is complicated by the continued imprisonment of Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, long seen as Erdoğan's strongest rival, who has been jailed since March 2025 on corruption charges and was separately handed a two-year sentence and a political ban, while critics point to a wider pattern of arrests of activists, journalists and opposition politicians as evidence of deepening authoritarianism (Senior AKP lawmaker on a parliamentary vote for early elections).

Economy & Business

Growth is losing momentum even as inflation slowly cools: GDP expanded 2.3% year-on-year in the second quarter, down from an upwardly revised 2.6% in the first, with a 12.7% plunge in exports dragging on the total even as domestic demand, construction and government spending held up (Turkey's GDP growth loses momentum). Annual inflation eased to 31.51% in August, its third straight monthly slowdown, and the central bank has held its benchmark rate at 37% for four consecutive meetings, leaning on foreign-exchange interventions rather than rate cuts to manage a lira that touched a record low near 48.5 to the dollar this month (Turkish Lira). Flagship carrier Turkish Airlines is absorbing its own share of the squeeze, with executives citing an Iran-conflict-driven spike in jet fuel costs that could add as much as $3.5 billion to this year's fuel bill even as the airline pushes ahead with fleet expansion and a planned 2028 return of a premium economy cabin (Turkish Airlines to launch premium economy).

Military & Security

Turkey's security posture along its southern border remains largely unchanged despite the PKK's announced dissolution: Ankara has continued backing Damascus's new government in its campaign against the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces while insisting its military outposts across northern Iraq and Syria are permanent fixtures of national security policy rather than bargaining chips (Turkey is searching for a way out of Syria's impasse). Turkish officials say operations against remaining militants will continue "until there is not a single terrorist left," even as Abdullah Öcalan's call for PKK fighters to disarm continues to work its way through implementation (Conflict Between Turkey and Armed Kurdish Groups). A NATO summit hosted in Ankara this month gave Turkey and the European Union an opening to recast their fraught relationship around shared security concerns, from Russia's war in Ukraine to Washington's reduced engagement in European defence, even as the two sides remain at odds over Cyprus, migration and Turkey's stalled EU accession bid (NATO summit gives Europe and Turkey a strategic reset opportunity).

Tourism

Turkey is on track to keep its place as the world's fourth most-visited country after welcoming 60.6 million international visitors and more than $56.3 billion in tourism revenue in its most recent full year, moving ahead of Italy, Mexico, the UK, Germany, Japan and Greece (Turkey Tourism 2026: the world's 4th most visited country). The picture has softened slightly this year, though: foreign arrivals slipped 2.3% year-on-year to 27.9 million in the January–July period, with July arrivals down 0.3%, even as Russia, Germany and the UK continue to anchor the country's top source markets (How Iran, Russia and other markets are driving Turkey's tourism). Istanbul, Antalya and Bodrum remain the country's tourism engines even as the softer numbers prompt operators to lean harder on off-peak and shoulder-season promotion heading into autumn.

Site to visit: Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in central Anatolia, is famed for its cone-shaped "fairy chimney" rock formations and the cave churches, dwellings and underground cities that early Christian and Byzantine inhabitants carved into the soft volcanic stone. The classic way to see it is a sunrise hot-air balloon flight over the valley, typically lasting 50–75 minutes and priced from around €85 upward depending on operator and season, with the calmest winds and clearest skies running April through October and quieter, cheaper flights from November to March.

Science & Technology

Turkey's earthquake scientists continue refining the picture of the fault networks that threaten Istanbul: new research shows variations in underground temperature and sediment thickness segment the Main Marmara Fault in ways that help determine where ruptures start, how far they travel and where they stop, part of a decade-long study of moderate quakes progressing eastward toward the city (How hidden factors beneath Istanbul shape earthquake risk). That research is feeding into disaster planning, with AFAD-linked techno-clusters spanning universities and private companies developing AI-assisted tools to flag structurally fragile buildings ahead of the long-anticipated İstanbul earthquake (Turkey: earthquake technologies market intelligence). Beyond seismology, Turkey's startup scene has leaned further into artificial intelligence this year, with 37 AI-focused ventures among the roughly 97 funded Turkish startups that raised a combined $181 million in the first seven months of 2026, even as gaming studios continued to pull in the largest share of venture capital (Startups in Turkey News, September 2026).

🔥 Trending
Parlar Pastanesi
Food / consumer phenomenon · Fatih, Istanbul

The bakery going viral across Turkish and international TikTok right now isn't a startup at all but a 68-year-old family pastry shop in Istanbul's Fatih district. Parlar Pastanesi was opened in 1958 by Kosovar migrants Süleyman and Abdül Parlar and is now run by second-generation owner Fatih Parlar, still serving the same syrup-soaked revani, tel kadayıf and tulumba it always has, with no rebrand and no marketing budget behind its sudden fame (Parlar Pastanesi, Ekşi Sözlük). Food creators filming its dripping, syrup-soaked revani and Fatih sarması have racked up millions of views over the past year, turning a neighbourhood institution into a must-visit stop for foreign food tourists, with queues now regularly running past half an hour outside its modest storefront (Parlar Pastanesi, Kültür Envanteri). The momentum is purely organic: no influencer deal or paid campaign kicked it off, just diners filming what has always been sitting on the counter.

Saturday, September 19, 2026 · Africa

Nigeria

Africa · population ~225M

Politics & Government

President Bola Tinubu ordered a full investigation and demanded the prosecution of any officials found culpable after 37 detainees, including children, died inside a Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps facility in Minna, Niger State, following raids on suspected illegal gold mines in the nearby Wushishi-Lukoto area. Niger State Governor Umaru Bago suspended NSCDC commandant Suberu Siyaka Aniviye and said investigators are examining possible suffocation and exposure to hazardous mining substances as the cause of death, after a survivor described severe overcrowding inside the cells. Separately, and with an eye on the 2027 election cycle, the Lagos chapter of the ruling APC inaugurated a 1,235-member campaign council, including a 36-member Security and Intelligence Directorate, on 18 September.

Economy & Business

Nigeria's economy is gaining momentum, with GDP growing 4.43% year-on-year in the second quarter, its strongest pace in two years, led by agriculture, telecoms, real estate and trade. The naira has been unusually stable, holding around N1,329/$ for four straight sessions in mid-September, its longest stretch of stability since July 2025, while external reserves have crossed $54 billion for the first time since December 2008. Tax collections reached roughly N27.1 trillion in the first seven months of the year, and officials are framing the numbers as evidence the reform programme is stabilising the economy even as households continue to absorb elevated living costs.

Military & Security

The northeast insurgency has resurged: the JAS faction of Boko Haram attacked Darul Jamal, a recently resettled community of returned displaced people in Bama LGA, Borno State, killing at least 60 people including soldiers, in one of the deadliest single assaults on civilians in months. Analysts tracking the conflict note that while JAS has turned back to targeting civilians, the rival ISWAP faction continues to focus its attacks on military personnel and assets, and banditry persists largely unchecked across the northwest.

Society & Culture

Anger over the Minna detainee deaths spilled into the streets this week: authorities imposed an overnight curfew after security forces opened fire as hundreds of protesters marched through the city. In Lagos, the Academic Staff Union of Universities declared a total strike across three state universities after the government missed a 13 September deadline to implement its December 2025 welfare agreement, and the Nigeria Labour Congress and Trade Union Congress have signalled they may call a nationwide strike over worsening insecurity. In Ondo State, student protesters stood down after Governor Lucky Aiyedatiwa promised to resolve a separate university funding dispute within two weeks.

Tourism

The Federal Ministry of Art, Culture, Tourism and the Creative Economy formally launched "Ember to Remember 2026," a 100-day national tourism season running September through December, at a stakeholders' conference in Lagos on 14 September. The campaign is part of a push toward a $100 billion tourism and creative-economy target, building on roughly 1.2 million international arrivals in 2025, up 20% year-on-year. International carriers including Emirates, Qatar Airways and British Airways are adding flight frequency to Lagos and Abuja as more international hotel chains open in the country.

Site to visit: The Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the edge of Osogbo in Osun State, is a dense stretch of riverside forest regarded as the dwelling place of the Yoruba river goddess Osun, dotted with shrines, sculptures and artworks built up over centuries of worship. Entry costs only around N1,000 (roughly $0.65) and includes a guide, and the grove is busiest and most vivid during the two-week Osun-Osogbo Festival each August, though it is open to quieter visits year-round via road from Osogbo.

Science & Technology

Uber wound down its ride-hailing operations in Nigeria and Uganda effective 2 September, citing a strategic review, after years of rising fuel and commission disputes and intensifying competition from Bolt and inDrive. Starlink, meanwhile, has halted new residential subscriptions in Lagos, Abuja and other major cities after its network hit capacity, leaving only a pricier N159,000-a-month Priority plan for new business customers. On the innovation side, the National Agency for Science and Engineering Infrastructure is moving to commercialise homegrown solar-irrigation, electric-vehicle, CNG and medical-diagnostic technologies, part of a startup ecosystem that now counts roughly 1,490 active companies, the largest in West Africa.

🔥 Trending
Zaylevelten
Music · Ikorodu, Lagos

Working under the producer alias Tenski, he built his self-produced album "then 1t g0t crazy" around gritty street vernacular and rapid flow-switching between English, Pidgin, Yoruba and Igbo, and the viral single "Maye" turned his ad-libs "Maye" and "Yami" into everyday slang on Nigerian TikTok. South Africa

Africa · population ~61M

Politics & Government

President Cyril Ramaphosa was booked off from public engagements on medical advice on 17 September after returning from the BRICS leaders' summit in India, asking cabinet ministers to represent him at scheduled events in his absence. The move came as tension inside the Government of National Unity resurfaced: the Democratic Alliance approached the Electoral Court to challenge the ANC's candidate lists in six municipalities after the ANC missed the late-August submission deadline, even as officials insisted the coalition "is intact" ahead of the 4 November local government elections. Ramaphosa also extended the deadline of the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry into policing corruption this week to allow further expert testimony.

Economy & Business

South Africa's economy contracted 0.2% in the second quarter, ending six consecutive quarters of growth, as mining (-3.0%), manufacturing (-1.8%) and trade (-1.9%) all fell back under the fallout from the Iran war's fuel-price shock. Employment slipped and the official unemployment rate climbed to 33.6%, up from 32.7% in the first quarter, as 345,000 more people joined the ranks of the unemployed. There was some relief on prices, with headline inflation cooling to 4.3% in July from 5.0% in June after the South African Reserve Bank held its repo rate at 7%, and the rand has firmed to trade near 16 to the dollar in early September even as the National Treasury trims its full-year growth forecast to 1.5%.

Military & Security

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry resumed hearings this week with its focus on the Johannesburg Metropolitan Police Department, where internal-affairs investigator Elmarie de Beer testified about an alleged criminal syndicate operating inside the JMPD's Tactical Response Unit, including claims tied to a R14.9 million gemstone theft and protection of a suspect with cartel links. The commission has voiced alarm that serious misconduct cases against officers are being allowed to "die" internally rather than reach prosecution. Away from Johannesburg, Western Cape police reported nearly 70,000 arrests and more than 600 firearms seized over three months of operations against gangs and extortion, though several Cape Town precincts remain among the country's worst crime hotspots.

Society & Culture

South Africa is mourning nine women found dead in the Ekurhuleni area since July; at a 17 September Memorial Dialogue against Gender-Based Violence and Femicide, Ramaphosa's message of condolence was delivered by Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia in his absence. The country is also watching a fast-rising mpox cluster, with 18 confirmed cases and one probable case reported, concentrated in the Western Cape. The episodes land against the backdrop of this year's anti-migrant "March and March" mobilisation, which saw more than 900 people arrested nationwide around a symbolic deadline for undocumented foreigners to leave the country.

Tourism

South Africa's Tourism Month 2026 is under way under the theme "Growing South Africa's Tourism Sector in the Digital Era," with Limpopo named the province of focus. International arrivals continue to climb, with 5.58 million foreign visitors recorded between January and June, up 12.3% year-on-year, and July alone bringing 991,696 arrivals, a further 12.5% gain. To boost domestic travel, SANParks opened participating national parks free of charge during National Parks Week from 7 to 13 September, alongside discounted Sho't Left Travel Week packages encouraging South Africans to holiday at home.

Site to visit: Robben Island, the UNESCO World Heritage Site in Table Bay where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned for 18 of his 27 years in custody, remains one of the country's most powerful historic sites, its limestone quarry and cell blocks preserved much as they were under apartheid. Tours run about 3.5 to 4 hours and combine a roughly 30-minute ferry crossing with a bus tour of the island and a walk through the prison led by a former political prisoner; the only way to reach the island is by ferry from the Nelson Mandela Gateway at the V&A Waterfront, and sailings are weather-dependent, so booking well ahead is recommended.

Science & Technology

South Africa was named Country of Honor at the 2026 Pujiang Innovation Forum, which closed in Shanghai on 14 September, as officials pressed the case for deeper international research cooperation. At home, delegates at a parallel policy forum stressed that the country needs to build a stronger science talent pipeline by investing in young researchers well before they reach university. South African consumers, meanwhile, are feeling a global squeeze: a worldwide RAM shortage driven by AI data-centre demand has pushed local prices for MacBooks and games consoles sharply higher, with some laptop models up more than 30% in a single quarter.

🔥 Trending
Timbuk2
Tech / consumer platform · Cape Town

Timbuk2 is a Cape Town AI startup, founded by data scientist Matimba Julian Nkuna, that has built grassroots momentum by paying ordinary township residents to map the informal businesses — spaza shops, hair salons, car washes — that keep local economies running but are invisible to platforms like Google Maps. Using nothing but their phones, these "data agents" photograph shopfronts and log business details through Timbuk2's dashboard for cash payouts, turning mapping into a genuine local income stream rather than an app promotion. When collectors walked the streets of Tlhabane, Rustenburg, they found that 79% of the spaza shops there were previously undocumented, underscoring how much of the country's informal economy has been operating entirely off the map. The model has spread by word of mouth among township youth looking for legitimate earning opportunities, with Timbuk2 now aiming to extend it to other African countries.

Friday, September 18, 2026 · Asia

Vietnam

Asia · population ~98M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng led a delegation to New Delhi on 12–13 September for the 18th BRICS Summit, where Vietnam took part as one of the group's ten partner countries. On the sidelines at Hyderabad House, Hưng held bilateral talks with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi that focused on deepening defence and security cooperation, including a proposed foreign-and-defence-minister "2+2" dialogue mechanism, alongside expanded energy and critical-minerals ties and a push to lift bilateral trade to $25 billion by 2030. Vietnamese officials framed the trip as advancing the country's wider multilateral diplomacy, coming as Hanoi seeks to broaden its partnerships beyond its traditional major-power relationships.

Economy & Business

Vietnam's economy is entering the back half of 2026 from a position of strength, with GDP growing 8.18% year-on-year in the first half of the year, among the fastest paces in Asia. Index provider FTSE Russell has confirmed that Vietnam's upgrade from frontier to secondary emerging-market status takes effect on 21 September, a reclassification that follows Hanoi's removal of pre-funding requirements for foreign equity trades and is expected to draw new index-tracking capital into the stock market. Even so, Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng used a mid-September government session to demand "highest determination" from ministries to hit the government's full-year growth target with only a few months left on the calendar.

Military & Security

Defence Minister Phan Văn Giang travelled to Beijing for the 13th Xiangshan Forum, where he met Chinese Central Military Commission Vice Chairman Zhang Shengmin on 16 September to discuss coordinating positions on major security issues and deepening bilateral military cooperation. The meeting came shortly after a batch of amended defence legislation, including a revised Law on National Defence and Law on Officers of the Vietnam People's Army, took effect on 1 September as part of a restructuring meant to build a leaner, more modern force. The changes are tied to the ruling party's target for the PAVN to become a "revolutionary, regular, elite and modern" military by 2030, with modernisation increasingly weighted toward maritime and aerial capability rather than the land-centric posture of past decades.

Society & Culture

Days of torrential rain across northern and north-central Vietnam this week pushed several rivers to dangerous levels and submerged more than 2,000 homes, with Ninh Bình and Thanh Hóa provinces, south of Hanoi, hit hardest and over 1,000 households cut off by floodwater; some areas recorded more than 50 centimetres of rain in a matter of days. Roughly 10,014 hectares of rice and other crops were damaged or destroyed, with the heaviest losses in Ninh Bình, Thanh Hóa and Nghệ An, and in Thanh Hóa authorities told schoolchildren to stay home because flooded roads made the commute unsafe. Forecasters are now warning of a second wave, with a cold air mass expected to bring five days of heavy rain to the south, including flood-prone Ho Chi Minh City, running from 17 through around 21 September.

Tourism

Vietnam's tourism rebound continued through the summer, with 15.9 million international arrivals recorded in the first eight months of 2026, up 14.4% year-on-year and the strongest eight-month total on record, against a full-year target of 25 million. Russian arrivals were a standout, growing 165.7% year-on-year to more than a million and making Russia the third-largest source market behind China and South Korea, while the five-day National Day holiday in late August drew roughly 6.5 million domestic and international travellers. With the peak season over, airlines have begun cutting fares on domestic routes as demand eases into the autumn low season, and the country is gearing up for the Mid-Autumn Festival on 25 September.

Site to visit: Hạ Long Bay, a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site in Quảng Ninh province some 180 kilometres east of Hanoi, is famed for the roughly 1,600 limestone karsts and islets rising from its emerald waters, many honeycombed with caves and hidden lagoons reachable only by small boat. Most visitors join an overnight cruise from Hanoi that combines cave stops, kayaking and a night anchored among the karsts; general bay entrance fees run about 260,000–310,000 VND (roughly US$11–13), and the cooler, drier months from October to December offer the clearest skies for photography.

Science & Technology

Deputy Prime Minister Hồ Quốc Dũng chaired a national conference on 15 September to roll out Decision No. 1425/QD-TTg, the government's first National Digital Architecture Framework, which is meant to give ministries and localities a common blueprint for connecting and sharing government data rather than building duplicate, incompatible systems. Under the same push, the National Technology Innovation Fund will prioritise artificial-intelligence applications across six sectors: manufacturing and processing, agriculture, healthcare and pharmaceuticals, logistics and supply chains, trade and corporate management, and state administration. Officials have cast the framework as the technical backbone for Vietnam's broader digital-transformation drive, aimed at cutting duplicated investment across agencies while improving services for citizens and businesses.

🔥 Trending
Tuyệt Đối Điện Ảnh ("Absolute Cinema")
Internet culture / slang phenomenon · Nationwide, Vietnamese social media

The phrase making the rounds on Vietnamese TikTok, Facebook and Threads right now is "tuyệt đối điện ảnh," a Vietnamese-language remix of the international "Absolute Cinema" meme, which traces back to a 2020 photo of director Martin Scorsese and spread through Twitch gaming chat before crossing into Vietnam's Gen Z internet vernacular. Locally the phrase has broken free of its film-meme origins: young Vietnamese now caption any strikingly beautiful real-life moment — a dramatic sunset, light falling through a window at dusk — as "tuyệt đối điện ảnh," treating everyday life as if shot through a director's lens (Chus.vn's explainer on the trend). There is no brand, agency or celebrity behind its spread; it has travelled the way most of Vietnam's Gen Z slang now does, organically through short-form video and reaction comments, and outlets tracking 2025–2026 youth-language trends have listed it among the year's most recognisable examples of the format (Happynest's guide to joining the trend).

Thursday, September 17, 2026 · Europe

France

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

President Emmanuel Macron's authority keeps eroding: an Ipsos bva-CESI poll for La Tribune Dimanche, fielded 9–10 September, put his approval at a record-low 16%, down five points since July, with favourable views collapsing even among his own centrist voters (Macron, Lecornu popularity falls to record lows). Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu fares little better at 20% and is simultaneously trying to hold his fragile minority government together as he opens the fight over the 2027 budget, telling ministers in a mid-September letter that non-defence spending must stay "strictly at the same level" as 2026 because "we no longer have the possibility of financing everything, but we still have the freedom to choose" (France maps out deep 2027 budget cuts). Amid the domestic turbulence Macron kept up a busy diplomatic calendar, hosting Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi at the Élysée on 14 September to sign accords on energy, air defence and development finance aimed at diversifying Middle East oil and gas export routes (France, Iraq deepen energy, defence ties).

Economy & Business

The growth picture darkened again this week: INSEE and Economy Minister Roland Lescure confirmed on 11 September that France's 2026 GDP forecast has been cut to just 0.4%, less than half of last year's 0.9% expansion and down from an already-reduced 0.7% June estimate, after output contracted 0.2% in the first quarter and stagnated in the second (France cuts growth forecast as economy lags behind neighbours). The statistics agency also warned that the labour market is now "more degraded than elsewhere in Europe," with the private sector expected to shed roughly 52,000 jobs this year and unemployment climbing to 8.6% by December, up from 8.0% a year earlier (French economy set for sharply slower growth). The weak numbers are colliding directly with the 2027 budget fight, where Lescure wants the deficit held under 5% of GDP, a target the government says will require roughly €2.5 billion of extra savings on top of the spending freeze (France calls for tough budget cuts ahead of 2027 election).

Military & Security

France's Air and Space Force opened its most ambitious power-projection exercise yet on 8–9 September, sending four Rafale F4 fighters plus refuelling tankers and transport aircraft from Istres air base on a 40,000-kilometre, 40-day loop through the Arctic, the Indo-Pacific and the Middle East, codenamed Pégase 26 (French military jets launch a 40-day global flight). The roughly 300-strong contingent flew nearly eleven hours and refuelled five times mid-air to reach Alaska, where the Rafales trained alongside US Air Force F-22 Raptors, before continuing on to Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, India, Qatar and Réunion ahead of a final stop in Egypt (What French Rafale jets aim to prove with PEGASE 26). The mission is explicitly framed around validating a new Arctic air route as competition over the high north intensifies among NATO members and Russia, and it doubles as a demonstration that France's nuclear-armed air force can reach and defend partners far from the mainland (France's newest global air mission covers three continents).

Society & Culture

Former Paris mayor Anne Hidalgo told a Senate inquiry committee on 15 September that "collective failure" within city institutions had allowed a decade-long wave of abuse against children in Paris's state nurseries and after-school programmes, with police now examining more than 200 allegations of mistreatment, violence and sexual abuse (Paris ex-mayor says 'collective failure' led to abuse). The hearing came a day after a Paris court handed down the first conviction in the scandal, ordering an ankle tag for a convicted school aide, a verdict that did little to quiet furious parents, and prosecutors have since opened investigations into Hidalgo and current mayor Emmanuel Grégoire over their alleged failure to protect children in danger (First conviction fails to quell parents' anger). The scandal is unfolding alongside a slow-burn wave of labour unrest, with SUD-Rail confirming that rail workers will join public-sector unions in a nationwide strike on 29 September over pay, after the SNCF group posted a €1.2 billion first-half profit while offering only a 0.18% general pay rise (French rail workers call September strike).

Tourism

France is on course to hold its title as the world's most-visited country, having drawn roughly 102 million international visitors in 2025 and running at a pace that could push 2026 arrivals higher still, even as authorities focus on getting visitors to spend more rather than simply arrive in greater numbers (France is holding at 102 million visitors). This summer's heatwaves and wildfires nudged domestic holidaymakers away from the traditional Mediterranean coast and toward Normandy and Brittany, and the shift has held into autumn even as September-through-December air bookings run about 4% higher year-on-year, led by a 19% jump in demand from Southeast Asia (France tourism proves resilient despite heat and wildfires). Paris, meanwhile, is mid-way through its September cultural calendar, with Paris Design Week running across the month and a slate of heritage-themed events drawing visitors into the capital's museums, ateliers and historic quarters (Paris unveils September 2026 travel experiences).

Site to visit: Mont-Saint-Michel, the tidal island abbey off the Normandy coast that has drawn pilgrims since the 8th century and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979, is France's second most-visited monument after Versailles, pulling in around three million people a year. Its Romanesque and Gothic abbey crowns a granite outcrop ringed by continental Europe's highest tides, with a medieval village of stone houses and cobbled lanes climbing toward it. Mornings before 10am in autumn and winter are the quietest time to visit, and the island is reachable on foot, by bike, or by free shuttle from the mainland car parks.

Science & Technology

Paris hosted the inaugural International Space Summit at the Grand Palais on 9–10 September, drawing representatives from 90 countries and around 60 heads of state, government or ministers alongside nearly 1,100 space-sector companies, opened by President Macron and closing with European governments pledging more than $22 billion in new space commitments (International Space Summit in Paris). The gathering focused on space security and defence, the commercial space economy, responsible governance and long-term sustainability as low Earth orbit grows more crowded and contested (Europe bets $22B on space sovereignty at Paris summit). It followed within days of Germany's Isar Aerospace becoming the first commercial company to reach orbit from continental European soil, launching its Spectrum rocket from Andøya, Norway on 5 September — a milestone French officials cited as evidence Europe's push for launch independence is gaining real traction (Private rocket reaches orbit from European soil).

Wednesday, September 16, 2026 · Asia

Japan

Asia · population ~124M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi enters the second year of her premiership with a reshuffle built around continuity rather than change: after reshuffling LDP executive posts on 16 September and her cabinet the following day, she is widely expected to keep Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama, Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi in their posts, with Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara and senior LDP figures also staying put. It is her first reshuffle since taking office last October, and comes as she works to stabilise her governing coalition ahead of the LDP's 2027 leadership contest. The emphasis on stability over a fresh start reflects how little room Takaichi has to spend political capital on personnel risk this early in her term.

Economy & Business

Growth is real but slowing: revised figures released in early September put second-quarter GDP at an annualised 1.4%, still down from the first quarter's pace, as private consumption made no net contribution to output amid elevated living costs. The Bank of Japan's policy board meets on 17–18 September, with markets pricing in another rate rise partly to relieve pressure on a yen that touched a 40-year low against the dollar last month. Households are still absorbing the fallout from last year's rice shortage: prices have eased from their peak but remain well above pre-crisis levels, keeping food inflation a live political issue.

Military & Security

Tokyo's threat assessment hardened again this quarter. The Cabinet's Defence of Japan 2026 white paper, approved in early August, names China the country's "greatest strategic challenge" and warns that the PLA is projecting power ever further beyond the First Island Chain into the Pacific. It also flags deepening alignment between Beijing and Moscow, pointing to joint China-Russia bomber flights near Japan following the two leaders' appearance alongside Kim Jong Un at a military parade last autumn. In response, Japan continues to build a "southern shield" of anti-access capability along its southwestern island chain, with the country's foundational Three Strategic Documents due an accelerated revision this year.

Society & Culture

Japan crossed a demographic milestone this week: the health ministry confirmed on 15 September that the number of centenarians has topped 100,000 for the first time, reaching 107,677 — up almost 8,000 on last year and continuing a 56-year run of growth, with women accounting for nearly 88% of the total. The milestone lands alongside continued overall decline, with Japan's population still falling as the birth rate sets fresh record lows, and officials now estimate the country will need roughly 2.4 million elderly-care workers this year, up from 2.1 million in 2024. The juxtaposition — record longevity next to a shrinking, ageing workforce — continues to frame nearly every domestic policy debate.

Tourism

Inbound tourism is setting records again: foreign arrivals between January and September rose 17.7% to around 31.65 million, the fastest pace ever to clear 30 million within a year. Rather than simply celebrate the numbers, authorities are layering on new charges: a tripled departure tax of ¥3,000 took effect on 1 July, Kyoto's accommodation tax was raised sharply at the top end in March, and the Yoshida Trail up Mount Fuji still caps climbers at 4,000 a day and charges ¥4,000 to climb. Fushimi Inari, the Arashiyama bamboo grove and Shibuya Crossing remain the most congested pressure points as the country shifts from pure visitor-volume promotion toward a more managed model.

Site to visit: The Sado Island Gold Mines in Niigata Prefecture, inscribed as Japan's newest UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2024, preserve nearly 400 years of hand-worked gold and silver mining tunnels that once funded the Tokugawa shogunate. Visitors take a car ferry or high-speed jetfoil from Niigata Port to Ryotsu, then a roughly 70-minute bus ride to the historic mine complex; entry to the associated gold park runs about ¥2,300 in September, and autumn's crisp air and turning leaves make it the season locals recommend.

Science & Technology

Japan's chip strategy is moving from paper to output. TSMC has finalised plans for its first 3-nanometer production in Japan, a roughly $17 billion Kumamoto plant, while domestic challenger Rapidus says it can price its 2-nanometer chips at or below TSMC's levels once mass production begins in 2027, with 2nm test-chip runs for AI customers due by year-end. On the research side, RIKEN has switched on RIKYU, a new supercomputer built around 1,600 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs, dedicating it to "AI for Science" work spanning materials, climate and drug discovery as part of a wider 2,140-GPU build-out with Nvidia supporting Japan's sovereign AI push.

Food / consumer phenomenon · Fukuoka (Hakata), spreading nationwide

The country's most viral food right now came not from a chain or a marketing budget but from one small Hakata bakery. Full Full, a modest Fukuoka shop, built its name on a split baguette stuffed with a butter-and-mentaiko (spicy cod roe) filling that oozes out with each bite — a Fukuoka original dating to the early 2000s that for years sold only a handful of loaves a day. Videos of the filling spilling out on camera turned it into a nationwide craze this year: the shop now bakes and sells thousands of loaves on its busiest days, and copycat versions have spread through bakeries and convenience stores across Japan. A recent TBS segment summed up its appeal as endlessly moreish — crispy outside, "juwa" (juicy) inside — and it has grown with no celebrity backer and no ad spend, purely on word of mouth and short-form video during a year of otherwise high grocery prices.

Wednesday, September 9, 2026 · Asia

India

Asia · population ~1.44B

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Narendra Modi spent the early part of the week on a domestic circuit, visiting Gujarat and Maharashtra on 8 September and appearing at the centenary celebrations of Shri Ram College of Commerce in Delhi. The judiciary delivered a long-awaited verdict on 5 September when a special NIA court in Bastar convicted all ten accused in the 2013 Darbha valley Naxal ambush that wiped out much of Chhattisgarh’s Congress leadership, closing a case that had run for thirteen years. Domestic attention was pulled back to Delhi a day later when a five-storey building collapsed in the capital, killing seven. The governing BJP continues to operate from a position of unusual strength after its breakthrough in West Bengal, though the Election Commission’s contested revision of the electoral rolls remains a live grievance for opposition parties heading into the next cycle of state votes.

Economy & Business

India remains the fastest-growing major economy, with GDP expanding 7.8% year-on-year in the June quarter and 7.7% across the full FY2026 — the strongest showing since the post-Covid rebound. The rupee has been the softer spot: it hit an all-time low of 96.8 to the dollar in May as the Middle East conflict pushed up oil import costs, and has since clawed back to roughly 94, with the RBI rebuilding reserves and analysts expecting a 94–96 band through year-end. Trade policy has done much of the heavy lifting: a February deal with Washington cut reciprocal tariffs on Indian goods from 25% to 18%, and in January India and the EU finally closed a free trade agreement after twenty years of negotiation. Consumption and investment are carrying growth in roughly equal measure, though exporters remain exposed to a volatile global tariff environment.

Military & Security

The most consequential development of the week was quiet and procedural: on 6 September the Indian and Chinese armies held their first-ever Corps Commander-level meeting in the Eastern Sector, at the Vacha-Damai Border Personnel Meeting Point in Arunachal Pradesh, with Lt Gen Girish Kalia leading a twelve-member Indian delegation. That meeting extends a dialogue mechanism that had been confined to Ladakh since Galwan, and follows an eight-point consensus on border management that added new meeting points and two military hotlines in the Eastern and Middle Sectors. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh travelled to Sri Lanka from 8 to 10 September for consultations with the Colombo leadership, part of a broader effort to keep Indian Ocean partners close. Further afield, the IAF’s Sarang team — the world’s only five-helicopter display formation, flying the indigenous ALH Dhruv — performed at Egypt’s El Alamein International Airshow.

Society & Culture

The youth protest movement that erupted in July continues to reshape Indian politics. Triggered by the leak of a medical entrance exam paper that forced more than two million candidates to re-sit, the satirically named Cockroach Janta Party grew from an internet joke into a national movement demanding the education minister’s resignation. Students defied a Delhi lockdown to march on Parliament and camped for weeks at Jantar Mantar; police tear-gassed and baton-charged the crowds, and at least 178 people were injured. Human Rights Watch subsequently accused authorities of using excessive force. Modi has promised fast-track courts for exam-leak offenders but has declined to sack the minister — and the underlying grievances, a high cost of living and a shortage of quality graduate jobs, have not gone anywhere.

Tourism

India’s inbound tourism recovery has stalled. Foreign tourist arrivals fell 8.1% to 9.15 million, still well short of the 10.9 million recorded in 2019, and industry forecasts suggest arrivals will stay broadly flat this season as higher oil prices and conflict-driven airfares bite. Operators point to visa friction, transport complexity, safety perceptions and tourist scams as the recurring complaints, alongside sharper competition from Southeast Asian rivals. The brighter number is spend rather than headcount — the WTTC reports record international visitor spending — while domestic travel continues to grow briskly enough to keep hotel occupancy healthy.

Site to visit: Hampi, in Karnataka’s Ballari district, is the boulder-strewn ruin of Vijayanagara, once among the largest cities in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986, with more than 1,600 monuments spread across 4,100 hectares along the Tungabhadra river. Most temples are free to enter; the Vittala Temple complex with its famous stone chariot charges roughly ₹40 for Indian visitors and ₹600 for foreigners. Come between October and February, and allow two to three days — the site is far too large to rush.

Science & Technology

ISRO opened September with a significant launch, putting EOS-05 into orbit aboard a GSLV-F17 from Sriharikota — India’s first dedicated imaging satellite designed to work from geosynchronous orbit some 36,000 km up, giving continuous rather than intermittent coverage for weather, disaster and agricultural monitoring. At the Bangalore Space Expo, Bengaluru’s Astrogate Labs unveiled AstroLink Micro and announced India’s first indigenous optical inter-satellite link terminal, targeting flight readiness in 2027; the firm is already the only Indian supplier of laser communication terminals to ISRO. Behind both is a deliberate policy shift, with ISRO and DRDO transferring mature technologies to private industry while the agency concentrates on uncrewed Gaganyaan trials and the architecture for the planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station.
