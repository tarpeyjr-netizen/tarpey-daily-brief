Tuesday, July 28, 2026 · Asia

South Korea

Asia · population ~52M

Politics & Government

President Lee Jae-myung's administration is pushing three AI-era megaprojects spanning semiconductors, data centers, and physical AI as a state-led production strategy, even as a controversial "fake news" law took effect this month, drawing warnings from journalist groups about its chilling effect on reporting. Domestically, a court ruling has left Seoul mayor Oh Se-hoon's political future in question, and prosecution reform remains a live flashpoint following police-handling failures, according to Korea Pro's rundown of the country's defining political year. On the peninsula, defense and diplomatic maneuvering continue apace per AEI's latest Korean Peninsula update.

Economy & Business

Seoul revised its 2026 growth outlook up to 3 percent, citing a semiconductor supercycle and easing Middle East uncertainty, alongside a "3-4-5" vision targeting top-four exporter status and $50,000 GNI per capita. Q2 GDP beat estimates, expanding 0.6% quarter-on-quarter on AI-fueled chip demand, with real gross national income up 15.6% year-on-year — the largest jump in 38 years. Seoul's startup ecosystem is also thriving, with enterprise value near $357 billion and 39 unicorns concentrated in AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and robotics.

Military & Security

Defense Minister Ahn Gyu-back is pushing commanders to finalize a target year for transferring wartime operational control from the US to South Korea by year's end. The Agency for Defense Development launched an in-house generative AI system, Add+i, to support research while guarding against leaks of military secrets. US and South Korean air forces ran the "Buddy Squadron" combined exercise July 14–16, and Seoul is deepening ties with Ukraine partly to manage the fallout of North Korea's battlefield cooperation with Russia, per AEI's peninsula coverage.

Society & Culture

The UNESCO World Heritage Committee's annual session opened in Busan on July 19, spotlighting the country's cultural heritage on a global stage. Protest culture continues to evolve, with a new generation of young demonstrators — some drawing organizational energy from K-pop fandom — reshaping how civic movements mobilize, following earlier rallies at Olympic Park over voting-rights and election-process concerns.

Tourism

South Korea welcomed 10.7 million international visitors in the first half of 2026, up 21.3% year-on-year and now tracking toward a government target of 22–23 million for the year. Chinese arrivals are a major driver, with roughly 2.56 million Chinese visitors between January and May fueling demand for foreigner-only casinos and hospitality. The country also expanded visa-free access to 110 countries this month, while summer crowds flock to the WATERBOMB Festival in Seoul and the Boryeong Mud Festival at Daecheon Beach.

Site to visit: Gyeongbokgung Palace, the largest of Seoul's five grand royal palaces and the primary seat of the Joseon dynasty, remains the city's signature historic landmark, with its throne hall, Gyeonghoeru pavilion, and changing-of-the-guard ceremony drawing visitors year-round. It's open 9am–6pm (closed Tuesdays) through October, with adult admission just 3,000 won — or free if you visit wearing a hanbok, per this visitor guide.

🔥 Trending
FoodNeverComes
Tech / App · South Korea (nationwide)

Built by an independent developer known only as Malhee, FoodNeverComes lets users browse restaurants, customize an order, and watch a rider icon crawl across a delivery map — except nothing ever arrives. Instead, the app tallies up the money and calories "saved" by not actually ordering, tapping into the dopamine hit of anticipated reward without the spend. It's part of a broader wave of Korean "dopamine sites" recreating the thrill of shopping with zero transaction, and it spread almost entirely through word of mouth and social sharing before catching international attention this summer.

Monday, July 27, 2026 · Europe

Germany

Europe · population ~84M

Politics & Government

Security dominated the headlines after a vehicle was driven into the crowd at Berlin's Christopher Street Day celebrations, an attack authorities believe was Islamist-motivated terrorism. Chancellor Friedrich Merz responded with a reshuffle of top government posts as his coalition works to counter the far right's rising poll numbers. On the domestic policy front, Merz's government is pushing a pension reform that would gradually raise the retirement age, alongside income tax cuts for low- and middle-income earners and a broader push to cut red tape. The moves come as the coalition tries to show it can deliver on economic renewal while managing a tense security and political climate.

Economy & Business

Germany's private sector unexpectedly returned to growth in July, with S&P Global's composite PMI climbing to 51.2 from 49.5, beating forecasts and marking the first expansion in four months on the back of a manufacturing upturn. The government says the economy is stabilizing, though renewed fighting in the Middle East remains a risk to the recovery, even as easing oil prices have lifted business and consumer sentiment. Underpinning the optimism is a sweeping reform package from the ruling coalition covering tax, labour and pensions, including roughly €10 billion in annual income tax relief for lower- and middle-income earners starting January 2027.

Military & Security

Germany is set to raise defence and security spending to more than €200 billion by 2030, roughly a third of the federal budget, as it pursues major military modernization. The army's Territorial Defence Division plans to more than double in size, from 6,500 to over 10,000 personnel by 2035, adding units to protect critical infrastructure and bolster NATO logistics. Berlin and Paris are also deepening nuclear cooperation, with Merz confirming Germany will join upcoming French nuclear exercises as the two countries coordinate more closely on strategic matters. Meanwhile, German firm Diehl Defence unveiled its new IRIS-T SLS MK4 mobile air defence system at ILA 2026, aimed at countering drones and low-altitude threats.

Society & Culture

An estimated 20,000 people protested in Erfurt against the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) as the party held its national convention, blocking roads and gathering under signs reading "Stop AfD Nazis." Delegates at the convention re-elected co-leaders Alice Weidel and Tino Chrupalla to another term as the party seeks to project unity ahead of further gains in the polls. The demonstrations were largely peaceful, though an AfD constituency office and police were targeted with paint bombs and fireworks. The gathering's timing, coinciding with the 100th anniversary of a Nazi Party meeting in the same region, added to the controversy.

Tourism

Germany welcomed an estimated 10 to 11 million international visitors between January and June, continuing steady year-to-date growth fuelled by strong European demand and rising long-haul travel. The summer season alone is forecast to generate nearly €57 billion in revenue, with full-year holiday travel revenue expected to approach €85 billion. The national tourist board's 2026 strategy is pushing the country's image beyond castles and heritage toward sustainability, city breaks and culinary experiences, even as German outbound travellers themselves are trimming holiday budgets amid cost-of-living pressures.

Site to visit: Neuschwanstein Castle, King Ludwig II's fairy-tale 19th-century retreat in the Bavarian Alps, was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2025 alongside his other royal residences, cementing its status as one of Europe's most photographed landmarks. The castle draws over a million visitors a year and is best reached by bus or a scenic uphill walk from the village of Hohenschwangau; early-morning or shoulder-season visits are recommended to avoid the heaviest crowds, and advance online ticket booking is strongly advised.

🔥 Trending
Kauta
Music · Bonn

Kauta, a German-Moroccan singer from Bonn, has become one of German-language pop's fastest-rising new voices, blending urban pop with oriental influences and multilingual, deeply personal lyrics. Her single "Unsichtbar" became her breakout moment, racking up millions of streams and building a grassroots following now approaching 675,000 on TikTok and 60,000 on Instagram. The momentum was entirely organic, built on short-form video and word of mouth rather than major-label push, and it carried her into her first-ever headline tour, "Kauta's Diary," which kicked off in January 2026. Her rise is being watched as an example of how independent artists with a distinct, culturally blended sound can break into Germany's mainstream pop scene from outside Berlin's usual industry circuits.

Sunday, July 26, 2026 · Asia

Iraq

Asia · population ~43M

Politics & Government

Iraqi Kurdish politics shifted on July 2 when the New Generation party and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan formalized a new alliance, reshaping the region's power balance. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi traveled to Washington in mid-July for talks aimed at deepening ties with the Trump administration and unlocking new oil and gas deals. Domestically, security forces arrested the director general of the state-owned Oil Products Distribution Company on corruption charges as part of a broader anti-graft push, while Ankara has signaled it will seek new terms as the Iraq–Turkey pipeline agreement expires this month.

Economy & Business

Iraqi institutions signed 48 agreements worth more than $60 billion during the Washington visit, opening new energy, infrastructure, and investment partnerships, even as Baghdad separately opened IMF talks on economic diversification. The reform push comes against a grim backdrop: regional conflict has driven oil production down from roughly 4.3–4.5 million barrels a day in February to just 817,000 bpd by June. A domestic silver lining: the Iraq Development Fund has flagged $40 billion in import-substitution opportunities that could instead be produced locally.

Military & Security

Iraq's security picture remains dominated by spillover from the wider 2026 Iran war, with explosive-laden drones striking a northern Iraq base hosting American troops after another round of U.S. strikes on Iran. A U.S. service member was killed on July 19 during a controlled detonation of unexploded ordnance, part of a campaign that has wounded more than 400 troops since February. Amid this, the Iraqi PM and the Pentagon confirmed the remaining U.S. troops will fully withdraw by the end of September, closing out a 23-year deployment.

Society & Culture

Sweltering heat reaching 44°C in the city of Kut triggered protests over chronic power cuts, underscoring how decades of conflict have left the electricity grid unable to meet summer demand. Authorities also arrested two Harki tribal leaders in Erbil on murder and incitement charges tied to clashes last November, and a government probe found a deadly fire at a Kut shopping mall stemmed from lax public-safety enforcement. Separately, the arrest of a US-sanctioned deputy oil minister accused of aiding Iran-linked oil-smuggling networks led to the seizure of roughly $11 million in cash.

Tourism

Iraq's tourism sector is drawing wider international attention: outbound Iraqi travel to Turkiye hit a record 60,625 visitors in April, overtaking every other Arab nation. At home, the Iraqi Tourism Authority unveiled a strategy targeting 10 million annual visitors by 2035, helped by the UK and other European governments easing Iraq's travel security classification. Religious tourism remains the backbone of the industry, with millions flocking to the holy cities of Najaf and Karbala for events like Arbaeen, while an expanding e-visa system is making weekend trips to sites like the Erbil Citadel or the southern marshes increasingly accessible.

Site to visit: The Erbil Citadel, a fortified mound settlement UNESCO recognizes as the oldest continuously inhabited site in the world, has stood at the heart of Erbil since ancient Assyrian times and joined the World Heritage List in 2014. The site is open to visitors free of charge amid ongoing restoration — wear sturdy shoes for the cobbled lanes and pair the walk with the four small on-site museums covering carpets, clothing, and stonework.

Science & Technology

Iraq's research community is turning toward sustainability: the government's Green Academy initiative is offering prizes of up to 10 million IQD for top applied environmental research, part of a broader push to pair emissions cuts with green-investment opportunities. Universities are also expanding scientific cooperation abroad, including a joint sustainable-development and climate-change conference held in late July between Al-Furat Al-Awsat Technical University and Egypt's Delta University. Yet the research momentum contrasts with grid reality: a recent academic review found renewables contribute barely 3.4% of electricity generation, with thermal power still dominant.

🔥 Trending
Lezzoo Taxi
Tech / Ride-hailing · Baghdad & nationwide

Homegrown ride-hailing app Lezzoo has become a genuine word-of-mouth phenomenon in Iraq, with its taxi service surpassing 200,000 rides in under three months of launch and now covering more than half the national market. Built by local entrepreneurs rather than a foreign platform, Lezzoo tripled its serviceable market in cities like Karbala, Najaf, and Nineveh over the past year, surpassing four and a half years of prior growth in just twelve months. Its rapid, organic uptake — driven by affordable fares and app-based convenience in cities where reliable taxis have long been scarce — has made it one of the most talked-about consumer tech stories in Iraq this year.

Saturday, July 25, 2026 · Africa

Ethiopia

Africa · population ~128M

Politics & Government

Ethiopia's National Dialogue Conference opened in Addis Ababa on July 15, bringing together roughly 4,000 delegates for a month-long process meant to work through federalism, citizenship, and constitutional reform, a follow-on from June's general election that returned Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's party to power. Parliament has also passed the largest federal budget in the country's history as the government pushes an ambitious reform agenda, and Addis Ababa took over the rotating presidency of the Organization of African, Caribbean and Pacific States this month. Not everyone is convinced reconciliation is close at hand: Bloomberg reported skepticism that a month of talks can bridge the country's deep political divisions, and the dialogue's final-phase conference still has to grapple with the unresolved status of Tigray.

Economy & Business

Export revenue crossed $11 billion for the fiscal year, well above the government's $9.8 billion target and sharply higher than last year's $8.3 billion, with gold contributing nearly $5.5 billion and coffee $3.1 billion — though relying on just two commodities for roughly four-fifths of export earnings leaves the economy exposed to price swings. The World Bank credited Ethiopia's reform program with opening access to foreign exchange, stabilizing the financial system, and improving conditions for businesses to invest and hire, growth the IMF ties to a $3.4 billion funding arrangement tied to structural reforms. The banking sector is opening up too, with a 2025 proclamation allowing foreign ownership of up to 49% of Ethiopian banks for the first time. Attention is now turning to the Grand Renaissance Dam, expected to be formally inaugurated later this year.

Military & Security

Tensions between Ethiopia, Eritrea and the Tigray region remain elevated, with the International Crisis Group warning the Horn of Africa sits on a "powder keg" as federal troops have redeployed toward Tigray since February and flights to Mekelle have been repeatedly cut. Clashes that broke out in late January between the Tigray People's Liberation Front and federal forces have continued intermittently, and the Atlantic Council cautions that neither Asmara nor Mekelle wants a full-scale war, but any miscalculation could escalate quickly given Addis Ababa's accusations that Eritrea is coordinating with a TPLF faction. Ethiopia's push for Red Sea access has become entangled with the standoff, adding a strategic dimension few expect to resolve soon.

Society & Culture

The National Dialogue is as much a cultural moment as a political one: allAfrica describes it as an attempt to install a new political culture built on managed disagreement rather than winner-take-all contests, with the conference's opening debate touching federalism and citizenship. That optimism sits alongside continued unrest — demonstrators gathered at Meskel Square on July 18 to protest abductions and forced recruitment of young people in Tigray, and Human Rights Watch continues to track reports of repression tied to the conflict. Diaspora advocacy is also growing, with Ethiopian Americans organizing campaigns to draw attention to the country's humanitarian situation.

Tourism

Ethiopia is in the middle of a tourism boom, with international arrivals up 15 percent year-on-year, with Addis Ababa alone recording nearly 700,000 foreign visitors in the first half of the fiscal year and generating about 2.6 billion birr in revenue. Growth is being driven by expanding airline routes and new infrastructure, including a new international airport under construction near Bishoftu, alongside rising interest from UAE, Qatari, Turkish, British and American travelers. Not all the news is reassuring: a US travel advisory update in April kept Ethiopia at Level 3 (Reconsider Travel) while flagging new risk factors around exit bans and safety, a reminder that the tourism surge is unfolding against an unsettled security backdrop.

Site to visit: The rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Amhara highlands, are eleven monolithic churches carved directly out of volcanic rock in the 12th and 13th centuries and remain an active pilgrimage site for Ethiopian Orthodox Christians today. Entry runs around $50 for a multi-day pass covering all the churches, and the site is best visited in the dry season (October–March), reached via a short domestic flight from Addis Ababa to Lalibela's airport.

Science & Technology

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed opened a new Science Museum in Addis Ababa this month, with exhibits spanning artificial intelligence, climate resilience, agriculture, sustainable energy and aeronautics — part of a broader tech push that has also seen Apple hold talks with Ethiopian officials on expanding its presence in the country. The government's national coders initiative also passed a symbolic milestone, surpassing 5 million enrollments in programming, data analysis, Android development and AI fundamentals since its 2024 launch. On the environment side, Ethiopia marked the 60th anniversary of its Wildlife Conservation Authority with reforms aimed at modernizing sanctuary oversight and expanding community involvement in conservation.

🔥 Trending
Addis AI
Tech / Voice AI · Addis Ababa

Addis AI, a voice-first artificial intelligence platform that answers questions entirely in spoken Amharic and Afaan Oromo, has quietly become one of Ethiopia's most talked-about homegrown tech products since its December 2024 launch. The app now serves more than 100,000 users, generating between 6,000 and 10,000 daily interactions, with students and small business owners driving much of the growth — a sign of pent-up demand for tools that work in local languages rather than English. Its rise has spread mostly by word of mouth through Addis Ababa's university and small-business circles, arriving just as the country's broader coding and AI push primes a much larger pool of users and developers to build on top of it.

Friday, July 24, 2026 · South America

Chile

South America · population ~19M

Politics & Government

President José Antonio Kast, in office since March, continues to lean on economic reform as the centerpiece of his agenda: his government is staking ever more on tax cuts and deregulation to revive growth, even as economists grow skeptical the measures will deliver quickly enough. The right-wing and center-right coalitions backing Kast hold a working but not overwhelming majority in Congress — 76 of 155 Chamber seats and 25 of 50 in the Senate — which has shaped the pace of his legislative push. Claudio Alvarado has served as government spokesperson since May, fronting an administration still contending with the unresolved aftermath of Chile's failed constitutional reform efforts. The tax-and-deregulation strategy has become the clearest signal of how Kast intends to govern for the remainder of the year.

Economy & Business

Congress this week approved most of Kast's economic and tax package, including a gradual cut to the corporate tax rate from 27% to 23% meant to spur investment. The move comes as economic activity unexpectedly contracted in May, pushing unemployment to a five-year high, though Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz has argued the economy hit a turning point in June and should recover through the second half of 2026. GDP growth is projected at a modest 1.8% for the year, rising to 2.6% in 2027 on the back of higher copper prices. Santiago's startup ecosystem remains a bright spot, with Start-Up Chile and CORFO continuing to back new ventures through equity-free grants.

Military & Security

Border security remains the Kast administration's signature security initiative: the "Border Shield Plan" is sealing roughly 1,000 kilometers of Chile's northern frontier with Peru and Bolivia using trenches, fencing, and surveillance technology, at a cost of roughly $18.5 million so far. Military and police presence along the border has risen an estimated 89%, and the government reports a 95% drop in clandestine crossings during the first half of 2026 compared with a year earlier. Later phases are expected to add thermal cameras, radar, and facial-recognition drones. Separately, Chilean organizations are contending with a rising tide of cyberattacks, averaging some 1,700 incidents per week in 2026 as the country's digital infrastructure becomes a bigger target.

Society & Culture

Tensions over Kast's austerity program spilled into the streets in a large student and teacher march against education budget cuts, part of a roughly $6 billion spending reduction the government plans over 18 months. What began as a peaceful demonstration turned confrontational, with police using water cannons and tear gas and University of Chile health volunteers treating dozens of injured students. The backlash has drawn criticism not only from opposition parties but from factions within Kast's own governing coalition, underscoring how contested the austerity push remains. Education and healthcare access continue to be flashpoints in a country with a long history of social mobilization around those issues.

Tourism

Chile is on pace for a record tourism year, having welcomed more than 3.1 million international visitors in the first half of 2026, with government forecasts pointing to over 6 million arrivals for the full year, led by travelers from Argentina and Brazil. Regulators launched a new National Tourism Activity Index (INATUR) on July 2 to give a clearer statistical picture of the sector, which now contributes about 2% of GDP and supports roughly 600,000 jobs. The Metropolitan Region has also kicked off its 2026 winter tourism season, with an expanded ice-skating program and family-friendly attractions around Santiago drawing strong domestic interest.

Site to visit: Torres del Paine National Park, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in Chilean Patagonia famed for its granite towers, glaciers, and turquoise lakes, remains one of South America's premier trekking destinations, anchoring multi-day routes like the W Trek. Entrance tickets must be purchased online in advance and run about $18 in low season (April–October) versus $35 in high season, with most visitors basing themselves in nearby Puerto Natales.

🔥 Trending
AKRIILA
Music · Maipú, Santiago

AKRIILA (Fernanda Sepúlveda), a 22-year-old rapper and singer-songwriter from Maipú, has become one of Chile's most talked-about breakout acts by pioneering a global strain of "neoperreo" — reggaeton reworked with heavy 808s, autotune, and hyperpop textures. Her debut album epistolares, built from hundreds of phone notes and poems she'd accumulated over years, earned her a spot in OnesToWatch's Class of 2026 for fusing experimental production with pop-ready hooks. The momentum crested with a 20-song set at Lollapalooza Chile 2026 that included an unreleased track, and has since carried her to European festival stages like WARM UP Murcia. Her rise reflects a wider moment for Santiago's underground scene, where queer and feminist-inflected reggaeton is finding an international audience without major-label polish.

Thursday, July 23, 2026 · South America

Colombia

South America · population ~52M

Politics & Government

Colombia is mid-transition after a razor-thin presidential runoff in which Abelardo de la Espriella narrowly defeated Iván Cepeda, 49.6% to 48.7%. A new Congress was sworn in on July 20 for the 2026–2030 term, with a center-right and right-wing majority pushing the leftist Historic Pact into opposition. Outgoing president Gustavo Petro has called the incoming government illegitimate and urged supporters to defend his social and economic reforms as the August 7 handover approaches. De la Espriella has meanwhile promised to cut government spending by up to 40% and loosen labor rules.

Economy & Business

Growth is accelerating: GDP expanded an estimated 2.7% in the second quarter of 2026, up from 2.1% a year earlier, driven by consumption and services even as investment and mining stay soft. Unemployment sits near its lowest May level in years and remittances keep flowing, supporting household spending. Bogotá and Medellín remain hubs for a startup scene of 2,100+ active companies spanning fintech, SaaS, healthtech and agritech. Labor costs are rising too: from July 2026 the standard workweek drops to 42 hours, pushing the effective hourly minimum wage up roughly 23.7–28.5%, which is stirring inflation and informal-labor concerns.

Military & Security

The Colombian military freed 39 people kidnapped by the ELN guerrilla group on a highway in the remote Chocó region, though two soldiers were killed in the operation. At the UN Security Council, briefers said "pockets of insecurity" still challenge what they otherwise called a vibrant democracy, even after peaceful elections. The Red Cross has flagged worsening humanitarian conditions in conflict zones, driven by rising clashes between security forces and armed groups as well as infighting among criminal factions, as the country marks the 10-year milestone of its peace accord.

Society & Culture

Colombia marked its 216th Independence Day on July 20 with a mix of celebrations and demonstrations, including a Historic Pact-organized "Civil Disobedience" march to Bolívar Square and a rival mobilization from the Alliance for Life. Outgoing president Petro urged citizens into the streets to defend his reforms ahead of the handover. The date also coincided with Santa Marta's 500th anniversary celebrations nationwide, while earlier in the month cities saw both anti-U.S. policy protests and Pride marches advocating for LGBTIQ+ rights.

Tourism

Colombia is on pace for a record tourism year, with international arrivals approaching 6 million visitors in 2026 and international ticket sales up 16.7% year to date. Cartagena has picked up a major international travel and cuisine award, reinforcing its draw for culture and heritage travelers, while the accommodation sector is shifting notably toward short-term rentals over traditional hotels. Most arriving travelers skew young, with nearly two-thirds aged 18–49.

Site to visit: The Walled City of Cartagena, a UNESCO World Heritage site built between the 16th and 18th centuries as a defense against pirates and invaders, remains one of South America's best-preserved colonial old towns. Wandering the streets and plazas is free, though individual museums and churches charge separate admission; the dry season from February to mid-March offers the best balance of good weather and lighter crowds, and mornings before 7am are the coolest time to explore.

Science & Technology

Bogotá hosted CCB TechDay 2026 this week, its third edition, drawing more than 3,500 participants from Colombia's tech ecosystem with Poland — a European leader in cybersecurity and AI — as guest country. In the Amazon, a new Living Lab of Sustainable Tourism and Bioeconomy launched in Puerto Nariño, pairing local knowledge with travel technology, while Bogotá's "Ruta TravelTech" showcased tech-enabled tourism cases in partnership with the TransMilenio transit system.

Music · Barranquilla
