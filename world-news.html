Saturday, November 28, 2026 · Asia

South Korea

Asia · population ~52M

Politics & Government

The ruling Democratic Party has formally decided to pursue the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae and aims for a parliamentary vote by the end of December. It cites the Supreme Court's handling of President Lee Jae Myung's election law case, a dispute over judicial nominations and alleged misuse of official funds. The move drew a warning from the International Bar Association, which called it a "dangerous precedent" for judges, and the Korean Bar Association also urged reconsideration. On the diplomatic front, Chinese Ambassador Dai Bing told a Seoul press conference that the Korea-U.S. alliance should not become a "tool" against China, and he urged Seoul to keep its strategic autonomy. In the cabinet, Lee has nominated senior presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung to replace Culture Minister Chae Hwi-young, pending a confirmation hearing.

Economy & Business

A Reuters poll expects November exports to have jumped 96.8% year on year, the fastest pace since January 1974. Chip sales rose 294.4% in the first 20 days of the month, and the official figures are due on December 1. The Bank of Korea sharply raised its 2027 growth forecast to 4.1% from 2.9%. President Lee picked Meritz Financial Group vice chairman Kim Yong-beom to lead a new capital market reform committee focused on minority shareholder rights and a Kosdaq overhaul. Kakao founder Kim Beom-su's acquittal became final after prosecutors declined to appeal. He had been charged with stock manipulation in the 2023 battle with Hybe over SM Entertainment.

Military & Security

Analysts are asking whether the inactivation of the U.S. Army's 5-17 Air Cavalry Squadron at Camp Humphreys leaves a capability gap. The unit flew AH-64E Apaches and RQ-7B Shadow drones, and USFK has not said where its helicopters and personnel went. Ambassador Dai separately raised concern over "strategic flexibility" for U.S. Forces Korea, saying it could imply targeting China. At a Hankook Ilbo roundtable, young South Koreans debated whether North Korea is still the country's "main enemy". Some pointed to military service, while others named semiconductor and energy competition as bigger threats.

Society & Culture

Fears of AI-assisted cheating are clouding this year's college entrance exam. The students believed to have earned perfect CSAT scores are avoiding the spotlight because of the suspicion. The Education Ministry reported 111 electronic-device violations, and police are investigating cases involving smart glasses and miniature cameras. The government also says it will tighten visa checks on foreign entertainers to curb trafficking. In Seoul's Hongdae district, live music clubs face an inspection blitz under rules that bar dancing in venues registered as restaurants.

Tourism

Korea expects about 23 million foreign tourists this year, yet many of them struggle with Seoul Station's confusing transfer gates between the airport railroad and the subway, and the operator is adding videos and clearer signs. In Myeong-dong, Lotte Department Store opened Lumiere Seoul, an 840-square-meter underground plaza, along with a 77-meter exterior media screen. Lotte's sales to foreign shoppers have topped 1 trillion won a year. The 4,500-kilometer Korea Dulle Trail, which loops the coast and the DMZ across 284 courses, is marking 10 years since its first section opened.

Site to visit: Changdeokgung, a Joseon-era royal palace in central Seoul and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is best known for its Huwon (Secret Garden) of pavilions and ponds set into a wooded hillside. The garden can be seen only on a guided tour. According to this visitor guide, palace entry is 3,000 won, the garden tour costs an extra 5,000 won and the palace is closed on Mondays. Book garden tickets online and take the subway to Anguk Station (Line 3, Exit 3).

Science & Technology

President Lee broke ground on GS Group's AI data center campus in Donghae, Gangwon Province, which is planned at up to 120 trillion won and 2.4 gigawatts. He called the global AI race "a silent war at the national level," and the first phase is set to begin operating in 2029. In Seoul, the Seoul Facilities Corporation unveiled an AI device that listens to impact sounds to detect concrete deterioration under bridges. In field tests it matched 106 of 107 damaged points found by conventional inspections.

🔥 Trending
Daily tear-off calendars
Consumer / publishing trend · Seoul

Paper tear-off calendars are winning over young Koreans, and Yes24 reports daily-calendar sales up 39.3% from 2021. Publisher Minumsa's classical "Insaeng Illyeok" sells out its runs of 15,000 to 20,000 copies each year. Professor Kim Young-min's "Gongburyeok" raised more than 22 times its crowdfunding goal on Kyobo. The trend grew on social media, where users post handwritten passages and diary notes on torn-off pages, and it got a further boost when RM of BTS and Jang Won-young of IVE shared pages from Minumsa's classics calendar.

Friday, November 27, 2026 · Europe

Germany

Europe · population ~84M

Politics & Government

The Bundestag spent the week in four days of final debate on the 2027 federal budget, a record spending plan that DW says has exposed a coalition on the brink. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the budget accurately reflects a country in "a difficult situation geopolitically, technologically, and demographically," while AfD co-leader Tino Chrupalla called it a "debt orgy." Merz also defended the cuts and warned against waning support for Ukraine, and the final vote was scheduled for Friday, with higher duties on spirits and alcopops and a future sugar tax among the measures to close a €34 billion gap. Public opinion is moving the other way on foreign policy: a Körber Foundation survey found 48% of Germans want more restraint in international affairs, up five points on last year. Meanwhile President Frank-Walter Steinmeier used his farewell trip to Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire and Kenya to urge German business to "be curious, take risks."

Economy & Business

After more than 24 hours of talks in Mannheim, IG Metall and employers agreed a pilot pay deal covering 1.7 million metal and electrical workers in North Rhine-Westphalia and Baden-Württemberg: 2.0% from May 2027, 2.3% from April 2028, and one-off payments of €150–€450. In return, companies can depart from the 35-hour week and ask staff to forgo pay to fund investment, provided they make binding job commitments; a regional union leader called it "a compromise under difficult conditions." The deal lands as Volkswagen seeks a two-year wage freeze and the car industry counts more than 123,000 jobs lost between 2019 and 2025. Looking outward, German firms are eyeing Africa as trade shocks hit traditional markets, and just over 2,000 German companies are staying in Ukraine despite an estimated €500 million in war damage.

Military & Security

Federal prosecutors on Thursday ordered the arrest of two Iraqi men in Heidelberg and Cologne, suspected members of a pro-Iranian group accused of plotting an attack on Cologne's synagogue, with raids on 17 sites across three states. The same day in Berlin, the trial of a German-Ukrainian businesswoman accused of spying for Russia opened after health-related delays; her arrest in January led Germany to expel Russia's deputy military attaché. On spending, the 2027 budget puts defence at €109 billion plus the special military fund, roughly €139 billion in total and the highest in the Federal Republic's history, while Merz told lawmakers that "Russia wants more than just Ukraine."

Society & Culture

Reported domestic violence hit an all-time high: some 280,000 people were physically abused in their own homes in 2025, more than 70% of them female, and misogynistic hate crimes rose by almost half to 819 cases. Civil society groups warn that cuts to the "Demokratie Leben" programme, set to fall by about €20 million to €170 million, threaten work against antisemitism and racism. In North Rhine-Westphalia, a new schools law bans face veils in classrooms, a practice the state concedes is virtually unseen. Young people also protested in 73 towns and cities on November 20 over jobs, housing and climate.

Tourism

Christmas market season is in full swing, with The Local picking five of the best markets in Berlin and Nuremberg's famous Christkindlesmarkt running from November 27 to December 24. For travellers on a budget, Deutsche Bahn will sell Flixtrain tickets on bahn.de and the DB Navigator app from December 7 for journeys from December 13, ending the need to book combined itineraries separately. Flixtrain, DB's largest long-distance competitor, plans to roughly double its 16-train fleet by 2028.

Site to visit: Cologne Cathedral, the UNESCO-listed Gothic masterpiece beside the city's main station, is an easy first stop on a Rhineland Christmas-market trip. Note that since July 1, 2026, tourist visits require a ticket; visiting hours are generally 10:00 a.m.–5:45 p.m. Monday to Saturday and 1:30–4:30 p.m. on Sundays, while the North Transept remains free for prayer.

Science & Technology

Germany's digital driving licence has slipped to mid-December; it will live in the KBA's free i-Kfz app, but pulling photos from passport and ID registers needs more time. In entertainment tech, prominent dubbing artists, including the long-time German voices of Cameron Diaz, Margot Robbie and Scarlett Johansson, are refusing to sign Netflix contracts with an AI-training clause. The actors' union BFFS argues the deal is still a step forward because explicit consent is required before recordings become synthetic voices. Matti Klemm, the voice of Jason Momoa, is taking Netflix to court to learn how his past recordings were used.

🔥 Trending
“Gen Z regelt”
Youth protest movement · Nationwide (Frankfurt, Cologne, Berlin, Munich)

A youth initiative run by young people aged 18 to 28 has gone from Halloween rallies to repeat nationwide action days, demanding social justice, better education, affordable living costs and a political voice; the military-service debate adds to the anger. Ahead of its November 20 day of action, the group reported more than 17,000 online sign-ups for marches in 39 cities, with Frankfurt drawing the most interest, ahead of Cologne, Berlin and Munich. DW counted Gen Z protests in 73 German towns and cities that day, and noted that participants resent being talked about rather than spoken to.

Thursday, November 26, 2026 · Asia

Iraq

Asia · population ~43M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's push to centralise power escalated this week when he ordered the federal Oil Ministry to take over oilfields in areas under Kurdish security control across Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh provinces. He also told the border crossings authority to assume control of all crossings in the Kurdistan Region and suspended the leadership of the North Oil Company pending an investigation. Kurdistan Democratic Party leader Masoud Barzani called the move "troubling" and said it undermined federalism. A day later, Iraq's Federal Supreme Court ruled that federal forces may deploy to all international borders, including in the Kurdish region, and that the federal oil ministry may oversee and audit oil and gas fields. The Kurdistan Regional Government called the measures "an assault" on its constitutional rights, and analysts expect the ruling to be hard to enforce.

Economy & Business

Iraq's public finances remain under severe strain. A Shafaq News analysis estimates that about 15 million people, roughly a third of the population, depend on state salaries, pensions or welfare. That burden comes as central bank reserves fell from $97.4 billion at the end of 2025 to $80.6 billion by July, and oil exports collapsed during the Hormuz disruption. Following October's devaluation of the dinar to 1,520 per dollar, the gap between the official and parallel rates has narrowed to about 12%. The draft 2027 budget, which projects 217 trillion dinars ($142.8 billion) of spending and assumes $58 oil, missed its legal deadline and is still with the cabinet. Baghdad's crackdown on oil smuggling is partly an attempt to bring every barrel under the state marketer SOMO.

Military & Security

The government's showdown with the Iran-backed Kataib Hezbollah ended in a last-minute compromise over the group's Jurf al-Sakhar stronghold, reportedly brokered with the involvement of Quds Force chief Esmail Qaani. The town lies about 60 km southwest of Baghdad, and on November 20 Iraqi forces began deploying there: an army brigade, Federal Police, an Energy Police regiment to guard oil pipelines, and two Popular Mobilisation Forces brigades. Al-Zaidi told the troops they were "not waging war." Analysts quoted in a follow-up assessment warn that no disarmament has been announced and the group's missile and drone facilities have not been dismantled. One described the deal as swapping "Shia militias for Shia militias, but in uniform." The deadline for militias to place their weapons under state control has already slipped to June 2027.

Society & Culture

On Wednesday, Baghdad launched a National Women, Peace and Security Agenda platform to mark the 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence. The package has 5 billion dinars (about $3.3 million) in funding and includes rehabilitating 75,000 hectares of women-owned farmland and expanding loans to women. In Duhok province, Yazidi custodians and volunteers from more than 30 villages have begun the centuries-old olive harvest at Lalish Temple. The oil from its roughly 1,400 trees is used only to light the temple's lamps. The country is also mourning bodybuilder Sajad Jawad, who died at 30, three days after winning gold at the Asian Championships in Thailand. The federation has not said how he died.

Tourism

The biggest cultural tourism story is the Iraq Museum's reopening in Baghdad on December 1, after a two-year renovation and earlier than the January date first planned. The reopened museum will have refreshed Assyrian halls with monumental lamassu and new Abbasid and Islamic galleries. Officials have said they expect the museum to earn significant revenue from foreign visitors' admission fees. The ministry also plans to revive overseas travelling exhibitions after a 50-year hiatus, with Germany expected to host the first stop. The push comes as regional conflict has slowed arrivals and disrupted flights this year.

Site to visit: Babylon, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019, was the capital of Hammurabi and Nebuchadnezzar on the lower Euphrates. Visitors can see a 1960s replica of the Ishtar Gate, the partly reconstructed Southern Palace and Saddam Hussein's hilltop palace overlooking the ruins. The site is near Hillah, about 85 km south of Baghdad, an easy day trip by road, and the cooler months from autumn to spring are the most comfortable time to visit.

Science & Technology

The fragility of Iraq's power grid was exposed again when a fire at the Hartha power station north of Basra killed at least four people and injured 35. The blaze spread to nearby homes and forced the 800-megawatt plant offline, and its cause has not been confirmed. Elsewhere, safety regulation appears to be working: Najaf's civil defence directorate reports a 95% fall in fire incidents in 2026, with 97 fires so far this year against 2,117 in 2023. It credits daily inspections of businesses and projects that fail safety standards.

🔥 Trending
Adel Haji Bakr's Old Kirkuk Dioramas
Folk art & heritage · Kirkuk

Adel Haji Bakr, a Kurdish artist and technical teacher, is rebuilding old Kirkuk in miniature. Working on his own, he has made 35 detailed foam-and-cork dioramas of the city's historic landmarks. He frames the project as a way to save a disappearing heritage shared by Kirkuk's Arabs, Kurds, Turkmen and Christians, including citadel houses demolished under the former regime. The project has no outside backing, but growing public interest in the models has become an extra source of income for his family. He says hundreds more buildings are still waiting to be documented.

WEDNESDAY, November 25, 2026 · AFRICA

Ethiopia

Africa · population ~128M

Politics & Government

Addis Ababa's diplomacy is working several fronts at once as fears of a wider Horn of Africa war grow. On November 25, Ethiopia and the UAE held a second round of political consultations on expanding economic and security cooperation, days after Ethiopian and US officials discussed trade, investment and financing for the planned Bishoftu International Airport. In Washington, a US lawmaker has drafted legislation that would offer Ethiopia and Eritrea trade incentives, including formal trade talks and access to US farm goods, if they take steps toward lasting peace. Beijing is also involved: Al Jazeera reports that China is walking a "thin line" between Addis Ababa and Asmara after sending envoys to both capitals. At home, the Federal High Court struck down parts of a Ministry of Finance VAT directive that forced lawyers to register for VAT whatever their income, ruling that the ministry had overstepped Parliament's two-million-birr threshold.

Economy & Business

A new report warns that a possibly exceptionally strong El Niño, combined with higher fuel prices linked to the Iran war, could push about 905,000 more Ethiopians into poverty and 2.8 million into undernourishment in 2027, most of them in rural areas. On trade logistics, Ethiopian and Djiboutian officials discussed port efficiency and corridor upgrades ahead of the next rainy season; Djibouti still handles the bulk of the landlocked country's sea trade. In banking, United Bank for Africa chairman Tony Elumelu said the lender shelved its Ethiopia expansion over a 10% foreign investment cap. In aviation, Ethiopia won a seat on the expanded ICAO Council with 133 votes.

Military & Security

Tensions with Eritrea rose sharply after residents reported overnight drone and artillery strikes around Badme on November 18–19, amid a military buildup on both sides of the border. In Tigray, Amnesty International said artillery strikes on civilians in Alamata should be investigated as war crimes. In the south, Islamic Relief estimates that nearly 700,000 people have been displaced by the Moyale conflict across Somali and Oromia regions, while renewed fighting along the Afar–Somali border in Sitti Zone is driven by drought and land disputes. Relations with Sudan stayed tense as well: after RSF-attributed drone strikes hit Khartoum, including a UN compound, Sudanese military sources again alleged the drones were launched from Ethiopia, a claim Ethiopia has denied in the past.

Society & Culture

Ethiopia launched a polio vaccination campaign targeting 11.3 million children this week, as Addis Ababa hosted CPHIA 2026, Africa's flagship public health conference. There, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus urged African states to reclaim control of health financing, saying the continent loses more to illicit financial flows and debt service than it receives in aid. The Red Cross warned of a worsening humanitarian crisis in Oromia as conflict and displacement continue. Press freedom also came under scrutiny, with an Addis Standard op-ed questioning the Ethiopian Media Authority's indefinite suspension of The Reporter.

Tourism

A record 60,000 runners took to the streets of Addis Ababa for the 26th Great Ethiopian Run, which had separate starts for elite, recreational and high-school runners and a new multi-year presenting partner in Bank of America. Travel elsewhere in the country remains restricted: on November 19 the US renewed its travel warning for Afar, Amhara and Tigray and kept its countrywide Level 3 "reconsider travel" advisory. The same alert noted that Ethiopian Airlines resumed flights to Shire on November 17, but flights to Axum remain suspended.

Site to visit: The Rock-Hewn Churches of Lalibela are eleven monolithic churches carved out of the rock in the late 12th and early 13th centuries under King Gebre Meskel Lalibela. They sit at about 2,480 m in the Amhara highlands and were among the first sites UNESCO listed, in 1978. They are still in active use and draw large crowds of pilgrims for Genna (Ethiopian Christmas) and Timkat, but Amhara is currently under a US travel warning, so check advisories before planning a trip.

🔥 Trending
Abayitu oxygen device
Health tech / grassroots invention · Adola, Guji Zone, Oromia

Mudhataa Bariisoo, a 30-year-old biomedical engineering technician at Adola Hospital in Guji Zone, built Abayitu, a low-power device that extracts and purifies oxygen from the air. After it won a national innovation competition, Addis Standard profiled the device, which already feeds oxygen directly into Adola Hospital's medical gas pipeline. Mudhataa says his goal is to "solve Ethiopia's medical oxygen shortages."

Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · South America

Chile

South America · population ~19M

Politics & Government

President José Antonio Kast's government is fighting on several fronts in Congress. On November 23 the Chamber of Deputies backed most Senate amendments to the 2027 budget by 138 votes, but disagreements on ten line items, including Culture, Women and Gender Equity, memory sites and municipal security funding, sent the bill to a joint committee that must finish before the November 29 constitutional deadline. Justice Minister Fernando Rabat faces a constitutional accusation. The commission reviewing it was handed 1,886 sentence-reduction decrees signed since March 11; its chair had expected about 56. Its report is due November 27 and a floor vote is projected for November 30. A Criteria poll taken November 19–20 put Kast's approval at 23%, up from 18% a week earlier, with 68% disapproving. Abroad, the operator of the Chilean-flagged cargo ship Danstar, which is due to restore a direct Punta Arenas–Falklands link in December, said it will seek clearance only for departure and arrival, not from Argentina.

Economy & Business

The central bank reported that third-quarter GDP fell 1.1% year on year, worse than its preliminary estimate. At the same time, COMEX copper has fallen to about US$5.70 a pound, roughly 17% below its September record, which is testing the government's deficit-reduction plan. That plan is also absorbing the cost of a fuel-price freeze through March 2027, which the government agreed to after a truckers' blockade. State miner Codelco used Asia Copper Week in Shanghai to offer private investors 60 undeveloped properties covering about one million hectares. Cochilco projects that national mine output will fall 8.4% this year to 4.94 million tonnes. Markets steadied on Monday: the IPSA rose 0.96% to about 10,450, and the dollar fell to about 1,016 pesos after reaching a 12-month high the previous Friday.

Military & Security

The Public Prosecutor's third organised-crime report, presented November 18, found that organised-crime homicides fell 46% in 2025, from 199 to 107 victims. Over the same period, the number of organised-crime cases rose 6.8% to 6,121, driven by small-scale drug dealing, and identified trafficking victims rose 37%. Chile's vote at the OAS to apply the 1947 Rio Treaty (TIAR) to drug trafficking has drawn criticism. Former ambassador Pablo Cabrera said it is meant to give multilateral backing to the US-led "Shield of the Americas". Separately, El Mostrador reports that Navy intelligence has begun examining a cyberattack on the Foreign Ministry's consular system, which sources say had still not been contained more than three weeks after it began.

Society & Culture

Conaf director Aída Baldini warned that Chile faces one of its most difficult wildfire seasons in years, saying she had never seen a risk map with the whole country at high risk. El Niño heat is expected to hit vegetation that grew after an exceptionally wet winter. The government has budgeted US$166 million for the season, with 8,000 brigade members and 177 aircraft. Santiago is still dealing with February's gas-tanker explosion in Renca: a burns victim died after nine months in critical condition, and responsibility for the blast is still disputed. In culture, Shawn Garry's Después de Elena won the Golden Colón at Spain's Huelva festival, and Alfredo Castro won best performance. At home, Chilean Cinema Day week (November 23–29) offers 138 free or CLP 1,000 screenings at 41 independent venues in 14 regions.

Tourism

Pisco producers in the Limarí Valley, in the Coquimbo Region, are building a "Pisqueras Ancestrales" distillery-tourism circuit around Ovalle and Monte Patria ahead of the summer season. Pisquera Tulahuén expects more than 3,000 visitors this year, compared with about 30,000 at some distilleries in the better-known Elqui Valley, where arrivals have suffered from damaged transport links and worries about the weather. Producers say direct sales to visitors carry higher margins, and Casa Juliá plans a museum and heritage tours. The expected El Niño heatwaves and fire risk are a concern for summer travellers heading to forested parks.

Site to visit: Torres del Paine National Park in Patagonia is famous for its granite towers, glaciers and turquoise lakes, and its "W" trek is among the best-known multi-day hikes in South America. November to March is the main hiking season. Since May 1, 2026, the park has used a new entry system with a single-day pass and a 2-plus-day pass valid for up to 10 days, sold in advance through Conaf's pasesparques.cl. Most visitors arrive via Puerto Natales.

Science & Technology

The Foreign Ministry breach has turned into a test of the state's cyber defences. According to El Mostrador, the attack began with a phishing email to a diplomat and planted malware that replicates every ten minutes. Officials decided not to take the consular platform offline because more than a million Chileans abroad depend on it, and the National Cybersecurity Agency (ANCI) has opened its own investigation. Separately, Conaf's fire-season plan relies on a "single strike" rapid-attack method and a "red button" ignition-risk alert. The alert flags areas where the probability of ignition exceeds 70% and winds reach at least 20 km/h.

🔥 Trending
Crezta! Limón y Merkén
Food & consumer brand · Chile (nationwide)

Crezta! is a Chilean seasoning that blends lemon powder, salt and smoked merkén chili. Founder Santiago Piano first made it to rim michelada glasses, but fans now put it on chicken, seafood, fruit and avocado toast. After eight months of losses, with roughly two months of savings left, Piano started a 45-day social-media challenge to save the company, and one video passed 3 million views and turned sales around. The brand is now sold at more than 80 points of sale and has launched in the Líquidos liquor chain and Jumbo supermarkets.

Monday, November 23, 2026 · South America

Colombia

South America · population ~52M

Politics & Government

The standoff between President Abelardo de la Espriella and his predecessor sharpened over the weekend. At a press conference on the national strike, de la Espriella asked US authorities to investigate Gustavo Petro and, in his words, "hopefully imprison" him, calling the previous administration a "criminal enterprise" and warning opponents, including June runoff rival Senator Iván Cepeda, not to "mess with democracy." On Sunday the ICT ministry asked X to suspend Petro's @petrogustavo account, citing possible incitement to violence after he posted a call for Colombians to "demand Abelardo's resignation now." Relations with Washington drew attention too: during a brief stop in Bogotá on November 18, US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the two governments were "completely on the same page", and both men dismissed a New York Times report that Bogotá had rejected joint US military operations on Colombian soil.

Economy & Business

The government's fiscal plan is at the centre of the debate. It would lower the income-tax threshold from about $1,419 to about $985 a month, raise healthcare contributions and cut corporate taxes, with next year's deficit projected at 9.4% of GDP. In industry, Nestlé said on November 17 that it will permanently close its 75-year-old La Rosa plant in Dosquebradas, Risaralda, after the August 10 earthquake damaged about 70% of the site. Production of brands such as Saltinas and Cocosette will move to Ecuador, Brazil and Chile. On the same day, the president and Grupo Salinas chairman Ricardo Salinas Pliego announced that Mexican brand Italika plans a motorcycle assembly operation in Barranquilla, which the presidency says will create nearly 1,000 direct and indirect jobs.

Military & Security

Attacks on the armed forces continued. EFE reported that an explosives attack using improvised launch ramps on an army battalion in La Jagua de Ibirico, Cesar, killed one soldier and wounded 12 in an area where both the ELN and the Clan del Golfo operate. Two weeks earlier, a drone attack on a base in Santa Rosa del Sur, Bolívar, killed three. The government struck back with an airstrike in Dagua, Valle del Cauca, that killed five ELN members from a unit led by the commander known as "Bertha." It was the seventh bombing operation de la Espriella has ordered since taking office on August 7, and he said his order was to "maintain the offensive."

Society & Culture

Unions, students and opposition groups held the second nationwide protest against the government on November 19. The marches were mostly peaceful, but some ended in clashes with riot police using tear gas and water cannon, and turnout was uneven across major cities. An ECLAC report put Colombia's extreme poverty rate at 13.4%, the fourth highest in Latin America and the Caribbean, though at a three-year low. Universidad de los Andes and the Safe Speeds Alliance said 7,468 people died on Colombian roads from January to September, up 19.3% on the same period last year and a record. Motorcyclists account for almost all of the increase.

Tourism

Barranquilla hosted the Copa Sudamericana final on November 21, which Boca Juniors won 2-0 over Atlético Mineiro at the renovated "Supermetro" stadium, now seating 65,000. The mayor reported 100% hotel occupancy. Before the match, city officials expected more than 125,000 visitors and an economic boost of about US$241 million. Bogotá also has a new food honour: Condé Nast Traveler España named it Best International Dining Destination at its 2026 Hotel & Mantel Awards, ahead of New York, Dubai and Bangkok.

Site to visit: The Castillo de San Felipe de Barajas in Cartagena is often described as the most imposing Spanish fortress in the Americas, and its tunnels and hidden passageways are part of the city's UNESCO World Heritage listing (1984). It is open daily from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. and is less than 10 minutes from the walled old town. Full-price tickets cost COP 38,000, and Colombians with a cédula get in free on November 29.

Science & Technology

The ICT ministry has filed charges against Starlink Colombia and four mobile operators, including Comcel and Tigo, for allegedly failing to block signals around prisons. A raid on Bogotá's La Picota prison found six Starlink antennas, 582 phones and 3,754 SIM cards, and the ministry found Starlink Wi-Fi at three other prisons. If the violations are confirmed, the companies face fines of up to 15,000 monthly minimum wages, suspension of operations or loss of their licences.

🔥 Trending
Aria Vega
Music · Barranquilla

Barranquilla singer-songwriter Aria Vega, "La Nena de Quilla," won Best New Artist at the 2026 Latin GRAMMYs in Las Vegas and thanked her city and the Caribbean coast in her speech. She calls her sound "Costeñita Core," a mix of the music, fashion and slang of Colombia's north coast. Her breakthrough was the "Chévere" remix with Ryan Castro. Her team says it spent 14 weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard Colombia Hot 100 and inspired more than 2.2 million TikTok videos, though those figures are not independently verified. Rolling Stone said she is bringing the sound of the Caribbean coast into the mainstream.
