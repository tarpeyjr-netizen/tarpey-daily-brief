Thursday, September 17, 2026 · Europe

France

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

President Emmanuel Macron's authority keeps eroding: an Ipsos bva-CESI poll for La Tribune Dimanche, fielded 9–10 September, put his approval at a record-low 16%, down five points since July, with favourable views collapsing even among his own centrist voters (Macron, Lecornu popularity falls to record lows). Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu fares little better at 20% and is simultaneously trying to hold his fragile minority government together as he opens the fight over the 2027 budget, telling ministers in a mid-September letter that non-defence spending must stay "strictly at the same level" as 2026 because "we no longer have the possibility of financing everything, but we still have the freedom to choose" (France maps out deep 2027 budget cuts). Amid the domestic turbulence Macron kept up a busy diplomatic calendar, hosting Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi at the Élysée on 14 September to sign accords on energy, air defence and development finance aimed at diversifying Middle East oil and gas export routes (France, Iraq deepen energy, defence ties).

Economy & Business

The growth picture darkened again this week: INSEE and Economy Minister Roland Lescure confirmed on 11 September that France's 2026 GDP forecast has been cut to just 0.4%, less than half of last year's 0.9% expansion and down from an already-reduced 0.7% June estimate, after output contracted 0.2% in the first quarter and stagnated in the second (France cuts growth forecast as economy lags behind neighbours). The statistics agency also warned that the labour market is now "more degraded than elsewhere in Europe," with the private sector expected to shed roughly 52,000 jobs this year and unemployment climbing to 8.6% by December, up from 8.0% a year earlier (French economy set for sharply slower growth). The weak numbers are colliding directly with the 2027 budget fight, where Lescure wants the deficit held under 5% of GDP, a target the government says will require roughly €2.5 billion of extra savings on top of the spending freeze (France calls for tough budget cuts ahead of 2027 election).

Military & Security

France's Air and Space Force opened its most ambitious power-projection exercise yet on 8–9 September, sending four Rafale F4 fighters plus refuelling tankers and transport aircraft from Istres air base on a 40,000-kilometre, 40-day loop through the Arctic, the Indo-Pacific and the Middle East, codenamed Pégase 26 (French military jets launch a 40-day global flight). The roughly 300-strong contingent flew nearly eleven hours and refuelled five times mid-air to reach Alaska, where the Rafales trained alongside US Air Force F-22 Raptors, before continuing on to Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, India, Qatar and Réunion ahead of a final stop in Egypt (What French Rafale jets aim to prove with PEGASE 26). The mission is explicitly framed around validating a new Arctic air route as competition over the high north intensifies among NATO members and Russia, and it doubles as a demonstration that France's nuclear-armed air force can reach and defend partners far from the mainland (France's newest global air mission covers three continents).

Society & Culture

Former Paris mayor Anne Hidalgo told a Senate inquiry committee on 15 September that "collective failure" within city institutions had allowed a decade-long wave of abuse against children in Paris's state nurseries and after-school programmes, with police now examining more than 200 allegations of mistreatment, violence and sexual abuse (Paris ex-mayor says 'collective failure' led to abuse). The hearing came a day after a Paris court handed down the first conviction in the scandal, ordering an ankle tag for a convicted school aide, a verdict that did little to quiet furious parents, and prosecutors have since opened investigations into Hidalgo and current mayor Emmanuel Grégoire over their alleged failure to protect children in danger (First conviction fails to quell parents' anger). The scandal is unfolding alongside a slow-burn wave of labour unrest, with SUD-Rail confirming that rail workers will join public-sector unions in a nationwide strike on 29 September over pay, after the SNCF group posted a €1.2 billion first-half profit while offering only a 0.18% general pay rise (French rail workers call September strike).

Tourism

France is on course to hold its title as the world's most-visited country, having drawn roughly 102 million international visitors in 2025 and running at a pace that could push 2026 arrivals higher still, even as authorities focus on getting visitors to spend more rather than simply arrive in greater numbers (France is holding at 102 million visitors). This summer's heatwaves and wildfires nudged domestic holidaymakers away from the traditional Mediterranean coast and toward Normandy and Brittany, and the shift has held into autumn even as September-through-December air bookings run about 4% higher year-on-year, led by a 19% jump in demand from Southeast Asia (France tourism proves resilient despite heat and wildfires). Paris, meanwhile, is mid-way through its September cultural calendar, with Paris Design Week running across the month and a slate of heritage-themed events drawing visitors into the capital's museums, ateliers and historic quarters (Paris unveils September 2026 travel experiences).

Site to visit: Mont-Saint-Michel, the tidal island abbey off the Normandy coast that has drawn pilgrims since the 8th century and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979, is France's second most-visited monument after Versailles, pulling in around three million people a year. Its Romanesque and Gothic abbey crowns a granite outcrop ringed by continental Europe's highest tides, with a medieval village of stone houses and cobbled lanes climbing toward it. Mornings before 10am in autumn and winter are the quietest time to visit, and the island is reachable on foot, by bike, or by free shuttle from the mainland car parks.

Science & Technology

Paris hosted the inaugural International Space Summit at the Grand Palais on 9–10 September, drawing representatives from 90 countries and around 60 heads of state, government or ministers alongside nearly 1,100 space-sector companies, opened by President Macron and closing with European governments pledging more than $22 billion in new space commitments (International Space Summit in Paris). The gathering focused on space security and defence, the commercial space economy, responsible governance and long-term sustainability as low Earth orbit grows more crowded and contested (Europe bets $22B on space sovereignty at Paris summit). It followed within days of Germany's Isar Aerospace becoming the first commercial company to reach orbit from continental European soil, launching its Spectrum rocket from Andøya, Norway on 5 September — a milestone French officials cited as evidence Europe's push for launch independence is gaining real traction (Private rocket reaches orbit from European soil).

Wednesday, September 16, 2026 · Asia

Japan

Asia · population ~124M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi enters the second year of her premiership with a reshuffle built around continuity rather than change: after reshuffling LDP executive posts on 16 September and her cabinet the following day, she is widely expected to keep Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama, Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi in their posts, with Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara and senior LDP figures also staying put. It is her first reshuffle since taking office last October, and comes as she works to stabilise her governing coalition ahead of the LDP's 2027 leadership contest. The emphasis on stability over a fresh start reflects how little room Takaichi has to spend political capital on personnel risk this early in her term.

Economy & Business

Growth is real but slowing: revised figures released in early September put second-quarter GDP at an annualised 1.4%, still down from the first quarter's pace, as private consumption made no net contribution to output amid elevated living costs. The Bank of Japan's policy board meets on 17–18 September, with markets pricing in another rate rise partly to relieve pressure on a yen that touched a 40-year low against the dollar last month. Households are still absorbing the fallout from last year's rice shortage: prices have eased from their peak but remain well above pre-crisis levels, keeping food inflation a live political issue.

Military & Security

Tokyo's threat assessment hardened again this quarter. The Cabinet's Defence of Japan 2026 white paper, approved in early August, names China the country's "greatest strategic challenge" and warns that the PLA is projecting power ever further beyond the First Island Chain into the Pacific. It also flags deepening alignment between Beijing and Moscow, pointing to joint China-Russia bomber flights near Japan following the two leaders' appearance alongside Kim Jong Un at a military parade last autumn. In response, Japan continues to build a "southern shield" of anti-access capability along its southwestern island chain, with the country's foundational Three Strategic Documents due an accelerated revision this year.

Society & Culture

Japan crossed a demographic milestone this week: the health ministry confirmed on 15 September that the number of centenarians has topped 100,000 for the first time, reaching 107,677 — up almost 8,000 on last year and continuing a 56-year run of growth, with women accounting for nearly 88% of the total. The milestone lands alongside continued overall decline, with Japan's population still falling as the birth rate sets fresh record lows, and officials now estimate the country will need roughly 2.4 million elderly-care workers this year, up from 2.1 million in 2024. The juxtaposition — record longevity next to a shrinking, ageing workforce — continues to frame nearly every domestic policy debate.

Tourism

Inbound tourism is setting records again: foreign arrivals between January and September rose 17.7% to around 31.65 million, the fastest pace ever to clear 30 million within a year. Rather than simply celebrate the numbers, authorities are layering on new charges: a tripled departure tax of ¥3,000 took effect on 1 July, Kyoto's accommodation tax was raised sharply at the top end in March, and the Yoshida Trail up Mount Fuji still caps climbers at 4,000 a day and charges ¥4,000 to climb. Fushimi Inari, the Arashiyama bamboo grove and Shibuya Crossing remain the most congested pressure points as the country shifts from pure visitor-volume promotion toward a more managed model.

Site to visit: The Sado Island Gold Mines in Niigata Prefecture, inscribed as Japan's newest UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2024, preserve nearly 400 years of hand-worked gold and silver mining tunnels that once funded the Tokugawa shogunate. Visitors take a car ferry or high-speed jetfoil from Niigata Port to Ryotsu, then a roughly 70-minute bus ride to the historic mine complex; entry to the associated gold park runs about ¥2,300 in September, and autumn's crisp air and turning leaves make it the season locals recommend.

Science & Technology

Japan's chip strategy is moving from paper to output. TSMC has finalised plans for its first 3-nanometer production in Japan, a roughly $17 billion Kumamoto plant, while domestic challenger Rapidus says it can price its 2-nanometer chips at or below TSMC's levels once mass production begins in 2027, with 2nm test-chip runs for AI customers due by year-end. On the research side, RIKEN has switched on RIKYU, a new supercomputer built around 1,600 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs, dedicating it to "AI for Science" work spanning materials, climate and drug discovery as part of a wider 2,140-GPU build-out with Nvidia supporting Japan's sovereign AI push.

🔥 Trending
Mentai France (明太フランス)
Food / consumer phenomenon · Fukuoka (Hakata), spreading nationwide

The country's most viral food right now came not from a chain or a marketing budget but from one small Hakata bakery. Full Full, a modest Fukuoka shop, built its name on a split baguette stuffed with a butter-and-mentaiko (spicy cod roe) filling that oozes out with each bite — a Fukuoka original dating to the early 2000s that for years sold only a handful of loaves a day. Videos of the filling spilling out on camera turned it into a nationwide craze this year: the shop now bakes and sells thousands of loaves on its busiest days, and copycat versions have spread through bakeries and convenience stores across Japan. A recent TBS segment summed up its appeal as endlessly moreish — crispy outside, "juwa" (juicy) inside — and it has grown with no celebrity backer and no ad spend, purely on word of mouth and short-form video during a year of otherwise high grocery prices.

Wednesday, September 9, 2026 · Asia

India

Asia · population ~1.44B

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Narendra Modi spent the early part of the week on a domestic circuit, visiting Gujarat and Maharashtra on 8 September and appearing at the centenary celebrations of Shri Ram College of Commerce in Delhi. The judiciary delivered a long-awaited verdict on 5 September when a special NIA court in Bastar convicted all ten accused in the 2013 Darbha valley Naxal ambush that wiped out much of Chhattisgarh’s Congress leadership, closing a case that had run for thirteen years. Domestic attention was pulled back to Delhi a day later when a five-storey building collapsed in the capital, killing seven. The governing BJP continues to operate from a position of unusual strength after its breakthrough in West Bengal, though the Election Commission’s contested revision of the electoral rolls remains a live grievance for opposition parties heading into the next cycle of state votes.

Economy & Business

India remains the fastest-growing major economy, with GDP expanding 7.8% year-on-year in the June quarter and 7.7% across the full FY2026 — the strongest showing since the post-Covid rebound. The rupee has been the softer spot: it hit an all-time low of 96.8 to the dollar in May as the Middle East conflict pushed up oil import costs, and has since clawed back to roughly 94, with the RBI rebuilding reserves and analysts expecting a 94–96 band through year-end. Trade policy has done much of the heavy lifting: a February deal with Washington cut reciprocal tariffs on Indian goods from 25% to 18%, and in January India and the EU finally closed a free trade agreement after twenty years of negotiation. Consumption and investment are carrying growth in roughly equal measure, though exporters remain exposed to a volatile global tariff environment.

Military & Security

The most consequential development of the week was quiet and procedural: on 6 September the Indian and Chinese armies held their first-ever Corps Commander-level meeting in the Eastern Sector, at the Vacha-Damai Border Personnel Meeting Point in Arunachal Pradesh, with Lt Gen Girish Kalia leading a twelve-member Indian delegation. That meeting extends a dialogue mechanism that had been confined to Ladakh since Galwan, and follows an eight-point consensus on border management that added new meeting points and two military hotlines in the Eastern and Middle Sectors. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh travelled to Sri Lanka from 8 to 10 September for consultations with the Colombo leadership, part of a broader effort to keep Indian Ocean partners close. Further afield, the IAF’s Sarang team — the world’s only five-helicopter display formation, flying the indigenous ALH Dhruv — performed at Egypt’s El Alamein International Airshow.

Society & Culture

The youth protest movement that erupted in July continues to reshape Indian politics. Triggered by the leak of a medical entrance exam paper that forced more than two million candidates to re-sit, the satirically named Cockroach Janta Party grew from an internet joke into a national movement demanding the education minister’s resignation. Students defied a Delhi lockdown to march on Parliament and camped for weeks at Jantar Mantar; police tear-gassed and baton-charged the crowds, and at least 178 people were injured. Human Rights Watch subsequently accused authorities of using excessive force. Modi has promised fast-track courts for exam-leak offenders but has declined to sack the minister — and the underlying grievances, a high cost of living and a shortage of quality graduate jobs, have not gone anywhere.

Tourism

India’s inbound tourism recovery has stalled. Foreign tourist arrivals fell 8.1% to 9.15 million, still well short of the 10.9 million recorded in 2019, and industry forecasts suggest arrivals will stay broadly flat this season as higher oil prices and conflict-driven airfares bite. Operators point to visa friction, transport complexity, safety perceptions and tourist scams as the recurring complaints, alongside sharper competition from Southeast Asian rivals. The brighter number is spend rather than headcount — the WTTC reports record international visitor spending — while domestic travel continues to grow briskly enough to keep hotel occupancy healthy.

Site to visit: Hampi, in Karnataka’s Ballari district, is the boulder-strewn ruin of Vijayanagara, once among the largest cities in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986, with more than 1,600 monuments spread across 4,100 hectares along the Tungabhadra river. Most temples are free to enter; the Vittala Temple complex with its famous stone chariot charges roughly ₹40 for Indian visitors and ₹600 for foreigners. Come between October and February, and allow two to three days — the site is far too large to rush.

Science & Technology

ISRO opened September with a significant launch, putting EOS-05 into orbit aboard a GSLV-F17 from Sriharikota — India’s first dedicated imaging satellite designed to work from geosynchronous orbit some 36,000 km up, giving continuous rather than intermittent coverage for weather, disaster and agricultural monitoring. At the Bangalore Space Expo, Bengaluru’s Astrogate Labs unveiled AstroLink Micro and announced India’s first indigenous optical inter-satellite link terminal, targeting flight readiness in 2027; the firm is already the only Indian supplier of laser communication terminals to ISRO. Behind both is a deliberate policy shift, with ISRO and DRDO transferring mature technologies to private industry while the agency concentrates on uncrewed Gaganyaan trials and the architecture for the planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station.

A twenty-something composer from Chennai has produced the year’s biggest song in India without a film attached to it. Released in March on Think Music’s independent imprint, “Pavazha Malli” pairs Sai Abhyankkar’s composition and vocals with Shruti Haasan and lyrics by Vivek, and in five months became the first Tamil music video of 2026 to cross 250 million views. Tuesday, September 8, 2026 · North America

Mexico

North America · population ~130M

Politics & Government

President Claudia Sheinbaum delivered her second annual report to the nation on September 1, framing Mexico as stronger, safer and more prosperous while mounting a pointed defence of national sovereignty. The address leaned heavily on relations with Washington, and in the days since she has repeatedly accused the Trump administration of meddling in her government, alleging attempts to sow division within her own security cabinet. Her most striking constitutional proposal would bar dual nationals from running for president or state governor, a measure her allies present as insulating Mexican politics from foreign influence and critics read as a broad tightening of eligibility rules. Congress has meanwhile approved a reform narrowing the amparo injunction, limiting judicial protection against pretrial detention orders and in collective matters such as environmental cases.

Economy & Business

The economy has recovered its footing after a rocky start to the year: GDP expanded 1.4% in the second quarter, revised slightly down from the first estimate but comfortably ahead of the 1.3% consensus and a clear rebound from the first quarter's 0.6% contraction. Sheinbaum used her report to tout record foreign direct investment of nearly US $35 billion in the first half of 2026, alongside a firm peso and contained inflation. Full-year growth forecasts now cluster between 1.5% and 2.8%, with the wide band reflecting how much rides on trade policy. Bilateral goods trade with the United States runs at roughly $873 billion a year with more than 84% moving tariff-free under USMCA, and Sheinbaum has been talking up Mexico's negotiating position as tariff talks grind on; analysts see the peso anchoring near 17–18 on a smooth agreement and sliding toward 20 if talks break down.

Military & Security

Security policy remains dominated by the aftershocks of February's Tapalpa operation, in which the army, National Guard and air force killed CJNG leader "El Mencho" and six others. The reprisals were severe — the federal security cabinet logged 252 roadblocks across 20 states, with 25 National Guard members killed alongside a state prosecutor's agent, a custodial officer and two civilians. Sheinbaum has moved decisively away from her predecessor's non-confrontational posture toward an intelligence-led and more assertive strategy, with Security Minister Omar García Harfuch organising it around National Guard consolidation, investigative capacity, poverty alleviation and inter-agency coordination. She claimed a 51% drop in homicides since taking office, though analysts caution that cartels have reorganised into decentralised, technologically adaptive networks rather than weakened outright.

Society & Culture

The World Cup that Mexico co-hosted this summer became a lightning rod for domestic grievance rather than a unifying moment. Thousands of teachers organised by the CNTE union marched in Mexico City as the tournament opened, contrasting the billions committed to the event with austerity in health, education and housing. Broader social mobilisation has expanded across several states with little formal response from authorities. Rights monitors continue to flag persistent gaps in sexual and reproductive health care for rural and Indigenous women, stubbornly high regional maternal mortality, and a reported rise in suicides among women held in some prisons.

Tourism

The World Cup's tourism dividend landed well below the hype: Mexico had projected more than five million international visitors but received about three million, generating roughly $2.1 billion in economic impact across the three host cities, which drew a combined 7.5 million domestic and international travellers. Hotel performance actually slipped year over year — occupancy in Mexico City reached 56.5%, Guadalajara 56% and Monterrey 53.5%, each down on June 2025. Officials are nonetheless treating the tournament as infrastructure groundwork for a longer campaign to push Mexico into the global top five for international arrivals, a tier currently held by France, Spain, the United States, Italy and Türkiye. Friction around US travel is, somewhat counterintuitively, helping, redirecting regional visitors toward Mexican destinations.

Site to visit: Teotihuacán, the vast pre-Aztec city about 50 km northeast of Mexico City, centres on the Pyramid of the Sun and the Avenue of the Dead and remains one of the most visited archaeological sites in the Americas. Its builders' identity is still debated, which is part of the draw — the Aztecs found it already abandoned and named it "the place where the gods were created." Admission is 210 pesos for international visitors as of January 2026, the site opens 08:00–17:00 every day of the year, and the two on-site museums are included; allow three to four hours and arrive early to beat the midday heat.

Science & Technology

Mexico's semiconductor push is moving from strategy documents into physical laboratories. Puebla has inaugurated circuit characterisation and validation labs under Project Kutsari, the national design centre headquartered across Puebla, Jalisco and Sonora, while five public research centres have joined to launch iSensMEX, an open-innovation platform for advanced materials and integrated circuits. In Querétaro, QSM Semiconductores opened its first manufacturing plant on a MX$777 million investment, creating 210 specialised jobs in MEMS production. The OECD's assessment is that Mexico is strong in design, testing and packaging but does not yet fabricate silicon wafers at scale — and that unreliable power remains the binding constraint, with 91% of industrial parks reporting supply disruptions.

Armenta — Pórtate Bien
Music · Sinaloa / national

The most talked-about debut in música mexicana this year belongs to Armenta, whose album Pórtate Bien has been singled out as a genre-defying breakout that folds aching indie rock, corridos tumbados, flirtatious reggaetón mexa and tender boleros into one record. That independence is now carrying Mexican regional music up the global streaming charts, where it has begun challenging reggaeton's long dominance.

Monday, September 7, 2026 · Europe

Spain

Europe · population ~48M

Politics & Government

Pedro Sánchez's third minority government, in office since November 2023, continues to govern without a working majority, leaning on Sumar and a patchwork of regional nationalist parties for each vote it wins. The cabinet was reshuffled in March when María Jesús Montero stepped down as deputy prime minister and finance minister to contest the Andalusian regional election, with Carlos Cuerpo elevated to deputy PM and Arcadi España taking Finance. Sánchez used his end-of-session assessment in late July to argue the government is "driving Spain forward," pointing to growth and employment as evidence the legislature should run its full term. That case is complicated by a run of judicial trouble around the prime minister's circle, including the formal charging of his wife Begoña Gómez in April, and by commentators who argue his socialist coalition is running out of road; polling analysts, however, still see a path to 2027.

Economy & Business

Spain remains the growth outlier of the eurozone, having been the fastest-expanding major EU economy for close to three years and running at roughly 2.1% real growth on a nominal base of about $2.09 trillion. The unemployment rate fell to 9.87% in the second quarter, the lowest reading since early 2008, and while August shed almost 163,000 jobs as summer contracts expired, the 2.36 million total unemployed is still the lowest for an August since 2007. The European Commission's forecast attributes the strength largely to domestic demand, a resilient labour market and investment growth rather than external tailwinds. CaixaBank Research frames the year with cautious optimism, noting the upgraded starting point creates a favourable carry-over effect into 2027.

Military & Security

Defence spending is where Spain sits most awkwardly inside NATO: the alliance agreed in June to a 5%-of-GDP target by 2035, and Spain was the sole member of the thirty-two to refuse the commitment, with Sánchez negotiating a carve-out capping its military budget near 2.1% of GDP. In domestic terms the trajectory is nonetheless steeply upward — the budget has roughly tripled since 2018, and the Council of Ministers approved an additional €6.287 billion credit transfer to the Ministry of Defence in July, a record. Madrid's counter-argument, set out in its July "Spain in NATO" report, is that capability contributions matter more than headline percentages, citing the more than 9,000 Ukrainian personnel trained on Spanish soil since 2022 — roughly one in ten of all those trained in Europe. The constraint is political as much as fiscal: the left-wing and regional partners Sánchez depends on treat rearmament as a direct trade against social spending.

Society & Culture

The dominant domestic story is Ceuta, where a mass crossing from Morocco of more than 70,000 people on 30–31 July left at least 80 dead and has not been resolved a month on; roughly 50,000 people rallied in Madrid on 2 September as protests spread nationally. The new school year is opening into a wave of industrial action rather than calm: Catalan teachers are due to walk out on 8 September over class ratios, inclusive-education support and infrastructure, while Valencian unions have called demonstrations in Alicante, Valencia and Castellón for 12 September after months of deadlock over pay and staffing. Early-childhood educators — a sector that is 97% women and where pay often sits below the minimum wage — have coordinated nationally for the first time, giving the mobilisation unusual reach. Health workers remain restive too, after the ministry pushed through a new framework statute in June over a strike call.

Tourism

Spain is on course for a year without precedent: 58.1 million international visitors arrived between January and July, 4.6% up year on year, with spending at €82.05 billion — and July alone brought a record 11.5 million arrivals. The United Kingdom, France and Germany remain the core source markets, at 11.5, 7.2 and 6.9 million visitors respectively over the seven months, and forecasters now expect the full year to break 100 million foreign arrivals against 96.7 million in 2025. Part of the surge is displacement rather than growth, with travellers substituting Spain for eastern Mediterranean destinations amid regional tensions. The strain is concentrated: Barcelona, the Balearics and the Canaries are absorbing the bulk of the increase and overtourism has moved from a trade-press concern to open local politics.

Site to visit: The Alhambra in Granada — the Nasrid palace-fortress complex begun in the 13th century and the finest surviving expression of Islamic architecture in Western Europe — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Spain's single most-visited monument, drawing around three million people a year. Its intricately carved stucco, muqarnas ceilings and water-channelled courtyards sit against the Sierra Nevada, with the Generalife summer gardens on the adjacent hill. Practical detail: general admission is about €21 and only 6,600 timed tickets are released daily, so book up to three months ahead; April to June offers the best balance of warm weather and thinner crowds.

Science & Technology

A team at Madrid's CSIC has developed a passive-cooling nanomaterial that held coated surfaces 13°C cooler than uncoated ones in Madrid summer testing, reflecting heat away rather than pumping it out, with potential applications across roofs, façades, electronics and vehicles — a meaningful lever in a country where air conditioning drives summer peak demand. Regionally, Galicia has emerged as an unexpected research hub, hosting CESGA's 32-qubit QMIO quantum computer, the most powerful housed in a public institution in southern Europe, alongside a growing biomedical cluster. National infrastructure work continues through IMPaCT, which is building the plumbing for precision medicine inside the public health system. Spain now accounts for roughly 2.5% of global scientific publications, placing it ninth worldwide.

🔥 Trending
Alcalá Norte
Music · Ciudad Lineal, Madrid

Alcalá Norte are three friends from Madrid's Ciudad Lineal district — Jaime Barbosa, Juan Pablo Juliá and Álvaro Rivas — who formed in 2019 and became the most-discussed band in Spanish rock almost by accident. Their single "La Vida Cañón" took its lyric from a 1935 Mundo Gráfico interview in which a man from a Lavapiés tenement described what he would do if he won the lottery, and set it to Smiths-inflected post-punk that uses the M-30 ring road as its emotional axis. The song racked up hundreds of thousands of streams within weeks and drew public praise from Mikel Erentxun and Rosalía; the debut album that followed put them into Promusicae's vinyl Top 100 and Spotify's viral Top 50 inside a month. What has kept the momentum is precisely its unglamorous specificity — a band that is unapologetically de barrio, singing about the outer-ring Madrid that the country's cultural industries usually skip, now booked across the festival circuit including Primavera Sound.

Sunday, September 6, 2026 · South America

Venezuela

South America · population ~29M

Politics & Government

Acting president Delcy Rodríguez, who was sworn in after U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in January and flew him to the United States on drug-trafficking charges, told reporters at Miraflores Palace this week that national elections will be held when the country is "ready" — declining again to name a date. Her caretaker government passed the 180-day limit the 1999 constitution sets for an acting presidency back in early July, and analysts have begun describing her approach as normalization without transition — restoring international business ties while deferring the vote. Washington-facilitated talks between the government and a segment of the opposition, which opened in August, are scheduled to resume in October, though María Corina Machado remains outside the process and Edmundo González Urrutia is still in exile. Pressure from Congress has grown in parallel, with senators publicly urging that elections proceed rapidly rather than being traded away for energy access.

Economy & Business

The economy is running two directions at once: annual inflation climbed to roughly 576% in July from 544% in June, still the highest rate in the world, even as forecasters project double-digit GDP growth for 2026 off an extraordinarily low base. The engine is oil — output has pushed back above one million barrels per day on the strength of joint ventures with Chevron, Repsol, Eni and Maurel & Prom, and crude shipments to the U.S. have averaged around 329,500 bpd, a roughly 192% jump over the 2025 average. U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright was in Caracas this week to sign fresh agreements with Chevron and Eni, and 2026 oil revenues are projected near $22 billion, the best showing since 2018. The structural problem is unchanged: hydrocarbons still supply more than 83% of hard-currency inflows, leaving household purchasing power hostage to a single commodity while GDP sits around $111 billion after an 80% collapse from its 2013 peak.

Military & Security

The Caribbean remains heavily militarized under Operation Southern Spear, the U.S. naval deployment framed as a counter-narcotics campaign against Latin American trafficking networks. Lethal strikes on vessels alleged to be running cocaine for Tren de Aragúa have continued through 2026, including the killing of a senior gang figure in a June strike. Legal scholars and several members of Congress continue to dispute the basis for the strikes, noting that maritime interdiction rules ordinarily permit lethal force only against an imminent threat, and that officials have not publicly explained how those aboard the targeted boats were identified as gang members. Domestically, the sequence of events since January — a decapitated leadership, an unelected caretaker and a foreign naval presence offshore — leaves the security picture unusually fluid.

Society & Culture

Recovery from the twin magnitude-7.2 and 7.5 earthquakes that struck north-central Venezuela on June 24 continues to dominate daily life. The quakes and more than thirty aftershocks devastated communities around Caracas and La Guaira; official tallies count thousands dead, some 16,700 injured, nearly 18,000 displaced and 38 health facilities damaged. PAHO reports the response has moved into an early recovery phase, shifting from emergency triage to restoring routine services and heading off disease outbreaks in crowded shelters. Aid agencies emphasise that the emergency window has closed but reconstruction will run for months and, in the worst-hit neighbourhoods, years — with durable housing for displaced families and rebuilding schools named as the central long-term tasks.

Tourism

Air links to Venezuela are reopening faster than at any point in a decade. Copa Airlines has confirmed a new Panama City–Porlamar service to Isla Margarita beginning November 26, running Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays and making the island Copa's sixth Venezuelan destination after Caracas, Valencia, Maracaibo, Barcelona and Barquisimeto — which puts Margarita within one connection of more than eighty cities across the Americas. American Airlines restored daily Miami–Caracas flights on April 30, restoring the most commercially important U.S. link. Operators are pitching a three-part product — Caribbean beaches on Margarita and Los Roques, tepui-and-waterfall adventure travel in the Gran Sabana, and heritage tours in Caracas — though prospective visitors should note that Western governments still maintain restrictive travel advisories and the earthquake zone around the capital remains in reconstruction.

Site to visit: Canaima National Park, a 30,000-square-kilometre UNESCO World Heritage Site in Bolívar state, is built around the flat-topped tepui mountains and Angel Falls, the tallest uninterrupted waterfall on earth at 979 metres. There is no road access — visitors fly into Canaima village from Caracas or Ciudad Bolívar, then continue by motorised curiara up the Carrao and Churun rivers. Practical detail: go during the June-to-December wet season, when the river is deep enough for boats to reach the falls and the cascade is at full volume; in the dry months it can thin to a trickle.

Science & Technology

The electricity grid remains the country's binding technical constraint. Monitors recorded at least 35 blackouts in the first quarter of 2026, with average outages lasting four hours and thirteen minutes and voltage instability rising nearly 300% year-on-year — failures attributed less to missing hardware than to deferred maintenance and disinvestment. Since March the electric energy ministry has been led by Rolando Alcalá, a Simón Bolívar University-trained electrical engineer who has prioritised the stalled Tocoma project, maintenance at the Guri hydroelectric complex, and re-engagement with Siemens and General Electric on thermoelectric plant recovery. Independent estimates put the cost of rebuilding the national grid at between $12 billion and $24 billion, a figure that dwarfs current public investment and that analysts argue must be resolved before any manufacturing revival is realistic.

🔥 Trending
Cashea
Fintech / Consumer · Caracas

Cashea is a Venezuelan buy-now-pay-later app that has quietly become one of the most widely used pieces of software in the country, and its rise is a direct consequence of a vanished banking system: consumer credit in Venezuela collapsed from roughly $15 billion to $1.7 billion as the economy contracted, and only about 7% of adults hold a credit card. Founded in Caracas to fill that vacuum, the app lets shoppers split purchases into interest-free instalments at partner stores, using in-app behaviour rather than a credit bureau to set limits — and it grew from 1.2 million users in its first year to more than 5 million largely through word of mouth. It now reports around 10 million accounts, reaching an estimated 35% of the adult population — more Venezuelans have a Cashea account than a credit card. In 2026 the company raised $100 million from international backers and says the entire sum will be deployed inside Venezuela.
