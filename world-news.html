Thursday, October 29, 2026 · Oceania

Australia

Oceania · population ~26M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese ended a difficult parliamentary week bogged down in questions over a $6 million invitation-only grant to the Marrickville Golf Club, which he had defended as funding to fix sewage overflow into a local river; officials told Senate questioners that sewerage works were not part of the project, and Albanese later conceded the link “was not correct”. The Coalition has seized on the saga while Opposition figure Angus Taylor released costings for spending cuts. Separately, One Nation has refused the prime minister's offer of an extra parliamentary staffer, with Pauline Hanson accusing him of meddling in staffing allocations. In Tasmania, a Greens bill banning conversion practices passed the lower house.

Economy & Business

Annual inflation rose to 4.3 per cent in September from 4 per cent in August, driven by housing (up 5.2 per cent) and a 9.4 per cent jump in fuel prices; markets put the chance of a 0.25-point Reserve Bank hike at about 85 per cent ahead of its meeting next week. Treasurer Jim Chalmers has lowered expectations for further fuel discounts. Elsewhere, the failed float of AI data-centre firm Firmus has cast doubt on billions of dollars in AI chip commitments, and the federal government conditionally committed $180 million to the Donald rare earths and mineral sands mine in western Victoria, which local farmers oppose.

Military & Security

Security attention this week centred on the alleged attack on independent MP Helen Haines, who was hit by a vehicle outside her Wangaratta electorate office on 19 October and lost both legs; she has since come out of an induced coma and is stable. The man accused, Brendan Nugent, faces charges including intentionally causing serious injury and harming a Commonwealth public official, and Victoria Police are leading the investigation. An initial prison assessment explored delusions and possible psychosis but found no sign of ideological extremism. He remains in custody ahead of a court date in February.

Society & Culture

Hundreds of nurses and prison guards walked off the job in Canberra in what is described as the largest coordinated ACT public-sector strike since self-government in 1989. Doctors working in Northern Territory prisons say limited access to detainees is hindering care, while experts report a “systemic” rise in Canberra children with complex developmental needs. In the arts, Biennale of Sydney chief Barbara Moore is being made a knight of France's Order of Arts and Letters.

Tourism

Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport officially opened to passengers on 25 October with a Jetstar flight to the Gold Coast, and its first international flight, from Auckland, landed the next day. The curfew-free airport is intended to operate around the clock, though the planned Metro rail link was not running at launch, so visitors should arrange ground transport in advance. At Uluru, rangers and tour operators warn that invasive buffel grass is spreading around the base of the rock, threatening the landscape's World Heritage values.

Site to visit: Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park in the Northern Territory is a UNESCO World Heritage site, jointly managed with its Anangu traditional owners, and home to the great sandstone monolith Uluru and the domed rocks of Kata Tjuta. A park pass is required (multi-day passes cost roughly A$38 for adults; check Parks Australia for current prices), and the cooler months of May to September are the most comfortable for walking; the park is a short drive from Ayers Rock (Connellan) Airport.

Science & Technology

Western Australia is poised to become the first Australian jurisdiction to legalise plug-in “balcony” solar panels and batteries, opening consultation on safety rules; the state estimates households could save about $240 a year, or up to $500 with a battery. In energy and research, internal messages suggest CSIRO worked to manage its public image while helping Santos investigate a long-undisclosed methane leak at Darwin LNG, and a leaked letter shows the national regulator raised concerns with Santos over its Barossa project.

🔥 Trending
Native-ingredient fine dining from the National Indigenous Culinary Institute
Food & culture movement · Sydney / nationwide

The National Indigenous Culinary Institute, set up in 2012 by restaurant critics and chefs including Neil Perry and Matt Moran, runs an apprenticeship that turns Indigenous cooks into qualified chefs. Its graduates are carrying bush flavours into mainstream kitchens, from finger lime salsa to strawberry gum macarons built on French patisserie technique. More than 100 chefs have completed the program, and the 2026 class of 11 is its largest yet, though only about a third of apprentices finish. Alumni such as Luke Bourke, head chef at Felons Seafood on Manly Wharf, are now earning industry recognition.

Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · Asia

South Korea

Asia · population ~52M

Politics & Government

President Lee Jae Myung used a national safety meeting at Cheong Wa Dae to urge faster action against preventable deaths on the eve of the fourth anniversary of the Itaewon crowd crush, noting that the Framework Act on Life and Safety takes effect December 3. Families of foreign victims, meanwhile, say footage of the final moments shows too thin a police presence. Lee also met former Assembly speaker Woo Won-shik to stress national unity, while the Supreme Court issued a new detention warrant for former first lady Kim Keon Hee.

Economy & Business

Seoul stocks surged on Wednesday as the KOSPI closed at 6,012.44, up 6.63%, after SK hynix reported third-quarter revenue of 104.6 trillion won, forecast chip shortages through 2030 and announced a 100 trillion won shareholder return plan. The won, meanwhile, had earlier strengthened to about 1,329 per dollar on robust semiconductor exports, its strongest in two years, and the IMF has raised its 2026 growth forecast for Korea to 3.3 percent. Power-equipment makers such as HD Hyundai Electric and LS Electric are racing to expand U.S. capacity for AI data centers, and Homeplus stores now face power cuts over unpaid bills.

Military & Security

Tension along the inter-Korean border continues: Seoul's military said it saw no signs of newly deployed North Korean weapons after Kim Yo Jong said loudspeakers would be put on standby and new "counter measures" were on alert. Pyongyang denies responsibility for a DMZ explosion last month that injured three South Korean soldiers. On the alliance front, Washington stressed conditions for wartime operational control transfer as Seoul pushes for a target year, and the annual defense chiefs' talks are unlikely to take place this year according to the Korea Times.

Society & Culture

Firefighters contained the main blaze at Seoul's Guryong shantytown with no casualties reported, and the Korea Herald looks at the neighborhood's uncertain future. In Gyeonggi, pediatric doctors are threatening to resign after a disputed police raid on a hospital, and nationwide cold wave advisories took effect on Tuesday.

Tourism

The culture minister says Korea is nearing its goal of 30 million foreign visitors and wants to spread the boom beyond Seoul: arrivals have already passed 2025's full-year total of 18.94 million, and the 2027 tourism budget rises 19% to 1.756 trillion won, with five regional zones built around airports in Gimhae, Daegu, Cheongju, Muan and Yangyang. Regional airports will also expand international flights by 11.6% this winter, including new China routes from Gimhae and Cheongju.

Site to visit: Bulguksa Temple on Tohamsan in Gyeongju is a Buddhist temple whose current form dates to 751–774 under the Silla kingdom, and it was inscribed by UNESCO in 1995 together with the nearby Seokguram Grotto. It is a regional destination well suited to Korea's push beyond Seoul; reach Gyeongju by KTX to Singyeongju station and continue by local bus or taxi, and aim for late autumn or spring for the best scenery.

Science & Technology

Suspected AI-assisted cyberattacks that began with a Shinhan Bank breach reported on September 30 have spread across most financial segments, and the Ministry of Science and ICT will now review security at more than 170 critical telecom and internet facilities, including systems run by SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus. The Korea Herald reports the wave has widened, including a data breach at Daishin Securities affecting over 38,000 customers.

🔥 Trending
HANRORO
Indie singer-songwriter · Seoul (Hongdae scene)

HANRORO, a 26-year-old Korean-literature major who writes all her own lyrics, grew from a 400-seat Hongdae hall in 2023 to two sold-out shows at a 5,000-seat Korea University arena in November 2025, with her three EPs taking the top three spots on Yes24's weekly album chart. Her 2026 run has taken her abroad: she played Summer Sonic in Japan and sold out her first Tokyo solo show, and student crowds sing along at university festivals, including Kangwon and Inha universities in September. Her rise came through word of mouth and live shows rather than a major-label push.

Tuesday, October 27, 2026 · Europe

Germany

Europe · population ~84M

Politics & Government

Berlin is searching for a successor to Frank-Walter Steinmeier, with the Federal Assembly due to vote on January 30 and no candidate yet agreed; CSU politician Ilse Aigner is gaining support, while Chancellor Friedrich Merz's planned joint proposal with the CSU and SPD leaders is still pending. Merz also said Germany will actively support Serbia's European path after a student-led movement defeated Aleksandar Vucic's party in Sunday's election. On the opposition side, about 421,000 alleged internal emails from AfD accounts appeared online after a cyberattack; the party confirmed the hack, but the authenticity of the material has not been verified. Separately, the justice and construction ministers proposed a "Building Type E" bill to relax optional construction standards and cut building costs by an estimated 10 to 15 percent.

Economy & Business

The Ifo business climate index rose for a sixth straight month to 90.2 points in October, its highest level since May 2023, and the government has raised its 2026 growth forecast to 1.3 percent from 0.5 percent in April. Carmakers remain a flashpoint: IG Metall president Christiane Benner condemned Audi CEO Gernot Doellner for appearing at a Trump campaign event in Georgia, where a $4.2 billion Audi plant in Macon was announced. Audi says the plant would create about 4,500 jobs, but Volkswagen's supervisory board has yet to approve it, and the union fears for German jobs such as those at Neckarsulm.

Military & Security

In Neustadt am Rübenberge near Hanover, a car drove into a group late Monday, injuring four to five people, some seriously but none in danger of death; police at a nearby traffic stop fired on the vehicle and the suspects fled on foot. Investigators believe the group was likely targeted deliberately. In Bavaria, police detained a 33-year-old man after a fire at the Kehlsteinhaus; prosecutors say arson appears likely and have not ruled out a political motive.

Society & Culture

Youth organisers expect about 130,000 people at "Gen Z" protests in 23 cities on Saturday, October 31, over school conditions, cost of living, conscription, surveillance and climate. Politicians are split: the CDU's Alexander Throm warned of hijacking by extremists, while the Greens stressed the right to peaceful protest. Meanwhile, the interior ministry has put forward a plan to block housing socialisation in Berlin.

Tourism

A fire on Friday night badly damaged the Kehlsteinhaus ("Eagle's Nest"), the mountaintop building above Obersalzberg that has housed an exhibition and restaurant since 1952 and draws about 350,000 visitors a year. About 200 firefighters responded and no one was hurt; Bavaria's interior minister said the site will not be left in ruins. Elsewhere, storms Boelo and Austen knocked out power to about 10,000 homes around Freiburg and disrupted flights across northern Europe over the weekend. Travellers should check access and flight status before heading out.

Site to visit: The Kehlsteinhaus sits at 1,834 metres on a summit above Obersalzberg in the Bavarian Alps, built in 1937-38 and now a restaurant and exhibition on its Nazi history. It is notable for its survival through WWII undamaged and for its dramatic alpine setting. Because of the fire and police cordon, check current access before planning a visit.

🔥 Trending
Gen Z regelt
Youth movement · Germany (Cologne, Frankfurt, Hamburg, Berlin, Munich)

The youth alliance Gen Z regelt is organising nationwide demonstrations for Halloween, with registrations showing about 130,000 expected attendees across 23 cities. The group runs events directly in five major cities and partner groups organise the other 18, with the biggest crowds expected in Stuttgart, Erfurt and Bonn. Its position paper describes the protests as nonpartisan and covers six topics, from school austerity to conscription. The movement follows earlier protests this year by tens of thousands of mostly high-school students against military conscription, as young Europeans push to be heard.

Monday, October 26, 2026 · Asia

Iraq

Asia · population ~43M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's government has made the 2027 federal budget its central project, describing it as a performance-based economic roadmap that guarantees public salaries and prioritises electricity and solar investment. The bill was scheduled to reach parliament by mid-September, and the Kurdistan Region is pressing for a 14% share against the 12.67% it now receives, with oil revenues and salaries still disputed between Erbil and Baghdad. Separately, a PUK lawmaker said the cabinet will be completed once the parliamentary recess ends. Note: the most recent budget and cabinet reporting found dates from August and September; no newer update was located this week.

Economy & Business

The Central Bank of Iraq devalued the dinar on 7 October, moving the official rate from 1,300 to 1,500 per dollar, a 13.3% cut in the dinar's value. Oil, which supplies about 90% of government revenue, has recovered to roughly 75% of pre-war export levels in September after the Strait of Hormuz closure in March, and non-oil revenue rose 24% in January-August. Foreign reserves fell 23% between end-2025 and end-September, and at the IMF-World Bank meetings on 17 October the IMF's managing director said Iraq has requested an IMF programme. The draft 2027 budget assumes oil at $58 a barrel and spending of 217 trillion dinars, leaving a deficit above 40 trillion.

Society & Culture

Shafaq News reported in late July on young Iraqis building careers as content creators, with monitoring centres estimating more than 32 million people, about 70% of the population, use social media. Official figures are unavailable, so the estimate should be treated with caution. This item is older than seven days; no fresher society coverage was found in the searches run.

Tourism

Religious tourism remains the backbone of Iraq's visitor economy. Karbala's governor said more than 22 million people entered the city for this year's Arbaeen pilgrimage in early August, including about 5 million foreign pilgrims from 172 countries, which officials called a record. Authorities reported no terrorist attacks and 46 deaths, all in traffic accidents. These figures come from Iraqi officials and were not independently verified, and no tourism news from the past seven days was found.

Site to visit: The ruins of Babylon, south of Baghdad near Hillah, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the remains of the capital of Nebuchadnezzar II, including a reconstructed Ishtar Gate. Visit between October and April to avoid extreme summer heat, and check current security and entry-fee conditions before travelling.

Science & Technology

Iraq's electricity ministry plans up to 10 GW of new solar capacity, and the prime minister has called 2027 the year of doubling electricity production and distribution. Iran halted gas supplies to Iraqi power plants when the war began, and many households outside Kurdistan receive only 6 to 10 hours of power a day. The reporting is from late August; no October update was found.

Sunday, October 25, 2026 · Africa

Ethiopia

Africa · population ~128M

Politics & Government

Ethiopia's federal government is consolidating its hold over Tigray after the fighting that began in late September. Federal forces retook the regional capital Mekelle earlier this month, and Addis Ababa appointed an interim Tigray administration on October 16, according to Africanews. On October 20 the prime minister warned Eritrea of "severe consequences" if it backs Ethiopia's enemies. Ethiopia and Eritrea had already broken diplomatic relations on October 1, and the US Secretary of State has since urged de-escalation.

Military & Security

The TPLF says the war is not over. Its leader Debretsion Gebremichael said in a broadcast on Wednesday that fighters are still resisting federal forces and claimed to have killed hundreds of federal soldiers in northern and central Tigray last week. Those claims are unverified. The same report says a drone strike killed four people in southern Tigray earlier this week, and that other strikes reportedly hit civilians and a school in Amhara, where the army is fighting Fano militants. Analysts warn the army's advance could raise the risk of war with Eritrea, and media access to the affected areas remains restricted, with communications disrupted.

Saturday, October 24, 2026 · South America

Chile

South America · population ~19M

Politics & Government

A justice scandal dominated the week. Justice Minister Fernando Rabat confirmed on October 21 that the ministry had granted 56 good-conduct sentence reductions to people convicted of child sexual abuse or rape of a minor since March, and on October 23 the ministry paused processing new reductions. Rabat says the decisions follow Supreme Court doctrine and that a 2022 reform cannot be applied retroactively, while former minister Hernán Larraín disputes that reading and the courts have issued conflicting rulings. The opposition is weighing a constitutional accusation, and the government is backing urgent bills in Congress, including a Senate proposal to move such decisions from commissions to judges. The episode lands as President José Antonio Kast faces falling approval and protests over his 2027 budget, which Congress has 60 days to amend.

Economy & Business

Mining news was mixed. Regional authorities unanimously approved the US$870 million Tovaku copper mine, a Pucobre (60%) and Codelco (40%) venture planned to produce 46,000 tonnes of cathodes a year, though two state water agencies raised concerns. Meanwhile lithium fell to US$16,050 a tonne on October 20, its lowest since January, on weaker expected Chinese battery demand, and three special lithium contracts remain under review at the audit office. The central bank renewed its five-year yuan-peso swap with China at about US$7.5 billion as a precautionary backstop, while the peso hovers near 1,000 per dollar after the World Bank cut its 2026 growth forecast to 0.8%.

Tourism

Santiago's events calendar is filling up: Ed Sheeran is scheduled for two concerts at Estadio Bicentenario on November 21 and 22, and the Teatro a Mil festival returns January 3 to 24 with about 90 works. Chile's official tourism board also reports that the country won four categories at the 2026 World Travel Awards South America, adding to a run of international travel recognition.

Site to visit: Torres del Paine National Park in Magallanes is Chilean Patagonia's best-known hiking destination, with granite towers, glaciers and the W and O treks. Its gateway town, Puerto Natales, now sees around 360,000 visitors a year, nearly double a decade ago. Park entry for foreigners starts at roughly CLP 14,000, and the southern-hemisphere summer from November to March is the main season.

🔥 Trending
Q Are
Music (K-pop-inspired boy band) · Santiago

Q Are is a six-member Santiago boy band that writes its own songs, choreography and visuals in the K-pop mould but aims for something "fresh and Chilean." NPR's profile describes how K-pop grew from a niche interest to a mainstream force among young Chileans, with Korean content overtaking anime as the dominant foreign pop culture. The group has toured Chile and Brazil, draws fans from across the Spanish-speaking world, Korea and Europe, and has a debut album due later this year. Its rise coincided with BTS's October stadium dates in Santiago.
