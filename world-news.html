Wednesday, September 23, 2026 · South America

Colombia

South America · population ~52M

Politics & Government

President Abelardo de la Espriella, the far-right former lawyer who took office on 7 August after narrowly beating Iván Cepeda, hardened his line on the ELN this week, rejecting the guerrilla group's proposed "hostage exchange" for the police officers and a civilian it still holds and demanding their immediate, unconditional release while ordering the armed forces to intensify search operations. His young government is also facing its first transparency fight: a Colombian judge this week ordered the Ministry of Defence to disclose full records of the president's flights after his early trips became the subject of public-spending questions. The scrutiny has not dented his standing so far — a CB Global Data survey put his positive image at 53.6% in mid-September, placing him third among Latin American leaders behind Mexico's Claudia Sheinbaum and El Salvador's Nayib Bukele, just over a month into his term.

Economy & Business

Vice President José Manuel Restrepo took Colombia's investment pitch to Wall Street this week, presenting a portfolio of 176 projects worth more than US$150 billion to over 60 investors in New York during UN General Assembly week, part of the government's "Misión Crecer" push to lift growth toward 6% through energy, infrastructure, agribusiness and AI ventures. Markets have been rewarding the optimism at home: the COLCAP index gained a fourth straight session on 22 September, up 2.6% over the week, as buyers rotated out of Ecopetrol and into telecoms, gas distribution and infrastructure stocks, while the peso held steady near 3,209 to the dollar. The broader recovery remains modest rather than dramatic, with GDP growing 2.2% year-on-year in the first quarter against elevated inflation near 5.9% and unemployment around 9%.

Military & Security

Security forces killed José Luis Pérez Villanueva, alias "Cholo," the second-in-command of the Autodefensas Conquistadoras de la Sierra Nevada, in a raid near Santa Marta on 21 September, a paramilitary group whose leadership had been designated a "peace manager" under the previous government; the operation triggered a retaliatory 48-hour armed strike that saw trucks and buses burned and merchants threatened across the city and along the Tayrona road. Days earlier, Medellín police arrested three men, including an army major, accused of brokering ties between ELN guerrillas and a FARC dissident faction that Antioquia's governor said had allied against the rival Clan del Golfo for control of illegal income streams. The crackdown follows President de la Espriella's decision earlier this month to shut the country's last remaining peace negotiating table and strip armed groups of their political status, reactivating arrest warrants that had been suspended for talks — a hardening stance now playing out in near-daily clashes across the north and northeast.

Society & Culture

Migration pressure from Venezuela has eased markedly this year: Migración Colombia recorded roughly 56,000 irregular-transit detections through mid-July, down almost 39% from the same period in 2025, even as the country continues hosting some three million Venezuelan refugees, by far the largest such population in the region. Culturally, the town of Tocancipá just wrapped its 26th Festival of Colombian Culture, drawing more than 4,000 performers and delegations from five other Latin American countries to mark the town's 433rd anniversary, while Medellín's 20th International Book and Culture Festival closed on 20 September with a free, ten-day programme built around the theme "Journeys Through Reading."

Tourism

Colombia is riding a genuine tourism wave into its 2026 high season: Lonely Planet named the country's Coffee Region, Tayrona National Natural Park, La Guajira, Bogotá and the Amazon among its must-visit South American destinations for 2026, while Cartagena separately landed a top-25 spot on the outlet's global Best in Travel list. International arrivals are climbing to match the attention, with travel from the US, Mexico, Brazil, Spain and Panama helping push growth of roughly 16.7% this year and Bogotá alone on pace for more than 15 million total travellers, an aviation and hotel boom that is reshaping the capital's short-term rental market.

Site to visit: The Coffee Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2011 centred on the postcard towns of Salento and Filandia in the Zona Cafetera, preserves a century-old tradition of small-farm coffee cultivation on steep Andean slopes alongside the towering wax palms of the nearby Cocora Valley. A traditional finca tour costs as little as 20,000–53,000 COP (roughly $5–13) including a tasting, reached by hourly jeeps from Salento's main square; the driest, clearest months from December to March and July to August are the easiest time to hike the Cocora trail, and the region is best accessed by flying into Armenia's airport before a short bus or taxi ride into town.

Science & Technology

President de la Espriella named Nubank founder and OpenAI board member David Vélez as an unpaid advisor this week to lead what the president called "the revolution of artificial intelligence in Colombia," tasking him with helping build a smaller, more efficient state, overhaul education for an AI-driven economy, and get social programmes to reach citizens more directly through technology. The appointment, agreed after a three-hour meeting between the two men in Barranquilla, comes with an explicit ambition: making Colombia the first Latin American country to be "native" to artificial intelligence. It builds on an already sizeable base, with the country's roughly 2,295 active startups still led by fintech even as SaaS, agritech, logistics and healthtech ventures gain ground in Bogotá and Medellín.

🔥 Trending
Alejo Portela
Music / vallenato · Bogotá (performing in New York City)

Alejo Portela is a vallenato musician, arranger and photographer from Bogotá who has spent recent years busking with his accordion in the New York City subway, trying to carry Colombia's signature folk genre to audiences who had never heard it. His moment came when a passer-by kicked his microphone to the ground mid-performance; rather than react, Portela kept playing, and a bystander's video of the incident spread far beyond Colombia, eventually racking up tens of millions of views (From Colombia to New York: How Alejo Portela Is Taking Vallenato Beyond Borders). What could have been a forgettable subway indignity instead turned into an organic international introduction to vallenato, with messages of support pouring in from as far as Africa, Australia and India, and Portela has kept performing and posting since, now with a global audience discovering the genre through him rather than through any label or campaign.

Tuesday, September 22, 2026 · North America & Asia

Cuba

North America · population ~12M

Politics & Government

Washington kept up its squeeze on Havana this month, adding a fresh round of sanctions on 4 September that targeted Cuban state companies and, pointedly, 31-year-old Fidel Ernesto Castro, grandson of 95-year-old former leader Raúl Castro, part of what officials describe as an intensifying campaign against the island's finances (US continues to squeeze Cuban economy with new round of sanctions). Facing the country's sharpest economic contraction in years, President Miguel Díaz-Canel used the moment to signal an opening rather than defiance, saying Cuba would be "delighted" to welcome American companies willing to invest on the island, a striking departure from six decades of ideological resistance to foreign private capital (Cuba Economy Set to Contract as Díaz-Canel Courts U.S.). The overture sits awkwardly alongside Havana's public anger at Washington's parallel sanctions drive, underscoring a government caught between courting rapprochement on trade and rejecting the very sanctions regime it blames for the crisis.

Economy & Business

Cuba is on pace for the steepest economic contraction anywhere in Latin America this year: the UN's ECLAC now projects a 10.3% drop in GDP for 2026, sharply worse than the 6.5% decline it forecast in the spring, as disrupted oil supplies, collapsing tourism revenue and deepening financial isolation compound one another, leaving GDP per capita at just $1,082 against a regional average above $10,000 (Cuba Faces Steepest Economic Decline in Latin America by 2026). The energy side of that crisis turned acute again on 18 September, when a transmission-line failure triggered Cuba's sixth nationwide blackout since President Trump halted fuel shipments to the island in January, leaving millions without power and Havana's roughly 862,000 customers waiting days for a full restoration (Cuba hit with another nationwide blackout amid US fuel blockade). Even so, officials are dangling ecotourism and investment incentives to outside capital as the state-planned economy buckles under the strain.

Military & Security

The U.S. escalated its pressure campaign on 17 September, designating eleven Cuban entities and individuals tied to the island's nickel-mining and military-industrial network, from state firms SERCONI, CEPRONIQUEL and CEDINIQ, which fund and coordinate mineral extraction for the military, to the directors of research bodies SIMPRO, CIDNAV and CIDAI (Further Sanctions on Cuba's Mineral Wealth and Military Modernization Apparatus). Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the move targets "a system that enriched the elite" at ordinary Cubans' expense, blocking any assets the designated firms and officials hold under U.S. jurisdiction (U.S. sanctions Cuba's nickel reserves, military research entities). The action, issued under a Trump executive order covering Cuba's defence, mining, energy and financial sectors, adds another layer to the fuel and trade squeeze Havana blames for months of rolling blackouts and shortages.

Society & Culture

Cuba is living through what monitors are calling a summer of protest that has stretched into autumn: rights group Cubalex counted 766 demonstrations in the first half of 2026 alone, a wave driven less by ideology than by the practical misery of blackouts running up to 20 hours a day, food and medicine shortages, and a sharp rise in internet costs (Frontline Update: Cuba's Summer of Protest). Human Rights Watch's newest World Report finds the government responding with intensified repression, arbitrarily detaining and harassing critics, independent journalists and artists even as four UN special rapporteurs went public earlier this year over what they called a "systematic pattern of criminalization of dissent" (World Report 2026: Cuba). The unrest is unfolding against a mass exodus, with government figures showing the island has lost roughly 10% of its population in recent years, a toll independent researchers believe may run higher still.

Tourism

Cuba's tourism engine, once a crucial cushion for the economy, has gone into freefall this year: arrivals fell 62% between January and July compared with the same period in 2025, with just 419,000 visitors landing on the island against 1.1 million a year earlier, as Canada, historically Cuba's largest source market, recorded a 70% plunge (Cuba's tourism plummets 62% after U.S. pressure and energy embargo). The slump follows the Trump administration's ban on cruise ships, private planes and yachts and the closure of the educational-travel exemption that had underpinned much of the recent U.S. visitor boom, leaving Havana and Varadero hotels struggling to fill rooms (Cuba Tourism Plunges 60% as Havana and Varadero Face a Dramatic Visitor Crisis). Even amid the collapse, authorities used September to roll out new ecotourism tax incentives, alternative business models and cheaper beach-package deals aimed at coaxing travelers back for the 2026–27 season (Cuba Shocks Tourists With New Tourism Rules, Ecotourism Tax Perks and Cheap Beach Deals).

Site to visit: Old Havana and its Fortification System, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982, packs more than a thousand colonial-era buildings into four historic plazas ringed by a chain of Spanish coastal fortresses built to protect the New World's busiest shipbuilding port. Wandering the cobbled streets and plazas is free, though the fortresses and city museums charge only a few dollars' entry each, and the cooler, drier months from November to April are the easiest time to explore on foot before the midday heat and hurricane-season humidity set in.

🔥 Trending
Dichavate
Music / reparto movement · Havana

The song that dominated Cuban streets, TikTok and Latin charts this year needed no label and no ad budget: "Dichavate," released without warning on Christmas Eve by newcomers Ya Ice Dilan, Rey Tony and Helabusador with producer DJ Honda, is a product of reparto, the raw, DIY reggaeton-adjacent sound that has become the loudest grassroots export coming out of Havana's neighborhoods (Anatomy of a Hit: Breaking Down the Viral Reparto Song 'Dichavate'). None of the four had a marketing budget behind them — Helabusador has said they couldn't even run paid Spotify or YouTube campaigns because of the song's explicit lyrics — so they simply uploaded the video and let it spread (Breaking Down the Viral Reparto Hit 'Dichavate'). "That first week was absolute madness," Rey Tony recalled of the track's takeoff back home in Cuba, a virality that has since carried the song onto Billboard's Latin charts and pulled international stars like Bad Gyal and Maluma into its orbit, all without the state media apparatus that normally gatekeeps Cuban cultural exports.

Uzbekistan

Asia · population ~36M

Politics & Government

President Shavkat Mirziyoyev spent the past week deepening Uzbekistan's pivot toward East Asia, making a state visit to Seoul from 13 to 16 September where he met South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, National Assembly speaker Cho Jeong-sik and Prime Minister Han Duck-soo before laying out five priority areas for economic cooperation: trade, investment, critical minerals, transport and infrastructure (Uzbekistan, Korea to unlock 'joint leap in development'). The trip culminated in the first-ever Central Asia–Republic of Korea Summit on 16–17 September, which also drew the presidents of Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan to Seoul to discuss trade, digital transformation and science and technology cooperation across the region (Mirziyoyev's State Visit to South Korea Ahead of First Central Asia-Korea Summit). Back home, Mirziyoyev handed his powerful chief of staff, Saida Mirziyoyeva, an additional role managing the new Tashkent International Financial Center, consolidating control over the country's flagship push to build a regional financial hub (President of Uzbekistan to pay state visit to Republic of Korea).

Economy & Business

Uzbekistan's economy kept expanding at one of the fastest clips in Eurasia this year: the Eurasian Development Bank now projects 7.9% GDP growth for 2026, which would be the strongest expansion in a decade, while the Asian Development Bank and IMF put the figure closer to 6.5–6.7%, still among the highest growth rates in the region (EDB projects 7.9% GDP growth for Uzbekistan in 2026). GDP per capita reached roughly 27.95 million soums, about $2,370, in the first half of the year, up 6.6% in real terms, even as inflation is forecast to hold near 6.5% for 2026 before easing toward 5% in 2027 (Uzbekistan's GDP per capita rises 6.6% in real terms in 1H 2026). Homegrown manufacturers are riding the same wave: a growing "Made in Uzbekistan" push is carrying local textile, appliance and food brands into export markets as entrepreneurs try to convert the country's manufacturing growth into recognisable national brands (Made in Uzbekistan: local brands look to new markets).

Military & Security

Uzbekistan is stepping up multilateral military drills even as it modernises its air force: Azerbaijan is hosting the trilateral "Unity Force 2026" exercises with Turkey and Uzbekistan from 21 September to 1 October, spanning training grounds and the Caspian Sea as the three partners test coordinated response across land, air and naval domains (Azerbaijan Will Host Trilateral Military Drills with Turkey and Uzbekistan). The exercises follow staff-level talks between the Uzbek and Azerbaijani defence ministries under this year's bilateral military cooperation plan, part of a broader deepening of security ties between the South Caucasus and Central Asia (Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan sign 2026 military plan). Separately, Tashkent has confirmed it now operates Chinese-made J-10CE fighter jets, turning months of speculation into an acknowledged fact and marking a notable new entrant into Central Asia's defence market alongside Uzbekistan's traditional Russian-supplied fleet (Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan explore military cooperation).

Society & Culture

Uzbekistan is overhauling how it trains doctors and pharmacists: President Mirziyoyev reviewed proposals on 4 September for a sweeping restructuring of medical education aimed at bringing training standards in line with the world's leading universities, part of a wider push he has framed around 2026 as the "Year of Training Young People in Modern Professions" (A new system instead of "General Medicine"). On the ground, EU Ambassador Toivo Klaar led a "Team Europe" mission to the Fergana and Namangan regions on 15 September, meeting regional authorities to scope out European investment, expertise and business exchange away from the capital (Uzbekistan News: announcements of cultural and tourist events). Independent monitors flag a harder edge behind that outward-facing diplomacy, though: Human Rights Watch's newest World Report finds independent Muslims still being imprisoned, detained and fined for peaceful religious activity even as the government promotes its cultural and educational outreach abroad (World Report 2026: Uzbekistan).

Tourism

Uzbekistan's tourism boom kept accelerating through the summer: the country logged 9.1 million foreign visits in the first eight months of 2026, up 21.1% year-on-year, with tourism-service exports jumping 53.5% to $4.6 billion over the same period (Uzbekistan receives 9.1 mln foreign tourists in first eight months of 2026). Average spending per visitor has climbed to $503, more than double 2017 levels, as travellers increasingly go "beyond the classic route" of Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva into the Fergana Valley, Kashkadarya, Surkhandarya and the Aral Sea region around Muynak and Nukus (Beyond the Silk Road: visitors stay longer, spend more). Arrivals from China more than doubled and Malaysia's doubled too, while Germany, France and the UK all posted double-digit growth, helped by a visa-free regime that now covers citizens of 94 countries (Inflow of Tourists to Uzbekistan Increases by Over 21%).

Site to visit: The Registan in Samarkand, a plaza of three monumental 15th–17th century madrasahs tiled in turquoise and lapis mosaics, was the intellectual heart of the Silk Road under Timurid rule and remains Uzbekistan's most photographed landmark. A single ticket, roughly 100,000 UZS (about $8), covers all three madrasahs and their small museums, though viewing the square from outside is free; the madrasahs are open 8am–8pm, and the cooler shoulder months of September–October and April–May are the most comfortable time to explore on foot.

Science & Technology

Uzbekistan is making its boldest play yet to become a regional tech hub: the government is standing up "Enterprise Uzbekistan," a new free zone for technology and financial firms that will operate under English common law, pairing tax incentives with legal familiarity designed to draw foreign capital past neighbouring hubs (Uzbekistan's Tech Gambit: nation vies for global stage with new free zone). Officials are leaning on demographics to sell the pitch — about 65% of the country's 39 million people are under 35, and 146 universities now offer IT-focused programmes, with IT Park representatives touting Uzbekistan as "the number one English-speaking nation in Central Asia" at a 10 September presentation (Central Asia Rising: Uzbekistan's tech gambit). The push culminates this week in ICT Week Uzbekistan 2026, running 22–25 September at Tashkent's CAEx exhibition centre, where startups, venture investors and global technology firms are gathering with artificial intelligence and international expansion topping the agenda (ICT Week Uzbekistan 2026 focuses on startups and AI).

🔥 Trending
OZBE
Streetwear / cultural movement · Tashkent

The label capturing Tashkent's youth culture right now is OZBE, a homegrown streetwear brand built on the idea that Uzbek identity deserves a modern visual language rather than a folkloric costume. Representative Raupjon Eshtemirov has described taking Uzbek ornament, calligraphy and cultural symbols and reworking them into streetwear silhouettes aimed squarely at a generation for whom self-expression runs through clothing, not tradition for its own sake (Uzbekistan's New Visual Language: How OZBE Reimagines Culture Through Streetwear). The brand started small, with limited drops rather than a mass launch, and grew a genuine community around it organically rather than through paid marketing, gradually becoming a fixture that both Tashkent teenagers and visiting tourists now seek out as a way to wear a more contemporary version of Uzbekistan (What to buy in Tashkent: 15 trendy Uzbek brands). It's part of a wider wave of "Made in Uzbekistan" labels turning local heritage into something young Uzbeks actually want to wear, rather than save for festivals.

Monday, September 21, 2026 · Asia & Europe

Saudi Arabia

Asia · population ~37M

Politics & Government

Riyadh spent the past week welding together a tighter regional security front over the Red Sea: Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi in Cairo on 15 September to press for coordinated protection of shipping lanes as Houthi forces tighten their grip on Yemen's coast and the Bab al-Mandeb strait (Saudi Arabia and Egypt press for Red Sea security). Egypt's foreign ministry called Saudi security "inseparable from Egypt's own national security," an unusually blunt statement that signals the Gulf-Egypt coalition Riyadh assembled in July, which also counts Turkey, Pakistan, Jordan, Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain among its members, is hardening into a more active partnership (How far will Egypt go to help Saudi Arabia secure the Red Sea?). The diplomacy came only days after the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation's General Secretariat condemned as "heinous" a Houthi drone attack that Saudi officials said targeted Makkah, pledging its full solidarity with the kingdom (OIC condemns Houthi attacks on Makkah and the Madinah region).

Economy & Business

Saudi Arabia's non-oil economy keeps carrying growth even as oil activity drags on the headline number: GDP expanded 3.0% year-on-year in the first quarter, down from a revised 5.2% pace at the end of 2025 (Saudi Arabia GDP: growth decelerates in Q1 2026). The Ministry of Finance's pre-budget statement projects fuller-year growth of around 4.6% for 2026, with government revenue forecast at SR1.15 trillion ($305.9 billion) against SR1.13 trillion in spending and a SR166 billion deficit (Saudi Arabia set for 4.6% GDP growth in 2026). The kingdom's push to diversify beyond hydrocarbons was on display at Money20/20 Middle East, held in Riyadh this month and drawing more than 450 fintech firms, 1,050 investors and roughly 38,500 attendees, one of the clearest signs yet of its ambitions to become a regional financial-technology hub (Money20/20 Middle East).

Military & Security

Fighting reached the edge of Islam's holiest city for the first time this month: Saudi Arabia said its air defences intercepted a Houthi drone before it could cross into the airspace over Mecca on 16 September, calling the holy city a "red line," while the Iran-aligned group's military spokesman denied the target and said its objective was oil and military infrastructure "far removed from the holy places" (Saudi Arabia accuses Houthis of 'red line' attack on Mecca). The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation and much of the Muslim world rushed to condemn the strike, even as Houthi forces continue sweeping south along Yemen's Red Sea coast toward the Bab al-Mandeb strait (Saudi Arabia calls attempted Mecca attack a 'red line'). Washington moved to help restock Riyadh's depleted defences days later: the State Department cleared a possible $24.3 billion sale of 48 F-35 stealth fighters and 49 engines on 17 September, which would make Saudi Arabia the first Arab F-35 operator, on the heels of a separate $5 billion sale of more than 10,000 JDAM-ER precision bomb kits approved on 4 September (State Department approves sale of 48 F-35s to Saudi Arabia).

Society & Culture

The kingdom is gearing up for its 96th National Day on 23 September, with the official theme "Our Pride Lies in Our Nature" framing a nationwide calendar of fireworks, concerts, horse racing in Taif and live music in AlUla (Lifestyle Corner: Saudi National Day 2026). On the cultural-infrastructure side, Culture Minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah unveiled plans for a new Riyadh University of Arts, a practice-based institution with global academic partnerships and scholarships for emerging talent that is set to begin operations this year (Saudi Arabia to open region's first cultural university in 2026). Saudi Arabia is also preparing to host Intervision 2026 in Riyadh, the international song contest that held its inaugural edition in Moscow last year, part of a broader Vision 2030 push to position the kingdom as a global entertainment destination (Intervision 2026).

Tourism

Saudi Arabia's tourism industry kept up a packed events calendar this month, with WTM Spotlight Riyadh running 8–10 September and the Hotel & Hospitality Expo Saudi Arabia following at the Riyadh Front from 15–17 September with the Ministry of Tourism as strategic partner (Hotel & Hospitality Expo Saudi Arabia returns to Riyadh). The kingdom is scaling capacity to match record demand, with more than 362,000 hotel rooms in the development pipeline and first-quarter visitor numbers reaching 37.2 million (Saudi Arabia Hosts Hotel & Hospitality Expo Riyadh). The Ministry of Tourism also updated four regulations covering travel services, tour guides, hospitality facilities and private hospitality units this month, part of a continuing effort to keep pace with the sector's rapid growth (Saudi Arabia updates four tourism regulations).

Site to visit: Hegra, Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, sits in the desert near AlUla and preserves more than 100 monumental tombs the Nabataeans carved into sandstone outcrops between the 1st century BCE and 1st century CE. Entry is by timed, guided tour only, booked in advance through the official Experience AlUla platform, with standard day-tours starting around SAR 95 (roughly $25) per person; the cooler months from October to March, just beginning, are the most comfortable time to explore the site on foot.

Science & Technology

Saudi Arabia's innovation strategy is shifting from adopting foreign technology to owning it outright: newly approved National Intellectual Property Strategy and Policy documents emphasise commercialisation, homegrown R&D and stronger control over intangible assets and technology transfer (From "Made in Saudi Arabia" to "Owned in Saudi Arabia"). The country's startup ecosystem, now valued at roughly $41.5 billion across 2,320 companies and growing 49.6% annually, has pushed eight Saudi cities into the global top 1,000 startup-ecosystem rankings (Startups in Saudi Arabia News, September 2026). The LEAP tech conference, held in Riyadh from 31 August to 3 September, added to the momentum, drawing entrepreneurs and investors from across the region (LEAP Tech Event).

🔥 Trending
Qormuz
Fashion / cultural movement · Riyadh

Qormuz is a Riyadh fashion house founded in 2017 by Abdulrahman Al-Abed, then 28, built around reviving Saudi heritage textiles and silhouettes rather than importing Western streetwear trends. Its biggest moment came when a jubba it made for Saudi actor Mohamed Al-Shehri became the first traditional Arab dress worn on the Venice Film Festival red carpet, a clip that spread purely through social sharing to rack up more than 40 million organic views with no ad spend behind it (Saudi actor Mohamed El-Shehri wears Qormuz at Venice premiere). A separate design, the "Al-Dalam" dress inspired by the pigeon towers of Al-Kharj, later became the talk of the Saudi Cup and has itself passed 15 million views, cementing Qormuz's reputation as proof that Saudi-made fashion can travel the world on its own cultural weight (Qormuz bringing traditional Saudi culture to future generations). The label has expanded from menswear into womenswear, perfume and jewellery entirely on the back of that organic momentum.

Russia

Europe · population ~144M (transcontinental; European portion listed here)

Politics & Government

Russia's ruling United Russia party won a record 355 of 450 State Duma seats, its best-ever showing, in results announced by election chief Ella Pamfilova after the country's first parliamentary election since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in 2022 (United Russia Set for Record Parliament Majority). With about 95% of ballots counted, the party took 57.83% of the party-list vote and led in 208 of 225 single-member constituencies, while turnout topped 59%, a new record for a Duma election, even as voting on 18–20 September was overshadowed by Ukrainian drone strikes on Moscow itself (Russia's ruling pro-Putin party on track to win wartime election). Analysts say President Vladimir Putin is likely to read the result as a mandate to press on with the war and tighten domestic control further, with Yabloko standing as the only registered party to have campaigned openly against the conflict (Ruling party dominates early results in Russia's wartime vote).

Economy & Business

Russia's economy is stabilising at a much slower pace than before the war: GDP grew 1.3% year-on-year in the second quarter, recovering from a 0.2% contraction in the first, even as government forecasters have cut their outlook toward near-zero growth for the year ahead (Russia cuts GDP growth forecast to near zero). Inflation has proved sticky, running at 6.3% annually as of 7 September against the central bank's 4% target, prompting the Bank of Russia to hold its key rate at a still-restrictive 14% at its 11 September meeting even as Putin insisted price growth remains "under control" (Russia's GDP growth will be within 1% in 2026, inflation 'under control' — Putin). The squeeze is filtering down to households: a foreign summer holiday has become a rare luxury for ordinary Russians this year as wartime taxation and a strained rouble reshape everyday spending (For Russians, a summer vacation abroad is now a luxury few can afford).

Military & Security

On the battlefield, Russian forces posted a net gain of roughly 58 square miles of Ukrainian territory over the four weeks to 14 September, a reversal from the previous month's small net loss, even as Ukrainian troops recaptured the Donetsk-region village of Seredne and pushed Russian units back near Shandryholove (The Russia-Ukraine War Report Card, Sept. 16, 2026). Russia kept up its long-range bombardment through the week, launching two Zirkon anti-ship missiles and 174 drones and loitering munitions at Ukraine overnight into 19 September, with a separate strike hitting a shopping centre in Zaporizhzhia the previous day (ISW Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, September 19, 2026). Rhetoric toward NATO's eastern flank also sharpened, with former security council secretary Nikolai Patrushev warning that if Poland took military action against Russia, "Warsaw will cease to exist within two or three days," a response to Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski's remark that NATO could defeat Russia "quite quickly" ('Warsaw Will Disappear in 2 or 3 Days': Kremlin Threatens Poland).

Society & Culture

Sunday's Duma election doubled as a study in managed democracy: with genuine opposition candidates largely absent from the ballot and prize draws offered to encourage electronic voting, CNN and other outlets described a vote unfolding against rising Ukrainian drone strikes and mounting quiet war fatigue at home (Empty podiums, prizes for e-voting, no opposition). Culturally, Moscow leaned into soft-power diplomacy this month, sending the State Academic Igor Moiseyev Ensemble of Folk Dance to headline the 14th Russian Culture Festival in Beijing from 28 August to 2 September as part of a broader push to deepen cultural ties with China (Russian Culture Festival in China concludes). Beyond its own borders, Ukrainian officials and researchers say Russia has intensified a systematic campaign to erase Ukrainian heritage and national identity in occupied territories, from school curricula to museum collections, according to a new Atlantic Council analysis (Russia escalates war on Ukrainian heritage and national identity).

Tourism

Russia's domestic tourism sector is being squeezed by its own wartime economy: visits to Lake Baikal, the country's most iconic natural destination, have fallen by about a third this year as prices climb beyond what many Russian travellers can afford (For Russians, a summer vacation abroad is now a luxury few can afford). Regulators tightened consumer protections from September, requiring tour operators and travel agents to disclose fuller details on hotels, transport, meals and potential risks before a trip is booked (Russia to require fuller tour disclosure from September 2026). The industry gathered this month for the 32nd REST 2026 international tourism forum and exhibition at Moscow's Timiryazev Centre, part of a push to shore up a domestic travel market increasingly cut off from Western destinations (REST 2026 International Forum and Exhibition on Tourism).

Site to visit: The Kremlin and Red Square in Moscow, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990, remain Russia's signature must-see, pairing the seat of government power with St Basil's Cathedral's onion domes along the square's cobblestones. Tickets to the Kremlin's Cathedral Square and Armoury Chamber are sold online with reservations opening 34 days ahead and entry only through the Kutafya Tower; the shoulder seasons of April–June and September–October offer the mildest weather and smallest crowds.

Science & Technology

Russia's space programme kept its uncrewed resupply cadence running, launching a Progress cargo craft carrying roughly three tonnes of supplies to the International Space Station on 16 September (AnewZ Daily Brief, 18 September 2026). On the ground, state nuclear firm Rosatom used the INNOPROM India exhibition in Delhi from 9–11 September to expand cooperation with New Delhi on nuclear technology, additive manufacturing, composite materials and digital and logistics solutions, part of Moscow's push to deepen technology partnerships outside the Western sanctions regime (Rosatom presents technology solutions at INNOPROM India). That sanctions-evasion effort has a harder edge too: a new investigation published this month found Western-made components, including US-origin electronics, still turning up inside Russian ballistic missiles more than three years into the sanctions regime (US tech found in Russian ballistic missiles).

Sunday, September 20, 2026 · Europe & Asia

Ukraine

Europe · population ~44M (pre-war estimate)

Politics & Government

US efforts to broker an end to the war regained momentum this month: special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held a three-hour Kremlin meeting with Vladimir Putin on 6 September before travelling on to Kyiv to press President Trump's latest proposal, only for the push to be undermined almost immediately when Russian missiles and drones resumed strikes after a brief lull, killing five people (Trump's new Ukraine push is already being undermined). President Volodymyr Zelensky has said elections are possible only once a ceasefire holds for several months (Zelenskyy on election timing), a question now entangled with the biggest corruption scandal of his presidency: polling suggests roughly 57% of Ukrainians now blame him for graft in state institutions, nearly double last year's figure, with several rival challengers projected to beat him head-to-head in a runoff (Is Ukraine Turning on Zelensky?). He has kept up a full diplomatic calendar regardless, signing a Ukraine-Canada "100 Year Partnership" declaration with Prime Minister Mark Carney and holding a run of bilateral meetings with European leaders this month (Office of the President of Ukraine, news).

Economy & Business

Ukraine's economy is under mounting strain: GDP grew just 0.4% year-on-year in the second quarter, one-and-a-half times weaker than first reported, as Russian strikes on energy infrastructure, a colder winter and rising energy prices weigh on output (Ukraine's economy hobbled by Russian strikes). The National Bank of Ukraine estimates Russia's blockade of Black Sea ports alone will cost roughly $2.5 billion in lost foreign-exchange earnings in the second half of the year, on top of new EU quotas on steel imports, and officials now warn full-year GDP could shrink if the shelling campaign continues into a "very difficult" winter (Analysts revise GDP forecasts for 2026). Kyiv's own economists have revised down their growth estimates to match, underscoring how directly the war's trajectory is now dictating the economic outlook (Ukraine revises down Q2 2026 GDP growth estimate).

Military & Security

On the ground, analysts say Russia's rate of advance has slowed markedly through 2026, with Ukrainian forces holding or regaining ground northwest of Hulyaipole even as Moscow expands drone strikes on rail hubs and border crossings to strangle grain exports and military supply lines (Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, September 6, 2026). The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant remained at the centre of the crisis, cut off from external power for more than a week before a locally brokered ceasefire let repair crews back in; Energoatom has logged more than 750 drone and artillery attacks on the site since late April, and Rosatom's chief met the IAEA's Rafael Grossi in Vienna on 15 September over its "sharply deteriorated" safety situation (Energoatom warns of critical safety situation, Rosatom, IAEA discuss plant safety in Vienna). Kyiv, meanwhile, is weighing a "radical" overhaul of mobilisation rules, including raising the draft's upper age limit to 50–55, as it looks to replenish depleted units without lowering the conscription floor below 25 (Ukraine weighing 'radical' mobilization changes).

Society & Culture

Ukraine's cultural institutions notched a rare wartime win this week: on 19 September the country signed the Council of Europe's convention on co-producing audiovisual works in series form, opening the door for Ukrainian producers to co-finance and distribute television series with European partners and reach new international markets (Ukraine signs Council of Europe convention). The signing came a week after heritage professionals gathered in Kyiv for a "Preserving Cultural Freedom in Ukraine" panel at the Fall 2026 Ukraine Action Summit, reflecting on efforts by museums, archives and cultural institutions to safeguard collections while continuing to operate under Russian bombardment (Preserving Ukraine's Cultural Freedom). The same week also carried Ukrainian Cinema Day and Physical Culture and Sports Day on 12 September, modest but pointed reminders that cultural life continues as the country enters its fifth wartime autumn.

Tourism

Tourism in Ukraine remains shaped by the war rather than paused by it: major governments continue to warn against non-essential travel and martial law persists nationwide, yet a blend of heritage travel, solidarity visits and domestic breaks has kept parts of the country, especially the relatively untouched west, busier than the headlines suggest (Ukraine's tourism resurgence amid conflict). Lviv has emerged as the clearest beneficiary, its old town now home to displaced Ukrainians alongside visitors, with bars and restaurants reportedly full most nights even as air-raid alerts and curfews remain part of daily routine elsewhere in the country (Tourism in Ukraine 2026: is it safe to travel?).

Site to visit: The Lviv Historic Centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose cobbled Market Square, Armenian and Latin cathedrals and fortification towers survived the Soviet era almost untouched, making it one of the best-preserved old towns in Eastern Europe. Most churches ask only a small donation to enter, the Tourist Information Centre inside Lviv's Town Hall is free to browse, and the historic core is easiest to reach on foot or by tram from the city's main railway station, which has direct rail connections from Kyiv, Warsaw and Kraków.

Science & Technology

Ukraine's drone industry keeps setting the pace for the war: independent estimates put 2026 domestic drone production at five to six million units, while roughly 280 companies now build more than 550 distinct uncrewed ground vehicle models, some logging over 10,000 logistics missions a month (Drone superpower Ukraine is now Europe's first line of defense). New AI-enabled systems are increasingly able to strike targets with greater autonomy while routing around Russian jamming, a capability credited with helping Ukraine blunt Moscow's numerical advantage on parts of the front (How Ukraine's drone innovation reversed Russia's momentum). The push now has formal European backing, too: the European Commission hosted the first meeting of the EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance in Brussels on 11 September, bringing together 19 defence contractors, including Ukraine's own Athlon Avia, to coordinate next-generation drone and counter-drone production (Commission hosts first meeting of EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance).

Turkey

Asia · population ~86M

Politics & Government

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's inner circle has now confirmed what had long been assumed: a senior adviser said on 17 September that the 72-year-old leader intends to run again in an early presidential election likely in April 2028, the first official word on his 2028 candidacy, with Justice Minister Akın Gürlek adding that "an election will be held to re-elect our esteemed president" (Erdogan to compete in 2028 elections, officials say). Triggering an early vote requires three-fifths of parliament, 360 of 600 seats, which Erdoğan's AKP and its allies do not currently hold with 326 seats, leaving the government roughly 34 votes short and fuelling speculation over which smaller parties might be courted to close the gap (Top adviser says Erdoğan will run again in 2028). The timeline is complicated by the continued imprisonment of Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, long seen as Erdoğan's strongest rival, who has been jailed since March 2025 on corruption charges and was separately handed a two-year sentence and a political ban, while critics point to a wider pattern of arrests of activists, journalists and opposition politicians as evidence of deepening authoritarianism (Senior AKP lawmaker on a parliamentary vote for early elections).

Economy & Business

Growth is losing momentum even as inflation slowly cools: GDP expanded 2.3% year-on-year in the second quarter, down from an upwardly revised 2.6% in the first, with a 12.7% plunge in exports dragging on the total even as domestic demand, construction and government spending held up (Turkey's GDP growth loses momentum). Annual inflation eased to 31.51% in August, its third straight monthly slowdown, and the central bank has held its benchmark rate at 37% for four consecutive meetings, leaning on foreign-exchange interventions rather than rate cuts to manage a lira that touched a record low near 48.5 to the dollar this month (Turkish Lira). Flagship carrier Turkish Airlines is absorbing its own share of the squeeze, with executives citing an Iran-conflict-driven spike in jet fuel costs that could add as much as $3.5 billion to this year's fuel bill even as the airline pushes ahead with fleet expansion and a planned 2028 return of a premium economy cabin (Turkish Airlines to launch premium economy).

Military & Security

Turkey's security posture along its southern border remains largely unchanged despite the PKK's announced dissolution: Ankara has continued backing Damascus's new government in its campaign against the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces while insisting its military outposts across northern Iraq and Syria are permanent fixtures of national security policy rather than bargaining chips (Turkey is searching for a way out of Syria's impasse). Turkish officials say operations against remaining militants will continue "until there is not a single terrorist left," even as Abdullah Öcalan's call for PKK fighters to disarm continues to work its way through implementation (Conflict Between Turkey and Armed Kurdish Groups). A NATO summit hosted in Ankara this month gave Turkey and the European Union an opening to recast their fraught relationship around shared security concerns, from Russia's war in Ukraine to Washington's reduced engagement in European defence, even as the two sides remain at odds over Cyprus, migration and Turkey's stalled EU accession bid (NATO summit gives Europe and Turkey a strategic reset opportunity).

Tourism

Turkey is on track to keep its place as the world's fourth most-visited country after welcoming 60.6 million international visitors and more than $56.3 billion in tourism revenue in its most recent full year, moving ahead of Italy, Mexico, the UK, Germany, Japan and Greece (Turkey Tourism 2026: the world's 4th most visited country). The picture has softened slightly this year, though: foreign arrivals slipped 2.3% year-on-year to 27.9 million in the January–July period, with July arrivals down 0.3%, even as Russia, Germany and the UK continue to anchor the country's top source markets (How Iran, Russia and other markets are driving Turkey's tourism). Istanbul, Antalya and Bodrum remain the country's tourism engines even as the softer numbers prompt operators to lean harder on off-peak and shoulder-season promotion heading into autumn.

Site to visit: Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in central Anatolia, is famed for its cone-shaped "fairy chimney" rock formations and the cave churches, dwellings and underground cities that early Christian and Byzantine inhabitants carved into the soft volcanic stone. The classic way to see it is a sunrise hot-air balloon flight over the valley, typically lasting 50–75 minutes and priced from around €85 upward depending on operator and season, with the calmest winds and clearest skies running April through October and quieter, cheaper flights from November to March.

Science & Technology

Turkey's earthquake scientists continue refining the picture of the fault networks that threaten Istanbul: new research shows variations in underground temperature and sediment thickness segment the Main Marmara Fault in ways that help determine where ruptures start, how far they travel and where they stop, part of a decade-long study of moderate quakes progressing eastward toward the city (How hidden factors beneath Istanbul shape earthquake risk). That research is feeding into disaster planning, with AFAD-linked techno-clusters spanning universities and private companies developing AI-assisted tools to flag structurally fragile buildings ahead of the long-anticipated İstanbul earthquake (Turkey: earthquake technologies market intelligence). Beyond seismology, Turkey's startup scene has leaned further into artificial intelligence this year, with 37 AI-focused ventures among the roughly 97 funded Turkish startups that raised a combined $181 million in the first seven months of 2026, even as gaming studios continued to pull in the largest share of venture capital (Startups in Turkey News, September 2026).

Saturday, September 19, 2026 · Africa

Nigeria

Africa · population ~225M

Politics & Government

President Bola Tinubu ordered a full investigation and demanded the prosecution of any officials found culpable after 37 detainees, including children, died inside a Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps facility in Minna, Niger State, following raids on suspected illegal gold mines in the nearby Wushishi-Lukoto area. Economy & Business

Nigeria's economy is gaining momentum, with GDP growing 4.43% year-on-year in the second quarter, its strongest pace in two years, led by agriculture, telecoms, real estate and trade. The naira has been unusually stable, holding around N1,329/$ for four straight sessions in mid-September, its longest stretch of stability since July 2025, while external reserves have crossed $54 billion for the first time since December 2008. Tax collections reached roughly N27.1 trillion in the first seven months of the year, and officials are framing the numbers as evidence the reform programme is stabilising the economy even as households continue to absorb elevated living costs.

Military & Security

The northeast insurgency has resurged: the JAS faction of Boko Haram attacked Darul Jamal, a recently resettled community of returned displaced people in Bama LGA, Borno State, killing at least 60 people including soldiers, in one of the deadliest single assaults on civilians in months. Analysts tracking the conflict note that while JAS has turned back to targeting civilians, the rival ISWAP faction continues to focus its attacks on military personnel and assets, and banditry persists largely unchecked across the northwest.

Society & Culture

Anger over the Minna detainee deaths spilled into the streets this week: authorities imposed an overnight curfew after security forces opened fire as hundreds of protesters marched through the city. In Lagos, the Academic Staff Union of Universities declared a total strike across three state universities after the government missed a 13 September deadline to implement its December 2025 welfare agreement, and the Nigeria Labour Congress and Trade Union Congress have signalled they may call a nationwide strike over worsening insecurity. In Ondo State, student protesters stood down after Governor Lucky Aiyedatiwa promised to resolve a separate university funding dispute within two weeks.

Tourism

The Federal Ministry of Art, Culture, Tourism and the Creative Economy formally launched "Ember to Remember 2026," a 100-day national tourism season running September through December, at a stakeholders' conference in Lagos on 14 September. The campaign is part of a push toward a $100 billion tourism and creative-economy target, building on roughly 1.2 million international arrivals in 2025, up 20% year-on-year. International carriers including Emirates, Qatar Airways and British Airways are adding flight frequency to Lagos and Abuja as more international hotel chains open in the country.

Site to visit: The Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the edge of Osogbo in Osun State, is a dense stretch of riverside forest regarded as the dwelling place of the Yoruba river goddess Osun, dotted with shrines, sculptures and artworks built up over centuries of worship. Entry costs only around N1,000 (roughly $0.65) and includes a guide, and the grove is busiest and most vivid during the two-week Osun-Osogbo Festival each August, though it is open to quieter visits year-round via road from Osogbo.

Science & Technology

Uber wound down its ride-hailing operations in Nigeria and Uganda effective 2 September, citing a strategic review, after years of rising fuel and commission disputes and intensifying competition from Bolt and inDrive. Starlink, meanwhile, has halted new residential subscriptions in Lagos, Abuja and other major cities after its network hit capacity, leaving only a pricier N159,000-a-month Priority plan for new business customers. On the innovation side, the National Agency for Science and Engineering Infrastructure is moving to commercialise homegrown solar-irrigation, electric-vehicle, CNG and medical-diagnostic technologies, part of a startup ecosystem that now counts roughly 1,490 active companies, the largest in West Africa.

South Africa's economy contracted 0.2% in the second quarter, ending six consecutive quarters of growth, as mining (-3.0%), manufacturing (-1.8%) and trade (-1.9%) all fell back under the fallout from the Iran war's fuel-price shock. Employment slipped and the official unemployment rate climbed to 33.6%, up from 32.7% in the first quarter, as 345,000 more people joined the ranks of the unemployed. There was some relief on prices, with headline inflation cooling to 4.3% in July from 5.0% in June after the South African Reserve Bank held its repo rate at 7%, and the rand has firmed to trade near 16 to the dollar in early September even as the National Treasury trims its full-year growth forecast to 1.5%.

Military & Security

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry resumed hearings this week with its focus on the Johannesburg Metropolitan Police Department, where internal-affairs investigator Elmarie de Beer testified about an alleged criminal syndicate operating inside the JMPD's Tactical Response Unit, including claims tied to a R14.9 million gemstone theft and protection of a suspect with cartel links. The commission has voiced alarm that serious misconduct cases against officers are being allowed to "die" internally rather than reach prosecution. Away from Johannesburg, Western Cape police reported nearly 70,000 arrests and more than 600 firearms seized over three months of operations against gangs and extortion, though several Cape Town precincts remain among the country's worst crime hotspots.

Society & Culture

South Africa is mourning nine women found dead in the Ekurhuleni area since July; at a 17 September Memorial Dialogue against Gender-Based Violence and Femicide, Ramaphosa's message of condolence was delivered by Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia in his absence. The country is also watching a fast-rising mpox cluster, with 18 confirmed cases and one probable case reported, concentrated in the Western Cape. The episodes land against the backdrop of this year's anti-migrant "March and March" mobilisation, which saw more than 900 people arrested nationwide around a symbolic deadline for undocumented foreigners to leave the country.

Tourism

South Africa's Tourism Month 2026 is under way under the theme "Growing South Africa's Tourism Sector in the Digital Era," with Limpopo named the province of focus. International arrivals continue to climb, with 5.58 million foreign visitors recorded between January and June, up 12.3% year-on-year, and July alone bringing 991,696 arrivals, a further 12.5% gain. To boost domestic travel, SANParks opened participating national parks free of charge during National Parks Week from 7 to 13 September, alongside discounted Sho't Left Travel Week packages encouraging South Africans to holiday at home.

Site to visit: Robben Island, the UNESCO World Heritage Site in Table Bay where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned for 18 of his 27 years in custody, remains one of the country's most powerful historic sites, its limestone quarry and cell blocks preserved much as they were under apartheid. Tours run about 3.5 to 4 hours and combine a roughly 30-minute ferry crossing with a bus tour of the island and a walk through the prison led by a former political prisoner; the only way to reach the island is by ferry from the Nelson Mandela Gateway at the V&A Waterfront, and sailings are weather-dependent, so booking well ahead is recommended.

Science & Technology

South Africa was named Country of Honor at the 2026 Pujiang Innovation Forum, which closed in Shanghai on 14 September, as officials pressed the case for deeper international research cooperation. At home, delegates at a parallel policy forum stressed that the country needs to build a stronger science talent pipeline by investing in young researchers well before they reach university. South African consumers, meanwhile, are feeling a global squeeze: a worldwide RAM shortage driven by AI data-centre demand has pushed local prices for MacBooks and games consoles sharply higher, with some laptop models up more than 30% in a single quarter.

Timbuk2
Tech / consumer platform · Cape Town

Timbuk2 is a Cape Town AI startup, founded by data scientist Matimba Julian Nkuna, that has built grassroots momentum by paying ordinary township residents to map the informal businesses — spaza shops, hair salons, car washes — that keep local economies running but are invisible to platforms like Google Maps. Vietnam

Days of torrential rain across northern and north-central Vietnam this week pushed several rivers to dangerous levels and submerged more than 2,000 homes, with Ninh Bình and Thanh Hóa provinces, south of Hanoi, hit hardest and over 1,000 households cut off by floodwater; some areas recorded more than 50 centimetres of rain in a matter of days. Site to visit: Hạ Long Bay, a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site in Quảng Ninh province some 180 kilometres east of Hanoi, is famed for the roughly 1,600 limestone karsts and islets rising from its emerald waters, many honeycombed with caves and hidden lagoons reachable only by small boat. Most visitors join an overnight cruise from Hanoi that combines cave stops, kayaking and a night anchored among the karsts; general bay entrance fees run about 260,000–310,000 VND (roughly US$11–13), and the cooler, drier months from October to December offer the clearest skies for photography.

Officials have cast the framework as the technical backbone for Vietnam's broader digital-transformation drive, aimed at cutting duplicated investment across agencies while improving services for citizens and businesses.
