Friday, August 7, 2026 · Europe

Spain

Europe · population ~48M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is weathering a fresh political setback after his brother was found guilty of administrative misconduct, feeding a polling narrative that a large majority of Spaniards believe corruption has escalated under his tenure. On the international stage, Sánchez has kept Madrid firmly opposed to the ongoing 2026 Iran war, denying U.S. forces access to the joint bases at Rota and Morón and drawing threats of trade sanctions from President Trump. Meanwhile, opposition Partido Popular leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo is trying to seize the domestic agenda with a pledge to build one million homes if he wins the 2027 general election, framing Spain's housing squeeze as the country's central grievance.

Economy & Business

Spain has raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2.6%, with real output up 2.7% year-on-year in Q1 and Goldman Sachs highlighting that Spain is expanding roughly three times faster than the euro-area average. Job creation remains strong — social security affiliations rose 2.4% year-on-year in April even as headline unemployment sat at 10.3% — while inflation held at 3.2% in June for the third month running. To cushion households from Middle East–driven fuel spikes, the government has extended a temporary support package worth roughly €5 billion (0.3% of GDP).

Military & Security

Spain currently deploys more than 3,000 military personnel across 17 missions on four continents under UN, NATO and EU mandates, with the patrol boat Centinela continuing maritime security sweeps in the Alboran Sea and the Strait of Gibraltar. Defence contractor Indra announced on 30 July that it will build a second of three military vehicle plants in the northern corridor, and the U.S. State Department has cleared a possible $1.7 billion Foreign Military Sale to modernise Spain's F-100 frigates with upgraded AEGIS, sensors and combat systems. On the strategic ledger, Spain still spends only about 1.3% of GDP on defence, keeping it 17th globally — a figure allies continue to press Madrid to lift.

Society & Culture

Anti-tourism protests have intensified across the country this summer, with peaceful marches in Barcelona, Palma and the Canary Islands targeting housing costs, overcrowding of historic districts and mass-tourism's environmental footprint. In parallel, Health Minister Mónica García is trying to move her long-delayed Framework Statute for healthcare staff through Congress, but doctors and unions remain divided, demanding on-call hours count toward Social Security contributions and better compensation for night work. Transport disruption is also on the calendar, with rolling rail, bus and ferry strikes tracked into August putting further pressure on a system already strained by summer travel.

Tourism

Spain is on track to welcome close to 100 million foreign visitors in 2026, with official projections showing a cumulative 80 million arrivals by the end of August and the peak summer stretch alone forecast to generate a record €64 billion ($73 billion) in revenue. A growing share of that traffic is pivoting inland toward rural and cultural destinations as coastal hotspots push back against overcrowding. Attention next week will focus on Castilla y León, where roughly 500,000 visitors are expected for the total solar eclipse on 12 August, straining rural accommodation but offering a rare shoulder-season windfall.

Site to visit: The Alhambra in Granada — a sprawling Nasrid palace-fortress overlooking the city and one of the finest examples of Moorish architecture in the world — remains Spain's must-see UNESCO site. General adult admission is €21, but daily visitor caps mean tickets to the timed-entry Nasrid Palaces routinely sell out weeks in advance, so book online well before your travel dates.

Science & Technology

Spain is doubling down on non-dilutive startup funding, with the national Neotec Program offering up to €250,000 for young R&D-heavy tech companies and ICEX Innova Invest covering 25–50% of eligible costs — one reason August is peaking as Europe's busiest month for tech conferences, with more than 635 events concentrated in Madrid, Barcelona, Palma, Bilbao and Málaga. On the research front, Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya has reaffirmed itself as Spain's top university for EU Horizon Europe funding, having pulled in €103.4 million across 211 projects since 2021. Renewables continue to reshape the grid: Spain's expanding wind and solar base has helped keep electricity bills lower than most European peers even through the Middle East energy shock.

Thursday, August 6, 2026 · South America

Venezuela

South America · population ~29M

Politics & Government

Venezuela's government and opposition are set to hold new transition talks this week, with parliamentary chief Jorge Rodríguez and former lawmaker Dinorah Figuera leading negotiations while Nobel laureate María Corina Machado has been left out of the process. Around 300 pro-Machado protesters gathered in Caracas to demand her inclusion, with former political prisoner Williams Dávila telling AFP that excluding her is untenable "because she is a fundamental leader for all of us." Socialist lawmaker Francisco Arias pushed back, criticizing talks he says are "approved only by those who run the US government." The dueling positions underscore how fragile the path to elections remains seven months after the US operation that removed Nicolás Maduro from power.

Economy & Business

Venezuela's economy is showing its strongest momentum in years, with oil production climbing past 1 million barrels a day through new joint ventures with Chevron, Repsol, Eni and Maurel & Prom, with oil revenue projected to jump 76.8% to $22.1 billion this year — the highest since 2018. The government has also announced its first minimum-wage increase in four years. Even so, the IMF is warning the broader humanitarian and economic picture remains "quite fragile," citing triple-digit inflation and a sharply depreciating currency.

Military & Security

Seven months after the January 3 US raid that captured Nicolás Maduro and his wife Cilia Flores — an operation Venezuelan officials say killed at least 24 security personnel — the head of US Southern Command made a surprise visit to Caracas to meet with the country's acting leadership, the first such US military visit since the raid. Maduro faces narco-terrorism and cocaine-importation charges in New York.

Society & Culture

Rival political factions marched through Caracas this week ahead of the transition talks, with leftist demonstrators accusing interim leader Delcy Rodríguez of negotiating "with a gun to her head" while Machado supporters demanded a clear electoral timetable. The protests coincided with events across the country marking two years since the contested 2024 presidential election, with participants calling for the release of political prisoners and respect for the original results.

Tourism

The US State Department lowered its Venezuela travel advisory from Level 4 (Do Not Travel) to Level 3 (Reconsider Travel) in March, and American Airlines has become the first US carrier in seven years to resume nonstop flights to the country. Tourism is slowly picking up in beach destinations and organized-tour areas, and the US Embassy in Caracas has resumed limited consular services. Even so, the UK, Canada, Australia and India continue to urge caution given ongoing political instability and strained healthcare infrastructure.

Site to visit: Canaima National Park, a nearly 3-million-hectare UNESCO World Heritage Site in southeastern Venezuela, is home to Angel Falls — the world's highest uninterrupted waterfall at 979 meters. Entry costs $40 for adults ($20 for children), payable in cash at the Canaima airstrip, with the trip typically involving a flight to Canaima Camp followed by a 4–6 hour boat ride up the Carrao River. The rainy season (May–December) is best for navigable rivers and fuller falls.

🔥 Trending
Rawayana & Akapellah — "Veneka"
Music · Caracas

Venezuelan supertrio Rawayana, together with rapper Akapellah, sent "Veneka" to #1 on Spotify's Top 50 Venezuela chart within 24 hours of release after a leaked fragment of the track went viral first and forced an early release. The song reclaims "veneca" — a slur once used to demean Venezuelan migrants in neighboring countries — turning it into a tribute to Venezuelan women's resilience, independence and identity. It racked up 2 million streams in its first week, cementing Rawayana's role as an unofficial soundtrack for a diaspora scattered from Bogotá to Omaha.

Wednesday, August 5, 2026 · Africa

Egypt

Africa · population ~106M

Politics & Government

Egypt's Minister of Water Resources and Irrigation Hani Sewilam said this week that the current Nile flood season has recorded inflows "slightly below average", though he cautioned it is too early to declare a drought year. On the diplomatic front, Washington continues to lean on Cairo as a key partner, with the Trump administration looking to Egypt to help stabilize Gaza, according to a Congressional Research Service overview of US–Egypt relations. Domestically, rights groups remain critical: Human Rights Watch's World Report 2026 documents continued repression, while the BTI 2026 country report sees no political liberalization on the horizon under Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly's government.

Economy & Business

Egypt's economy is stabilizing but rate cuts remain distant: BMI, a Fitch Solutions company, trimmed its FY2026/27 growth forecast to 5 percent — still the strongest expansion in four years — while projecting inflation to average 14.4 percent this year, delaying interest rate cuts until 2027. The IMF recently completed its latest program review, unlocking about $1.8 billion and citing progress on exchange-rate flexibility and fiscal tightening, and annual headline inflation eased to 14.3 percent in June from 16.5 percent in May. Business sentiment is improving too — the non-oil private sector PMI edged up to 46.8 in July, its strongest confidence reading since mid-2022, and Standard Chartered expects robust FX inflows and reforms to boost the economy in 2026.

Military & Security

The US Embassy in Cairo issued a security alert on August 1 warning that Iran has expanded attacks to areas not previously targeted, including Egypt. Defense Minister Lieutenant General Ashraf Salem Zaher recently urged the armed forces to maintain "combat readiness", as Egyptian–Turkish military ties deepen through the joint "Anatolian Eagle" and "Golden Eagle" 2026 exercises. Meanwhile, Al Majalla profiles the newly opened Strategic Command Headquarters ("the Octagon") — at 22,000 acres now the largest defence facility in the world — and Egypt has boosted its military deployment in Somalia in a bid to shore up regional stability.

Society & Culture

Egypt's Ministry of Health and Population launched the fourth edition of its nationwide "100 Youm Se7a" (100 Days of Health) campaign across all governorates on August 1. The public-spending picture remains strained: Human Rights Watch reports that declining funding is undermining education and health care, with allocations falling short of constitutional obligations, and a record 34 percent of Egyptians experiencing multidimensional poverty in the latest government survey. On a brighter note, 45 Egyptian universities made the Times Higher Education Impact Rankings 2026, reflecting growing academic engagement with sustainable development goals.

Tourism

Egypt's tourism boom continues: the country received 6.1 million tourists in the first four months of 2026, up 7 percent year on year, buoyed by the Grand Egyptian Museum, upgraded Red Sea resorts, and new airport connectivity. The sector's 2026 momentum is part of a broader regional tourism surge, with the Ras El Hekma mega-development and a new AI-powered e-visa system drawing record visitors to Cairo, Giza, Dahab and Siwa. Analysts say the museum's opening has redefined Egypt's travel experience, positioning the country as a leading global cultural destination.

Site to visit: The Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM), on the Giza Plateau just 2 km from the Pyramids, is the world's largest archaeological museum devoted to a single civilization, housing over 100,000 artefacts including the complete Tutankhamun treasure collection. Opened in November 2025, it spans 7,000 years of history. Practical note: tickets must be booked in advance via the official GEM website — there are no onsite sales — with timed entry slots managing visitor flow.

Science & Technology

The China-Egypt Belt and Road Joint Laboratory on Renewable Energy is drawing attention for its photovoltaic R&D base on Karaman Island in Sohag, run by Egypt's Academy of Scientific Research and Technology, which has already produced Egypt's first locally made half-cut solar cell and high-efficiency solar module. In archaeology, Egypt is teasing a major pyramid discovery to be revealed in 2026, with teams deploying muon tomography, ground-penetrating radar, 3D modeling and AI-based anomaly detection to probe the pyramid complexes without excavation. Egypt is also hosting more than 228 technology conferences in 2026, with August among the busiest months for the sector.

🔥 Trending
Kiwe
Tech / Fintech · Cairo

Kiwe, a Cairo-born social payments app aimed at young Egyptians, is having a breakout year after securing final Central Bank of Egypt approval to launch its app and payment card. Pitched as a way to make money "simpler, more relevant, transparent and social," Kiwe lets users split bills, send money to friends, and manage spending in a feed-like interface that feels more like social media than banking — a formula resonating with Egypt's huge under-30 population. It's part of a wider homegrown wave that has made Egypt one of North Africa's most-watched fintech ecosystems, alongside names like Khazna and Thndr.

Tuesday, August 4, 2026 · Asia

China

Asia · population ~1.41B

Politics & Government

China's leadership used the Fourth Session of the 14th National People's Congress and the wider 2026 Two Sessions to lock in the year's agenda, with Beijing detailing a 2026 policy mix meant to bolster growth and innovation while signalling openness to outside investment. Political power keeps centralizing around Xi Jinping even as the government leans harder on national-security messaging, and this week's activity — from the Two Sessions follow-through to continued military anti-corruption fallout — underscores how tightly domestic governance and Xi's personal authority remain bound together heading into the back half of the year.

Economy & Business

The Communist Party's Politburo has vowed to step up macroeconomic policy support and shore up capital-market resilience for the rest of 2026, a signal that growth momentum still needs propping up. On the regulatory side, Beijing released a draft amendment to its e-commerce law for public consultation, aiming to tighten oversight of the platform economy and protect market participants. Infrastructure keeps expanding too — the national rail timetable added 106 passenger and 111 freight services from July 1 — while Chinese equities posted one of their strongest first-half performances in years, powered by hard-tech, AI, and robotics stocks.

Military & Security

A week of heightened Chinese military activity near Taiwan continued, with the PLA Eastern Theater Command shadowing a Dutch frigate's transit through the Taiwan Strait as Taipei tracked Chinese aircraft and vessels. Beijing and Moscow also carried out their 11th joint strategic air patrol, integrating bombers, fighter escorts, and electronic-warfare aircraft in a show of high combat readiness via China's military media. Domestically, the anti-corruption purge of the defense establishment deepened with the formal expulsion of two former defense ministers, while China's peacekeeping contingent in Lebanon passed its UN capability evaluation on August 3.

Society & Culture

Tibetan and human-rights activists protested China's new ethnic unity law outside a Bank of China branch in Taipei, marking one month since a Tibetan activist's self-immolation in protest of the policy. More broadly, researchers tracking dissent say protests appear to be increasing across China, often tied to economic frustration as a sluggish job market fails to meet expectations, prompting authorities to intensify surveillance and policing in response.

Tourism

China's 2026 Summer Culture and Tourism Consumption Season is in full swing through the end of August, with the Ministry of Culture and Tourism dangling roughly $66 million in travel subsidies alongside thousands of cultural events. Inbound travel is booming — the country logged 22.91 million foreign arrivals between January and June, up 20.4% year-on-year, driven by rising visitor numbers from the United States and Russia — even as outbound trips to Japan are expected to nearly halve amid a diplomatic dispute with Tokyo, even as total outbound journeys keep climbing overall.

Site to visit: The Great Wall of China, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, remains the country's signature landmark, with the Mutianyu and Badaling sections offering the most tourist-friendly access near Beijing. Entry runs a modest ¥40–45 (roughly $6), and the best conditions fall in April–May or September–October, when mild temperatures and clearer skies make the ridge-top walk far more comfortable than the summer crowds and haze.

🔥 Trending
Tianshui Malatang
Food · Tianshui, Gansu Province

A humble bowl of malatang — a build-your-own spicy hot-pot noodle dish — turned the once-overlooked city of Tianshui into China's latest "internet celebrity" travel destination. The trend traces back to short videos from a local vlogger showcasing the dish's affordability and variety, which spread rapidly on Douyin and Xiaohongshu and drew young travelers in by the busload; local authorities and food vendors then leaned in with their own promotion. It's the same organic-to-viral playbook that made Zibo's barbecue scene and Harbin's ice festival into national sensations, and three years on, Zibo is still sizzling with fresh flavors — evidence that grassroots social-media buzz can translate into durable tourism economies for China's smaller cities.

Monday, August 3, 2026 · Europe

United Kingdom

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

Britain got its seventh prime minister in a decade as Andy Burnham was formally appointed after Keir Starmer's exit, telling the country he would make the transition "a circuit-breaker for Britain" with a new political and economic model. Burnham has pledged an end to political instability, promising near-term cost-of-living relief and a first instruction to end rough sleeping, with a fuller ten-year plan to follow later this year. His government faces an immediate challenge from Nigel Farage's Reform UK, which continues to top national polls, even as Donald Trump has publicly criticized Britain's economic condition while welcoming Burnham's plans to fast-track North Sea oil and gas licensing.

Economy & Business

The Bank of England held its policy rate at 3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, a slightly more hawkish hold than expected with the vote split 6-3 as more committee members flagged upside inflation risk. Sterling ticked up modestly against the dollar following the decision, though analysts see a softer BoE stance weighing on the pound going forward, with a September meeting still live if a persistent Middle East shock alters the inflation calculus. Traders had already placed large bets against sterling in the run-up to the leadership change on concerns about a leftward policy shift under the new government.

Military & Security

The Ministry of Defence has launched a roughly £2 billion competition for a new Light Mobility Vehicle to replace ageing Land Rover and Pinzgauer fleets, explicitly excluding Chinese suppliers under powers in the Procurement Act 2023. The government is separately investing more than £5 billion over four years into autonomous systems, including uncrewed sub-surface vessels as part of a planned "Hybrid Navy," while the Royal Navy's Common Combat Vessel remains in early design ahead of the Type 45 destroyers' retirement in the mid-2030s. Defence contractor Leonardo is also holding onto its stake in German sensor maker Hensoldt, weighing closer cooperation on Eurofighter and the sixth-generation fighter programme.

Society & Culture

Britain's protest landscape remains tense, with Human Rights Watch warning that the Crime and Policing Act 2026 introduces stifling new curbs on the right to protest, including cumulative-disruption provisions. The Metropolitan Police has meanwhile moved to double its use of facial recognition technology and establish the UK's first permanent live facial-recognition camera structures. Large, competing demonstrations have continued to draw tens of thousands to central London, reflecting a broader climate of culture-war tension as Reform UK gains on the right and the Greens gain on the left.

Tourism

VisitBritain is forecasting 45.5 million inbound visits in 2026, up 4% on 2025, with visitor spending projected to reach £35.7 billion, surpassing pre-pandemic peaks. Long-haul markets are driving much of the growth, with American travellers alone expected to account for nearly a fifth of inbound spending this year. The government has set an ambition to hit 50 million annual visitors by 2030 and has stood up a new Visitor Economy Advisory Council to coordinate industry growth plans.

Site to visit: Stonehenge, the prehistoric stone circle on Salisbury Plain and one of Britain's most recognizable UNESCO World Heritage Sites, remains a must for first-time visitors curious about Neolithic monument-building some 5,000 years ago. Adult admission starts around £28.50, and the first morning slot (roughly 9:30am) or late afternoon are the best times to avoid crowds, aside from the free overnight access granted each year at the Summer Solstice.

Science & Technology

The Royal Society's Summer Science Exhibition is showcasing 13 exhibits this year, ranging from healthy brain ageing research to the physics of sand grains. Behind the scenes, leaders of UK Research and Innovation and the Science and Technology Facilities Council have written to Parliament's Science, Innovation and Technology Committee flagging research funding cuts, while the same committee has opened an inquiry into whether low-energy computing can offset AI's rising energy demands. On the applied research side, University of Surrey scientists found that leaving naturally occurring water inside sodium vanadium oxide during processing substantially improves battery performance, a promising lead for next-generation energy storage.

Sunday, August 2, 2026 · South America

Argentina

South America · population ~46M

Politics & Government

President Javier Milei's approval ratings have slipped amid signs of political fatigue, and increasingly public disputes within his coalition are fueling speculation about alternative right-wing candidates. In August, a scandal broke after leaked audio allegedly showed Milei's sister and cabinet chief Karina Milei receiving kickbacks tied to disability-medicine purchases. Earlier this year the Senate approved Milei's first budget as president in a 46-25 vote, projecting 5% growth and 10.1% inflation for 2026. On the foreign policy front, Argentina has become a vocal supporter of US and Israeli military action against Iran, per Human Rights Watch's 2026 country report.

Economy & Business

Argentina's economy grew 4.4% in 2025, and the IMF is projecting 3.5% growth for 2026, driven by energy investment and a strong agribusiness sector. Annual inflation has fallen sharply from 211% to roughly 33%, though monthly disinflation has stalled near 3% and remains the highest in the G20. The country posted its first primary fiscal surplus since 2008, Vaca Muerta oil output topped 840,000 barrels a day, and poverty fell to 28.2% in the second half of 2025, according to Americas Quarterly's 2026 snapshot. Still, the peso remains managed rather than free-floating and more than $20 billion in debt payments come due this year.

Military & Security

Argentina fields roughly 72,100 active troops on a $3.8 billion defense budget and is shifting toward NATO-standard equipment, including F-16 fighters, Stryker vehicles, and P-3C Orion patrol aircraft. A central doctrinal priority is the "Defense of the South," reinforcing military presence in Patagonia and the South Atlantic. The Navy has begun joint maneuvers with US forces, and the two countries are set to stage the "Atlantic Dagger" special-forces exercise. Budget constraints persist, with the 2026 defense bill allocating zero funds toward the Ushuaia Integrated Naval Base.

Society & Culture

Thousands marched in Buenos Aires against labor reforms that would restrict the right to strike, clashing with police as the Senate debated the legislation. The killing of a 14-year-old girl in central Argentina reignited the "Ni Una Menos" movement, with her family leading a march in Córdoba demanding accountability. Poverty stood at 31.6% in June and austerity measures have hit older people and people with disabilities especially hard, according to Amnesty International.

Tourism

Argentina logged more than 3.1 million foreign visitors in the first half of 2026, a 25-year high, with June alone bringing 329,200 non-resident tourists. Long-haul arrivals from outside neighboring countries hit their strongest run in 25 years, led by European travelers and Brazil. Premium destinations like Buenos Aires, Bariloche, and Mendoza are seeing record hotel occupancy as airlines add new routes. Notably, outbound travel by Argentines has outpaced inbound arrivals in recent months as a stronger peso makes trips abroad more affordable.

Site to visit: Perito Moreno Glacier, a UNESCO World Heritage-listed ice field in Los Glaciares National Park near El Calafate in Patagonia, is one of the few glaciers in the world still advancing rather than retreating, drawing crowds to its walkways to watch house-sized chunks calve into Lago Argentino. Foreign visitors pay roughly $45,000 ARS for entry, and the glacier is best reached via a 90-minute drive or bus ride from El Calafate, with early departures helping to avoid the midday crowds.

Science & Technology

OpenAI and Argentine energy firm Sur Energy signed a letter of intent for a $25 billion AI infrastructure investment dubbed "Stargate Argentina," including a planned data center in Patagonia. Argentina Space 2026, the country's first major international space and science convention, is set for November 11-13 in Salta. The startup ecosystem keeps expanding into agtech, biotech, and mining tech, with June founders concentrated around financial infrastructure and exportable software tools. Science funding has come under strain, though: federal science spending could fall to just 0.147% of the budget in 2026, and 61 employees lost their jobs at nuclear research agency CNEA on July 1 amid a protest that saw armed military police occupy the building.
