Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · Africa

Nigeria

Africa · population ~225M

Politics & Government

Nigeria formally opened presidential campaigning on Wednesday, setting up a January 16 vote in which President Bola Tinubu seeks re-election against 18 declared challengers. Analysts expect a three-man contest, with former Vice President Atiku Abubakar now running under the African Democratic Congress banner and Peter Obi — whose youth-driven 2023 run reshaped the last election — standing for the Nigeria Democratic Congress. Tinubu enters the race with a formidable structural advantage after a wave of defections left 31 of Nigeria's 36 state governors inside his All Progressives Congress, though critics argue the machine masks genuine unpopularity among young voters. The president's prompt assent to the Electoral Bill 2026 has meanwhile shaped the legal terrain the campaign will be fought on, and he has paired the campaign launch with a ₦58.18 trillion appropriation bill promising tighter budget discipline. Gubernatorial races follow on February 6.

Economy & Business

The macro picture is improving on paper but remains painful at household level: headline inflation eased to 15.91% in June, a dramatic retreat from the 30%-plus peaks of 2024, yet food prices accelerated for a fourth straight month and transport costs climbed as a March fuel-price shock continued to feed through. The naira has settled into a broad ₦1,410–1,519/USD band, supported by higher reserves and returning portfolio flows, while GDP growth is tracking around 3.8–4.6% on the back of policy consistency and steadier commodity prices. That divergence — stabilising aggregates alongside the worst cost-of-living squeeze in a generation — is the central economic fact of the campaign now underway. Analysts at CFG Advisory have cautioned that inflation could yet drift back toward 20% before the year closes.

Military & Security

Security has deteriorated sharply enough to become an election issue in its own right. On August 19, gunmen killed 32 worshippers at a mosque in Unguwan Mantau village, Malumfashi LGA, Katsina State — the latest in a pattern that produced 599 banditry incidents and 2,742 casualties nationwide in 2025, roughly double the prior year. An estimated 30,000 armed gang members now operate across largely unpoliced stretches of the north, burning villages and displacing farming communities. More alarming to defence analysts is convergence between criminal and jihadist networks: Borno-based Boko Haram (JAS) has reportedly supplied equipment, advisers and bombmakers to bandit allies in Kaduna, and several northwestern groups claim affiliations with JAS, ISWAP and al-Qaida-linked outfits in the Sahel. Human Rights Watch continues to document abuses on multiple sides of the conflict.

Society & Culture

The university sector is heading for another confrontation: ASUU has called nationwide campus protests for August 26, with the Federal University of Technology Akure branch describing federal neglect of universities as "wicked and heartless." The union's grievances are long-running — renegotiation of the 2009 ASUU–FGN agreement, sustainable university funding, and unpaid entitlements for retired academics — but the timing places them squarely inside the campaign season. On health, commentators marked mid-August with warnings that Nigeria's health system stands at a crossroads and needs urgent structural reform, while vaccination teams have been pushing polio campaigns deeper into northern communities where insecurity complicates access. Local grievances are also surfacing directly, with residents of Ofugo protesting over infrastructure neglect earlier this month.

Tourism

Nigeria is leaning hard into diaspora and carnival tourism. The 2026 season has produced a record volume of visa-on-arrival applications from the Americas and Europe, driven largely by Brazilian and Caribbean travellers tracing ancestral roots — a flow the April Lagos Fanti Carnival at Tafawa Balewa Square, built around Afro-Brazilian returnee heritage, drew some 40,000 participants to. Lagos will follow in September with the inaugural week-long Victoria Island Carnival, pairing parades and live music with fashion, food and art programming. Improved connectivity through Murtala Muhammed International Airport and the Lagos Blue Line rail have made the city measurably easier to move around, though the dry season from November to April remains the practical window for travel beyond Lagos.

Site to visit: The Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove in Osun State is a dense riverside forest studded with shrines, sculptures and art installations dedicated to Osun, the Yoruba goddess of fertility — the last intact grove of its kind and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005. Much of its modern sculptural landscape was created by Austrian-Nigerian artist Susanne Wenger and local collaborators from the 1950s onward, making it as much a living art project as a religious site. Entry is inexpensive (roughly ₦500–1,000, with a small extra camera fee) and includes a guide; the annual Osun-Osogbo festival in August draws the largest crowds.

🔥 Trending
Street-pop's independent wave
Music · Lagos, Zaria & Calabar

The story of Nigerian music in 2026 is less about the established Afrobeats aristocracy than about the street-pop artists routing around it entirely. Acts like Mavo and LeoStayTrill have broken through on pidgin-heavy hooks and chant structures engineered for TikTok and Reels, where a single dance challenge can take an unsigned track national inside a week. What makes this cycle different is the money: independent distribution, diaspora investment and grassroots festivals now let artists from Zaria to Calabar earn meaningfully without surrendering masters to a major label. Victony's "FRE$H" reaching No. 8 on Nigeria's Top 100 alongside his earlier hit "SLICK" has become the reference point for what a self-directed breakout year looks like.

South Africa

Africa · population ~61M

Politics & Government

President Cyril Ramaphosa has set 4 November 2026 as the date for local government elections, with roughly 29 million South Africans now registered and 2.1 million names added to the roll since the 2024 general election. The vote will be the first real electoral test of the Government of National Unity, the ANC–DA coalition that has held together longer than most analysts predicted when it formed. Ramaphosa also spent this week on the regional stage, accepting the SADC chairship on 17 August at the bloc's 46th summit in Durban. Municipal governance — water, electricity and refuse collection in failing metros — is expected to dominate the campaign rather than national coalition politics.

Economy & Business

South Africa's recovery remains real but shallow. Unemployment climbed to 33.6% in the second quarter, up from 32.7% in Q1 and the highest reading since mid-2022, with the number of jobless rising 4.2% to 8.48 million as employment edged down. Against that, GDP growth is projected at about 1.2% this year, supported by steadier electricity supply, firm mining output and slow-moving reforms in energy, logistics and water. The clearest good news came in June, when Fitch upgraded the sovereign to BB with a stable outlook, citing fiscal consolidation and an improved debt trajectory — the first ratings upgrade in years, and a signal that markets now see the GNU's fiscal stance as credible even if the labour market does not reflect it.

Military & Security

Police operations remain the government's most visible security instrument: Operation Shanela accounted for 16,208 arrests of undocumented foreign nationals last month alone, bringing the year-to-date figure to 59,947, alongside seizures of illegal firearms and drugs. NATJOINTS activated its election security plan for the 1–2 August voter registration weekend and reports the exercise passed without major incident, with a fuller operational plan now in place for November. The bigger story is institutional: the Madlanga Commission into political interference and corruption within the police and criminal justice system has recommended criminal and disciplinary action against suspended SAPS deputy national commissioner Shadrack Sibiya. That follows Ramaphosa's earlier decision to deploy the army in support of police against organised crime gangs, a move defence analysts have questioned on effectiveness grounds.

Society & Culture

Mental health has moved up the schools agenda: KwaZulu-Natal education MEC Sipho Hlomuka has urged schools to build mental health awareness into their culture, warning that mounting pressure on learners is feeding disengagement and dropout, and launching a Digital School Mental Health Pilot Programme with partner Cloud Therapist. In public health, a South African dermatologist has been appointed to lead a new International League of Dermatological Societies campaign against skin-lightening practices, which the body classes as a significant and under-regulated public health risk. Continentally, health regulators and educators have agreed a shared framework of Africa Health Professions Education Quality Standards. On lighter ground, Varsity Football returns this month with Friday-night men's fixtures and the women's championship opening its season.

Tourism

Tourism is the clearest bright spot in the economy: international arrivals passed 5.58 million in the first half of 2026, up 12.3% year on year, with April alone bringing close to a million visitors. Visitors are also staying far longer — average stays have stretched from 9.4 to 14.2 nights as "slow travel," remote work and study programmes reshape itineraries. Visa liberalisation has been the single biggest lever, with visa-free entry now extended to travellers from 46 countries, while infrastructure has caught up: cruise berth capacity at Cape Town's V&A Waterfront has doubled to 7,500 simultaneous disembarking passengers and Skukuza Airport's extended runway can now handle A330 long-haul services into Kruger. Lodges along the path of the 28 November total solar eclipse over the Eastern Cape report exceptionally strong advance bookings.

Site to visit: Robben Island, seven kilometres off Cape Town in Table Bay, held Nelson Mandela for 18 of his 27 years in prison and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose prison tours are led by former political prisoners. The standard visit runs roughly 3.5–4 hours and bundles the return ferry, an island bus tour of the leper colony and lime quarry, and the maximum-security block walkthrough. Ferries leave the V&A Waterfront at 09:00, 11:00, 13:00 and 15:00 — book well ahead, as sailings are weather-dependent and frequently sell out; tickets run about R600 for non-South African adults and R400 for SA citizens.

Tuesday, August 18, 2026 · Asia

Vietnam

Asia · population ~98M

Politics & Government

Hanoi's diplomatic calendar dominated the past week, with General Secretary and President To Lam arriving in Auckland on August 12 for a three-day state visit intended to deepen the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership with New Zealand. At home, the National Assembly Standing Committee resumed its fifth session on August 17, scrutinising three draft laws covering administrative procedures, telecommunications and radio frequencies, and architecture — part of the sweeping legislative overhaul the leadership has framed as groundwork for what state media calls the “new era of the Vietnamese nation's rise.” Party, State, National Assembly and Government delegations also visited the Lao Embassy in Ha Noi on August 10 to pay tribute to Comrade Saysomphone Phomvihane, underscoring the durability of the Vietnam–Laos relationship. Separately, Vietnam and Mexico agreed to expand parliamentary delegation exchanges between the National Assembly and Mexico's Senate and Chamber of Deputies.

Economy & Business

Vietnam's growth numbers remain among the strongest in Asia: the economy expanded 8.39% year-on-year in Q2 2026, lifting first-half growth to 8.18%, with industry and construction surging 10.51% and contributing just over half of total value added. The composition of that growth has shifted sharply, however — exports of goods and services climbed 20.18% but imports outpaced them at 26.44%, producing an estimated US$16.65 billion trade deficit in H1 against a US$7.6 billion surplus a year earlier. Phones and components still account for roughly a fifth of shipments, with textiles and computers/electronics each near 12%, and the United States (~19%), China (~16%) and Japan (~8%) remain the top destinations. Forecasters expect momentum to moderate to around 6.8% for the full year before recovering in 2027, leaving the import surge and external demand as the figures to watch.

Military & Security

Vietnam signed a 2026 Updated Joint Vision Statement on Defence Cooperation with Australia on August 12, expanding maritime security work, joint training and critical-minerals cooperation between the two middle powers. Analysts read the agreement as a quiet hedge against Beijing's expanding reach in the South China Sea and the Pacific, even as Hanoi keeps its distance from anything resembling an alliance. That balancing act is governed by the “Four Nos” — no alliances, no alignment against third states, no foreign bases, no use or threat of force — which has led Vietnam to avoid high-profile acquisitions or joint combat exercises with the United States while steadily building maritime resilience. Ship-tracking data compiled this year suggests friction with Chinese vessels is rising even as the two governments publicly warm ties.

Society & Culture

Public health and schooling drove the domestic agenda this week. Ho Chi Minh City became the first Vietnamese city — and the 57th globally — to join the Cities for Better Health network, a prevention and health-literacy initiative, and on August 13 the city launched a Child and Family Psycho-Social Support Office offering mental health assistance to children and families. The Ministry of Education and Training convened a workshop in Hanoi on August 11 on safe school meals and health communication, feeding into the School Health Programme for 2026–2035, which targets better school healthcare and learners' physical and mental development. Elsewhere, Vietnamese, Malaysian and Indonesian consulates in Perth jointly marked ASEAN's 59th anniversary on August 14, and a National Assembly delegation visited students in difficult circumstances at a secondary school in Lang Son Province on August 15.

Tourism

Vietnam is on pace for its best tourism year on record: roughly 12.3 million international arrivals in the first half of 2026, up about 14.9% year-on-year, keeping the country within reach of its 25-million annual target. Monthly arrivals topped two million for four consecutive months earlier this year — a first in the country's tourism history. China leads source markets with 2.7 million visitors and South Korea follows with 2.16 million, together about 40% of the total, while Russia has surged 185.8% to 742,700 visitors to become the third-largest market. Expanded e-visa eligibility and improving rail connectivity are widely credited for the broadening of demand beyond Northeast Asia into India and Europe.

Site to visit: Ha Long Bay, in Quang Ninh province northeast of Hanoi, is Vietnam's best-known UNESCO World Heritage site — nearly 2,000 limestone karsts and islets rising out of emerald water, most of them explored by overnight cruise rather than from shore. Cave entry fees such as Sung Sot are typically bundled into the cruise fare, though kayaking often costs extra. Budget roughly US$120–200 for a mid-range one-night cruise with meals, and aim for October to April, when skies are clearest and humidity lowest.

Science & Technology

Semiconductors and AI remain the centrepiece of Vietnam's industrial strategy. On August 13 in Hanoi, the National Innovation Center, under the Ministry of Finance, hosted a forum with Thermo Fisher Scientific on realising the country's semiconductor, AI and innovation ambitions, drawing regulators, universities, research institutes and industry partners. Japanese industry expert Tsuda Kenji argued that Vietnam should concentrate first on chip manufacturing and packaging, where it can build defensible advantages in the value chain rather than competing across the board. Intel reaffirmed plans earlier this year to expand its long-term semiconductor investment in Vietnam, and the diaspora-facing Vietnam Tech Summit 2026 convened August 7–8 at the Computer History Museum in Mountain View, California, under the theme “Build Your Edge in the AI Era.”

🔥 Trending
V-Folktriotism
Music · Nationwide (Ho Chi Minh City & Hanoi studios)

The most durable grassroots phenomenon in Vietnamese pop right now is “V-Folktriotism” — a bottom-up wave of producers reworking Quan Ho folk singing, Ca Tru and Cai Luong opera through EDM, rap and modern pop production. It broke into the mainstream with Hoa Minzy's Bắc Bling and the production collective DTAP's Made in Vietnam album, both of which topped playlists and spilled over into provincial tourism campaigns as listeners went looking for the places in the lyrics. Singer Phương Mỹ Chi, who placed third at Sing! Asia 2025, has become its most visible export, treating each track as what one RMIT analysis called a “cultural passport.” What makes the movement unusual is that it is largely artist- and fan-driven rather than state-directed, riding self-released streaming projects and TikTok rather than label marketing budgets — the same shift that has let unsigned indie acts displace mainstream singers on Vietnamese charts.

MONDAY, AUGUST 17, 2026 · EUROPE

France

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

French politics remains defined by the fragile arithmetic of a hung National Assembly, with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu still governing on borrowed time after President Emmanuel Macron reappointed him following his brief resignation in an effort to break the deadlock. The delayed 2026 budget was ultimately adopted only after two no-confidence motions failed, a reminder that every major bill now requires ad-hoc coalition-building rather than a stable majority. Constitutional friction has also resurfaced: the Constitutional Council struck down legislation that would have barred under-15s from social media, ruling the ban disproportionate to freedom of expression and flagging privacy risks in age-verification schemes, and the government has said it will redraft the measure. With the 2027 presidential race already taking shape — Raphaël Glucksmann has formally launched his candidacy — the incentive for opposition blocs to cooperate is shrinking rather than growing.

Economy & Business

The macro picture darkened again this month when INSEE reported that unemployment climbed to 8.3% in the second quarter, up from 8.1%, with 62,000 more people out of work over the quarter and 261,000 more over the year; youth unemployment reached 21.6%. Growth is barely positive — GDP rose just 0.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 and the IMF has trimmed its 2026 forecast to around 0.7% — while the European Commission still sees the deficit near 5.1% of GDP and debt heading toward 118% . Central government borrowing passed €107bn in the first half alone, overshooting plan by more than 14% as spending growth outpaced revenue. The one modest bright spot is business activity: the S&P Global composite PMI recovered to 49.4 in July from 47.2, still contractionary but the shallowest decline in months.

Military & Security

France continues to act as one of Ukraine's most forward-leaning European backers, having now committed roughly €6bn in cumulative defence assistance covering armoured vehicles, artillery, Mirage 2000-5F fighters and training. Air defence is the current priority: Armed Forces Minister Catherine Vautrin and her Ukrainian counterpart agreed this month to accelerate radar procurement and the transfer of Aster 30 surface-to-air missiles to close gaps in Ukrainian airspace coverage. Paris has also gone further than most allies on the post-ceasefire question, with Macron indicating France could contribute "several thousand" troops to a reassurance force and senior officers suggesting deployment could begin as early as next year. The adopted 2026 budget cleared the military spending increase Macron had promised, though analysts question whether the army has the depth to sustain a long-term expeditionary commitment.

Society & Culture

The dominant social story of the summer has been environmental rather than political: France is enduring its fourth heatwave of 2026, with temperatures touching 40°C and a wildfire season now rated the country's worst since 1949 at roughly 115,000 hectares burned. Around Bordeaux the response was extraordinary — more than 20,000 people evacuated in the Gironde and the entire Cap Ferret peninsula cleared of some 40,000 residents and holidaymakers — before thousands were allowed to return home in early August. Excess mortality has been substantial, with roughly 5,764 deaths above the seasonal baseline recorded between mid-June and early July. Underlying labour tensions have not gone away either: doctors staged a ten-day walkout over the 2026 social security budget earlier in the year, and teaching unions have repeatedly mobilised against what they call austerity in education.

Tourism

France retains its title as the world's most-visited country, with Atout France and the Economy Ministry confirming 102 million international arrivals in 2025 — above the Olympic-boosted 2024 record of 100 million — and the 2026 season tracking higher still, with some industry projections reaching 105–110 million. Commercial accommodation logged roughly 743 million overnight stays, up 7.5% year on year, with Marseille's cruise growth now prompting local debate about port emissions and crowding. The wildfires and repeated heatwaves have complicated the picture in the southwest, where Gironde evacuations disrupted peak-season bookings on the Atlantic coast and pushed some traffic toward cooler northern and Alpine destinations. Atout France's "Perspectives 2026" report frames the central challenge as managing record volumes without degrading the sites that generate them.

Site to visit: Mont-Saint-Michel, the tidal island abbey rising off the Normandy–Brittany border, is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of France's most recognisable silhouettes — a Benedictine monastery begun in the 10th century atop a granite outcrop encircled twice daily by some of Europe's largest tides. The walled village below is free to enter with no ticket or time slot, but the abbey itself charges €16 in high season (1 April–30 September) and €13 in low season, with free entry for under-18s and for EU residents aged 18–25. Budget time for the approach: free shuttles run from the mainland car parks, but walking takes 45 minutes to an hour, and the abbey's many stairs make access difficult for visitors with reduced mobility.

🔥 Trending
Mauvais Djo — "Pilé"
Music (Afro-French / Afrobeat) · Paris nightclub circuit

France's undisputed song of the summer came not from a major-label rollout but from a club MC: Mauvais Djo, who built his name hosting nights on the French nightclub circuit before recording as an artist and co-founding the hip-hop trio Triangle des Bermudes. "Pilé" circulated organically for months across social media, resonating first with African and diaspora audiences, then detonated into a mainstream phenomenon around Fête de la Musique and the World Cup, going platinum in France and charting across Europe. His follow-up "Maladie," released in March, peaked at No. 3 in France and No. 5 in Switzerland, confirming the run was not a one-off. The trajectory is a neat illustration of how Afro-French sounds have moved from the margins of the French charts to their centre — driven by dancefloors and short-form video rather than radio programmers.

Sunday, August 16, 2026 · Asia

Japan

Asia · population ~124M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, Japan's first woman premier, has spent August managing an unusually crowded security and diplomatic calendar, including a response to President Putin's visit to Etorofu Island on August 13, a summit call with Iran's president on August 12, and emergency instructions following a suspected North Korean ballistic missile launch. Her position remains commanding after the February 8 snap general election delivered the Liberal Democratic Party and its Japan Innovation Party coalition partner a two-thirds supermajority — the largest seat count in postwar Japanese electoral history. That strength has not made governing frictionless: Takaichi faced sustained opposition questioning in late July after her coalition pushed a divisive bill through by a razor-thin margin at the close of the parliamentary session. Analysts continue to frame 2026 as the test of whether Takaichi can convert her mandate into durable, stable LDP rule rather than another short-lived premiership.

Economy & Business

Japan's economy is running warmer than forecasters expected, with Q1 2026 GDP growing 1.8% on an annualized basis after revision — still ahead of the 1.3% market estimate — and 0.5% quarter-on-quarter, the strongest quarterly increase since early 2025. Growth was broad-based: private consumption picked up as cost pressures eased and wages held steady, public investment rose for the first time in three quarters on infrastructure spending, and net trade contributed positively. A persistently weak yen has kept Japanese exporters competitive, with motor-vehicle export volumes up 6% year-on-year in June while export value jumped 23%. Even so, full-year 2026 real GDP growth is still projected at a modest 0.8%, led by domestic demand and shadowed by policy risk.

Military & Security

North Korea has fired at least two missiles at Japan's doorstep in the past ten days, sharply raising the temperature around Japanese defence policy. Pyongyang launched a ballistic missile eastward at about 5:59am on August 12 that travelled roughly 690km and peaked near 90km altitude, falling outside Japan's exclusive economic zone; a related launch reached a 56-mile altitude before coming down west of Hokkaido. That followed a short-range launch on August 6, a day after Pyongyang explicitly warned of "fresh military steps" over Japan's expanding defence capabilities — specifically Japanese plans to field long-range missiles in Kyushu. Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi condemned the launches and lodged a formal protest, while Takaichi ordered agencies to secure aviation and maritime traffic, with Tokyo, Washington and Seoul coordinating on analysis.

Society & Culture

Japan crossed a demographic threshold this summer that had been decades in the making: the population of Japanese residents fell below 120 million for the first time in 42 years, standing at 119.74 million on January 1, down 0.76% year-on-year. Births fell to a record low of 671,236 last year — the tenth consecutive annual decline — though the drop was less severe than in prior years, and births to women aged 30–34 actually rose from 253,444 to 255,665, a rare bright spot. At the other end of the age curve, average life expectancy rose for both men and women in 2025, which the health ministry attributes to fewer deaths from cancer, heart disease and COVID-19. The combined effect — fewer births, longer lives — keeps compounding the fiscal and labour pressures that dominate Japanese domestic policy debate.

Tourism

Inbound tourism is booming and being taxed accordingly. Japan set a record 10.68 million Q1 arrivals in 2026, including its strongest single month ever in March at 3.6 million visitors, even as Chinese arrivals collapsed — down roughly 56% year-on-year in March amid political tensions — with South Korea stepping in as the dominant source market at a record 1.176 million arrivals, up 22%. To manage the strain on Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka and the Fuji corridor, the departure tax tripled from ¥1,000 to ¥3,000 per person from July 2026, bundled automatically into air and cruise fares for everyone aged two and up. Climbing Mount Fuji is no longer free on any official route, with a mandatory ¥4,000 hiking fee and a 4,000-climber daily cap on the Yoshida Trail. Officials frame the measures not as a brake on tourism but as an attempt to push visitors out of the overloaded golden-route cities into the regions.

Site to visit: Himeji Castle in Hyogo Prefecture is Japan's largest and most completely intact original feudal castle, and the first in the country inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site — a brilliant white hilltop complex nicknamed the "White Heron Castle" that survived both wartime bombing and the 1995 Kobe earthquake unscathed. It sits a short walk from Himeji Station on the Sanyo Shinkansen, making it an easy day trip from Osaka or Kyoto. Note the new pricing: adult admission rose to ¥2,500 for non-residents of Himeji as of March 2026, while all visitors under 18 now enter free; go outside the early-April cherry blossom peak unless you have booked a timed ticket weeks ahead.

Science & Technology

Japan is spending at national-project scale to reclaim a frontier position in chips and deep tech. Cumulative government R&D support for the Rapidus consortium reached roughly ¥2.35 trillion as of April 2026, with its first Hokkaido fab targeting a 2026–2027 start-up and a combined ~$19.3 billion in subsidies and capital support earmarked for 2nm development through fiscal 2027. METI is also building three specialised semiconductor R&D core facilities at a cost of about $870 million, stocked with high-end design software and equipment, while a 1.4nm program aims at 2029 production. Beyond silicon, the government intends to roughly double science and technology investment to ¥60 trillion (~$376 billion) over five years through fiscal 2030, concentrated on artificial intelligence, space and nuclear fusion.

🔥 Trending

Music · Osaka

Luv is a five-piece neo-soul, funk and jazz band whose members were all born in 2003 and are still university students — Hiyn on vocals and guitar, Zum on bass, Rosa on keys, Sho on drums and Ofeen on DJ duties. Formed in June 2023 out of childhood friendships and a university jazz club, they built a following domestically before their track "Send to You" broke out on English-language TikTok, sending them on tours through Taiwan and the United States. The appeal is partly the songwriting trick of braiding English hooks into Japanese lyrics, and partly the sheer improbability of a group of undergraduates carrying a genre movement that grew out of Japan's long-running love affair with city pop, funk and jazz.

Friday, August 14, 2026 · Asia

Iran

Asia · population ~90M

Politics & Government

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei spent the week consolidating control over the security apparatus, ordering the merger of the Armed Forces General Staff with the Khatam ol-Anbia Central Headquarters and installing former IRGC commander Mohsen Rezaei as secretary of the Supreme National Security Council on August 9. The appointments have entrenched a maximalist line in Tehran — absolute Iranian control of the Strait of Hormuz, removal of US forces from the region, and continuation of the nuclear programme. That hardening has produced friction inside the system: a Paydari Front lawmaker confirmed that parliamentarians are preparing impeachment proceedings against Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi for failing to extract those demands from Washington. Externally, more than 30 governments including France, Britain and Canada issued a joint statement on August 13 condemning Iran's use of capital punishment, accusing the Islamic Republic of executing protesters to silence dissent.

Economy & Business

Iran's economy is in its worst condition since the revolution, with annual inflation running at 87.9% in July, barely down from 88.6% in June and far above the IMF's 68.9% forecast for the year. The rial has collapsed to roughly 1.86 million to the dollar on the open market — a currency that traded near 70 to the dollar in 1979 — while the IMF projects the economy will contract 6.1% in 2026. The binding constraint is trade rather than sanctions alone: the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the US naval blockade reimposed on July 14 have severed most of Iran's oil exports and international commerce, with officials privately conceding the blockade's impact is devastating. Domestic industry is operating on rationed power and imported inputs that no longer arrive.

Military & Security

The Strait of Hormuz has displaced the nuclear file as Tehran's central strategic preoccupation, with a US military intelligence assessment concluding that Iran's priority has shifted from weapons development to control of the waterway. President Trump claimed on August 11 that the United States holds "100% control" of the strait and demanded reparations as a condition of talks; a spokesman for Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters flatly rejected the claim, insisting no vessel transits without Iranian authorisation. Tehran's push to convert that leverage into permanent revenue — transit fees, mine-clearance terms, shipping controls — is the main obstacle to extending the fragile US–Iran memorandum, which expires Sunday. Traffic tells the story: roughly 10 to 12 vessels are crossing daily, against 130 to 140 before the war.

Society & Culture

Extreme summer heat has collided with a failing grid and a water system near collapse, producing hours-long daily power cuts and recurring water shortages across Khuzestan, Ilam, Lorestan, East Azarbaijan, Alborz and Tehran. Official figures put the aquifer deficit at 130 billion cubic metres, and Iran's own meteorological authorities now use the phrase "water day zero" for the point at which supply systems simply stop — a condition hydrologists describe as "water bankruptcy," irreversible on human timescales. The resulting protests differ in character from earlier cycles: environmental grievance has fused with economic collapse, and demonstrators are demanding liveability rather than any single policy reversal. Rural depopulation from dried farmland is adding pressure to cities already short of housing and power.

Tourism

Aviation has recovered only partially since the war. Iran reopened its airspace on April 25 after a near two-month closure, restarting six airports including Mehrabad, Mashhad, Birjand, Gorgan and Zahedan, with Imam Khomeini International resuming international service to Istanbul and Muscat and later Baku, Najaf and Baghdad. The restoration is running on a four-stage phased plan, and Mashhad's reopening is expected to revive religious tourism to the Imam Reza shrine, historically the country's single largest visitor draw. Western governments have not softened their guidance: the US maintains a Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory and urges American citizens to leave Iran now, and Canada's advice is comparable. Practical constraints — blackouts, banking isolation and intermittent internet — make independent travel difficult even where flights operate.

Site to visit: Naqsh-e Jahan Square in Isfahan is one of the largest city squares on earth at 89,600 square metres, laid out by Shah Abbas I in 1598 and inscribed by UNESCO in 1979. Its four sides frame the Masjed-e Shah, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, the Ali Qapu palace and the Qeysarieh bazaar portal — the definitive assembly of Safavid architecture. The square itself is free to walk at any hour and is at its best in the late afternoon, when the tilework warms and families gather; the individual monuments charge separate, modest admission.

Noghteha
Tech / Messaging App · Iran (diaspora-supported, Tehran user base)

When the January 8 blackout cut 85 million Iranians off mid-protest, the workaround that spread was Bluetooth. Iranians turned en masse to Bitchat, the serverless mesh messenger that relays messages phone-to-phone over Bluetooth Low Energy with no accounts or phone numbers, pushing downloads past 28,000 and nearly quadrupling the prior two months combined. What makes the story local rather than imported is Noghteha — a fork of Bitchat rebuilt by Iranian developers with full Persian and right-to-left support, a redesigned interface and features tuned to how the blackouts actually behave on the ground. It has become the version people share by word of mouth in Tehran, precisely because it was made by people living the problem, and it works best in exactly the dense urban blocks where crowds gather.
