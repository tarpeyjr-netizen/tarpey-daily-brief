Saturday, August 1, 2026 · North America

Canada

North America · population ~38M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Mark Carney's minority Liberal government continues to govern with a razor-thin margin in the House of Commons after two Conservative floor-crossings, with speculation that further defections could hand Ottawa a working majority (Policy Magazine). Carney has called by-elections for August 31 in three ridings — Beaches–East York, Chicoutimi–Le Fjord and North Vancouver–Capilano (Radio-Canada) — while also pushing back on suggestions that Canada should use its energy exports as leverage in ongoing trade talks with Washington (KelownaNow).

Economy & Business

Canada's economy has slipped into a technical recession after GDP contracted in three of the last four quarters through the first quarter of 2026, just as tariff pressure from Washington intensifies (KelownaNow). President Trump imposed fresh 50% tariffs on a swath of Canadian goods — from machinery and electrical equipment to honey, dairy and hockey sticks — covering roughly $20 billion in trade and set to take full effect in mid-August (NPR), a hit RBC economists estimate could shave two to three tenths of a point off growth in 2026 and 2027 (RBC Economics). Carney reiterated that Canada "believes in the benefits of free and fair trade" even as international card spending jumped more than 35% during the opening weeks of the FIFA World Cup matches hosted in Toronto and Vancouver (Washington Post).

Military & Security

Canada's Armed Forces spent the past week running Arctic sovereignty exercises in one of the country's most remote regions, including drills to board and intercept a simulated suspect vessel using the new patrol ship HMCS William Hall (CBC News). Ottawa has also flagged an Arctic economic and security corridor as a project of national significance, and Defence Minister Bill Blair says northern military infrastructure spending will increase tenfold over the next 20 years, with new operational hubs planned for Iqaluit, Yellowknife and Inuvik (CBC News). The push comes as Canada works toward NATO's new defence-investment pledge while continuing to channel military aid to Ukraine.

Society & Culture

Canada saw a troubling rise in politically motivated violence in July, including an incel-ideology-driven shooting that researchers describe as part of one of the country's fastest-growing extremist threats (American Thinker). Protests also flared at Acadia University in Nova Scotia, where demonstrators climbed on and smashed the windshield of a provincial premier's SUV, and organized campaigns have targeted Israeli cultural pavilions at summer festivals in Edmonton, Winnipeg and Saint John (The CJN).

Tourism

Canada's tourism sector is on pace for a banner year, with visitor spending forecast to climb 6% to roughly $140.9 billion in 2026 as both overseas and American travel rebound (Globetrender). Nine in ten Canadians say they plan to travel domestically this year, favouring cities like Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal, Québec City and Ottawa (Travel And Tour World), and the ongoing FIFA World Cup matches in Toronto and Vancouver have driven a sharp jump in inbound international card spending (Washington Post).

Site to visit: Banff National Park, established in 1885 as Canada's first national park, spans 6,641 square kilometres of the Canadian Rockies and is anchored by Lake Louise, Moraine Lake and the Icefields Parkway. Parks Canada is offering free admission and 25% off camping and overnight stays from June 19 through September 7, 2026, though outside that window a daily pass runs about $11.25 CAD (Banff 2026 travel guide); it's roughly a two-hour drive from Calgary, and booking the Moraine Lake shuttle well ahead is essential during peak summer.

Science & Technology

Ottawa announced a wave of research funding this month, including $630 million in support for natural sciences and engineering researchers nationwide covering fields from Arctic research to AI, quantum science and biotechnology, with the University of British Columbia alone receiving $47.6 million for 199 new projects (UBC Research + Innovation). A separate $289 million round backed nearly 1,800 social sciences and humanities projects spanning climate adaptation and the societal impacts of emerging technology (Government of Canada). On the private side, New York-based AI voice startup ElevenLabs formally launched a Canadian presence, citing 30,000 existing users and a growing team across Montreal, Toronto and Vancouver.

🔥 Trending
Baby Nova
Music · Halifax, Nova Scotia

Halifax singer-songwriter Kayleigh O'Connor, who performs as Baby Nova, has become one of Canada's most talked-about new indie-pop voices since releasing her debut album Shhugar in January 2026 (Wikipedia). Its lead single, "Too Pretty for Buffalo," landed on Apple Music's best-of lists and helped push the record onto the 2026 Polaris Music Prize long-list, with critics drawing comparisons to Lana Del Rey (Yahoo Entertainment). O'Connor trained in circus performance growing up in Nova Scotia before turning to songwriting at age 12 and signing a deal to write for other artists at 16 — a backstory fueling grassroots fan interest as her confessional, humor-laced songwriting spreads well beyond the East Coast (The Luna Collective).

Friday, July 31, 2026 · Europe

Iceland

Europe · population ~0.37M

Politics & Government

Iceland's Electoral Commission opened early voting on July 27 ahead of the August 29 referendum on whether to resume EU accession negotiations, a vote Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir's coalition committed to holding after the 2024 election. The debate has picked up urgency amid Trump-era pressure over Greenland, the ongoing war in Ukraine, and a push for deeper energy cooperation with Brussels. Separately, the government has approved a new industrial policy charting economic growth and competitiveness goals out to 2035.

Economy & Business

Annual inflation climbed to 5.3% in July, more than double the central bank's 2.5% target, even as summer sales cut clothing and furniture prices and international airfares jumped nearly 16%. The broader economy still grew 2.7% in real terms in the first quarter, with the central bank governor forecasting a gradual cooling. Hospitality is feeling some strain: roughly 300 staff at the Hilton Reykjavík Nordica and Hilton Reykjavík Natura have been told they face layoffs.

Military & Security

UK Defence Secretary Dan Jarvis visited Iceland amid warnings of increasing Russian activity in the North Atlantic and Arctic, meeting Foreign Minister Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir aboard the carrier HMS Prince of Wales as NATO ran air-defence operations from a European carrier for the first time. The two discussed Joint Expeditionary Force cooperation ahead of a JEF defence ministers' meeting in Iceland this autumn and the coming NATO summit in Ankara. Iceland, which has no standing military, continues to host allied forces as part of its North Atlantic security role.

Society & Culture

Iceland's community of authors was targeted by a foreign AI-driven scam on July 27, with convincing messages praising writers' work and offering to promote their books abroad. The same week, a magnitude-4.1 earthquake struck southwest Iceland just after midnight, and a Coast Guard reconnaissance flight spotted unusually large icebergs, including one nearly 400 metres long, drifting off the northwest coast. Reykjavík's cultural calendar stayed lively regardless, with the Iceland Airwaves festival announcing a second wave of over 40 artists for its autumn lineup.

Tourism

Iceland expects roughly 2.24 million international visitors in 2026, and is bracing for a booking surge ahead of the August 12 total solar eclipse, which crosses directly over the Westfjords and Snæfellsnes Peninsula and has accommodation there nearing full capacity. A new visa-free entry policy now covers more than ninety countries, including Brazil, Japan, Australia, and Gulf states, even as industry leaders warn that proposed mileage charges could reshape how visitors travel around the country. Overall arrivals are expected to dip nearly 4% year-over-year as the sector shifts focus from sheer visitor volume toward managing where tourists go and their environmental impact.

Site to visit: Þingvellir National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site about 45 minutes from Reykjavík, marks both the site of Iceland's original open-air parliament (founded in 930) and the visible rift where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates pull apart. It's the first stop on the popular Golden Circle route and pairs well with Geysir and Gullfoss on a day trip; the park is open year-round, though summer offers the longest daylight and easiest walking conditions, and a rental car or guided tour is recommended since public bus service is limited.

🔥 Trending
Brynja Rán
Music (Indie-Soul) · Reykjavík

Brynja Rán is a Reykjavík indie-soul artist whose debut EP "Seven" has quietly passed one million streams, built on warm, groove-driven production recorded at Sundlaugin Studio, the space built and owned by Sigur Rós. She's since signed to the Icelandic label HHmusic and is preparing a full-length album for later this year, growing from a standing start to over 52,000 monthly Spotify listeners. The momentum is grassroots rather than industry-driven, spreading through word of mouth in Iceland's small but tightly connected music scene as tracks like "Bastard" and "Lullaby" find new listeners online.

Thursday, July 30, 2026 · South America

Peru

South America · population ~33M

Politics & Government

Keiko Fujimori was sworn in as Peru's president on July 28 after a razor-thin June runoff victory, becoming the country's ninth president in a decade and vowing to stabilize its turbulent politics and crack down on crime (Al Jazeera). The inauguration came days after her Popular Force party, in alliance with another conservative bloc, captured the presidency of Peru's newly restored Senate — the first bicameral Congress the country has seated in more than three decades (Washington Times). Analysts are now watching how the returning "Fujimorismo" government will translate its narrow mandate into economic and governing policy (Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer).

Economy & Business

Peru's economy grew 3.0% in the second quarter, led by a rebound in mining and construction as high global copper and gold prices push investor confidence to its highest level since the pandemic (Rio Times; IMF). That momentum has been uneven, though — growth hit its weakest pace of the year in May after an anchovy-fishing collapse tied to El Niño stripped roughly two percentage points off GDP (Undercurrent News). The central bank held its key rate at 4.25% for a tenth straight month, betting an inflation uptick will prove temporary (Bloomberg).

Military & Security

President Fujimori announced the military will temporarily lead security operations in crime-ridden areas during emergencies, echoing a policy from her father's presidency (AOL/AP). The move follows the July 20 enactment of Law 32735, which shifts prosecution of alleged crimes by soldiers and police to military courts rather than ordinary justice — a change Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International say entrenches impunity for security-force abuses (Human Rights Watch). Peru's overall security threat score remains moderate nationally, with risk concentrated well above the norm in Huánuco region compared with Lima (GeoBit).

Society & Culture

Protesters again took to Lima's streets as Fujimori was sworn in, continuing a wave of unrest that first flared when thousands marched against her presidential bid back in May (Al Jazeera; Al Jazeera). Demonstrators are demanding accountability for civilians killed in earlier anti-government crackdowns, a grievance now colliding with the government's new military-justice law shielding security forces from ordinary courts. The protest movement shows little sign of easing as it carries into the start of the new administration.

Tourism

Peru's Ministry of Culture has locked in new daily visitor caps at Machu Picchu — 4,500 tickets in low season and 5,600 during peak dates — under a master plan running through 2031 that also introduces defined circuits and advance-booking requirements to curb overtourism (NomadLawyer; Forbes). The controls arrive just as the citadel was named the top destination in Central and South America for 2026 by U.S. News & World Report, underscoring continued strong demand despite the new restrictions (Travel and Tour World).

Site to visit: Huascarán National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Cordillera Blanca, protects Peru's highest peak and a landscape of turquoise glacial lakes, including the popular Laguna 69 day hike. Entry runs about S/30 (roughly $9.40) for a single day, payable at ranger checkpoints or through a tour operator, with the May–September dry season the best window for trekking (Peru Expeditions).

Science & Technology

A Ministry of Energy and Mines conference this week pushed to accelerate development of lithium and uranium, newly classified as minerals of strategic national importance under a June 2 supreme decree (Peru Support Group). Canadian miner American Lithium's Falchani project — one of the largest hard-rock lithium discoveries in the world — is now targeting construction in 2027 with an estimated $847 million investment, while its Macusani deposit ranks as Latin America's largest undeveloped uranium resource (Rio Times).

🔥 Trending
Pacific AI Dynamics
Tech / AI · Arequipa

Pacific AI Dynamics, a startup rooted in Arequipa's mining heartland, has been quietly gaining traction among Peruvian operators by pointing computer-vision cameras at haul trucks and crushers to predict mechanical failure before it happens. Trained on a proprietary dataset built for the dust, altitude, and humidity of the Andes, its system is credited with preventing more than 120 mining downtimes in 2025, saving clients millions of soles — and because the company charges only a percentage of what it saves, word of mouth across the mining sector has done most of the marketing (Nucamp).

Wednesday, July 29, 2026 · Oceania

Australia

Oceania · population ~26M

Politics & Government

Anthony Albanese's government continues to govern under the 48th Parliament, which opened in July 2025 following the government's re-election. Treasurer Jim Chalmers' May 2026 federal budget funded a new tax cut for low- and middle-income earners that took effect on July 1. The Prime Minister's office has kept up a steady run of media releases through late July, though no major legislative fight has broken out this week.

Economy & Business

Growth forecasts have turned more cautious: Deloitte Access Economics cut its 2026-27 growth forecast from 1.9% to 1.3%, citing rising rates, weak confidence and stalling housing investment. From July 1 the National Minimum Wage rose 4.75% to $26.44 an hour, and employers must now pay superannuation alongside wages under the new Payday Super scheme. Business investment has picked up on the back of a local data-centre boom and continued green-energy spending, per Janus Henderson's July outlook, even as a prolonged cost-of-living squeeze weighs on households.

Military & Security

Admiral Mark Hammond took over as Chief of the Defence Force from Admiral David Johnston on July 9, and a new Defence Delivery Group stood up July 1 to eventually become an independent acquisition agency. The 2026 Defence Innovation, Science & Technology Strategy was unveiled at ADSTAR 2026 with delegations from Canada, Japan, the UK and the US in attendance. Navy frigates HMAS Perth and HMAS Sydney are both on regional deployments across South and East Asia, while the Air Force's Exercise Pitch Black runs over northern Australia through early August.

Society & Culture

NAIDOC Week ran under the theme "50 Years of Deadly", with marches and community events held across the country in early July. In Sydney, a queer-friendly arts venue in a former deconsecrated church, Divine Playhouse, was ordered by its landlord to stop operating after Christian groups protested and pushed the state government to claw back its arts grant. Melbourne lived up to its reputation as "protest capital", with four separate rallies — anti-trans, pro-trans, anti-war and anti-imperialist — converging on the city in a single day, alongside a Sydney demonstration calling for the closure of the Pine Gap facility.

Tourism

Australia's tourism recovery has pushed past pre-pandemic levels, with close to 9.2 million visitors arriving in the 12 months to May 2026, up 9.5% year-on-year and equivalent to 98.2% of 2019 levels. Nine of Tourism Australia's priority markets — including the UK, India, South Korea and France — are now back at or above 2019 volumes, according to the Australian Bureau of Statistics. Industry watchers, however, are warning of a possible slowdown in the back half of 2026 even as the sector celebrates its record run.

Site to visit: Uluru, the giant sandstone monolith at the heart of Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park, has been UNESCO World Heritage-listed since 1987 for both its natural and cultural significance to the Anangu people, who have held it sacred for over 30,000 years. A three-day park pass costs $38 for adults (extendable to five days at no extra cost), and the shoulder seasons of April-May or September-October offer the best balance of mild weather and lighter crowds compared with the busy, pricier June-August peak.

🔥 Trending
Keli Holiday — "Dancing2"
Music · Australia (national)

"Dancing2" has become a genuine grassroots phenomenon, hitting number one on the ARIA singles chart and turning into a viral soundtrack on TikTok, where it has scored tens of thousands of user-made clips. Its spread has followed the now-familiar TikTok pattern — a hook-driven snippet catching on organically with creators, then pulling the full track up the charts behind it, a trajectory Australian music press has flagged as one of the year's most notable breakout stories.

Tuesday, July 28, 2026 · Asia

South Korea

Asia · population ~52M

Politics & Government

President Lee Jae-myung's administration is pushing three AI-era megaprojects spanning semiconductors, data centers, and physical AI as a state-led production strategy, even as a controversial "fake news" law took effect this month, drawing warnings from journalist groups about its chilling effect on reporting. Domestically, a court ruling has left Seoul mayor Oh Se-hoon's political future in question, and prosecution reform remains a live flashpoint following police-handling failures, according to Korea Pro's rundown of the country's defining political year. On the peninsula, defense and diplomatic maneuvering continue apace per AEI's latest Korean Peninsula update.

Economy & Business

Seoul revised its 2026 growth outlook up to 3 percent, citing a semiconductor supercycle and easing Middle East uncertainty, alongside a "3-4-5" vision targeting top-four exporter status and $50,000 GNI per capita. Q2 GDP beat estimates, expanding 0.6% quarter-on-quarter on AI-fueled chip demand, with real gross national income up 15.6% year-on-year — the largest jump in 38 years. Seoul's startup ecosystem is also thriving, with enterprise value near $357 billion and 39 unicorns concentrated in AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and robotics.

Military & Security

Defense Minister Ahn Gyu-back is pushing commanders to finalize a target year for transferring wartime operational control from the US to South Korea by year's end. The Agency for Defense Development launched an in-house generative AI system, Add+i, to support research while guarding against leaks of military secrets. US and South Korean air forces ran the "Buddy Squadron" combined exercise July 14–16, and Seoul is deepening ties with Ukraine partly to manage the fallout of North Korea's battlefield cooperation with Russia, per AEI's peninsula coverage.

Society & Culture

The UNESCO World Heritage Committee's annual session opened in Busan on July 19, spotlighting the country's cultural heritage on a global stage. Protest culture continues to evolve, with a new generation of young demonstrators — some drawing organizational energy from K-pop fandom — reshaping how civic movements mobilize, following earlier rallies at Olympic Park over voting-rights and election-process concerns.

Tourism

South Korea welcomed 10.7 million international visitors in the first half of 2026, up 21.3% year-on-year and now tracking toward a government target of 22–23 million for the year. Chinese arrivals are a major driver, with roughly 2.56 million Chinese visitors between January and May fueling demand for foreigner-only casinos and hospitality. The country also expanded visa-free access to 110 countries this month, while summer crowds flock to the WATERBOMB Festival in Seoul and the Boryeong Mud Festival at Daecheon Beach.

Site to visit: Gyeongbokgung Palace, the largest of Seoul's five grand royal palaces and the primary seat of the Joseon dynasty, remains the city's signature historic landmark, with its throne hall, Gyeonghoeru pavilion, and changing-of-the-guard ceremony drawing visitors year-round. It's open 9am–6pm (closed Tuesdays) through October, with adult admission just 3,000 won — or free if you visit wearing a hanbok, per this visitor guide.

🔥 Trending
FoodNeverComes
Tech / App · South Korea (nationwide)

Built by an independent developer known only as Malhee, FoodNeverComes lets users browse restaurants, customize an order, and watch a rider icon crawl across a delivery map — except nothing ever arrives. Instead, the app tallies up the money and calories "saved" by not actually ordering, tapping into the dopamine hit of anticipated reward without the spend. It's part of a broader wave of Korean "dopamine sites" recreating the thrill of shopping with zero transaction, and it spread almost entirely through word of mouth and social sharing before catching international attention this summer.

Monday, July 27, 2026 · Europe

Germany

Europe · population ~84M

Politics & Government

Security dominated the headlines after a vehicle was driven into the crowd at Berlin's Christopher Street Day celebrations, an attack authorities believe was Islamist-motivated terrorism. Chancellor Friedrich Merz responded with a reshuffle of top government posts as his coalition works to counter the far right's rising poll numbers. On the domestic policy front, Merz's government is pushing a pension reform that would gradually raise the retirement age, alongside income tax cuts for low- and middle-income earners and a broader push to cut red tape. The moves come as the coalition tries to show it can deliver on economic renewal while managing a tense security and political climate.

Economy & Business

Germany's private sector unexpectedly returned to growth in July, with S&P Global's composite PMI climbing to 51.2 from 49.5, beating forecasts and marking the first expansion in four months on the back of a manufacturing upturn. The government says the economy is stabilizing, though renewed fighting in the Middle East remains a risk to the recovery, even as easing oil prices have lifted business and consumer sentiment. Underpinning the optimism is a sweeping reform package from the ruling coalition covering tax, labour and pensions, including roughly €10 billion in annual income tax relief for lower- and middle-income earners starting January 2027.

Military & Security

Germany is set to raise defence and security spending to more than €200 billion by 2030, roughly a third of the federal budget, as it pursues major military modernization. The army's Territorial Defence Division plans to more than double in size, from 6,500 to over 10,000 personnel by 2035, adding units to protect critical infrastructure and bolster NATO logistics. Berlin and Paris are also deepening nuclear cooperation, with Merz confirming Germany will join upcoming French nuclear exercises as the two countries coordinate more closely on strategic matters. Meanwhile, German firm Diehl Defence unveiled its new IRIS-T SLS MK4 mobile air defence system at ILA 2026, aimed at countering drones and low-altitude threats.

Society & Culture

An estimated 20,000 people protested in Erfurt against the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) as the party held its national convention, blocking roads and gathering under signs reading "Stop AfD Nazis." Delegates at the convention re-elected co-leaders Alice Weidel and Tino Chrupalla to another term as the party seeks to project unity ahead of further gains in the polls. The demonstrations were largely peaceful, though an AfD constituency office and police were targeted with paint bombs and fireworks. The gathering's timing, coinciding with the 100th anniversary of a Nazi Party meeting in the same region, added to the controversy.

Tourism

Germany welcomed an estimated 10 to 11 million international visitors between January and June, continuing steady year-to-date growth fuelled by strong European demand and rising long-haul travel. The summer season alone is forecast to generate nearly €57 billion in revenue, with full-year holiday travel revenue expected to approach €85 billion. The national tourist board's 2026 strategy is pushing the country's image beyond castles and heritage toward sustainability, city breaks and culinary experiences, even as German outbound travellers themselves are trimming holiday budgets amid cost-of-living pressures.

Site to visit: Neuschwanstein Castle, King Ludwig II's fairy-tale 19th-century retreat in the Bavarian Alps, was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2025 alongside his other royal residences, cementing its status as one of Europe's most photographed landmarks. The castle draws over a million visitors a year and is best reached by bus or a scenic uphill walk from the village of Hohenschwangau; early-morning or shoulder-season visits are recommended to avoid the heaviest crowds, and advance online ticket booking is strongly advised.

🔥 Trending
Kauta
Music · Bonn

Kauta, a German-Moroccan singer from Bonn, has become one of German-language pop's fastest-rising new voices, blending urban pop with oriental influences and multilingual, deeply personal lyrics. Her single "Unsichtbar" became her breakout moment, racking up millions of streams and building a grassroots following now approaching 675,000 on TikTok and 60,000 on Instagram. The momentum was entirely organic, built on short-form video and word of mouth rather than major-label push, and it carried her into her first-ever headline tour, "Kauta's Diary," which kicked off in January 2026. Her rise is being watched as an example of how independent artists with a distinct, culturally blended sound can break into Germany's mainstream pop scene from outside Berlin's usual industry circuits.
