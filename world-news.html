Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · South America

Chile

South America · population ~19M

Politics & Government

President José Antonio Kast's government is fighting on several fronts in Congress. On November 23 the Chamber of Deputies backed most Senate amendments to the 2027 budget by 138 votes, but disagreements on ten line items, including Culture, Women and Gender Equity, memory sites and municipal security funding, sent the bill to a joint committee that must finish before the November 29 constitutional deadline. Justice Minister Fernando Rabat faces a constitutional accusation. The commission reviewing it was handed 1,886 sentence-reduction decrees signed since March 11; its chair had expected about 56. Its report is due November 27 and a floor vote is projected for November 30. A Criteria poll taken November 19–20 put Kast's approval at 23%, up from 18% a week earlier, with 68% disapproving. Abroad, the operator of the Chilean-flagged cargo ship Danstar, which is due to restore a direct Punta Arenas–Falklands link in December, said it will seek clearance only for departure and arrival, not from Argentina.

Economy & Business

The central bank reported that third-quarter GDP fell 1.1% year on year, worse than its preliminary estimate. At the same time, COMEX copper has fallen to about US$5.70 a pound, roughly 17% below its September record, which is testing the government's deficit-reduction plan. That plan is also absorbing the cost of a fuel-price freeze through March 2027, which the government agreed to after a truckers' blockade. State miner Codelco used Asia Copper Week in Shanghai to offer private investors 60 undeveloped properties covering about one million hectares. Cochilco projects that national mine output will fall 8.4% this year to 4.94 million tonnes. Markets steadied on Monday: the IPSA rose 0.96% to about 10,450, and the dollar fell to about 1,016 pesos after reaching a 12-month high the previous Friday.

Military & Security

The Public Prosecutor's third organised-crime report, presented November 18, found that organised-crime homicides fell 46% in 2025, from 199 to 107 victims. Over the same period, the number of organised-crime cases rose 6.8% to 6,121, driven by small-scale drug dealing, and identified trafficking victims rose 37%. Chile's vote at the OAS to apply the 1947 Rio Treaty (TIAR) to drug trafficking has drawn criticism. Former ambassador Pablo Cabrera said it is meant to give multilateral backing to the US-led "Shield of the Americas". Separately, El Mostrador reports that Navy intelligence has begun examining a cyberattack on the Foreign Ministry's consular system, which sources say had still not been contained more than three weeks after it began.

Society & Culture

Conaf director Aída Baldini warned that Chile faces one of its most difficult wildfire seasons in years, saying she had never seen a risk map with the whole country at high risk. El Niño heat is expected to hit vegetation that grew after an exceptionally wet winter. The government has budgeted US$166 million for the season, with 8,000 brigade members and 177 aircraft. Santiago is still dealing with February's gas-tanker explosion in Renca: a burns victim died after nine months in critical condition, and responsibility for the blast is still disputed. In culture, Shawn Garry's Después de Elena won the Golden Colón at Spain's Huelva festival, and Alfredo Castro won best performance. At home, Chilean Cinema Day week (November 23–29) offers 138 free or CLP 1,000 screenings at 41 independent venues in 14 regions.

Tourism

Pisco producers in the Limarí Valley, in the Coquimbo Region, are building a "Pisqueras Ancestrales" distillery-tourism circuit around Ovalle and Monte Patria ahead of the summer season. Pisquera Tulahuén expects more than 3,000 visitors this year, compared with about 30,000 at some distilleries in the better-known Elqui Valley, where arrivals have suffered from damaged transport links and worries about the weather. Producers say direct sales to visitors carry higher margins, and Casa Juliá plans a museum and heritage tours. The expected El Niño heatwaves and fire risk are a concern for summer travellers heading to forested parks.

Site to visit: Torres del Paine National Park in Patagonia is famous for its granite towers, glaciers and turquoise lakes, and its "W" trek is among the best-known multi-day hikes in South America. November to March is the main hiking season. Since May 1, 2026, the park has used a new entry system with a single-day pass and a 2-plus-day pass valid for up to 10 days, sold in advance through Conaf's pasesparques.cl. Most visitors arrive via Puerto Natales.

Science & Technology

The Foreign Ministry breach has turned into a test of the state's cyber defences. According to El Mostrador, the attack began with a phishing email to a diplomat and planted malware that replicates every ten minutes. Officials decided not to take the consular platform offline because more than a million Chileans abroad depend on it, and the National Cybersecurity Agency (ANCI) has opened its own investigation. Separately, Conaf's fire-season plan relies on a "single strike" rapid-attack method and a "red button" ignition-risk alert. The alert flags areas where the probability of ignition exceeds 70% and winds reach at least 20 km/h.

🔥 Trending
Crezta! Limón y Merkén
Food & consumer brand · Chile (nationwide)

Crezta! is a Chilean seasoning that blends lemon powder, salt and smoked merkén chili. Founder Santiago Piano first made it to rim michelada glasses, but fans now put it on chicken, seafood, fruit and avocado toast. After eight months of losses, with roughly two months of savings left, Piano started a 45-day social-media challenge to save the company, and one video passed 3 million views and turned sales around. The brand is now sold at more than 80 points of sale and has launched in the Líquidos liquor chain and Jumbo supermarkets.

Monday, November 23, 2026 · South America

Colombia

South America · population ~52M

Politics & Government

The standoff between President Abelardo de la Espriella and his predecessor sharpened over the weekend. At a press conference on the national strike, de la Espriella asked US authorities to investigate Gustavo Petro and, in his words, "hopefully imprison" him, calling the previous administration a "criminal enterprise" and warning opponents, including June runoff rival Senator Iván Cepeda, not to "mess with democracy." On Sunday the ICT ministry asked X to suspend Petro's @petrogustavo account, citing possible incitement to violence after he posted a call for Colombians to "demand Abelardo's resignation now." Relations with Washington drew attention too: during a brief stop in Bogotá on November 18, US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the two governments were "completely on the same page", and both men dismissed a New York Times report that Bogotá had rejected joint US military operations on Colombian soil.

Economy & Business

The government's fiscal plan is at the centre of the debate. It would lower the income-tax threshold from about $1,419 to about $985 a month, raise healthcare contributions and cut corporate taxes, with next year's deficit projected at 9.4% of GDP. In industry, Nestlé said on November 17 that it will permanently close its 75-year-old La Rosa plant in Dosquebradas, Risaralda, after the August 10 earthquake damaged about 70% of the site. Production of brands such as Saltinas and Cocosette will move to Ecuador, Brazil and Chile. On the same day, the president and Grupo Salinas chairman Ricardo Salinas Pliego announced that Mexican brand Italika plans a motorcycle assembly operation in Barranquilla, which the presidency says will create nearly 1,000 direct and indirect jobs.

Military & Security

Attacks on the armed forces continued. EFE reported that an explosives attack using improvised launch ramps on an army battalion in La Jagua de Ibirico, Cesar, killed one soldier and wounded 12 in an area where both the ELN and the Clan del Golfo operate. Two weeks earlier, a drone attack on a base in Santa Rosa del Sur, Bolívar, killed three. The government struck back with an airstrike in Dagua, Valle del Cauca, that killed five ELN members from a unit led by the commander known as "Bertha." It was the seventh bombing operation de la Espriella has ordered since taking office on August 7, and he said his order was to "maintain the offensive."

Society & Culture

Unions, students and opposition groups held the second nationwide protest against the government on November 19. The marches were mostly peaceful, but some ended in clashes with riot police using tear gas and water cannon, and turnout was uneven across major cities. An ECLAC report put Colombia's extreme poverty rate at 13.4%, the fourth highest in Latin America and the Caribbean, though at a three-year low. Universidad de los Andes and the Safe Speeds Alliance said 7,468 people died on Colombian roads from January to September, up 19.3% on the same period last year and a record. Motorcyclists account for almost all of the increase.

Tourism

Barranquilla hosted the Copa Sudamericana final on November 21, which Boca Juniors won 2-0 over Atlético Mineiro at the renovated "Supermetro" stadium, now seating 65,000. The mayor reported 100% hotel occupancy. Before the match, city officials expected more than 125,000 visitors and an economic boost of about US$241 million. Bogotá also has a new food honour: Condé Nast Traveler España named it Best International Dining Destination at its 2026 Hotel & Mantel Awards, ahead of New York, Dubai and Bangkok.

Site to visit: The Castillo de San Felipe de Barajas in Cartagena is often described as the most imposing Spanish fortress in the Americas, and its tunnels and hidden passageways are part of the city's UNESCO World Heritage listing (1984). It is open daily from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. and is less than 10 minutes from the walled old town. Full-price tickets cost COP 38,000, and Colombians with a cédula get in free on November 29.

Science & Technology

The ICT ministry has filed charges against Starlink Colombia and four mobile operators, including Comcel and Tigo, for allegedly failing to block signals around prisons. A raid on Bogotá's La Picota prison found six Starlink antennas, 582 phones and 3,754 SIM cards, and the ministry found Starlink Wi-Fi at three other prisons. If the violations are confirmed, the companies face fines of up to 15,000 monthly minimum wages, suspension of operations or loss of their licences.

Sunday, November 22, 2026 · North America · Asia

Cuba

North America · population ~12M

Politics & Government

Camagüey governor Jorge Enrique Sutil Sarabia was removed from office in what the official announcement called a resignation "at his own request." The provincial party chief gave no reasons on November 14, and the allegations of fuel trafficking and influence peddling circulating on social media remain unconfirmed by any published document. Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez publicly thanked allies for donated solar panels and food shipments while making no mention of US humanitarian aid distributed through the Catholic charity Cáritas, which says it manages $60 million of a $100 million US offer. A report by analyst Juan Antonio Blanco argues that Havana's strategy after the November 3 US midterms is to buy time until Donald Trump's term ends in January 2029, in part by influencing US media and policymakers.

Economy & Business

Workers in Havana's San Miguel del Padrón say the state is handing its cafeterias and restaurants to private small businesses and giving staff three months to find other jobs, with some saying they have already gone months without pay. Neither the official press nor the state-run CTC union has reported how many people are affected, although the new Labour Code that took effect on September 25 sets formal steps for dismissals on economic grounds. In Cienfuegos, eight private pharmacy projects are being prepared under a July decree that lifted the general ban on private pharmacies. Each one must give 10% of its profits to a health institution and may sell only imported medicines bought through state importers.

Society & Culture

The Quisicuaba project in San Antonio de los Baños now houses 318 formerly homeless people in a renovated school, and its director estimates that 85–90% of them have a psychiatric disorder. Officials said the number of Havana residents hit by water shortages has fallen to 255,000 from 577,000, but some neighbourhoods report weeks without supply, and a five-litre jug of water sells for 2,000 pesos in Luyanó. In Sancti Spíritus, a bedbug infestation has spread through police stations, a prison and homes, and it forced the provincial court to close its headquarters.

Tourism

Tourism Minister Juan Carlos García Granda said on state television that the sector's workforce fell from about 103,000 at the end of 2025 to 83,000, with nearly 22,000 workers moved to other state jobs rather than formally laid off. Cuba received 506,074 international visitors through October, just 28.8% of its annual target of 1.76 million, after fuel shortages and cancelled flights. Meliá left the country in July after 36 years, and Iberostar stopped operating 12 of its 18 Gaviota-linked hotels. Reforms approved in June allow private hotel management and independent travel agencies, but they depend on scarce fuel and air links.

Site to visit: Old Havana, founded by the Spanish in 1519 and inscribed by UNESCO in 1982 together with its fortification system, is known for Baroque and neoclassical squares such as the Plaza de Armas, Plaza Vieja and the cathedral plaza, guarded across the bay by the Morro and La Cabaña fortresses. Wikivoyage calls walking by far the best way to see it. An official taxi from José Martí airport to central Havana costs about US$20–25.

Science & Technology

A new Ministry of Communications resolution, effective November 24, sets out how state institutions must adopt artificial intelligence through 2030, with pilot systems in 2028. Critics note that it creates no independent oversight body and sets no budget. At the same time, authorities are reportedly preparing a block-by-block survey of Starlink owners in Havana, after the Foreign Ministry told embassies on November 9 that the satellite terminals are banned for them too. One analysis found that downloading the 210 GB game GTA VI would take Cubans about 58 hours on fixed broadband, ranking Cuba 145th of 146 countries.

Uzbekistan

Asia · population ~36M

Politics & Government

A military court on November 20 sentenced Said Abdulaziz Yusupov to 10 years in prison for attempting to undermine the constitutional order and for aggravated defamation. Yusupov formerly ran a public fund supporting independent print media and news agencies. During President Shavkat Mirziyoyev's state visit to Muscat, Uzbekistan and Oman raised ties to a strategic partnership and signed a visa-free travel agreement allowing stays of up to 30 days. The deal takes effect 30 days after both sides complete domestic procedures.

Economy & Business

S&P Global Ratings on November 20 raised Uzbekistan's sovereign rating to BB+, the highest in the country's history and one notch below investment grade, while warning of its sensitivity to gold and energy prices. The BRICS-founded New Development Bank announced its first loan to Uzbekistan, worth $300 million, for the opening phase of a low-carbon energy programme spanning solar, wind, hydro and nuclear power. At a meeting in Parkent district, Mirziyoyev questioned why the country imports about $780 million a year of construction materials it could make itself. He noted that domestic ceramic tile capacity is three times demand.

Military & Security

A Senate committee on November 12 reviewed a bill to ratify an agreement establishing an SCO centre for countering security challenges and threats in Tashkent. The centre would cover information security and transnational organised crime, while Tashkent also deepens its security dialogue with Washington on counterterrorism and drug trafficking. Separately, the Interior Ministry has drafted a resolution to open temporary detention centres for foreign nationals awaiting deportation. The first would open in Tashkent in 2027 and five regional facilities would follow by 2030, with detention capped at one year.

Society & Culture

Final results of Uzbekistan's first census in 37 years put the population at 39.05 million, above earlier estimates. The census revised the urban share up to 54.5% and life expectancy up to 76.4 years. The Health Ministry reported a rise in acute respiratory infections in eight regions, including Karakalpakstan, Fergana and Khorezm, in October and November. Health Minister Eldor Adilov ordered reserve hospital beds and stricter control of antibiotic prescriptions, though cases for the first ten months were still 15% below 2025 levels.

Tourism

The government set targets of 20 million foreign tourists and $10 billion in tourism exports by 2030, up from 11.6 million visitors so far this year and more than 15 million expected by December. Investors are being offered 16% hotel-construction loans, tax breaks through 2030 and VAT exemptions for tour operators. Uzbekistan's first international tourism investment forum is due in Tashkent on November 25–27. Emirates is also considering regular flights to Uzbekistan, though no route or launch date has been set.

Site to visit: Samarkand's Registan, once the heart of the Timurid Empire, is framed by three madrasas: Ulugh Beg (1417–1420), Sher-Dor (1619–1636) and Tilla-Kari (1646–1660). It is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2001. Wikivoyage lists adult admission at 100,000 som, with winter hours of 09:00–20:00, and the fastest train from Tashkent takes about four hours.

Science & Technology

The Ministry of Digital Technologies has built an open Uzbek-language text corpus of nearly 6 billion words from 5.6 million documents, published on Hugging Face to improve how AI models handle Uzbek. Cooperation with the Qwen model has already begun. In the 2026 UN E-Government Development Index, Uzbekistan climbed 31 places to 32nd of 194 countries, entering the "very high" group. Within Central Asia it ranks second, behind Kazakhstan.

🔥 Trending
TASS Vision
Retail AI startup · Tashkent

TASS Vision is a Tashkent startup founded by Shahzod Umirzakov and Jamshidjon Khakimjonov that builds AI systems to analyse operations in traditional retail stores. As of summer 2026, its technology was in use in more than 3,000 stores across ten countries, including Kazakhstan, Turkey, Georgia and Peru. That makes it one of the clearest examples of a homegrown Uzbek product expanding abroad without the deep pockets of Uzum, the country's unicorn.

Saturday, November 21, 2026 · Asia · Europe

Saudi Arabia

Asia · population ~37M

Politics & Government

A royal decree from King Salman dismissed Lieutenant General Fahd bin Hamad al-Salman as commander of the Joint Forces fighting the Houthis on November 16 and named General Fayyad al-Ruwaili in his place. The change came a day after coalition forces abandoned their Red Sea coast push and pulled back from near Bab al-Mandeb to defend Aden. In diplomacy, Riyadh was among 16 countries backing new UK and Australian sanctions on Israeli settlement expansion, reaffirming support for a two-state solution on the 1967 borders. At the COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Saudi Arabia was among the oil producers whose objections helped strip all mention of fossil fuels from a draft deal, pushing the summit into overtime.

Economy & Business

A Houthi strike on November 19 caused material damage to the Public Investment Fund tower, the sovereign wealth fund's headquarters and Riyadh's tallest skyscraper. The attacks briefly lifted crude, with Brent edging up to $92.22 a barrel on Friday while still heading for a third straight weekly loss ahead of OPEC+ meetings on November 29. The Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority said it will impose a top-up tax from early 2027 on multinational groups with revenue of €750 million or more whose effective rate falls below the 15% global minimum. Central bank data showed point-of-sale spending fell 6.7% to SR14.03 billion in the week to November 14, even as apparel spending jumped 16.3%.

Military & Security

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh early on Saturday, the third straight day of emergency alerts in the capital. A day earlier the coalition reported a "special operation" against Hodeidah port and military targets in Sanaa, while the Houthis threatened Jeddah, other Red Sea ports and the Al-Shuqaiq power station. On the ground in Yemen, the Houthis edged closer to Aden as government forces withdrew from Bab al-Mandeb. The WHO put the death toll from the escalation that began in August at 2,260 as of November 16, with UNICEF reporting more than 330,000 people displaced.

Society & Culture

Education Minister Yousef Al-Benyan, on an official visit to Washington, witnessed the signing of nine agreements between Saudi education bodies and American universities and companies. The deals cover educational technology, curricula, assessment and digital content, in line with the ministry's push to digitise the sector. The visit followed a separate announcement of new US–Saudi initiatives in advanced technology education. Arab News also profiled a blind Saudi adventurer who reached Everest Base Camp.

Tourism

The fourth Al-Qasab Salt Season opened in the Riyadh region's "Village of Salt" and runs until November 28, with a model saltworks, salt-based dishes, palace and museum tours and a Salt Race on November 21; organisers expect more than 100,000 visitors. In Jeddah, a two-day Malaysia Carnival at Cenomi U-Walk Mall promoted Visit Malaysia 2026–2027 to Saudi travellers with cooking demonstrations and tour packages. Travellers should note that the Houthis have declared Saudi airspace "unsafe" for airlines, after attacks on Riyadh airport in October killed 18 people.

Site to visit: Hegra, near AlUla, was Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, a Nabataean city whose monumental tombs are carved straight into sandstone outcrops. The site can only be seen on an organised tour, since self-driving inside is not allowed; the official Hegra Day Tour by air-conditioned coach is listed from SAR 95 per person.

Science & Technology

Researchers at KAUST have developed a coating for overhead power lines, made from a silicone-based polymer with boron nitride particles, that reflects sunlight and sheds heat so conductors can carry more current in extreme summer temperatures. Saudi Arabia ranked first globally in the Online Service Index of the UN E-Government Development Index 2026 and placed in the overall top five. Ericsson's Ante Mihovilovic said Saudi telecom operators are looking beyond data revenue as 5G Standalone and network APIs open services for gaming, logistics and industry.

Russia

Europe · population ~144M (transcontinental; European portion listed here)

Politics & Government

Putin, Trump and Xi met on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen and issued a broad declaration on nuclear risk reduction and UN reform, but announced no agreements on Ukraine or sanctions relief. Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov rejected NATO-style security guarantees for Ukraine as unacceptable "quasi-membership." Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Estonia's plan to close its road crossings from December 1 amounted to a "new Iron Curtain" and promised a response. At home, a Moscow court jailed former first deputy defence minister Ruslan Tsalikov for 10 years for embezzling 6.58 billion rubles via army supply contracts.

Economy & Business

The MOEX index fell 2.3% to 2,364 points on November 20, its lowest level since October 9, after the summit brought no breakthrough. Three tankers of Russian diesel bound for the US diverted to Brazil after American buyers balked at sanctions risks despite a US waiver. Ukrainian drone attacks have cut Russian refinery crude processing by 45% since late October, according to Reuters. Drones also hit Acron's Dorogobuzh fertiliser plant in Smolensk region, which makes about 11% of Russia's ammonium nitrate.

Military & Security

A Russian ballistic missile attack hit three bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv; Ukraine said eight people were killed and 28 wounded across the country overnight. The strikes followed Putin's vow to respond after Ukraine hit the Crimean Bridge, where Deputy PM Marat Khusnullin said there is no timeline yet for repairs to the collapsed road span. Poland said three objects, including a Kh-101 missile, violated its airspace, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it "an intentional provocation." Russia's Defence Ministry said it intercepted 310 Ukrainian drones in a single night.

Society & Culture

Putin gave Patriarch Kirill the state prize for humanitarian work on his 80th birthday, a 10-million-ruble award usually given to one person a year. A plaque honouring Cheka founder Felix Dzerzhinsky went up on Moscow's Slavyanskaya Square, calling him an "outstanding statesman." Students and academics lose another link to Europe as the EU bars anyone living in Russia from Erasmus+ in 2027, regardless of citizenship.

Tourism

Moscow's four airports imposed temporary flight restrictions 82 times in October, and the Transport Ministry now wants to steer subsidised routes away from the capital; Sheremetyevo, Vnukovo and Domodedovo handled 4.2% fewer passengers in the first nine months of 2026. Overland travel to the Baltics is also narrowing: Narva mayor Jaan Toots asked for humanitarian corridors before Estonia closes its road border on December 1. Some Russian travellers arriving at Berlin and Frankfurt airports report being asked to sit for interviews with German intelligence officers after passport control.

Site to visit: Kizhi Pogost, on an island in Karelia's Lake Onega, is a UNESCO-listed ensemble of wooden churches and a bell tower, best known for the many-domed Church of the Transfiguration. Most visitors make a day trip from Petrozavodsk; the museum lists a boat transfer of about 1 hour 15 minutes, and summer is the practical season, since winter access depends on hovercraft or snowmobile.

Science & Technology

China's Commerce Ministry now requires export licences for certain drone engines, radio modules, speed controllers and electric motors used in Shahed-type drones assembled at Alabuga in Tatarstan. Bloomberg reported that Russia gets nearly 90% of its critical components and technologies from China. In a Meduza interview, researcher Anna Nadibaidze argued that Russia's military AI is more about decision-support systems than "killer robots," and that it still depends on Western chips despite sanctions.

Friday, November 20, 2026 · Europe & Asia

Ukraine

Europe · population ~44M (pre-war estimate)

Politics & Government

Diplomacy over ending the war moved fast this week. Kyiv held peace talks with US and European officials, and US envoy Jared Kushner said the process was edging toward a deal, although Moscow has not accepted European involvement in the next round. Trump then met Putin one-on-one in China. On Thursday European "Coalition of the Willing" leaders gathered in Berlin to reaffirm support for Ukraine. There Kushner said Russia was "comfortable" with NATO Article 5-style security guarantees, Germany's Friedrich Merz called the Shenzhen summit a "photo op", and France's Emmanuel Macron said Ukraine's NATO accession should be back on the table. Brussels kept up the pressure as EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas accused Moscow of prolonging the war with extreme demands.

Economy & Business

Kyiv is facing what the head of parliament's budget committee called "the most acute funding shortfall since the start of the full-scale invasion", with about 40% of GDP going to defence. The shortfall follows the missile-forced shutdown of the Kametstal steelworks and a roughly 90% drop in Black Sea port calls in August. To help close a projected €70 billion gap next year, the European Commission proposed a €45 billion loan, which Ukraine would repay only after Russia pays reparations. The war's economic effects are also spreading beyond Ukraine: analysts warn that the collapsed span of the Kerch Bridge now blocks freighters carrying grain and steel through the Kerch Strait, which could push up global grain prices.

Military & Security

Ukraine carried out what Zelenskyy called an "unprecedented" operation against the Kerch Bridge. After weeks spent suppressing Russian air defences, it brought down one arch with heavy drones and cruise missiles, halting road traffic and limiting trains to a single track. A drone attack the same day killed two people in Crimea. Russia hit back across Ukraine, and strikes killed at least four people in Odesa and a woman in Zaporizhia region. Putin has vowed retaliation, and minor damage to the Dutch and Austrian embassies in Kyiv led the Netherlands and Sweden to summon Russia's ambassadors, according to reports from Berlin.

Society & Culture

Prince Harry made his fourth unannounced trip to Ukraine since 2022, meeting wounded veterans in Lviv at Ukraine's National Invictus Games. About 500 people registered for a planned 60-member team for Birmingham 2027, the largest squad Ukraine has ever sent. In sport, freediver Nataliia Zharkova set a world record for the deepest competitive freedive. Elina Svitolina finished runner-up at the WTA Finals after losing 7-5, 6-3 to Russia's Mirra Andreeva. The human cost of the strikes stayed in view as the country mourned a mother, athlete and drone operator killed in a Russian attack.

Science & Technology

The Kerch strike showed Ukraine's expanding domestic missile arsenal. Zelenskyy cited Neptune and Flamingo missiles, and Netherlands-based Destinus said seven of its Ruta cruise missiles hit the bridge. Air defence is a weaker point. Zelenskyy says speed caps on Starshield satellite terminals make Ukrainian interceptor drones lose contact at about 161 km/h, too slow to catch jet-powered Shaheds. The Air Force puts its interception rate at around 60% for jet-powered drones, against more than 90% for propeller models.

🔥 Trending
UFORCE
Defence-tech startup · Kyiv / London

Ukrainian-founded uncrewed-systems group UFORCE raised $700 million at a $6 billion valuation. It is the largest venture round ever raised by a startup with Ukrainian roots. Its products grew out of battlefield needs, including Magura naval drones, Nemesis aerial systems and Liut ground robots. The company has offices in Kyiv and Washington and its headquarters in London. The UK National Wealth Fund co-led the round alongside Valor Equity Partners, and a partnership with ReconCraft will produce Magura drones in the US.

Turkey

Asia · population ~86M

Politics & Government

Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan told parliament that Ankara has nearly finished draft legislation on the six remaining EU visa-liberalisation criteria, to be submitted after the budget debate. He also said Bulgaria had temporarily eased Schengen entry rules for Turkish truck drivers. The government also launched investment-based residence permits, offering three years for investments of $150,000 and five years for $200,000. On the regional stage, President Erdogan and Qatar's emir signed a mutual defence agreement in Istanbul, the latest of several new regional security pacts.

Economy & Business

The central bank raised its year-end 2026 inflation forecast to 29.5%, blaming the prolonged Iran war and higher oil prices. Governor Fatih Karahan pledged to keep policy tight after an October cut to 36%. Gross reserves fell from $210 billion to $161 billion between late February and early November. Commentators note the bank has pushed single-digit inflation out to 2029. Meanwhile, a probe into a financial crime network behind September's investment-fund defaults has reached senior regulators. Prosecutors are seeking to investigate former banking watchdog chief Sahap Kavcioglu, who resigned last week.

Military & Security

Baykar delivered the first three Kizilelma unmanned fighter jets to Turkish security forces after 600 test and training sorties. The company says that makes Türkiye the first country to field such an aircraft operationally. Britain's defence minister Luke Pollard visited Ankara and said the UK sees Türkiye as "an important long-term defense and industrial partner". Türkiye is buying 20 Eurofighters and 12 C-130J transports, and the first two C-130Js are due in December. The Qatar pact adds to the Turkey-Pakistan-Saudi alliance that parliament ratified earlier this month.

Society & Culture

The state meteorological service issued a yellow warning for 17 provinces including Istanbul, forecasting southerly gales of up to 85 kph that cancelled some ferry services, with a sharp temperature drop expected midweek. Tourism

The Mediterranean resort of Antalya is busy as host of the UN climate summit. Blue Zone registrations at COP31 reached 102,610 in the first week, close to COP28's record, and the public Green Zone had already drawn 212,000 visitors. The venue says it sorted about 100 tons of waste, 80% of it recyclable, and runs generators partly on biodiesel made from used restaurant oil. Travellers this weekend should note the nationwide rain outlook and the cancelled Istanbul ferry services.

Site to visit: Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia, in central Türkiye's Nevşehir Province, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985. Its landscape of wind-carved "fairy chimneys" in volcanic tuff is dotted with cave churches whose painted interiors go back to Byzantine times. The site is about a one-hour drive from the airports at Kayseri and Nevşehir, and spring and autumn are the most comfortable times for hiking valleys such as Love Valley.

Science & Technology

As COP31 president, Türkiye has set a target of electricity making up 35% of final energy consumption by 2035. A summit working group warned that grid-connection waits of five to ten years could hold that back, and it recommended firm permit timelines and grid investment made ahead of demand. Negotiations in Antalya meanwhile turned tense, with tensions rising over the draft climate deal as the summit entered its final days.

🔥 Trending
Ziyafet Sofrası (Banquet Table)
Independent theatre · Üsküdar, Istanbul

Tiyatro Külliyen's small stage play about daily prayer has done what few Turkish productions manage. It reached its 616th performance after 13 years and is still selling seats. Director Osman Doğan's adaptation is set in a Sufi lodge in Üsküdar and uses humour and drama to explore social prejudice and spiritual values. Its following has spread by word of mouth, with tours across Türkiye and as far as Chicago and Oslo. The company has cut it from two acts to a single 70-minute act, a change Doğan blames on audiences' shrinking attention spans.

Thursday, November 19, 2026 · Africa

Nigeria

Africa · population ~225M

Politics & Government

A constitutional amendment allowing states to run their own police forces is now on President Bola Tinubu's desk. Chief of Staff Femi Gbajabiamila said on November 18 that the bill had been transmitted to the president after all 36 state assemblies approved it, well above the 24 states the constitution requires. Tinubu will review it with the Attorney General before signing it at a formal ceremony, and a separate national policing bill is still being drafted. On the campaign trail, the APC has expanded Tinubu's 2027 rally appearances from 12 states to 19 plus the FCT, with stops scheduled through Lagos on January 13. Meanwhile, the opposition ADC has challenged the APC to back up claims that it sponsored campaign violence.

Economy & Business

State oil company NNPC Ltd and the Central Bank of Nigeria have signed a memorandum of understanding to address foreign-exchange problems and channel export proceeds into the economy. NNPC Group CEO Bayo Ojulari said the deal will also help the company raise capital against its balance sheet and assets with CBN guidance, and joint committees will track progress. No monetary value was disclosed. In aviation, Air Peace now runs nine daily flights between Lagos and Abuja as of November 17, citing demand on one of the country's busiest corridors.

Military & Security

After viral videos showed soldiers and security operatives meeting armed men in a forest, the Nigerian Army admitted holding talks with bandits in Kwara State. An Operation Savannah Shield spokesperson called it a "calculated engagement" that recovered 12 AK-47 rifles and freed 10 kidnap victims. Critics accused troops of fraternising with the gunmen. The admission comes as a recent report warns that the Islamic State is incentivising ISWAP to target military weapons stockpiles in Nigeria and neighbouring countries.

Society & Culture

Nigeria's diphtheria outbreak has reached 28,151 confirmed cases and 1,126 deaths in 26 states and the FCT, and MSF warns that antitoxin stocks are so low its teams in the north are rationing treatment. Kano alone accounts for about 43% of confirmed cases. In culture, Fela Anikulapo-Kuti's induction into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame on November 14 has renewed attention on the Afrobeat pioneer's legacy. The federal government has also approved a new university dedicated to public relations.

Tourism

The festival calendar is busy as Nigeria's year-end tourism season gets underway. The Arewa Festival celebrating northern Nigerian culture comes to Abuja's Arts & Crafts Village this weekend, November 21–22, with 50 VIP tickets priced at ₦50,000. The National Festival of Arts and Culture is scheduled to return to Enugu from November 21 to 28 after last year's edition was postponed. For domestic travellers, Air Peace's expanded Lagos–Abuja shuttle adds prime-time departures from both cities.

Site to visit: The Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Osun State, is one of the last stretches of primary high forest in southern Nigeria. Its shrines and sculptures honour Osun, the Yoruba goddess of fertility. The best time to visit is during the annual Osun-Osogbo Festival in August, when thousands of pilgrims process to the river. The grove is on the edge of Osogbo, about a four-hour drive northeast of Lagos.

Science & Technology

As the COP31 climate talks in Antalya near their close, African civil society groups are pressing for concrete action and climate finance for the continent. A report also suggests that about six billion tonnes of emissions from burning wood may be missing from official climate reporting. This matters for countries like Nigeria, where many households depend on firewood and charcoal. Separately, the WHO has called for urgent action to curb drug resistance in Africa.

South Africa

Africa · population ~61M

Politics & Government

Post-election coalition bargaining dominates politics. The ANC and ActionSA are closing in on a deal for Gauteng's three metros, under which ActionSA would take the Johannesburg and Tshwane mayoralties and the ANC would lead Ekurhuleni. The DA won the most seats in all three metros. The two parties are still short of a majority everywhere: they hold 106 of 270 seats in Johannesburg, where 136 are needed. Herman Mashaba's bid for the Joburg mayoralty remains the main sticking point, and Stephen Grootes argues the talks are full of smoke and mirrors.

Economy & Business

Eskom has gone from a power deficit to a 2–3GW surplus, and it is courting data centres as new baseload customers. Board chair Mteto Nyati said South Africa already hosts 70% of Africa's data centres. Eskom posted a FY2026 net profit of R30.3 billion, more than double the previous year, after diesel use fell 62%. Whoever takes over Johannesburg will also inherit a R10.3 billion wage debt.

Military & Security

Tamryn Raju, a KwaZulu-Natal mother, was shot dead in her Phoenix home on November 18. In the days before, she had posted TikTok videos about witnesses at the Madlanga Commission, which is investigating alleged cartel infiltration of the criminal justice system. Her relative, tow-truck operator Clive Naicker, had testified before the commission two days earlier. Police have not confirmed any link. In northern KZN, an Ethiopian shopkeeper was killed and his shop set alight near Nongoma, amid a wave of at least 10 anti-migrant arson attacks around Durban since early October.

Society & Culture

New WHO estimates show South Africa recorded about 53,000 tuberculosis deaths in 2025, even though TB incidence has fallen 63% since 2015. Just over half of those deaths were among people living with HIV. At the Anele Tembe inquest, testimony found that DNA evidence could not establish whether the late rapper AKA contributed to possible bloodstains in the hotel room.

Tourism

Airlines are adding capacity for the December peak. FlySafair is growing its fleet to 39 Boeing 737-800s and raising daily flights from about 173 to between 186 and 200, and it expects more than a million passengers in December alone. The airline launched a third daily Johannesburg–Harare rotation on November 16. Extra Johannesburg–George flights start November 26, and a Johannesburg–Lusaka route follows on February 1. South African Airways also announced six new routes on November 18.

Site to visit: Robben Island, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is where Nelson Mandela spent 18 of his 27 years in prison. It is now a museum that stands for the triumph of democracy over oppression. Ferries leave from the Nelson Mandela Gateway at Cape Town's V&A Waterfront, and tours are often led by former political prisoners. Summer tours sell out quickly, so book ahead.

Science & Technology

At COP31 in Antalya, Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa's plan for 5.2GW of new nuclear capacity by 2039 has drawn protests and threats of legal action from groups including Earthlife Africa. Eskom submitted a feasibility programme on November 10, and no reactor technology has been chosen. Closer to home, experts say the science is now settled on restoring Lower Tokai's fynbos in Cape Town.
