Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · Asia

Vietnam

Asia · population ~98M

Politics & Government

Vietnam's leaders spent the week abroad while the legislature took a break. General Secretary and President To Lam attended the 20th Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh, where he met French President Emmanuel Macron and called for stronger multilateralism. He then went to Shenzhen ahead of APEC leaders' week. Prime Minister Le Minh Hung spent four days in Manila for the 49th ASEAN Summit, where he co-chaired the Mekong-Japan Summit with Japan's Takaichi Sanae and set out three strategic directions for the East Asia Summit. At home, the 16th National Assembly's second session finished its first phase after Hung answered lawmakers' questions for the first time on November 12. Lawmakers return on November 20–21 to finish a session that is expected to consider 43 draft laws and resolutions.

Economy & Business

Trade is still the main engine of growth. Vietnam's import-export turnover topped US$1 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, and seafood exports reached nearly US$10.5 billion, up more than 12%, with shrimp alone earning US$4.2 billion. Chilean President José Antonio Kast was in Hanoi on November 16 for a business forum on expanding Chile-Vietnam trade. On November 17, the VNR500 list of the country's 500 largest enterprises was published for its 20th year. In the Assembly, the finance minister said 5 trillion VND is ready as starting capital for a new National Housing Fund.

Military & Security

Public Security Minister General Luong Tam Quang met his Chinese counterpart Wang Xiaohong in Shenzhen on November 17. They agreed to step up security and law-enforcement cooperation. Quang suggested the two ministries share experience in securing major political events, since Vietnam will host APEC Economic Leaders' Week in 2027. Wang asked for closer coordination against transnational crime and easier cross-border travel for tourists and business people.

Society & Culture

The central region is being hit by repeated floods. As of November 12, heavy rain from Thanh Hoa to Dak Lak had flooded more than 2,700 houses and forced nearly 1,900 households to evacuate, mostly in Gia Lai and Dak Lak. Hanoi Times reports a fourth consecutive flood as rivers from Ha Tinh to Khanh Hoa rise again this week, while a strong cold spell moves into the north. In Hanoi, a ceremony on November 13 marked 950 years of Quoc Tu Giam, the country's first national academy. To Lam asked schools to carry its spirit of learning into daily school life.

Tourism

Vietnam received 19.5 million international visitors in the first ten months of 2026, about 78% of its full-year target of 25 million. China, South Korea and Russia were the biggest source markets. Quang Ninh, home to Ha Long Bay, welcomed its 5 millionth foreign visitor of the year. The World Golf Awards also named Vietnam Asia's Best Golf Destination for the tenth year in a row.

Site to visit: Hanoi's Temple of Literature (Van Mieu – Quoc Tu Giam) combines a temple to Confucius with the imperial academy that trained Vietnam's scholars for centuries, and it is marking 950 years this month. New self-guided programmes have visitors complete tasks and collect stamps around the courtyards, and there are 3D projections of the site's history. A site map and leaflet in Vietnamese, English and French have been available since November 3.

Science & Technology

Long Thanh International Airport in Dong Nai, Vietnam's biggest infrastructure project, held its first full passenger trials on November 16. Fifty staff played passengers and went through check-in, security, immigration and customs, ahead of commercial flights from 00:00 on December 24. The first schedule allows about 366 flights a week. In Shenzhen, To Lam spoke at a Vietnam-China Greater Bay Area technology connectivity forum and called for closer cooperation on science, technology and skills.

🔥 Trending
Vucar
Startup / AI used-car marketplace · Vietnam

Vucar is a homegrown startup that runs used-car sales as online auctions. Dealers bid against each other for a car after Vucar has inspected it, checked its documents and photographed it. The owner decides whether to accept, and Vucar handles the paperwork, payment and handover. Its pricing model is trained on more than 4 million car listings, and AI agents answer questions from sellers and dealers. On November 13 it closed a pre-Series A round led by OSK-SBI Venture Partners, with Monk's Hill Ventures and Antler also investing, as car sales in Vietnam rose 31% in the first half of 2026.

Tuesday, November 17, 2026 · Europe

France

Europe · population ~68M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu's government faces another nationwide public-sector strike today. Unions representing about 5.8 million state employees want across-the-board pay rises, and Friday's talks with Lecornu did not satisfy them. Earlier this month the National Assembly unanimously rejected the revenue section of the 2027 budget, and no conventional majority looks available. That leaves the Article 49.3 procedure, ordinances or a special law as the remaining routes. The 2027 presidential race is also shaping politics: Olympic judo champion Teddy Riner, now a candidate, rejected President Macron's endorsement as a "poisoned chalice". Polls project Marine Le Pen leading the first round. Abroad, Paris has asked the European Commission to examine whether Algeria is breaching its EU association agreement through what France calls an unofficial boycott of French goods.

Economy & Business

Deficit pressure is at the centre of the budget fight. The government wanted to cut the deficit to 5% of GDP in 2027 through a €54 billion effort. Budget Minister David Amiel warned that the amended version lawmakers voted down could have pushed it to 5.9%. The broader picture is weak: the government had already cut its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5%, and unemployment rose to 8.3% in the second quarter. Trade friction with Algeria is now hurting supply chains. Algiers reportedly halted urea shipments that supplied roughly 30% of French imports and has bought no French wheat since July 2024. In the French tech sector, nine startup deals raised €75.6 million in the week to November 13.

Military & Security

France has blamed an Iranian cyber unit linked to the Revolutionary Guards for an October attack on refrigeration software in about 700 supermarkets. The hackers pushed thermostats as high as 80°C overnight, and Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called it the first large-scale sabotage cyberattack on French soil. The same analysis notes that cybercriminals stole more than €100 million from banking group Crédit Mutuel Arkéa this month, and that France has still not implemented the EU's NIS2 directive. The man accused in the deadly October shooting in Trappes, which prosecutors treat as a racist terror attack, told investigators he acted "on behalf of France". A France 24 analysis also examines how the authorities have used anti-terrorism powers to deter protesters.

Society & Culture

On Saturday, protests took place across France for a fifth Saturday in a row. The interior ministry counted about 106,000 marchers, while student unions claimed 200,000. Demonstrators oppose rising living costs, alleged police violence and a bill that would strengthen legal protection for officers who use their weapons. High-school blockades have now lasted more than a month, and unions rejected a 0.6% minimum-wage increase as "crumbs." In culture, Gérard Depardieu dropped his appeal, making his 2025 sexual-assault conviction final. French cricket has fallen into crisis after a funding scandal at its federation.

Tourism

Visitors in Paris should expect disruption today: several metro stations were due to close from 11am ahead of the main 2pm strike rally. The protests have not dented France's annual numbers, and the country is projecting a record €80 billion in international tourism receipts for 2026, helped by long-haul arrivals from the Americas. The Alsace Christmas season starts soon, with Colmar's markets opening on November 23 and Strasbourg's on November 27. Strasbourg's markets draw close to two million visitors a year, and hotels there often sell out far in advance.

Site to visit: Strasbourg's Grande Île, the medieval island centre that is a UNESCO World Heritage site, is built around the soaring Gothic cathedral and the half-timbered lanes of Petite France. From November 27 to December 24, 2026, eight Christmas markets spread from Place Kléber to Cathedral Square, linked by a light trail through Petite France and the Neustadt. The markets are free to enter, and the TGV from Paris takes about two hours.

Science & Technology

France's bid to host Europe's AI infrastructure is being tested: developers have requested 21 GW of grid capacity for data centres. The pitch rests on electricity that was more than 95% low-carbon in 2025, but grid bottlenecks, planning hurdles and local opposition stand in the way. Robotics firm Wandercraft says it wants to build one humanoid robot every hour. In French Guiana, France 24 reports that illegal gold mining is damaging rainforest and Indigenous communities.

🔥 Trending
Orakl Oncology
Biotech / AI startup · Villejuif (Paris region)

Orakl Oncology was spun out of the Gustave Roussy cancer centre in 2023. It grows small lab copies of patients' tumours, called organoids, tests drugs on them, and feeds the results to an AI model that predicts how cancer drugs will perform in clinical trials. This week it led the French tech funding rankings with a €30 million Series A, the largest of nine French deals. The company plans to expand from colorectal and pancreatic cancer into lung and gastric cancer and to build teams in the US and UK. Coverage notes that its accuracy claims rest on a preprint that has not yet been peer reviewed.

Monday, November 16, 2026 · Asia

Japan

Asia · population ~124M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi leaves on Monday for a four-day trip to the Philippines and China. In Manila she attends ASEAN-related meetings, including a Japan–ASEAN summit on Tuesday and what would be the first Quad leaders' summit since September 2024. She then goes to Shenzhen for the APEC summit on Thursday. Tokyo is uneasy that Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin plan to hold their first-ever three-way meeting on the APEC sidelines. Officials have asked Washington to tread carefully amid talk of a “Yalta 2.0” and fears about what it could mean for Taiwan and Ukraine. A weekend Kyodo poll found cabinet approval up slightly to 50.4%, but about 63% doubt her flagship plan to cut the consumption tax on food from 8% to 1% for two years will ease living costs, and 85.6% want her to explain it further.

Economy & Business

The yen slid toward 160 per dollar, and top currency diplomat Atsushi Mimura warned that the moves were “too rapid,” adding that it was “too early” to forget the July intervention. The Bank of Japan's 1.25% policy rate is still far below US levels, and markets are watching for a December hike. Higher rates are good for lenders: MUFG, SMFG and Mizuho all raised their full-year profit forecasts, and MUFG now expects a net profit of ¥2.8 trillion. With Middle East tensions keeping energy prices high, the cabinet approved ¥651.8 billion from reserves for winter utility subsidies covering December through February, which it says will save an average household about ¥7,000. The Fair Trade Commission also closed its probe into Amazon Japan after the company agreed to stop requiring vendors to match rival sites' prices to win prominent placement.

Military & Security

The government is weighing about ¥60 trillion for its next five-year defence buildup, which starts in April. That is up from roughly ¥43 trillion in the current plan but short of the ¥70–80 trillion the Defense Ministry wanted for drones, ammunition and missiles, because officials want to avoid a big tax rise while the food tax cut is coming. The pressure from Washington is growing: the Trump administration has reportedly asked Tokyo to roughly triple host-nation support for US forces to more than ¥3 trillion over five years, from ¥1.06 trillion now. Japan is said to be considering about ¥1.5 trillion, including spending to harden bases against attack, and both sides aim to settle by the end of the year.

Society & Culture

November 15 was the 49th anniversary of the abduction of 13-year-old Megumi Yokota by North Korean agents in Niigata. Her mother Sakie, now 90, again pleaded for a resolution. Takaichi calls bringing every abductee home her “duty” and hopes for a summit with Pyongyang. In Tokyo, shoppers stood in the rain to applaud as Hands Shibuya closed for good. The store opened as Tokyu Hands in 1978 and was a fixture of the district's youth culture. A day earlier, about 2,300 people dressed as Santa paraded through Shibuya, Harajuku and Yoyogi Park in an annual charity event.

Tourism

With record inbound tourism, Nara Municipal Hospital has had 51 ambulance cases from deer-related injuries so far this year, the most it has recorded. More than 70% of the injured were foreign visitors, and Nara Park received over 3.5 million foreign visitors in 2025. Officials are urging tourists to keep away from rutting stags and not to tease the deer with crackers. Narita Airport has set out a plan to merge its three terminals into one in the 2030s, with new runways to lift capacity from 57 million to 75 million passengers a year as Haneda nears saturation. Airlines are also building routes: ANA and Vietnam Airlines signed a framework to pursue a joint venture after a decade of partnership.

Site to visit: Naha's Shuri Castle was the royal seat of the Ryukyu Kingdom from the 15th to the 19th century, and its site is part of the UNESCO-listed Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu. Its vermilion main hall burned down in 2019, and the rebuilt hall has a completion ceremony on November 22 and opens to the public on November 23. Much of the castle park, including free areas with views over Naha, stayed open during the rebuild, and the castle is a walk uphill from Shuri Station on the Yui Rail monorail.

Science & Technology

The government is considering binding safety obligations, with penalties, for AI businesses. That would toughen the 2025 AI law, whose measures are currently nonbinding, and a working group is due to outline its review by year-end. Digital infrastructure had a rough week. NTT Docomo's 5G standalone network went down nationwide for the third time this month, after outages on November 5 and 11. Keisei Electric Railway said up to 3.37 million Skyliner booking records may have leaked, one of several recent corporate breaches. On climate, Japan was one of nine countries handed a satirical “Fossil of the Day” award at COP31 in Antalya because its leaders did not attend.

🔥 Trending
Bonbon Drop stickers (“Bondoro”)
Collectible stationery · Osaka

Bonbon Drop stickers, known as “Bondoro,” are glossy, puffy 3D stickers made by Osaka stationery maker Q-LiA and released in 2025. They grew into a playground trading craze, and a Nifty Kids survey found that 77.5% of elementary schoolers say they are hooked on sticker collecting, with drop stickers by far the favourite. They are now so scarce that knockoffs are circulating. The craze has spread to adults as a nostalgic, screen-free hobby, and “Bondoro” is on the 2026 Buzzword of the Year shortlist, with the winner due December 1.

Sunday, November 15, 2026 · North America

Panama

North America · population ~4M

Politics & Government

Panama pushed back sharply this week after US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth told a Pentagon briefing on November 10 that Washington and Panama “jointly control” the canal. Foreign Minister Javier Martínez-Acha replied that “the Panama Canal belongs exclusively to Panamanians,” stressing that security cooperation with the US is not exclusive and gives no foreign country a role in running the waterway, and former lawmakers cited the neutrality treaty and the constitution. President José Raúl Mulino also worked the phones with neighbours, agreeing with Costa Rican President Laura Fernández that their joint fight against narcoterrorism has been effective and inviting her to the CAF forum in Panama in January 2027. Early on November 14, someone threw an explosive device at the San Miguelito home of La Prensa associate deputy editor Rolando Rodríguez, destroying two cars and damaging the house. No one was hurt. Mulino condemned the attack, and the IAPA and CPJ called it a grave assault on press freedom.

Economy & Business

The Panama Canal raised daily transit capacity to 34 slots from November 10, adding a Panamax slot for Super-category vessels and lifting the maximum draft in the Neopanamax locks to 15.24 metres (50 feet) until further notice. The business chamber CCIAP welcomed the heavier public-investment tilt of the $35.11 billion 2027 state budget, in which Social Security takes the largest share at $8.5 billion, but warned that the money has to turn into finished projects and earthquake recovery. After a bank collapse in Paraguay, the Panama Banking Association said a similar failure could not happen in Panama’s dollarised system, citing more than $150 billion in assets and capital and liquidity ratios roughly double the legal minimums. The country has neither a central bank nor public deposit insurance.

Military & Security

Migrant smuggling through the Darién remains a prosecution priority. A man caught in Operation Redil was sentenced to 10 years in prison after admitting his role in a network that charged about $1,500 a head to move Ecuadorian and Cuban migrants from the Colombian border toward Costa Rica. Days earlier, authorities arrested a Guatemalan man on similar charges. The Mulino–Fernández call framed the Panama–Costa Rica security partnership as a “joint and very firm effort” against organised crime.

Society & Culture

A month after the magnitude 7.7 earthquake of October 9, which killed three people and was followed by more than 1,400 aftershocks, about 2,700 people remain in roughly 100 shelters. The housing ministry says more than 300 homes are under construction, 70 roads have reopened and 39 of 54 monitored heritage sites are being shored up, while 16 sites near the Azuero–Soná fault will not be rebuilt. Chitré is still struggling with water supply. The Agricultural Marketing Institute plans 10,000–15,000 free Christmas food boxes for quake-affected families from early December, pending Assembly approval of funds. On health, Minister Fernando Boyd Galindo said a free two-dose dengue vaccine will join the national schedule, with the first doses expected in the first half of 2027 for adolescents aged 10 to 15. The education ministry, the UN and UNICEF also launched “Riesgolandia Panamá,” a disaster-preparedness board game for primary schools nationwide.

Tourism

The quake is still hurting the Azuero coast. In Pedasí, boat operators are taking turns on scarce trips to Isla Iguana and have asked for a six-month suspension of the island’s entrance fees, even as Playa Venao’s main hotels are operating and the town insists it is “open and safe.” Nationally, Global Guardian’s 2027 risk map ranks Panama, alongside Costa Rica, as Central America’s safest destination, with warnings limited to parts of the capital and the Darién. Tocumen Airport, nearing its 25-million-passenger capacity, has put a $208 million Terminal 2 pier extension out to bid (bids due November 23) as it targets 35 million passengers by 2030. When an FAA communications failure briefly shut US airspace on November 13, 18 Copa flights were affected and two turned back to Panama. Copa is offering penalty-free changes.

Site to visit: Panama City’s Archaeological Site of Panamá Viejo and Historic District of Panamá pairs the ruins of the first European city on the Pacific coast of the Americas, founded in 1519 and sacked by the pirate Henry Morgan in 1671, with Casco Viejo, the walled district built after the city moved. Casco Viejo’s plazas, churches and seafront promenade are free to explore on foot and a short taxi ride from most hotels, while Panamá Viejo charges admission for its ruins and museum. The December–April dry season is the most comfortable time to visit.

Science & Technology

Panamanian students won five medals (one gold, two silver, two bronze) at the XVIII Latin American Astronomy and Astronautics Olympiad, held in Paraguay November 1–9. Asdrúbal Castillo of the San Miguelito Cultural Institute took gold. The team qualified through the national space science olympiad run by Senacyt and the City of Knowledge Foundation. Marina operator Giuseppe Cicatelli has proposed turning Panama’s docks into ocean-monitoring stations that would track sea level, temperature and water quality on both coasts. The idea has no budget or partners yet, and he suggests starting with a pilot. Researchers also flagged priority conservation areas for Latin America’s most threatened birds.

🔥 Trending
Kenny Dancer (“La Ministra”)
Dancer & comedy creator · Chilibre, Panamá province

Kenny Dancer is the persona of dancer, choreographer and marketer Amílcar Montezuma from Chilibre. He built a following during the pandemic with “La Ministra,” a comic character who parodied masks, quarantines and health decrees and kept growing because followers asked for more. In the November 4 Patriotic Symbols Day parade on Vía España he set La Ministra aside and performed in a custom costume made by local Panamanian designers and artisans. His Instagram clip of the parade made the rounds in local viral-video coverage.

Saturday, November 14, 2026 · Asia

Iran

Asia · population ~90M

Politics & Government

Iran and the United States remain in a state of “no war, no peace”: full-scale fighting is paused, but no formal settlement exists, and parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says the ball is in Washington's court on Tehran's seven conditions, which include lifting the naval blockade, sanctions relief and the release of frozen funds. On November 13 President Trump said “no deal” was necessary, while advisers remain split between economic pressure and a negotiated exit. The same day the IAEA urged Iran to restore inspectors' access, calling the lack of information on its nuclear material, including an underground enrichment plant at Isfahan that inspectors have never been allowed to visit, “a matter of proliferation concern.” Iran's refusal to discuss its nuclear programme before its ceasefire terms are accepted is stalling diplomacy, and Trump is expected to raise Iran with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China next week.

Economy & Business

The US naval blockade, which has halted Iranian crude loadings since mid-August, is biting hard: IEA estimates cited by Iran International put October output at 1.86 million barrels a day, 46% below pre-war levels and now under what Iran's own refineries consumed in 2024–25. Washington is also squeezing overland trade, with CENTCOM standing up Task Force Iron Gate on November 7 to target land and Caspian routes, and reportedly asking Baghdad to close all but two border crossings with Iran. Turkey is allowing only about 200 trucks a day through the Bazargan crossing against the 500 Tehran requested. Household strain is visible in reports of cash-strapped Iranians offering organs for sale to cover debts and living costs.

Military & Security

Tension around the Strait of Hormuz flared again this week: the Revolutionary Guards claimed on November 13 that they struck US early-warning radar sites in Bahrain, but US Central Command said it shot down two Iranian drones with no damage, in what Reuters called Iran's first claimed attack on US assets in a Gulf country since early September. Iran framed the strike as retaliation for an attack on a telecom tower in Sirik, Hormozgan province, and has since threatened an “unlimited response”. On November 14 Iran's navy said a tanker hit a mine and caught fire east of the strait, an unverified claim that came as the UN called for an end to attacks on civilian shipping. In London, two Iranian men were found guilty of spying on dissident journalists.

Society & Culture

A Tehran Revolutionary Court has sentenced comedian Saeed Shakiba to five years in prison over a satirical video imitating the voice of former supreme leader Ali Khamenei, according to rights group HRANA. Schools are struggling as winter arrives, with reports of students shivering in unheated classrooms in Tabriz amid government warnings of gas cuts. A senior population official warned that fertility has dropped to 1.34 children per woman and floated a projection of the population falling from 87 million to 31 million over 75 years, far steeper than UN estimates. A health official also said around 20 million Iranians have diabetes or prediabetes as medicine prices climb.

Tourism

Travel to Iran remains effectively off the table for most foreigners. The Tehran flight information region reopened to overflights in October, but SafeAirspace still rates Iran “Do Not Fly”, and France issued a fresh NOTAM on November 12 telling its operators to stay out until at least November 25. EASA's conflict-zone bulletin advising airlines to avoid Iranian airspace at all altitudes runs to November 16 unless renewed, and US operators remain barred under the FAA's SFAR 117. Many governments maintain do-not-travel advisories.

Site to visit: Isfahan's Meidan Emam (Naqsh-e Jahan Square), laid out by Shah Abbas I in the early 17th century, is one of the largest city squares in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site framed by the Shah Mosque, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, Ali Qapu Palace and the gateway to the Qeysarieh bazaar. The square itself is free to walk at any hour (the mosques and palace charge admission), and spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons; check current travel advisories before planning any trip.

Science & Technology

Iran's push into solar power has stalled: imports of Chinese panels fell by more than 80% in 2026, to just 138 MW worth about $15 million from January to September. That comes as officials say about 4,000 MW of generating capacity was damaged in the conflict, against a need for more than 5,000 MW of new capacity every year. Solar capacity had jumped 128% in 2025 to about 1,781 MW, while wind has stayed flat at 378 MW for years.

Friday, November 13, 2026 · Africa

Kenya

Africa · population ~56M

Politics & Government

Kenya's 2027 presidential race has tightened sharply: a new TIFA Research poll released this week puts President William Ruto and Edwin Sifuna level at 31% each, with Kalonzo Musyoka sliding to 9% from 25% a year ago and 15% of voters undecided. TIFA's survey of 2,036 respondents across all 47 counties suggests no candidate would clear the constitutional threshold in a first round, forcing a runoff. Sifuna leads in Western, Nairobi and Mt Kenya, while Ruto dominates Northern Kenya and the Central Rift; Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi Movement now polls at 17%, ahead of ODM at 10%. On the diplomatic front, Labour Cabinet Secretary Alfred Mutua held talks in Riyadh on November 12, where the two governments agreed to finalise a skilled-worker recruitment deal within a month as Nairobi lobbies for Saudi restrictions imposed over Ebola to be lifted.

Economy & Business

The government says it has secured 293,600 job opportunities for Kenyans in Saudi Arabia, spanning healthcare, construction, hospitality, transport and domestic work, including a pilot on voluntary recruitment fees. Central Bank Governor Kamau Thugge explained why the shilling has held near KSh129–130 to the dollar since March 2024, crediting interbank market reforms, a 2024 Eurobond and reserve rebuilding of up to $2.5 billion expected this year. The CBK's weekly bulletin of November 6 showed the rate steady at KSh129.96. Along the northern border, fighting in Ethiopia has stranded Mombasa–Addis Ababa trucks near Moyale for about two weeks, with local traders reporting price rises of around 20%.

Military & Security

Clashes between Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional forces around Moyale have displaced more than 50,000 people, according to the Kenya Red Cross, many of them sheltering with host families on the Kenyan side. Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja said more officers will be deployed along the border and that Kenya is engaging Ethiopia's federal government to end the fighting. One Kenyan has been killed by a stray bullet, and analysts warn of armed fighters crossing the frontier, making the crisis a security problem as much as a humanitarian one. Officials admit there are no contingency food stocks, and UNHCR has called for a ceasefire.

Society & Culture

In a landmark verdict, a Mombasa court sentenced cult leader Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison on November 6 for the starvation deaths of 191 people, 190 of them children, linked to the Shakahola Forest tragedy in which more than 400 died. The case has renewed calls for tighter regulation of religious organisations. On the health front, Kenya has discharged its last Ebola patient and begun a 42-day countdown to being declared free of the disease, after nine confirmed cases and two deaths, with prevention measures running until December 23. The WHO praised Kenya's response, which traced all cases to a single contact list with no community transmission.

Tourism

At World Travel Market in London, the Kenya Tourism Board set a target of 199,645 UK arrivals in 2026, a 16.8% rise on last year, while pitching coastal, cultural, wellness and community experiences beyond the classic safari. KTB chief June Chepkemei also said Kenya is aiming for 60% growth in conference tourism next year, building on 643,068 business visitors in 2025 and new venues such as the 10,000-delegate Bomas International Convention Complex. The Kenya Wildlife Service is offering discounts of up to 30% for conferences held in national parks, and Samburu County is waiving half of park entry fees for targeted groups.

Site to visit: Lamu Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001, is the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa, with coral-stone houses, Lamu Fort and narrow lanes where donkeys rather than cars do the hauling. Visitors usually fly to Manda Airport, which has daily flights from Nairobi and Mombasa, and cross to Lamu Island by boat; the Lamu Cultural Festival, with dhow races and traditional dance, is held in late August.
