Friday, November 13, 2026 · Africa

Kenya

Africa · population ~56M

Politics & Government

Kenya's 2027 presidential race has tightened sharply: a new TIFA Research poll released this week puts President William Ruto and Edwin Sifuna level at 31% each, with Kalonzo Musyoka sliding to 9% from 25% a year ago and 15% of voters undecided. TIFA's survey of 2,036 respondents across all 47 counties suggests no candidate would clear the constitutional threshold in a first round, forcing a runoff. Sifuna leads in Western, Nairobi and Mt Kenya, while Ruto dominates Northern Kenya and the Central Rift; Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi Movement now polls at 17%, ahead of ODM at 10%. On the diplomatic front, Labour Cabinet Secretary Alfred Mutua held talks in Riyadh on November 12, where the two governments agreed to finalise a skilled-worker recruitment deal within a month as Nairobi lobbies for Saudi restrictions imposed over Ebola to be lifted.

Economy & Business

The government says it has secured 293,600 job opportunities for Kenyans in Saudi Arabia, spanning healthcare, construction, hospitality, transport and domestic work, including a pilot on voluntary recruitment fees. Central Bank Governor Kamau Thugge explained why the shilling has held near KSh129–130 to the dollar since March 2024, crediting interbank market reforms, a 2024 Eurobond and reserve rebuilding of up to $2.5 billion expected this year. The CBK's weekly bulletin of November 6 showed the rate steady at KSh129.96. Along the northern border, fighting in Ethiopia has stranded Mombasa–Addis Ababa trucks near Moyale for about two weeks, with local traders reporting price rises of around 20%.

Military & Security

Clashes between Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional forces around Moyale have displaced more than 50,000 people, according to the Kenya Red Cross, many of them sheltering with host families on the Kenyan side. Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja said more officers will be deployed along the border and that Kenya is engaging Ethiopia's federal government to end the fighting. One Kenyan has been killed by a stray bullet, and analysts warn of armed fighters crossing the frontier, making the crisis a security problem as much as a humanitarian one. Officials admit there are no contingency food stocks, and UNHCR has called for a ceasefire.

Society & Culture

In a landmark verdict, a Mombasa court sentenced cult leader Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison on November 6 for the starvation deaths of 191 people, 190 of them children, linked to the Shakahola Forest tragedy in which more than 400 died. The case has renewed calls for tighter regulation of religious organisations. On the health front, Kenya has discharged its last Ebola patient and begun a 42-day countdown to being declared free of the disease, after nine confirmed cases and two deaths, with prevention measures running until December 23. The WHO praised Kenya's response, which traced all cases to a single contact list with no community transmission.

Tourism

At World Travel Market in London, the Kenya Tourism Board set a target of 199,645 UK arrivals in 2026, a 16.8% rise on last year, while pitching coastal, cultural, wellness and community experiences beyond the classic safari. KTB chief June Chepkemei also said Kenya is aiming for 60% growth in conference tourism next year, building on 643,068 business visitors in 2025 and new venues such as the 10,000-delegate Bomas International Convention Complex. The Kenya Wildlife Service is offering discounts of up to 30% for conferences held in national parks, and Samburu County is waiving half of park entry fees for targeted groups.

Site to visit: Lamu Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001, is the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa, with coral-stone houses, Lamu Fort and narrow lanes where donkeys rather than cars do the hauling. Visitors usually fly to Manda Airport, which has daily flights from Nairobi and Mombasa, and cross to Lamu Island by boat; the Lamu Cultural Festival, with dhow races and traditional dance, is held in late August.

🔥 Trending

Music (rap) · Nairobi, Kenya

The young rapper, who released his first song in 2023, has become the breakout voice of Kenyan street rap, with tracks that resonate with listeners in Nairobi's estates. His hit ‘Chinje’ passed 20 million YouTube views in September, making it the most-watched Kenyan rap song ever, and his channel has topped 145 million lifetime views. His Spotify audience grew nearly 1,500% year-on-year in early 2026, and he was named Kenya's most-discovered artist in 2025 Wrapped. His ‘Hongo ya Karao Vol. II’ also topped the YouTube Music chart.

Thursday, November 12, 2026 · Asia

Israel

Asia · population ~7M

Politics & Government

Two weeks after an inconclusive October 27 election left Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc with 53 seats and the opposition with 48, President Isaac Herzog handed Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government, giving him 28 days plus a possible 14-day extension. The 26th Knesset, which includes 47 first-time MKs, was sworn in on November 10, and on its first day Eisenkot's bloc submitted bills for a state commission of inquiry into October 7, prime-ministerial term limits and repeal of the judicial overhaul laws. Netanyahu answered with a furious speech demanding a “unity government” and attacking Arab parties, whose support Eisenkot may need. United Torah Judaism's spiritual leadership has meanwhile reportedly authorized talks with the Eisenkot bloc, though the yeshiva draft remains the main sticking point.

Economy & Business

S&P Global raised its growth forecasts for Israel to 5.5% for 2026 and 7% for 2027, citing Nvidia-linked activity, while leaving the credit rating unchanged and warning that defence spending will keep fiscal pressure high. In banking, the Bank of Israel granted Revolut a conditional banking licence, only the third new bank licence since 2019 in a market dominated by five groups. On aid, Netanyahu's office said on November 11 that Israel will “gradually” phase out US military assistance, pushing back on President Trump's claim that the roughly $4 billion a year would end by late 2026; the current $3.8 billion-a-year agreement runs to September 2028.

Military & Security

An overnight Israeli strike on the Nasser Hospital complex in Khan Younis killed at least six people and wounded at least 20, according to Hamas authorities. The IDF and Shin Bet said the target was a senior Hamas commander, reportedly the head of the Khan Younis Brigade, and that the complex was used for weapons storage and command centres. Who decides on military action during the post-election period has also become a fight: Defence Minister Israel Katz insisted the caretaker government needs no opposition approval for operations, while Eisenkot said Netanyahu is refusing to give him security briefings and asked the attorney general to step in. Reports also say Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir has again warned against Netanyahu pressing for action against Iran.

Society & Culture

Haredi protesters blocked a road outside Beit Shemesh and Jerusalem's light rail on November 11 after three yeshiva students with standing draft orders were jailed for 20 days. Police broke up both protests, and rioters reportedly hit an i24 reporter with a stone. On the other side of the political divide, right-wing activists warned of “civil war” if a government is formed with Arab-party support. In Tel Aviv, Fashion Week (November 9–12) put former hostage Omer Shem Tov and disabled IDF veterans on the runway. Veterans walked on prosthetics or in wheelchairs in designer Golan Elbaz Frydman's “Fragments” collection.

Tourism

Israel welcomed 154,200 visitors from the US and Canada in July–September, up 8.6% year on year. The Tourism Ministry is backing the rebound with a $2.8 million “I Am Israel” campaign, and United and El Al restored several US routes in late October. Air Canada plans to resume Toronto–Tel Aviv flights on November 30, but its Dubai service stays suspended until mid-January. In the south, a 4.5-magnitude earthquake centred near Taba on November 11 briefly shut Ramon Airport near Eilat for inspections after windows shattered. Full operations resumed after checks, and no injuries were reported.

Site to visit: Masada, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001, is Herod the Great's clifftop fortress above the Dead Sea. It was the last stand of Jewish rebels against Rome in 73 AD, and its Roman siege works are among the best preserved anywhere. Hikers climb the steep Snake Path from the Dead Sea side, usually starting before sunrise to avoid the heat, or ride the cable car from 8 am; an entrance fee applies either way, and you should carry water because it is only available at the top.

Science & Technology

Weizmann Institute and Hebrew University researchers reported in Nature an experimental blood test that detects early breast cancer with 95% accuracy. The test reads epigenetic tags on circulating DNA-protein spools using single-molecule microscopy and AI, and it also predicts recurrence with 81% accuracy. In a separate Weizmann-led pair of papers, scientists programmed gels with DNA so they build their own motor proteins and move, which could eventually lead to soft robotics and targeted therapies. On infrastructure, the cabinet approved three gas-fired power plants and two agrivoltaic solar farms to keep up with electricity demand driven by AI data centres, population growth and electric vehicles.

🔥 Trending

Indie rock band · Holon

Producers Chen Cordoba and Aviv Bernholz rehearsed in a small room in their hometown of Holon. With singer Eitan Peled, they formed Temper City, and the band's first-ever single, “Self Aware,” went viral on TikTok, helped along by a clip from an account with zero followers. An album is planned for later this year.

Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · Europe

Finland

Europe · population ~6M

Politics & Government

Prime Minister Petteri Orpo’s government survived a no-confidence vote by 95 to 77 on November 4, after the SDP-led opposition, backed by the Centre Party, Greens, Left Alliance and Movement Now, accused the cabinet of failing ordinary people as fuel and energy costs rise. It was the 44th such motion against the government or its ministers since it took office in 2023. A day later, Yle’s party poll put Orpo’s National Coalition Party at 15.4%, its lowest in more than 30 years of Yle polling. That leaves the NCP third, behind the leading Social Democrats and the Finns Party (16.7%), while the Left Alliance hit a record 12.6%. On the diplomatic front, President Alexander Stubb phoned the leaders of the UAE, Egypt and Qatar about the Middle East and Russia’s war in Ukraine. The calls followed his earlier conversation with Vladimir Putin, held ahead of planned peace talks in Abu Dhabi.

Economy & Business

The Finnish Tax Administration says fewer than one in six crypto traders declared their dealings. Only about 16,000 people reported trades for last year, against an estimated 100,000 or more active residents. From next year, domestic brokers will report trades automatically, pre-filling roughly 50,000 tax returns, and foreign platforms will start sharing data under the international CARF framework. With public finances under strain, an Iro Research poll found that 65% of Finns oppose pension cuts in the next parliamentary term. Only 19% back cuts, and younger adults and men are the most open to them.

Military & Security

In the early hours of Wednesday, the Defence Forces restricted airspace and sea areas over the eastern Gulf of Finland around Kotka and Hamina, up to the Russian border, for about five hours. They called it a precaution against possible drones, after overnight drone alerts in Latvia, Lithuania and Poland. On November 5, Helsinki District Court convicted two Fitburg crew members of aggravated sabotage: the Russian captain got three and a half years and the Azerbaijani boatswain two years and ten months. The court found the ship dragged its anchor about 130 km along the seabed on New Year’s Eve 2025, damaging two telecom cables. The verdict can be appealed. Meanwhile, Finnish peacekeepers are heading home as UNIFIL winds down in Lebanon. More than 17,000 Finns have served in the mission since 1982, and about 180 are still deployed.

Society & Culture

Police logged 1,941 suspected hate crimes last year, a third straight annual record and more than double the 2020 figure. About 70% were linked to ethnic or national background. Researchers and parents also warn of the normalisation of racist language among young people: a Finnish Red Cross survey found that 95% of 15–24-year-olds had seen or experienced racism online. Separately, a Yle MOT investigation revealed that the social insurance agency Kela tracked the mouse clicks of about 2,000 staff in 2023. The monitoring led to dismissals, and the Labour Court has since ruled one of them unlawful.

Tourism

Lapland’s holiday season opened on November 10 when samba dancers led Rovaniemi’s Christmas procession. The 60 dancers of local samba school Samba de Aurora marched alongside Santa and Mrs Claus to launch the city’s Christmas street. Air travellers had a scare on November 5, when hoax bomb threats targeted Finnair flights to Rome and Vienna; both aircraft landed safely and were searched. Police are also urging Finns planning trips abroad to renew early: more than a million passports issued in 2022 expire in 2027, and ten-year passports are not expected until early 2028.

Site to visit: Suomenlinna, the UNESCO-listed 18th-century sea fortress spread across a cluster of islands off Helsinki, is one of the most visited sights in Finland and still a lived-in neighbourhood. There is no entrance fee for the island itself. HSL ferries from the eastern end of Market Square make the trip in about 15 minutes; it is in zone A, and tickets must be bought before boarding.

Science & Technology

Finland’s data centre boom drew a public backlash on November 7, when protesters rallied against data centres in Helsinki, Lahti, Tampere, Oulu, Jyväskylä and Turku. Police estimated about 1,300 people in the capital. Organisers including the Finnish Association for Nature Conservation want construction paused until regulation catches up with the projects’ impact on nature and electricity use. In health research, Finland has the world’s highest rate of type 1 diabetes, with about 1,000 new diagnoses a year, and the cause is still unknown. University of Helsinki researcher Lena Thorn points to an environmental trigger, since Finland’s risk is far higher than Russia’s despite similar genetics.

🔥 Trending
Parents’ anti-racism action group
Grassroots movement · Helsinki

A grassroots group of parents pushing back against normalised racism is growing in Finland. Zeynep Falay, a Helsinki resident since 2002, and Michelle Sahal Estimé started it with posts on LinkedIn. Sahal Estimé called the response “really overwhelming,” and the group has signed up dozens of new members and now meets regularly to plan its next campaign. It aims to give parents tools to spot and handle racist language and symbols reaching their children, to engage schools and young people, and eventually to push for legislative change.

Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · South America

Brazil

South America · population ~215M

Politics & Government

Two weeks after the runoff, Flávio Bolsonaro is still contesting Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's victory, which came by a margin of more than 4.4 million votes. He says he will seek to annul the vote, and his challenge centres on a Supreme Court order that extended voting hours on election day. The Workers' Party has meanwhile asked the Supreme Court to order a Federal Police inquiry into alleged US-linked online campaigning for the Bolsonaro side, and no court has ruled on either matter. Abroad, Lula is preparing to attend the ASEAN summit.

Economy & Business

The central bank's Copom held the Selic at 13.75% on November 4, unanimously ending a run of five consecutive quarter-point cuts. Consumer prices rose 4.58% in the 12 months to September, just above the 4.5% ceiling of the tolerance band, and the real was trading near R$5.50 per dollar. Copom described a challenging mix that demands "serenity and caution" but did not say the cuts were over. Separately, the government ranked 27 critical minerals by priority to steer up to BRL 7 billion in incentives for domestic processing of materials such as graphite, nickel and lithium.

Military & Security

Police ran an operation against cargo theft on the BR-116 highway, mobilising more than 150 officers from eight states with helicopters, drones and sniffer dogs against 14 targets after a six-month investigation. It follows the federal decision in July to deploy soldiers to airports and ports against organised crime, a mobilisation reported to run until May 2027.

Society & Culture

Marcelo Kariri Menezes and Idjahure Terena were sworn in on November 4 as the first Indigenous diplomats in Brazil's Foreign Service. They join the 2026-2028 class at the Rio Branco Institute, where nearly half of the roughly 60 students come from underrepresented groups and women make up a record 45 percent. Acting Foreign Minister Maria Laura da Rocha called their arrival "an act of resistance."

Tourism

Brazil recorded about 6.45 million international arrivals in the first eight months of 2026, according to tourism data reported by the Rio Times, with São Paulo (1.89 million) and Rio de Janeiro (1.67 million) leading. European arrivals rose about 14% year on year in January-August, and August set a record for the month. Carnival alone drew roughly 300,000 foreign visitors, and Embratur is aiming for 10 million foreign visitors in 2026 after closing 2025 at about 9.2 million.

Site to visit: Iguaçu National Park in Paraná is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, home to a sweeping chain of roughly 275 waterfalls on the border with Argentina and surrounded by Atlantic rainforest. Visitors usually base themselves in the city of Foz do Iguaçu, which has its own airport, and walk the park's falls trails and walkways; check current ticket prices before you go.

Science & Technology

New MapBiomas land-cover maps show that the plateau feeding the Pantanal has lost 6.7 million hectares of native vegetation between 1987 and 2025. The Pantanal's average flooded area fell 53%, from 5.99 million to 2.79 million hectares, comparing 1985-1994 with 2015-2025. On the climate front, Brazil handed the COP presidency to Türkiye on November 9 at the start of COP31 in Antalya, leaving behind two nonbinding roadmaps on phasing out fossil fuels and ending deforestation by 2030.

🔥 Trending
“Brasil com S” by DJ M4IA
Music / AI-made viral track · Minas Gerais

Producer Guilherme Maia, known as DJ M4IA, built a phonk-style track that simply chants the names of Brazil's World Cup squad, and it passed one billion plays across streaming and social video. Its short, repetitive chorus made it an easy soundtrack for short videos, including among non-Portuguese speakers. He used generative AI for most stages, from lyric structure and vocals to a music video made from text prompts. It fits a wider wave in which Brazilian funk and phonk are reaching global audiences.

Sunday, November 8, 2026 · North America

Mexico

North America · population ~130M

Politics & Government

President Claudia Sheinbaum used her Friday press conference to call for a “cleanup” of the National Electoral Institute after a contracting scandal that forced INE president Guadalupe Taddei to resign. Reports say electoral-materials contracts went to companies linked to a businessman close to Taddei, and 25 people, including both of them, were reportedly added to the Finance Ministry’s Blocked Persons List. Sheinbaum argued the corruption stretched back through the tenure of her predecessor at the institute, Lorenzo Córdova, and said an audit must establish the full extent. Separately, she said U.S. immigration raids were “very likely” a factor in Latino voters’ shift toward Democrats in the U.S. midterms, and confirmed she will travel to China November 15–20 for the APEC summit in Shenzhen, including a bilateral meeting with Xi Jinping.

Economy & Business

The Trump administration declined in July to endorse a 16-year renewal of USMCA, so the pact now faces annual reviews, and Mexico is pressing Washington for a bilateral deal on steel, aluminum and auto tariffs. The Rio Times reports that annual inflation was 3.45% in September and 3.62% in the first half of October, public debt stood at 19.554 trillion pesos (52.6% of GDP) at the end of September, and the peso was around 18.76 per dollar on November 5 after eight weeks of losses. Oil revenue fell 21.5% in real terms in the first nine months of the year. On the bright side, Mexico-Canada goods trade rose more than 18% in the first eight months of 2026, and the Buen Fin shopping weekend (November 13–17) is forecast to generate 235 billion pesos in sales, 7.3% more than last year.

Military & Security

Federal forces killed Edgar Aarón Vázquez Alvarado, known as “El General,” in a shootout in Naucalpan near Mexico City on November 5; officials describe him as the founder of Cártel Nuevo Imperio, and no forensic confirmation of his identity had been published at the time of reporting. Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch reported 32 arrests in 38 raids across the State of Mexico the same day. Earlier in the week, 450 troops were sent to Matamoros after cartel retaliation that killed six people, including a U.S. citizen, and Sheinbaum condemned the killing of Morena councilman Gabriel Rivas Ríos in Cuernavaca. She has repeatedly rejected the idea of U.S. military action on Mexican soil.

Society & Culture

A ban on cell phones in primary and secondary schools took effect this week for basic-education classrooms. In Michoacán, more than 2,000 people marched with the Uruapan mayor for peace and justice on the first anniversary of the assassination of her partner. Mexico News Daily also reports unconfirmed talk of a 2027 visit by Pope Leo XIV to the Basilica of Guadalupe.

Tourism

Mexico received 3.76 million international tourists in August, and spending per visitor rose 8% despite a dip in arrivals. On the Caribbean coast, beach tourism is down 7% while medical tourism holds up in Cancún and Playa del Carmen, and a new connector road now links the Cancún airport to the hotel zone and the Nichupté Bridge. Aeroméxico announced four new international routes, including Milan–Cozumel, while American Airlines is cutting some Tulum and Los Cabos service.

Site to visit: Maya Ka’an, the inland region of Quintana Roo south of the Riviera Maya, made National Geographic’s “Best of the World 2027” list. It links 78 Indigenous Maya communities with jungle, lagoons and community-run tours, including kayaking on the Muyil lagoon and embroidery workshops in X-Pichil. Upgraded roads and the Maya Train connect it to the Cancún and Tulum airports.

Science & Technology

An outbreak of the avocado seed weevil forced producers to destroy about 52 tonnes of avocados across Michoacán, Jalisco, Colima and Nayarit, and export shipments were halted while authorities work out a timeline to resume them. The beetle’s larvae develop inside the seed, so infested fruit often shows no outward damage and insecticides cannot reach it; Mexican guidance calls for burying fruit at least a meter deep or burning it.

🔥 Trending
Mi Valedor
Street paper & social enterprise · Mexico City

Mi Valedor, now 11 years old, publishes a street magazine sold by “valedores,” people who live in vulnerable situations, and has grown into a community hub. After the pandemic it added free showers, laundry, meals, ID help, medical care and therapy, plus art workshops where valedores produce their own zines, now sold at museums and book and art fairs. The article estimates about 37 people it serves now live stably in housing, and one participant’s debut zine hangs at the Centro Nacional de las Artes. The project runs on donations and volunteers.

Saturday, November 7, 2026 · Europe

Spain

Europe · population ~48M

Politics & Government

Spain heads into a snap general election on November 29, and The Local reports that Pedro Sanchez's Socialists are trailing in the polls, with Vox expected to be the junior partner in any right-wing government. The campaign has been shaken by claims from jailed former government adviser Koldo Garcia that a People's Party lawyer offered him €1 million for damaging information on the prime minister, a story that traces back to an earlier report about a newspaper paying him; the PP and its lawyer deny the allegation. Sanchez and Argentina's Javier Milei also exchanged a brief, cold greeting at the 30th Ibero-American Summit in Madrid on Thursday, their first meeting since Milei called Sanchez a dictator last month.

Economy & Business

The Labour Ministry has proposed a 5.3% rise in the minimum wage to €1,286 a month over 14 payments (roughly €1,500 over 12), above the 4.9% suggested by the expert committee; unions UGT and CCOO are holding out for 7%, and the cabinet could approve it on November 17 or 24. In agriculture, the government has ordered olive oil producers to withdraw 12% of output and store it until September 2027, about 188,000 tonnes, to keep a forecast 1.6 million tonne harvest (up 23%) from collapsing prices. Spain produces about 40% of the world's olive oil, and the measure is reviewable from December 31.

Military & Security

Security headlines this week centred on transport. Flights across Europe have been hit by bomb hoaxes, a pattern The Local examines in a report on who may be behind them. Separately, the EU's public prosecutor has opened a fraud inquiry into rail-safety funding on the Madrid–Seville line (see Society below). I did not find reporting this week on defence spending or border incidents that I could verify.

Society & Culture

The European Public Prosecutor's Office is investigating possible misuse of EU funds on the line where a Renfe train hit a stationary Iryo train near Urda in October, killing 69 people; offices of ADIF, the rail safety agency and the Court of Auditors were searched, Transport Minister Oscar Puente resigned on Tuesday, and train drivers' union Semaf has called strikes for November 10 and 12. Unions have also called a nationwide general strike for November 11 over the housing crisis and cost of living. In sport, the footballers' union asked for this weekend's matches to be postponed after the death of Tenerife forward Jesus de Miguel following a collision in a second-division match.

Tourism

Norwegian has announced a direct Oslo–Seville route for 2027, flying twice weekly (Wednesdays and Saturdays) from March 20 to June 16 and again from August 18 to October 30, with low fares found from under 1,000 Norwegian kroner round trip. The airline will also fly Copenhagen–Seville and a summer Copenhagen–Santiago de Compostela service aimed at Camino pilgrims. Travellers should note the planned rail and general strikes on November 10–12 when planning trips.

Site to visit: The Alhambra in Granada is a hilltop Nasrid palace-fortress complex with the Generalife gardens, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its intricate Islamic-era architecture. It is among Spain's most visited monuments, so tickets should be reserved in advance through the official ticket site; check current prices and opening hours there.
