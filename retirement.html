Cost of Living

Estimates vary by source, but Greenville generally lands close to or modestly below the national average on overall cost of living — one index puts it around 108 (roughly matching the South Carolina state average), while others show it about 10% cheaper than the U.S. as a whole. Housing does most of the heavy lifting: per RentCafe, housing runs about 27% below the national average and utilities about 11% lighter, with two-bedroom rents averaging around $1,260/month. Groceries and clothing track a percent or two below national norms, though healthcare services (routine visits, dentistry) run about 8% above average — worth noting given the strong local hospital system covered below.

Taxes on Retirement Income

South Carolina does not tax Social Security or Railroad Retirement benefits at all, regardless of income level. Pension, 401(k), and IRA withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income, but retirees get real relief: filers under 65 can deduct up to $3,000 of qualifying retirement income, while those 65+ can deduct up to $10,000, plus a separate senior deduction of roughly $15,000 against any income — per Brevy's 2026 breakdown. The state's top marginal income tax rate is around 6.2% for 2026. On property tax, Greenville's median effective rate runs about 0.88%, and South Carolina's Homestead Exemption shields the first $50,000 of a home's fair market value from all property taxes for residents 65+, legally blind, or permanently disabled who've lived in-state at least a year.

Housing Market

The average Greenville home value sits around $330,858 per Zillow, up 2.3% over the past year, while Redfin's county-wide median sale price runs higher at about $368,146, up a more modest 0.4% year-over-year. The broader market has balanced out in 2026, with inventory up 21% and homes taking about 57 days to sell, versus 50 days a year ago — a cooling but still fairly firm market. A specific breakout for 2–3 bedroom homes was not readily available, but the overall five-year trend shows roughly 25% cumulative appreciation since 2021.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Greenville sits well inland, so it's spared the worst of direct hurricane landfall, but it still faces meaningful risk from inland flooding along the Reedy River and area creeks, plus a notably high hail-storm risk and moderate tornado exposure, according to Greenville County Emergency Management. The bigger story is statewide: South Carolina is projected to see the nation's second-highest homeowners insurance rate increases over the next decade, driven mainly by coastal hurricane and flood exposure rather than the Upstate specifically. Standard homeowners policies don't cover flood damage; South Carolina's roughly 220,000 active NFIP flood policies average about $710/year, and the cheapest statewide homeowners rates (around $1,086/year for $300,000 in dwelling coverage) are still well below what coastal SC counties are seeing.

Sports Proximity

Greenville has no major-league franchise of its own, but Charlotte — home to the Carolina Panthers (NFL) — is about 101 miles / 1h41m away, and Atlanta, with the Falcons (NFL) and Braves (MLB), is roughly 146 miles / under 3 hours. Locally, Greenville fields its own minor-league and lower-division pro teams: the Greenville Drive (Boston Red Sox High-A affiliate) at Fluor Field, Greenville Triumph SC (USL League One soccer), and the Greenville Swamp Rabbits (ECHL hockey, LA Kings affiliate). Furman University's Paladins provide Division I college athletics right in town, and Clemson's Tigers are a short drive into the Upstate.

Senior Services & Community

Greenville is well served for retirees, with 8 continuing-care retirement communities among roughly 28 senior living options overall, spanning independent living through skilled nursing. Notable options include Rolling Green Village, The Woodlands at Furman, and Cascades Verdae, all offering a full continuum of care on one campus. For lifelong learning, OLLI at Furman is one of the largest Osher programs in the country, serving more than 2,500 adults 55+ each year with 130+ courses a semester for a $65 annual membership — a strong signal of the area's civic and volunteer infrastructure for retirees more broadly.

Healthcare & Hospitals

Prisma Health Greenville Memorial Hospital anchors the region and is rated a U.S. News Best Regional Hospital, ranked #1 in the Greenville metro and #5 statewide, with high-performing marks in 10 adult procedures and conditions including cardiology, orthopedics, and colon cancer surgery. It's also been ranked among the nation's top 50 hospitals for gynecology, and Prisma Health facilities across the state earned 2026 U.S. News recognition for maternity care — a solid, comprehensive regional system for a metro this size.

Cost of Living

Asheville runs somewhere between 4% and 11% above the national average depending on the index used, with the gap driven almost entirely by housing: shelter costs run about 13% above average while groceries track roughly even with the rest of the country and transportation actually comes in 8% cheaper thanks to short commutes. Healthcare services run about 15% above the national average, which is worth weighing against the strength of the local hospital system below. A single person's total monthly budget lands around $2,756, roughly 2% higher than a year ago.

Taxes on Retirement Income

North Carolina is notably retiree-friendly on Social Security: it is not taxed at all, regardless of income level. Traditional 401(k), IRA, and most pension withdrawals, however, are taxed as ordinary income under the state's flat 3.99% rate for 2026 — though certain vested government pensions are fully exempt under the Bailey Settlement, and a homestead exemption softens property tax for qualifying residents 65+ with income under about $36,700. On property tax specifically, Buncombe County's 2026 rate is 61.54¢ per $100 of assessed value, and the City of Asheville's adjusted rate is 50.78¢ per $100 — combining to roughly 1.12% effective for in-city properties, notably lower for county-only addresses outside city limits.

Housing Market

The median Asheville home sits in the $462,000–$507,000 range depending on the data source and time window, with some measures showing prices down slightly year-over-year and others up modestly — a market that's cooled from its post-pandemic run but hasn't cracked. Three-bedroom homes are the most common size on the market (roughly 45% of listings) and, per recent tracking, 3- and 4-bedroom houses have actually ticked up about 3% even as the broader median softened. Inventory sits at a lean 3.2-month supply with homes selling in about 45 days on average, so it still leans toward sellers despite the headline price dip.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Asheville's mountain setting long carried a "climate haven" reputation, but Hurricane Helene's catastrophic inland flooding in September 2024 — over 14 inches of rain, deadly flash floods and mudslides, and more than $50 billion in regional damage — exposed real flood and wildfire exposure that FEMA flood maps hadn't captured; less than 1% of the worst-hit inland areas actually carried flood insurance at the time. The home insurance market has tightened in response: the state's rate bureau sought a 42% increase last year and regulators approved 7.5%, and some Western NC policies have shifted roofs to actual-cash-value coverage following Helene-related losses. Buyers should budget for flood insurance even well inland and confirm current homeowners' coverage terms before closing.

Sports Proximity

Asheville has no pro franchise of its own, but Charlotte — home to the Carolina Panthers (NFL) and Charlotte Hornets (NBA), plus MLS and NASCAR — is a straight shot 130 miles / about 2 hours east. Atlanta's Falcons and Braves are roughly 208 miles / 3.5 hours southwest. On the college side, UNC Asheville plays Division I in the Big South right in town, Clemson and Tennessee are each under two hours away, and the Triangle powerhouses (UNC, Duke, NC State) are a manageable 3–3.5 hour drive.

Senior Services & Community

The area is well built-out for retirees: roughly 46 senior housing options span independent living, 55+ active-adult communities, assisted living, and continuing-care retirement communities, with monthly rates generally between $3,000 and $12,890. Givens Gerber Park is a notable example — an urban 55+ rental community with an on-site clinic and community nurse. UNC Asheville also runs continuing-education and lifelong-learning programs popular with retirees, and the city's dense nonprofit and arts scene offers ample volunteer opportunities.

Healthcare & Hospitals

Mission Hospital, the region's flagship (part of HCA Healthcare), is rated a U.S. News Best Regional Hospital and was the only Western North Carolina hospital named to the 2025–2026 Best Hospitals list, ranking among the state's top 10 on patient safety and outcomes. Healthgrades separately named it one of America's 100 Best Hospitals for several specialties including stroke care, coronary intervention, and orthopedic surgery — solid marks for a metro this size.
