Charleston

Charleston County, South Carolina · Historic Lowcountry port city on the Atlantic coast
Population~167,000 city · ~439,000 county · ≈800,000–870,000 metro

ClimateHumid subtropical — mild winters (~50°F), hot humid summers (~90°F), 230 sunny days/yr
Nearest MountainsBlue Ridge foothills (Blue Ridge, GA / Upstate SC) — ~290–350 mi, 6–7 hr drive
Nearest BeachFolly Beach — ~12 mi/25 min; Isle of Palms — ~15 mi/28 min
Nearest AirportCharleston Intl (CHS) — in North Charleston, ~12 mi/20 min; ~56 nonstop destinations, 11 airlines

Crime vs. NationalViolent crime well below average (1 in 274); property crime above average (21/1,000)

Cost of Living

Charleston runs only modestly above the national baseline on most trackers, which surprises people given its reputation: RentCafe and related sources put the overall index around 2–6% above the U.S. average, with Numbeo estimating monthly costs near $2,622 for a single person and about $5,773 for a family of four. A one-bedroom apartment averages roughly $1,700 a month, and utilities run somewhat above the national norm at about $320/month, likely reflecting summer air-conditioning loads and coastal humidity. Healthcare is a genuine bright spot — routine doctor visits and dental care run about 24% cheaper than the U.S. average. As in most of the destinations profiled here, housing is the category that can push a retiree's real cost well above these city-wide averages, particularly on or near the peninsula and barrier islands.

Taxes on Retirement Income

South Carolina does not tax Social Security benefits at all, regardless of income level, per ACTS Retirement. Pension income and 401(k)/IRA withdrawals are only partially taxed: filers 65+ can deduct up to $10,000 of qualifying retirement income, and a separate age-65 deduction of up to $15,000 per person applies against other income, though that age deduction shrinks by whatever retirement-income deduction is claimed to avoid double-counting — in practice the combined shelter lands near $15,000 per retiree rather than $25,000. The top marginal state income-tax rate is about 5.21% for 2026. On property, Charleston County's effective rate runs roughly 0.5–0.53% of market value, and South Carolina's Homestead Exemption knocks $50,000 off the assessed value of a primary residence for anyone 65 or older, disabled, or legally blind, worth an estimated $250–$350 a year, per South Carolina Property Tax Exemptions.

Housing Market

Charleston's housing market has cooled into balance after several white-hot years. Zillow puts the average city home value near $525,534, up about 4.0% year over year, while William Raveis reports a citywide median sale price of $615,000 (May 2026), up a more modest 1% year over year, and Houzeo shows a $625,000 median, up 2.8%. Inventory has risen roughly 5% year over year to about 3.6 months of supply, with active listings more than double 2021 levels — a meaningfully more negotiable market for buyers than a few years ago. Single-family attached homes (a common retiree-scale 2–3 bedroom option) carry a median near $450,000, well below the luxury and downtown/waterfront segment, which lists at $1M and up. Overall, appreciation has slowed to a low single-digit pace rather than reversing.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Hurricanes, storm surge, and increasingly frequent high-tide "nuisance" flooding are Charleston's dominant hazards; NOAA's 2026 Atlantic season outlook calls for a below-average year (8–14 named storms), but the Lowcountry's exposure is structural rather than seasonal, given its low elevation and tidal creeks. Charleston County holds more NFIP flood policies than any other county in South Carolina — over 60,700 — and coastal wind-and-flood coverage combined can run $4,000–$8,000+ a year, per LocatingCHS. South Carolina coastal premiums have risen roughly 30–50% since 2020, driven in part by Hurricane Ian (2022) and Hurricane Florence's 2018 inland flooding. A standalone NFIP flood policy typically adds $700–$1,500 a year on top of a standard homeowners policy, with high-risk zones running well above that. Prospective buyers should confirm flood-zone designation and elevation before making an offer, particularly on or near the peninsula and barrier islands.

Sports Proximity

Charleston has no major-league franchise but a genuinely deep minor-league and college scene: the Charleston RiverDogs (Tampa Bay Rays' Single-A affiliate) play at the riverside Joseph P. Riley Jr. Park, the Charleston Battery compete in USL Championship soccer, and the SC Stingrays play ECHL hockey at a 14,000-seat downtown arena. College sports are a real draw: College of Charleston Cougars field 20 varsity teams in the Coastal Athletic Association, and The Citadel Bulldogs compete in the Southern Conference, sharing Riley Park for baseball. For NFL/NBA-level sports, Charlotte's Panthers and Hornets are roughly a 3.5-hour drive, and Atlanta's four major franchises are about 4.5 hours away, making Charleston more of a college-and-minors town day to day, with occasional road trips for the majors.

Senior Services & Community

Charleston supports three continuing-care retirement communities, according to U.S. News: Bishop Gadsden Episcopal Retirement Community on James Island and Franke at Seaside in Mount Pleasant are the best-known, both offering the full continuum from independent living through memory care, alongside a broader roster of independent and assisted-living options across the metro. For continuing education, the College of Charleston's Center for Creative Retirement runs an Osher-style lifelong-learning program for retired and semi-retired adults, with courses spanning history, biography, and other subjects taught largely by community instructors. Civic and volunteer life leans on the city's dense museum, preservation, and nonprofit sector (Historic Charleston Foundation, the Gibbes Museum, and a large network of church and preservation-society volunteers), typical of a historic tourist city with an engaged retiree population, though a comprehensive volunteer-program roster was not readily available in this research pass.

Healthcare & Hospitals

For the 12th consecutive year, MUSC Health-University Medical Center was ranked the No. 1 hospital in both South Carolina and Charleston in the U.S. News & World Report 2026–27 Best Hospitals rankings, with multiple specialties, including its Obstetrics & Gynecology program, rated High Performing well above the national average. Roper Hospital, part of Roper St. Francis Healthcare, is a Best Regional Hospital ranked No. 3 in South Carolina and No. 2 in Charleston, nationally ranked in one adult specialty and high performing in 13 procedures and conditions including aortic valve surgery, COPD, and heart arrhythmia care. Roper Hospital, Bon Secours St. Francis Hospital, and Roper St. Francis Mount Pleasant Hospital were also named among Newsweek/Statista's America's Best-in-State Hospitals for 2026, giving the metro two well-regarded, competing health systems for a market this size.

Naples

ClimateSubtropical — mild dry winters (mid-70s°F), hot humid summers (upper 80s–90s°F)
Nearest MountainsNone in-state — nearest real hill/hiking terrain is North Georgia (Appalachian foothills), roughly 500+ miles / 8–9 hr drive
Nearest BeachNaples Municipal Beach & Pier — on-site, in downtown Naples
Nearest AirportRSW (Southwest Florida Int'l), ~30 miles/~40 min — ~78 nonstop destinations

Crime vs. NationalViolent crime ~71% below average; property crime ~20% below average

Cost of Living

Naples runs moderately to significantly above the U.S. average depending on the source, reflecting how heavily housing and insurance skew the numbers: BestPlaces puts its cost-of-living index at 113 (13% above the national average), while ERI estimates it closer to 29% above average. Budgeting sites peg a single person's monthly costs (excluding rent) around $1,300–$1,400, and a family of four around $4,900–$5,700 per month including housing, per Wise and Numbeo. Housing and homeowners insurance are consistently cited as the biggest drivers pushing Naples above the national baseline, while the absence of state income tax offsets some of that burden for retirees. Day-to-day essentials like groceries and healthcare services track closer to the national average than housing does.

Taxes on Retirement Income

Florida levies no state income tax, so Social Security benefits, pension income, and 401(k)/IRA distributions are all untaxed at the state level, a point confirmed across SmartAsset and Edelman Financial Engines; there is also no estate or inheritance tax. Property taxes are assessed locally by the Collier County Property Appraiser, and full-time residents qualify for Florida's standard $50,000 homestead exemption on assessed value. Seniors meeting income limits (roughly $38,700 household adjusted gross income for the 2026 exemption year in Collier County) can claim an additional exemption of up to $50,000. Once homesteaded, Florida's Save Our Homes cap limits annual increases in assessed value to 3% or the inflation rate, whichever is lower, which can meaningfully shelter long-time owners from Naples' rising market values. Federal taxes on retirement withdrawals still apply regardless of state residency.

Housing Market

Naples housing prices vary widely by data source and segment: Redfin reported an August 2026 median sale price around $1.2M (down 5.7% year-over-year) while Zillow's broader home-value index sat near $549,000 in June 2026, down 4.7% year-over-year — the gap reflects Redfin's sample skewing toward luxury waterfront sales. For the 2–3 bedroom single-family segment more typical of retiree buyers, local market reports cite median single-family prices near $812,500, up about 1.6% year-over-year even as condos fell roughly 3.4% to a $450,000 median, and per-square-foot prices citywide were down about 10.3% from a year earlier. Overall, the market is described as cooling from its pandemic-era peak, with softer luxury and condo pricing but relative resilience in standard single-family homes. Buyers should expect meaningful variation between beachfront Old Naples and inland/Golden Gate-area homes.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Hurricanes are Naples' dominant natural-hazard risk: Hurricane Ian made a direct, catastrophic hit on Southwest Florida in September 2022, part of a storm that caused over $100 billion in damage nationally, and Collier County has since also absorbed damage from Hurricanes Helene, Milton, and Debby in 2024, per Al Jazeera and local damage reporting. Florida remains the most expensive state in the country for homeowners insurance, with statewide averages cited between roughly $7,560 and $8,460 per year in 2026 (about three times the national rate), according to Insurify. There are early signs of stabilization — state-backed Citizens Property Insurance has shrunk from a 1.4 million-policy peak to roughly 279,000 policies and announced an average 8.7% rate decrease for spring 2026 renewals — but private insurers remain selective and premiums stay well above pre-2022 levels. Prospective retirees should budget for high windstorm/flood premiums and factor in whether a home has updated roofing and hurricane-rated construction, which materially affects insurability and cost.

Sports Proximity

Naples has no major-league franchise of its own, but Miami's teams (Dolphins, Heat, Marlins, and Inter Miami CF) are the closer big-market option, while Tampa Bay's Buccaneers, Rays, and Lightning are reachable via roughly a 166-mile, 2–2.5 hour drive up I-75. Locally, Naples recently gained its own professional team, FC Naples, a soccer club playing at the Paradise Coast Sports Complex. College sports fans have Florida Gulf Coast University (FGCU) Eagles athletics just about 21 miles away in Fort Myers, an NCAA Division I program in the ASUN Conference best known for its "Dunk City" men's basketball run and a record-setting women's basketball program. Spring training baseball is also within reach elsewhere in Southwest Florida for fans wanting a lower-key game-day experience.

Senior Services & Community

Naples has an unusually deep bench of senior housing options given its retiree-heavy demographics, ranging from 55+ manufactured-home communities like Naples Estates to full continuing-care retirement communities (CCRCs). Moorings Park is the area's flagship example, a CCRC spread across roughly 83 acres with about 399 independent-living residences plus its Orchid Terrace assisted-living and memory-care center, located minutes from downtown and the beach, according to U.S. News. Other options include The Glenview at Pelican Bay and Amberlin South Naples, giving prospective retirees a range from resort-style active-adult living to full life-care campuses. The area also supports active civic and volunteer life through organizations tied to its many arts venues (including the Naples Players' Sugden Community Theatre on Fifth Avenue South) and a robust nonprofit and philanthropic sector typical of an affluent retiree community, though a comprehensive lifelong-learning program roster was not readily available in this research pass.

Healthcare & Hospitals

NCH Healthcare System (Naples Comprehensive Health) is the area's dominant health system and was named one of Healthgrades' 2025 America's 50 Best Hospitals, placing it in the top 1% of hospitals nationwide for overall clinical quality. NCH is reportedly the only hospital system in the country to hold Healthgrades' top recognition simultaneously in Cardiac Care, Orthopedic Surgery, and Overall Clinical Excellence, per NCH's own reporting of the award. Its flagship NCH Baker Hospital is also ranked a U.S. News Best Regional Hospital (16th in Florida) and rated "high performing" in 13 adult procedures and conditions, from abdominal aortic aneurysm repair to heart arrhythmia care, according to U.S. News & World Report. Patient recommendation rates run around 85%, and the system operates multiple campuses and outpatient locations across Collier County, giving retirees relatively concentrated, high-quality care close to home for a market this size.

Scottsdale

65+ Population~26% (vs. ~17% nationally); median age 49
ClimateHot desert — very hot dry summers (105°F+), warm sunny winters (~70°F)
Nearest MountainsMcDowell Sonoran Preserve — in city limits, 0–15 mi; 30,500 acres of trails
Nearest BeachSaguaro Lake ~30 mi / 45 min; Pacific coast (San Diego) ~360 mi / 5.5 hr

Cost of Living

Scottsdale is the expensive corner of an otherwise affordable metro. Estimates cluster between 9% and 15% above the U.S. average overall — RentCafe puts it about 15% above national and 10% above the Arizona average, while Redfin lands nearer 9%. Housing does essentially all of the work: shelter costs run roughly 42–53% above the national average, with average rent around $2,033 a month. Groceries, utilities, and transportation sit close to national norms, and Arizona's lack of a grocery tax helps at the margin. Wise pegs monthly costs near $2,705 for a single person and about $5,956 for a family of four — meaning a retiree who buys or owns outright absorbs the premium up front rather than month to month.

Taxes on Retirement Income

Arizona is one of the more retiree-friendly tax states in the West. Social Security benefits are fully exempt from state tax, and military retirement pay is fully exempt as well; everything else — private pensions, 401(k) and traditional IRA withdrawals — is taxed at a flat 2.5%, among the lowest rates in any state that taxes income at all. Government and Arizona-subdivision pensions get an additional subtraction of up to $2,500, and for 2026 filers 65 and older can claim a $6,000 senior bonus deduction ($12,000 for a couple both over 65). Property taxes are genuinely low: Maricopa County's effective rate averages roughly 0.45–0.48% of market value, so an $850,000 Scottsdale home runs about $3,800–$4,100 a year. Owners 65+ who meet the income test (about $46,416 for a single owner) can lock in their assessed valuation through the Senior Property Valuation Protection Option, which also enrolls them automatically in the state's Elderly Assistance Fund.

Housing Market

Scottsdale is the priciest large-city housing market in Arizona and has cooled from frenzy to steady appreciation. Zillow puts the average home value at about $854,000, up 2.3% year over year, while Redfin reports a median sale price near $959,000, up 8.4% — the spread reflects Redfin's sale-weighted mix skewing toward the luxury end. The market splits sharply by geography: North Scottsdale's golf and gated enclaves carry a median near $1.3 million, while South Scottsdale near Old Town and Tempe runs closer to $880,000. On the 2–3 bedroom segment specifically, local market reporting shows roughly +4.3% appreciation over the trailing twelve months (about $928K in July 2025 to $968K in June 2026), with entry-level 3-bedrooms in 85257 South Scottsdale starting near $675,000, central 85250/85258 running $850,000–$1,150,000, and North Scottsdale 3-bedrooms at $1,150,000–$1,450,000. Forecasts for the balance of the decade generally call for a moderate 4–6% annual pace rather than a repeat of the 2021 spike.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Scottsdale trades hurricane and earthquake exposure for heat, fire, and flash flood. ClimateCheck rates heat risk as extreme, projecting residents will average roughly 47 days a year above 109°F by 2050 — a genuine health consideration for older buyers, not just a comfort issue. Wildfire risk is high for about 70% of buildings, concentrated in the desert-interface neighborhoods along the northern and eastern edges, and roughly 78% of buildings carry meaningful flood risk from monsoon-season downbursts (June–September) that overwhelm washes and low-lying streets. The insurance market has tightened accordingly: Arizona homeowners are seeing 20–40% rate increases in 2026, with some individual renewals jumping 20–50% in a single year, and insurers are actively re-mapping wildfire zones around the metro's outer suburbs. Phoenix-area premiums now average roughly $1,450–$2,400 a year, and non-renewal rates have reached as high as 4.8% in some Arizona counties — worth checking a specific address's wildfire designation before making an offer.

Sports Proximity

Few retirement destinations match Scottsdale for sports access. All of Arizona's major franchises are inside a 35-minute drive: the Phoenix Suns (NBA) and Arizona Diamondbacks (MLB) play downtown about 13 miles away, the Phoenix Mercury (WNBA) share Footprint Center, and the Arizona Cardinals (NFL) are roughly 30 miles west in Glendale. The NHL's Coyotes left for Utah in 2024, so hockey is the one gap. Scottsdale's signature draw is Cactus League spring training — Scottsdale Stadium hosts the San Francisco Giants downtown, Salt River Fields at Talking Stick sits minutes east, and thirteen more clubs train within the metro each February and March. Arizona State's 26 Big 12 teams are about 8 miles south in Tempe, and the PGA Tour's WM Phoenix Open at TPC Scottsdale draws the largest galleries in golf.

Senior Services & Community

With a quarter of the city already 65 or older, the senior infrastructure is deep — dozens of 55+ active-adult communities, independent living campuses, and assisted living options operate in Scottsdale, and four continuing-care retirement communities serve the city directly, with two ranked by U.S. News. Vi at Silverstone is the flagship example: a Type A CCRC in North Scottsdale on a 165-acre master-planned site, offering independent living through memory and skilled nursing care with predictable lifetime pricing. For continuing education, the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute at ASU runs university-quality short courses for adults 50 and better, including a Peace Corps partnership program. The city's Granite Reef and Via Linda senior centers anchor civic and volunteer life, and the greater Phoenix nonprofit and museum scene absorbs a large retiree volunteer corps.

Healthcare & Hospitals

Healthcare is arguably Scottsdale's strongest card. Mayo Clinic–Arizona, whose main hospital campus sits in northeast Phoenix on the Scottsdale line, is the state's No. 1 hospital for the thirteenth consecutive year, is on the U.S. News Best Hospitals Honor Roll, and is nationally ranked in 10 adult specialties while rated high performing in 22 procedures and conditions. Locally, HonorHealth Scottsdale Shea Medical Center ranks #2 in the Phoenix metro with high-performing ratings in 16 adult procedures, and HonorHealth Scottsdale Thompson Peak in North Scottsdale ranks #4. For the 2026–27 edition U.S. News evaluated 120 Arizona hospitals and named just seven Best Regional Hospitals — three of them serve Scottsdale residents directly, an unusual concentration of top-tier care for a city this size.

Cost of Living

Hilton Head is an expensive place to live by South Carolina standards, though estimates vary widely by methodology: USCostOfLiving puts the overall index at 133 (33% above the U.S. average) and Salary.com at 139, while BestPlaces scores it far lower at 104.3. Housing is the dominant driver of the gap — median home value on the island runs roughly $650,000–$790,000 against a national figure near $360,000. Non-housing categories (groceries, utilities, healthcare) sit much closer to national norms, so a retiree who already owns outright feels the premium far less than a new buyer. The practical takeaway: budget generously for housing and insurance, and treat everything else as roughly average. Note that the island skews resort-priced on dining and services relative to nearby Bluffton and the mainland.

Taxes on Retirement Income

South Carolina is among the friendlier states for retirees: it does not tax Social Security benefits at all, and residents 65+ can deduct up to $10,000 of pension/401(k)/IRA income plus a broader $15,000 per-person deduction against other income (ACTS Retirement). Income above those exclusions is taxed on a graduated scale topping out around 5.2% for the current tax year, with roughly 4.4% applying to most middle-bracket retirement withdrawals (Taxstra). Property taxes are the real advantage: Beaufort County's effective rate is roughly 0.48% of market value, with a median annual bill near $2,473 — among the lowest in the state. Owner-occupants are assessed at 4% rather than the 6% second-home/investor ratio, a large distinction on a resort island, and residents 65+ qualify for the state's $50,000 Homestead Exemption. Establishing legal primary residency is therefore the single highest-value tax move for a Hilton Head retiree.

Housing Market

The island's market has flattened after several years of sharp appreciation. Zillow pegs the average home value at about $789,690 as of mid-2026, up 2.9% year over year, while Houzeo reports a median sale price near $801,250 — essentially flat (0%) against last year. Detached single-family listings, which is where most 2–3 bedroom inventory sits, are running considerably higher, with one mid-2026 read putting the median single-family price around $1,025,000; the more attainable 2–3 bedroom stock is concentrated in villas and condos in Shipyard, Palmetto Dunes and Hilton Head Plantation, where prices have been roughly flat to modestly up. Inventory has loosened to about a four-month supply, homes are taking ~54 days to sell (up 9% YoY), and sellers are netting about 95.9% of ask — a meaningfully more negotiable market than 2022–2023. Local forecasts call for 2–4% appreciation through 2026. Buyers looking for materially lower entry prices generally cross the bridge to Bluffton.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

This is Hilton Head's biggest asterisk. As a low-lying Atlantic barrier island, it faces hurricanes, storm surge, tidal and rainfall flooding, and secondary tornado risk — Hurricane Matthew (2016) forced a full evacuation and caused extensive tree and structural damage, and most of the island sits inside FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas. Home insurance is priced accordingly: Insurify puts the average Hilton Head premium at roughly $6,139/year for $300K dwelling coverage, versus about $3,153 statewide — roughly double. That figure is typically before separate windstorm/hail and flood policies, which most island properties require; NFIP flood premiums locally average in the $700–$800 range but scale sharply with elevation and zone. Fewer carriers actively write on the coast, so shopping the market and confirming an elevation certificate before making an offer matters. Realistically, budget $9,000–$12,000+ annually for full coverage on a typical island home.

Sports Proximity

Hilton Head has no resident major-league franchise, but it hosts one of the PGA Tour's signature events: the RBC Heritage each April at Harbour Town Golf Links in Sea Pines, a designated signature event drawing top-ranked fields. For everyday spectating, the Savannah Bananas are about 35 miles / under an hour away, and Savannah also offers Georgia Southern and Savannah State athletics. The nearest true major-league options are the Jacksonville Jaguars (NFL) at about 172 miles / 2h40m, Charlotte's Panthers and Hornets at roughly 247 miles / 3h50m, and Atlanta's Braves, Falcons, Hawks and Atlanta United at about 4 hours. College football fans are within reasonable range of both South Carolina (Columbia, ~3 hr) and Clemson (~4.5 hr). Day to day, the island itself is built around golf (30+ courses), tennis/pickleball, boating and cycling on 60+ miles of paths.

Senior Services & Community

With roughly four in ten residents over 65, the retiree infrastructure here is deep. The island has three full continuing-care retirement communities — The Cypress of Hilton Head, TidePointe (a Vi community), and The Seabrook of Hilton Head — plus roughly a half-dozen additional independent and assisted living options and a broader set of 55+ active-adult neighborhoods across Hilton Head and neighboring Bluffton (Sun City Hilton Head, at ~9,000 homes, is the region's largest, about 20 minutes off-island). For lifelong learning, the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute at USC Beaufort runs 1,000+ annual members aged 50+ through short courses taught by volunteer instructors, with in-person classes on the Hilton Head Island, Bluffton and Beaufort campuses plus online offerings. Volunteer outlets are plentiful — the Coastal Discovery Museum, Sea Turtle Patrol HHI, Second Helpings food rescue, Volunteers in Medicine, and the Community Foundation of the Lowcountry all draw heavily on retiree labor. The tradeoff is a seasonal, somewhat transient population and heavy spring/summer tourist traffic.

Healthcare & Hospitals

The island's primary facility is Hilton Head Hospital, part of Novant Health's Hilton Head Medical Center, a general medical and surgical hospital rated by U.S. News as high performing in three adult procedures and conditions, including heart failure, hip replacement and spinal fusion. Healthgrades named it one of America's 50 Best Hospitals for Vascular Surgery for 2026, and it carries an American Heart Association Get With The Guidelines Gold Plus stroke award. Beaufort Memorial Hospital in Beaufort (~45 min) and Memorial Health / St. Joseph's Candler in Savannah (~45 min) provide additional tertiary capacity, including higher-acuity trauma and cancer care. Specialist density is good for a community this size precisely because of the retiree-heavy demographic, though wait times lengthen in peak season. For the most complex cases, MUSC in Charleston is about two hours north.

Sarasota

Cost of Living

Sarasota is modestly more expensive than the country as a whole rather than dramatically so: BestPlaces puts the overall cost-of-living index at 105.3, about 5% above the US average. Housing does most of the lifting — roughly 13% above the national norm on the BestPlaces measure, with other trackers putting the gap far wider depending on whether they weight the barrier-island market. Day-to-day categories are much closer to par: groceries about 7% above average, healthcare 5%, utilities and transportation each around 2%. AreaVibes and Redfin's calculator both land in a similar band. The practical read: retirees who buy inland rather than on Siesta or Longboat Key can live here for close to national-average money, with insurance the main wildcard.

Taxes on Retirement Income

Florida is about as favorable as US retirement taxation gets. There is no state income tax at all, so Social Security, private and public pensions, annuity payments, and traditional 401(k)/IRA withdrawals are all untaxed at the state level; Roth distributions and investment income likewise escape state tax. Federal taxation of Social Security still applies, and you must actually establish domicile — file a Declaration of Domicile, get a Florida license, register to vote — to claim the benefit. The trade-off arrives on the property tax bill: Sarasota County's median effective rate runs about 1.06% of market value, against which homeowners get the standard Florida homestead exemption plus the Save Our Homes assessment cap. Sarasota County's Limited Income Senior Exemption adds up to $50,000 more for homesteaded residents 65+, but only if household adjusted gross income stays under $38,686 for 2026 — a threshold most retirees profiled here would exceed.

Housing Market

This is a buyer's market for the first time in years. Zillow puts the typical Sarasota home value near $462,000, down about 1.1% year over year, while Sarasota County as a whole sits around $399,000 and is off roughly 5.5%. Redfin shows a higher median sale price (mix-shifted upward by waterfront closings) but confirms the slowdown: homes took about 92 days to sell this July versus 75 a year earlier. For the mainstream 2–3 bedroom segment the trend is clearly downward — Houzeo pegs the overall median around $515,000 and falling double digits year over year, with roughly two months of supply and about a quarter of county listings having taken a price cut. Buyers today have negotiating leverage that did not exist in 2022–23; sellers of ordinary inland three-bedrooms are the ones absorbing the correction.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Hurricane and storm surge are the dominant risks, and Sarasota learned that the hard way in 2024 when Hurricane Milton shifted south of its forecast Tampa Bay landfall and came ashore near Siesta Key weeks after Helene, flooding Siesta, Lido and Longboat and causing damage in the hundreds of millions countywide. Low-lying barrier islands, extensive bay and Gulf frontage, and inland creeks mean wind, surge and flood exposure all stack on the same parcels; FEMA rates Sarasota County's overall risk index as relatively high. The insurance market reflects it — Florida premiums rose about 14% year over year in 2025 per the state Office of Insurance Regulation after roughly 300,000 Helene and Milton claims, and Insurify projects average annual Florida coverage costs well into five figures. Several national carriers have exited or stopped renewing coastal policies, leaving state-backed Citizens as the fallback for many island and near-shore homes, and separate NFIP or private flood coverage is effectively mandatory near the water.

Sports Proximity

Baseball is the local strength. The Baltimore Orioles hold spring training at Ed Smith Stadium inside the city itself, the Pittsburgh Pirates train at LECOM Park in Bradenton about 13 miles north, and the Tampa Bay Rays' spring home at Charlotte Sports Park is roughly 44 miles south — three Grapefruit League camps within an easy drive. For regular-season pro sports the anchor is Tampa Bay, about an hour up I-75: the Buccaneers at Raymond James Stadium, the Lightning at Amalie Arena, and the Rays in St. Petersburg. College athletics are thinner locally — New College of Florida and USF Sarasota–Manatee are small-scale, and IMG Academy in Bradenton is the notable elite-youth venue — so Division I fans generally drive to USF in Tampa. Golf, tennis and rowing (Nathan Benderson Park hosts national and international regattas) fill the participatory side.

Senior Services & Community

Sarasota is built around its retirees, with 29% of city residents already 65 or older. Roughly eight continuing-care retirement communities operate in Sarasota County, alongside dozens of independent living, assisted living and 55+ options; Plymouth Harbor on Longboat Key, Bay Village of Sarasota on Vamo Road, and Aviva Senior Living — the area's only single-site not-for-profit offering the full continuum of care — are the best-known names. Lifelong learning is unusually strong for a city this size: the Ringling College Lifelong Learning Academy and the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute at Ringling College, based at the Sarasota Art Museum campus, run hundreds of courses taught largely by retired professionals who moved here. That volunteer-taught model doubles as the area's most accessible civic on-ramp, supplemented by the Ringling Museum, Asolo Repertory Theatre, Marie Selby Botanical Gardens and a dense nonprofit sector that runs on retiree labor.

Healthcare & Hospitals

The dominant provider is Sarasota Memorial Health Care System, a public system whose flagship 900-plus bed hospital sits minutes from downtown. In the 2026–27 U.S. News & World Report Best Hospitals rankings it was named a Best Regional Hospital, ranked roughly 11th in Florida and first in the Sarasota–Bradenton metro, with national recognition across nearly 20 conditions and procedures. It has also held the top 5-star quality rating from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services for eleven consecutive years, and its Venice campus earned its first 5-star rating in 2026 — giving the system two 5-star hospitals. Healthgrades has separately placed it on its America's 250 Best Hospitals list with specialty awards in cardiac surgery, pulmonary and critical care. For a metro of this size, that is a genuinely strong healthcare anchor, backed by deep Medicare-oriented specialist supply.

Cost of Living

Galway runs meaningfully cheaper than Dublin and roughly on par with Cork: Numbeo puts total monthly costs around €2,514 versus Dublin's €3,043, with a single person's expenses excluding rent estimated near €1,019/month. Rent is the biggest line item for newcomers — a one-bedroom apartment in the city centre runs roughly €1,700–€1,813/month per Wise, though retirees buying outright avoid that cost entirely. Day-to-day essentials (groceries, transit, dining) track close to the Irish national average, and the compact, walkable core keeps transportation costs low for residents who don't need a car.

Taxes on Retirement Income

Under the US-Ireland tax treaty, US Social Security paid to an Irish resident is exempt from US withholding and taxed only in Ireland, subject to normal Irish exemption limits. Private pension and traditional 401(k)/IRA withdrawals fall under Article 17 of the treaty and are likewise taxed only in the country of residence — Ireland — via Irish Income Tax and the Universal Social Charge (not PRSI), with a US Foreign Tax Credit available to prevent double taxation. There's no dedicated Irish retirement visa; American retirees typically apply for Stamp 0 residency, which requires roughly €50,000/year in income per person plus private medical insurance. On property tax, Ireland's self-assessed Local Property Tax (LPT) uses market-value bands that were widened 20% for 2026, with local authorities able to adjust the base rate by up to ±15%; Galway City Council's specific local adjustment factor was not readily available.

Housing Market

The median house price across County Galway hit €380,000 in 2026, up 5.6% year-over-year and about 1% below the national median, according to HousePrice.ie. The broader Connacht-Ulster region (which includes Galway) is running hotter than the national trend, with price inflation near 8.8% amid persistently tight supply, even as list-price growth has cooled nationally to 3.8%. Premium city neighborhoods like Taylors Hill command a median of €755,000. A breakout specifically for 2–3 bedroom homes was not readily available, but with supply this constrained, that segment is likely tracking close to or above the county-wide pace.

Natural Disaster Risk & Insurance

Coastal flooding is Galway's dominant hazard: geospatial analysis cited by the Connacht Tribune found nearly 13% of the city's population living in areas vulnerable to coastal flooding, with roughly 7,500 people in high-risk zones around Salthill alone, and climate scientists warning a major storm surge is a matter of time as sea levels rise. Nationally, about 1 in 20 Irish homes struggle to secure flood cover, and standard homeowners policies typically cover storm and fire but exclude flooding in designated risk zones, making dedicated flood cover (checked via the OPW's flood mapping service) an important step before buying near the coast.

Sports Proximity

Galway is a genuine sporting hub for a city its size. Connacht Rugby, competing in the United Rugby Championship and Champions Cup, plays home matches at The Sportsground, a few minutes' walk from the city centre. Pearse Stadium in Salthill hosts Galway's GAA hurling and football intercounty games each spring and summer, and in 2026 it's also serving as the temporary home ground for League of Ireland side Galway United while their usual pitch undergoes drainage work. For those wanting Ireland's biggest stages, Dublin's national stadiums are a roughly 2.5-hour drive east.

Senior Services & Community

Galway has an established expat and retiree scene, drawing on more than 80 nationalities in residence and active Irish-American community groups tied to its sister-city relationship with Providence, Rhode Island. Nationwide organizations like Active Retirement Ireland, Age Action Ireland, and the Irish Senior Citizens' Parliament run local chapters offering walking groups, book clubs, and civic engagement for older residents. Specific continuing-education programming through the University of Galway aimed at retirees was not readily available in this week's research, but the city's dense festival, arts, and community-centre calendar provides ample volunteer and social opportunities year-round.

Healthcare & Hospitals

University Hospital Galway, run by the Saolta University Health Care Group, is a Model 4 tertiary hospital offering 24/7 emergency and acute care alongside specialist cancer, cardiac, and neuroscience services — the strongest option for retirees outside Dublin. Patient reviews are mixed: many praise the specialist surgical, dermatology, and urology teams, but long A&E waiting times are a recurring complaint. A JCI accreditation ranking for the hospital was not readily available; as a public HSE facility, most routine and specialist care would run through Ireland's public system, which private supplemental insurance (required for Stamp 0 residency) can help supplement.
