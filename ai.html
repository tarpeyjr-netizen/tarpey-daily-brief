SAP Joule

This Week in SAP Joule

Joule is the AI helper built into SAP, the software that big companies use to run things like payroll, shipping and buying supplies. It has been a quiet few days, so we stretched our look back to two weeks, and the big moment turned out to be SAP’s TechEd conference in late October.

At TechEd, SAP upgraded Joule Studio and Joule Work, along with the platform underneath them. Think of Joule Studio as a workshop where a company builds its own little AI workers, and Joule Work as the manager that makes sure those workers cooperate. The new tools make it easier to build and run those agents, and add stronger controls so a company can keep an eye on what they are doing. Some early customers can even connect n8n workflows, a popular tool for chaining tasks together, with wider access promised later.

The catch is patience. SAP says the new pieces are rolling out in phases, with several arriving as late as the first quarter of 2027. So for now this is a preview of where SAP is heading: an AI that does the paperwork, not one that just chats about it.

GitHub Copilot

This Week in GitHub Copilot

GitHub Copilot is the AI pair programmer that lives inside the tools developers use to write code. Nothing new landed in the last seven days, but GitHub’s Universe conference on October 28 was a big one, so we looked back two weeks.

The headline was Copilot Fusion reaching general availability. Instead of making you pick which AI model to use for each job, Fusion picks for you, like a taxi dispatcher choosing the closest car. GitHub is also giving paid plans a 30 percent discount on Fusion’s model costs through the end of November. The Copilot app also gained Projects in public preview, a way to keep an AI agent’s work organized, plus a computer-use feature on macOS and Windows that lets Copilot operate desktop apps, with your approval first. Agent Merge on pull requests is coming for paid plans too.

Behind the shiny features is a telling number: GitHub says developers started more than 40 percent of pull requests with an agent in September, up from 17 percent in March. Billing for cloud sandbox usage also starts this month, so expect the way Copilot is paid for to be a topic in the weeks ahead.

🔍 Tool Spotlight

Kimi K3.1 Pro

Kimi K3.1 Pro is the newest AI model from Moonshot AI, a Chinese company behind the Kimi assistant. It arrived on October 23 and you can try it in the Kimi app, in its coding tool called Kimi Code, and through an API, which is the doorway developers use to plug a model into their own apps. Like other top models, it can answer questions, write and fix code, and work through multi-step problems.

Why does it matter? The AI race is no longer just a few American labs. Strong models from companies like Moonshot give developers more choices and put pressure on prices. The surprising part is the promise attached to this release: Moonshot said it would publish the model’s open weights by November 4. Open weights are basically the model’s trained brain, which anyone can download and run themselves. We could not confirm whether that actually happened, so treat it as a promise until you see the files. Programmers, tinkerers and researchers are the people most likely to care, since they can inspect, tweak and build on an open model in ways a closed one never allows.

Anthropic Claude

This Week in Claude

Claude is the AI assistant made by Anthropic, and this week the company made headlines for something that has nothing to do with chatting: it asked the public to invest. On October 26, Anthropic filed paperwork with the US government for an initial public offering, which is the moment a private company starts selling shares on the stock market. Think of it like a lemonade stand that has grown so huge it is letting the whole neighborhood buy a piece.

The filing is not the finish line. Details like the share price and how many shares will be sold are still blank, and reports say the earliest the sales pitch to investors can begin is around November 10. Meanwhile, Anthropic kept busy on the science side. It announced it is working with 20 research groups and foundations on its AI for Science push, and shared early results from partnerships with six organizations that develop medicines. It also struck a deal with the US government's buying agency, giving public-sector customers a 40 percent discount on Claude products.

One more nerdy but important move: Anthropic is donating its “Agent Skills” standard to a neutral group called the Agentic AI Foundation. Skills are like little instruction booklets that teach an AI how to do a specific job, and handing the format to a neutral group means other companies can use the same booklets. The big question now is whether the IPO spotlight changes how fast Anthropic ships new things.

Google Gemini

This Week in Google Gemini

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, the one that lives in your phone, your search bar and your browser. This past week it felt less like a chatbot and more like a whole workshop, because Google kept dropping new tools almost every single day.

The headline is a new top-of-the-line model called Gemini 4 Carbon, announced on October 28. Google calls it its strongest model yet, with a big focus on cybersecurity and on “long-horizon” work, which just means jobs that take many steps over a long stretch of time, like a student working through a giant science project instead of answering one quiz question. It followed hot on the heels of showcase posts for Gemini 4 Argon, a sibling model, with Google highlighting everything from deep research to building interactive designs. Then, on October 29, came the feature most people will actually touch: Gemini can now create, preview and edit documents, PDFs, presentations and spreadsheets in a full-screen editor. Think of it as asking a very fast classmate to draft your slides and then tweaking them right there on the page.

Google also leaned hard into school. A new student hub got “immersive explorations” for diving into a topic, and Google shared how college students use Gemini to study and take notes. A new place to build and manage Connected Apps, which let Gemini reach into other services, arrived too, and so did XREAL AURA, wired glasses running Android XR that work with Gemini. The big picture: Google wants Gemini to be the thing you reach for to make stuff, not just ask questions, and the next few weeks will show how well Carbon holds up when everyone gets to try it.

🔍 Tool Spotlight

Jais 3

Most big AI models are built mainly around English, and they can feel a bit clumsy in other languages. Jais 3 is a different kind of project. It is a family of six open AI models built to be truly at home in Arabic, released in mid-October by the Institute of Foundation Models at MBZUAI in Abu Dhabi and a company called Inception42, with chip maker Cerebras helping out. Think of it as a whole toolbox instead of one tool: the models run from a tiny 0.9 billion parameters, small enough for a phone, up to a giant 375 billion parameter version meant for serious GPU clusters. There is also Jais Speech, which turns spoken Arabic into text.

What makes it interesting is that these models are meant to act like assistants, not just chatbots. They can plan several steps, search the web, and work with documents, spreadsheets and slides in Arabic. The makers say the 7 billion parameter model jumped from a score of 56.4 to 79.6 on their Arabic tests, though that is their own testing. Schools, businesses and developers across the Arab world are the obvious users, and because the weights are reportedly open, anyone can study or build on them. The surprising part is how it was made: it adapts an earlier model family called K2 Horizon, showing that one strong base can be taught new languages.

Source: HPCwire / MBZUAI and Inception42 press release, October 2026
