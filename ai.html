Anthropic Claude Code

This Week in Anthropic Claude Code

Claude Code is Anthropic’s coding helper that lives in your terminal. You describe what you want, and it reads your project, writes the code, and runs the commands. It doesn’t do big launch events. It ships small updates every few days, and this week brought three of them, one with a brand-new brain inside.

The big one came on November 9. Version 2.1.321 made Claude Fable 5.2, the model Anthropic announced that same day, the new default Fable model in Claude Code. It can hold a 1-million-token context window, which is roughly like keeping a whole shelf of textbooks open at once, so it can see a lot of your project at the same time. That update also closed several security gaps. One let login secrets for connected tools follow a redirect to a different website. Another let a file copied with cp or mv slip past rules meant to block it. Those are the kind of quiet fixes you never notice, which is exactly the point. A few days earlier, on November 6, version 2.1.319 let tasks from connected tools run in the background and report back when they finish, like starting the laundry and getting a ping when it’s done. It also added a setting that simply says “no” instead of stopping to ask permission, and the /usage screen started showing spending totals for teams. Then on November 10, version 2.1.322 brought “fast mode” to Google’s Vertex cloud in the global, US and EU regions. If Vertex turns it down, Claude Code just drops back to normal speed.

The so-what is that the tool is getting a stronger brain and tighter locks at the same time. One date to watch is November 30, when fast mode for the older Opus 4.8 model switches off for good. Anthropic is pointing people to Opus 5.5 instead.

Xai Grok

This Week in Xai Grok

Grok is the AI from xAI, the Elon Musk company that’s now part of SpaceX. Most people know Grok as a chatbot. But lately xAI has been pushing Grok Bot, a helper that actually does things for you, like booking, shopping and keeping track of tasks. This week Grok Bot officially grew up.

Grok Bot 1.0 arrived on or around November 11, according to Crypto Briefing’s report, about three months after the public test version opened in August. The big change is that a bot no longer has to belong to just one person. A whole team can share one, the way a group project shares one notebook. It now works inside Slack and Microsoft Teams. It can even join video calls on Zoom, Google Meet and Teams, where it follows the conversation, keeps track of deadlines and decisions, and flags anything left unfinished. If something needs a human, it can pass it along to a teammate. There’s a catch, though. Shared bots need a Team or Enterprise plan, and joining calls needs the pricier Ultra plan. Meanwhile, Grok Build, xAI’s coding tool for the terminal, got another small update on November 5. Version 1.0.77 stopped connections to outside tools from restarting for no reason and fixed a meter that showed how much the AI could still remember, which sometimes dropped to zero after you rewound a session.

The bigger picture is that xAI wants Grok to feel less like a chat window and more like a coworker who sits in your meetings. Whether teams trust an AI that listens in on every call is the real test, and the next few weeks of real-world use will tell.

Artlist AI

This Week in Artlist AI

Artlist started out as a library of music and video clips that creators could use legally. Now it’s also a one-stop AI studio, where you can create images, video, voiceovers and music using lots of different AI models in one place. It was a fairly quiet week, with no big product launch. Still, a few things are worth knowing.

On November 5, Artlist published a guide to its AI plans, and the key idea is “Unlimited” models. On some yearly plans, you can keep generating with certain models without spending credits, within limits on things like resolution and clip length. The AI Core plan unlocks 8 of these models, AI Creator unlocks 18, and Max Business unlocks 24, including xAI’s Grok Imagine. Around the same time, a pricing tracker spotted a new promise on Artlist’s pricing page: customer data will never be used to train AI models. That matters if you don’t want your work feeding someone else’s AI. And on November 9, the Artlist blog told the story of KOMPAN, a Danish company that builds playgrounds. It used Artlist’s AI video tools to make a launch film for a toy Porsche playground car that wasn’t in any playground yet. In the film, a young girl drives it on a pretend road trip to places like Italy’s Dolomites, built from roughly five-second AI clips.

The takeaway is that Artlist is spending this stretch explaining and proving its tools rather than launching new ones. Its last fresh feature was Projects on November 2, a new top layer for organizing folders. For anyone curious about making videos with AI, the clearer pricing and the no-training promise make it a little easier to know what you’re signing up for.

ElevenLabs

This Week in ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs is the company best known for AI voices that sound uncannily human, but this week it was all about the opposite job: listening. On November 10 it launched Scribe v3 and Scribe v3 Live, two new models that turn speech into text. One handles recordings, like a podcast you upload. The other works in real time, like live captions during a call.

The numbers are the story here. ElevenLabs says Scribe v3 makes 27 percent fewer word mistakes than the version before it, tested across 102 languages. The live version cuts “meaning-changing” mistakes by 43 percent, which matters because hearing “can’t” as “can” is a lot worse than missing a comma. The live model can now also tell who is talking in real time, and it can follow you if you switch languages mid-sentence. The biggest jumps came in trickier languages, with Greek improving by 77 percent in live mode. One energy company said its phone agent got Dutch postcodes right on the first try 40 percent more often. The same day, Adobe named ElevenLabs as one of the partners whose models creators can use inside Firefly, so its voices are showing up in even more places.

Why should you care? Every voice assistant is only as smart as its ears. If the AI mishears you, even a brilliant answer is an answer to the wrong question. Better listening means smoother customer-service bots, better captions and fewer “sorry, I didn’t catch that” moments. Paired with the voice upgrades listed in its November 2 changelog, ElevenLabs is clearly building toward AI you can just talk to.

Adobe Firefly

This Week in Adobe Firefly

Adobe Firefly is Adobe’s AI studio for making and editing images, video and designs, and this was its biggest week of the year. Adobe MAX, the company’s giant creative conference, kicked off in Miami Beach on November 10, and Firefly got a huge pile of upgrades.

The headline is the Creative Agent, which powers the Firefly AI Assistant, still in beta. You tell it what you want in plain words, and it gets to work. A new Plan Mode lets you look over the agent’s plan before it starts, like checking a recipe before someone cooks for you. A Model Selector lets you choose which AI model does the work, or lets Firefly pick. Adobe is also previewing “always-on” agents that handle boring chores in the background, like sorting files. For hands-on editing, Firefly Image now has Edit Object, Edit Text, Relight and Retouch, so you can change one part of a picture without wrecking the rest. Edit Text, Relight, Retouch and ready-made Quick Actions like Remove Object are available now. New beta tools include Firefly Design, one canvas for drawing, animation, 3D and print, and Firefly Graph for building reusable workflows. Firefly Builder, in private beta, turns a plain-English idea into a clickable prototype of a website or app with no code. Firefly also added outside models like Kling 4.0, Nano Banana 2.1 and GPT Image 2.6.

The bigger shift is that Adobe is opening its doors. According to Adobe’s main MAX announcement, new connectors in a preview let other companies’ AI agents edit real Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects files, with every layer still editable. MAX runs through November 12, so watch for hands-on reviews telling us whether all these agents actually save creators time.

🔍 Tool Spotlight

MiniMax H3-Image

H3-Image is a brand-new AI image model from MiniMax, a Chinese company that also runs the Hailuo AI video app. It launched on November 11, and it can create pictures from a text description, edit pictures you already have, and borrow style or details from up to 16 reference images at once. That last part is handy if you want the same character to look consistent across a whole comic. MiniMax says it can make huge 4K images in under 10 seconds, and that it tries to avoid the plastic, overly smooth look that gives away so many AI pictures. The surprising part is that the weights are open. Weights are the trained brain of the model, and MiniMax put them on Hugging Face so anyone with a powerful enough computer can download and run it. That matters because strong open models give artists and developers choices beyond the big paid apps. MiniMax also says its prices run about half to two-thirds of comparable services. One catch: the launch post doesn’t say which license comes with the weights, so check the fine print before building anything serious with it.

Anthropic Claude

This Week in Anthropic Claude

Claude is the AI assistant from Anthropic, the one that chats, writes, codes and increasingly does work on its own. This week the company kept the releases coming, and the biggest one landed just yesterday: Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, new models Anthropic says are built for the hardest technical tasks.

Think of it like a car maker rolling out a faster engine while also adding new features to the dashboard. The same day, Claude Enterprise got more visibility and control for security teams, which is the boring-but-important stuff that makes big companies comfortable letting an AI near their data. A day earlier, Anthropic filled its Claude Marketplace with new agents and products for security, productivity and creative work, a bit like an app store for AI helpers. And back on November 2, developers got a quiet but clever upgrade: long conversations in the Messages API now send only the new content each turn instead of the whole history again, which saves time and money.

The pattern is clear. Anthropic is pushing Claude from a thing you chat with toward a thing that handles real jobs, whether that is your budget or a company's security dashboard. Keep an eye on how people react to the 5.2 models over the next few days, because that is where the “wow, it can do that now” stories usually start.

Perplexity AI

This Week in Perplexity AI

Perplexity is the AI-powered answer engine that searches the web and hands you a summary with sources, instead of a list of blue links. This week it tried to become more than a search box by launching Teammates, AI agents meant to do ongoing work rather than answer one question and disappear.

Here is how it works. You give a Teammate a job, such as watching bug reports, tracking numbers, or researching a market. It plans the work, does it, and brings results back for you to check, then keeps monitoring afterward. Big goals can be split among extra agents, each running on its own isolated computer, and agents in the same project can share files and message each other like coworkers in a group chat. They plug into Slack, GitHub, Linear and Gmail, and you can jump in and take over whenever you like. You can pick which AI model powers them, and new Teammates default to Claude Opus 5.5. For now it is a research preview on the web for individual Max subscribers only, and heavier work burns through monthly credits faster. Reports also mention a smarter Comet Assistant in the company’s browser, announced around November 6, though sources disagree on the timing of a related patent-search feature.

The big idea is that Perplexity wants to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic on workplace automation, not just search. Teammates is early and pricey, so expect it to change a lot before it reaches everyone. Perplexity also has a legal fight with Dow Jones and the New York Post still going, which is worth watching.

Microsoft Copilot

This Week in Microsoft Copilot

Think of Microsoft Copilot as the AI helper that lives inside the apps millions of people already use for work and school, like Word, Excel and PowerPoint. This month the story is less about a brand-new trick and more about a big move-in day: the pieces Microsoft unveiled on September 25 are now rolling out.

That September redesign rebuilt the Copilot app around three ideas. Home blends everyday chat with “Cowork,” where the AI actually does tasks for you; Code helps you build apps; and Autopilot is an always-on agent, basically a helper that keeps working in the cloud even when you close your laptop. According to The Next Web, Autopilot is landing for early-access “Frontier” users in November. Reports also say Word, Excel and PowerPoint are being built right into the Copilot app, with a worldwide rollout expected around mid-November, controlled through the same admin settings companies already use. One catch: Code, Autopilot and some other agent features use usage-based billing, so the more the AI does, the more it can cost.

The big thing to watch is Microsoft Ignite, running November 17–20 in San Francisco and online, where the company says it will share more Copilot news. Microsoft says its rollout timelines are tentative, so what you see may depend on your account.

This Week in SAP Joule

Joule is the AI helper built into SAP, the software that big companies use to run things like payroll, shipping and buying supplies. It has been a quiet few days, so we stretched our look back to two weeks, and the big moment turned out to be SAP’s TechEd conference in late October.

The catch is patience. SAP says the new pieces are rolling out in phases, with several arriving as late as the first quarter of 2027. So for now this is a preview of where SAP is heading: an AI that does the paperwork, not one that just chats about it.

GitHub Copilot

This Week in GitHub Copilot

GitHub Copilot is the AI pair programmer that lives inside the tools developers use to write code. Nothing new landed in the last seven days, but GitHub’s Universe conference on October 28 was a big one, so we looked back two weeks.

The headline was Copilot Fusion reaching general availability. Instead of making you pick which AI model to use for each job, Fusion picks for you, like a taxi dispatcher choosing the closest car. GitHub is also giving paid plans a 30 percent discount on Fusion’s model costs through the end of November. The Copilot app also gained Projects in public preview, a way to keep an AI agent’s work organized, plus a computer-use feature on macOS and Windows that lets Copilot operate desktop apps, with your approval first. Agent Merge on pull requests is coming for paid plans too.

Behind the shiny features is a telling number: GitHub says developers started more than 40 percent of pull requests with an agent in September, up from 17 percent in March. Billing for cloud sandbox usage also starts this month, so expect the way Copilot is paid for to be a topic in the weeks ahead.
