Portrait of Cardinal Cristoforo Madruzzo

ArtistTitian (Tiziano Vecellio, c. 1488–1576)
StyleVenetian High Renaissance · Oil on canvas
Year1552 (signed “1.5.5.2. / TITIAN FECIT”)
MuseumSão Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), São Paulo, Brazil
Interesting Fact

Two of the most celebrated connoisseurs of the nineteenth century got this painting badly wrong. Giovanni Morelli — the man who invented the forensic method of attributing pictures by their small anatomical details — and the English critic Charles Ricketts both stripped it from Titian and reassigned it to Giambattista Moroni, judging it clumsy; Ricketts complained about the sitter’s “short thumbs.” Both men were working from photographs and had never stood in front of the canvas, which carries Titian’s signature and date in plain sight. The picture is also one of the very few full-length portraits Titian painted himself, and it spent centuries in the Madruzzo family’s castle at Trento before drifting through the American art market and landing in Brazil in 1951.
