Wind and Rain Returning by Boat (風雨歸舟圖)

ArtistDai Jin (戴進, 1388–1462)
StyleChinese landscape painting, Ming dynasty, Zhe School
YearMing dynasty, c. early–mid 15th century
MuseumNational Palace Museum, Taipei, Taiwan
Interesting Fact

This hanging scroll captures a sudden storm slamming a river crossing — small boats pitching, travelers leaning hard into the wind — a dramatic "wind-and-rain" motif Dai Jin inherited from Southern Song master Xia Gui but rendered with far more forceful, unrestrained brushwork, making it a defining image of the Zhe School he founded. Dai Jin's own career had a storm of its own: recommended to the Ming imperial court during the Xuande era, he was privately admired but publicly undone by a jealous rival painter, Xie Huan, who denounced one of Dai's four-seasons landscapes — which showed a fisherman wearing a red robe, a color reserved for officials — as a veiled insult to the emperor. The accusation got Dai Jin expelled from court; he returned home to Hangzhou and, out of favor with the throne, went on to found the school that would carry his province's name into art history.
