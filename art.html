Fuxi and Nüwa

ArtistUnknown, Tang dynasty workshop, Astana, Turpan
StyleTang dynasty funerary painting — color on silk
Yearc. 7th–8th century CE
MuseumNational Museum of China, Beijing, China
Interesting Fact

This silk hanging was not made to be seen. Painted for a tomb in the Astana burial ground near Turpan on the Silk Road, it was nailed face-down to the ceiling above the coffin so the dead could gaze up at the creator deities: Nüwa, who moulded humankind from yellow earth, holding compasses, and her brother-consort Fuxi holding a carpenter's square — the tools that measure heaven and earth. Their serpent tails coil around each other in a twisting double spiral, and modern viewers have repeatedly noticed the resemblance to a strand of DNA, a coincidence that has made this roughly 1,300-year-old funerary painting an unlikely favourite of geneticists. The sun above them carries the three-legged crow of Chinese myth, and the field of dots scattered across the background is a star chart.
