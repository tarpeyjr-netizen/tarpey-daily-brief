The Virgin of Chancellor Rolin (La Vierge du chancelier Rolin)

ArtistJan van Eyck (c. 1390–1441)
StyleEarly Netherlandish (Northern Renaissance / Flemish Primitives)
Yearc. 1435
MuseumLouvre Museum, Paris, France
Interesting Fact

Nicolas Rolin, chancellor to the Duke of Burgundy, paid to have himself painted kneeling at nearly the same scale and proximity to the Virgin Mary and Christ Child as any saint would be, an act of self-promotion so bold that contemporaries and later scholars accused the self-made chancellor of vanity and hubris rather than piety. Van Eyck packed the loggia's tiny background with symbolic sparring partners: peacocks (symbols of Christ) face off against magpies (symbols of evil) along the balustrade. Look closely at the two miniature figures leaning on the far wall overlooking the river below, and you'll find what many historians believe is van Eyck's own hidden self-portrait alongside an assistant, rendered at such minuscule scale it was really meant to be discovered rather than simply seen.
