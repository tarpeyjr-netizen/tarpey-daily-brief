The Four Seasons, Summer — Diana Surprised by Actaeon

ArtistEugène Delacroix, 1798–1863
StyleFrench Romanticism — mythological allegory
Year1856–1863
MuseumSão Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), São Paulo, Brazil
Interesting Fact

This canvas is one of four Delacroix painted for the Alsatian industrialist Frédéric Hartmann, each season cast as a mythological pairing rather than a landscape — a cycle finished in 1863, just months before the artist's death, so art historians now call them the "Hartmann Seasons." The scene freezes the instant the hunter Actaeon stumbles on Diana bathing with her nymphs: for that unintended glimpse of the goddess unclothed he is doomed to be turned into a stag and torn apart by his own hounds, two of which already crowd the foreground beside him. Delacroix, who never once traveled to Greece or Italy, built his mythological staging largely from prints and engravings rather than firsthand study of antiquity, which helps explain the painting's restless, almost feverish energy in place of classical calm.
