Jupiter and Antiope (Satyr and the Sleeping Nymph)

ArtistAntoine Watteau (1684–1721)
StyleRococo · mythological nude, oil on canvas
Yearc. 1714–1719
MuseumLouvre Museum, Paris, France
Interesting Fact

X-ray studies show that the sleeping Antiope once wore a cloth of modesty that was later painted out, and no one knows whether Watteau removed it himself or a later hand did. The satyr, really Jupiter in disguise, was once doubted as Watteau’s own work until X-rays, surviving sketches and an 18th-century engraving by the Comte de Caylus settled it. The nymph went on to a second life: a detail centered on her armpit has been the cover of Patrick Süskind’s novel Perfume since 1985, though the American paperback dropped it because it showed a nipple.
