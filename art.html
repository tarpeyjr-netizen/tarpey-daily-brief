Candombe

ArtistPedro Figari (Uruguayan, 1861–1938)
StylePost-Impressionism / Latin American Modernism
Yearc. 1930 · oil on cardboard, 40 × 49.5 cm
MuseumSão Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), São Paulo, Brazil
Interesting Fact

Figari spent almost his entire adult life as a lawyer, journalist and politician — not a painter. He was a public defender who won fame in the Almeida case by proving an army lieutenant innocent, and in 1905 published a series of newspaper polemics arguing for abolition of the death penalty, which Uruguay abolished two years later. Only in 1921, at the age of sixty, did he abandon law entirely for art, painting small oils on cardboard almost exclusively from memory. This canvas belongs to his best-known series: the candombe, the Afro-Uruguayan drum-and-dance tradition carried to the Río de la Plata by enslaved Africans, which Figari recorded at a moment when Uruguayan elites largely dismissed it as unworthy of fine art.
