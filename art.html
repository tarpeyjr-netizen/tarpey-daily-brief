Diana’s Bath

ArtistFrançois Clouet (c. 1510–1572)
StyleFrench Renaissance · School of Fontainebleau
Yearc. 1559–60
MuseumSão Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), São Paulo, Brazil
Interesting Fact

On its surface this is Ovid’s tale of Actaeon, the hunter turned into a stag for glimpsing Diana bathing — but two lurking satyrs hint at a second, courtly story. MASP notes the scene is often read as a coded allusion to the death of Henry II in 1559 and the accession of Francis II, with the red-clad Diana standing for Mary Stuart and the grieving seated woman for Catherine de’ Medici; earlier scholars instead saw Diane de Poitiers, or Charles IX and Marie Touchet. Three other versions survive in France (Rouen, Tours and the Sulzbach collection), and in 1955 the scholar Charles Sterling argued MASP’s panel might be a copy of a lost original.
