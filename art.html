The Chenghua Emperor Enjoying the Lantern Festival — 明憲宗元宵行樂圖 (detail)

ArtistAnonymous Ming court painters, working under imperial supervision
StyleMing dynasty court painting; gongbi ink and colour on silk handscroll
Year1485 (21st year of the Chenghua reign)
MuseumNational Museum of China, Beijing, China
Interesting Fact

The scroll runs more than twenty feet long, and the emperor appears in it over and over — more than a dozen times, in a different robe each time, as the viewer unrolls the silk and walks him through a single night of festivities. It is less a portrait than a piece of state choreography: acrobats, fireworks vendors, conjurers and costumed “tribute bearers” from imaginary distant lands were arranged inside the Forbidden City walls to show a ruler at the calm centre of a contented empire. The reality was thinner — Chenghua was a reclusive emperor who rarely met his own ministers, and the painting's crowded, cheerful streets exist mostly on silk.
