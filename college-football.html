Review: Oklahoma 28, Texas A&M 7

Final: No. 22 (CFP) Oklahoma 28, Texas A&M 7, in Norman, in front of 83,487.

How it turned: Six punts in the first quarter set the tone. A&M scored first early in the second quarter on a 96-yard drive that ended with Hart's 36-yard touchdown pass to Isaiah Horton. Oklahoma tied it on a 92-yard drive, with John Mateer hitting TE Rocky Beers from 4 yards out with 22 seconds left in the half. It was still 7-7 after three quarters, and then Oklahoma scored 21 in the fourth. Mateer threw a 5-yard TD pass to Tory Blaylock at 13:13, hit Isaiah Sategna III for 25 yards on the play right after Courtland Guillory intercepted Hart (3:50), and ran in a 7-yard score with 22 seconds left.

Top performers:

  • OU QB John Mateer: 23-of-34, 269 yards, 3 TD, 1 INT, plus 14 carries for 66 yards and 1 TD.
  • OU WR Isaiah Sategna III: 6 catches, 94 yards, 1 TD.
  • A&M QB Brady Hart: 8-of-20, 131 yards, 1 TD, 1 INT, sacked three times.
  • A&M WR Mario Craver: 4 catches, 78 yards.
  • A&M RB KJ Edwards: 16 carries, 49 yards.
  • A&M LB Ray Coney: 19 tackles.

Turning point: Oklahoma's 92-yard touchdown drive late in the first half came right after the defense had forced two Mateer turnovers. After its own 96-yard drive, A&M gained only 89 more yards. It finished with 185 total yards to Oklahoma's 421, and with 54 rushing yards on 32 carries (1.7 per carry).

What it means: A&M has lost four straight and is 4-7 (1-7 SEC). At best it can finish 5-7, one win short of the six usually needed for bowl eligibility. Oklahoma moves to 7-4 (5-3). (ESPN/AP; 12thMan.com)

What's next: No. 8 Texas visits Kyle Field on Friday night.

Penn State Big Ten Review

Next: Sat, Nov 28 at Maryland (4-7) · 3:30 PM ET · FS1 · SECU Stadium, College Park, Md. (regular-season finale)
  • Rutgers won the game on the final timed play and then converted a two-point try. It is Rutgers' first win over Penn State since 1988 and ends an 18-game losing streak in the series. Rutgers had been a more-than-four-touchdown underdog. (ESPN, Nov. 21)
  • Asked whether there was a whistle on Rutgers' late touchdown, Matt Campbell said officials reviewed it and "that is the call."
