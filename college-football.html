Pre-Game: Missouri State

No. 8 Texas A&M (0-0) opens Saturday at 7:00 p.m. ET on ESPN against Missouri State (0-0), a second-year FBS program starting a new era under head coach Casey Woods. The Aggies are prohibitive favorites and the game is a tune-up, but three things are worth watching: whether the reshuffled offensive line — Elko spent all of camp searching for his best five — holds up; whether Sanford is cleared and how much he plays; and how quickly Reed and Craver re-establish the connection that carried the 2025 offense. A clean, fast win keeps A&M's top-10 standing intact heading into a schedule that gets serious quickly. Anything close would be the story of the SEC's opening weekend, and not in a good way.

Pre-Game: Marshall

No. 18 Penn State (0-0) hosts Marshall (0-0) at 3:30 p.m. ET on FS1 in front of a Stripe Out crowd, and the occasion is bigger than the opponent: it is Matt Campbell's first game at Penn State, Becht's first start in the Big Ten, and the first look at a roster carrying roughly 50 newcomers, nearly half of them Iowa State transfers. A comfortable win is the expectation and tells us little; a one-score game would raise real questions about a No. 18 ranking built largely on projection.

Georgia Tech Review

Review: Colorado 14, Georgia Tech 13

Georgia Tech led for 58 minutes and lost the opener in the last 100 seconds. Colorado took over at its own 25 with 1:40 left, down 13–7, and Julian Lewis moved the Buffaloes on chunk plays before hitting tight end Charlie Williams on a 20-yard wheel-route touchdown with 37 seconds to go. Alberto Mendoza answered instantly with a 42-yard strike to Dalen Penson to set up a 45-yard Aidan Birr attempt as time expired — and Colorado's Boo Carter blocked it. Mendoza was excellent (17-of-23, 237 yards, 10.3 per attempt) and Jordan Allen caught eight balls for 113 yards, but the Yellow Jackets managed one offensive touchdown-free night: their only seven points came on Rakheem Smith's 100-yard kickoff return, with Birr adding field goals from 22 and 29 yards and missing from 35. Michigan transfer Justice Haynes was held to 64 yards on 19 carries, and Colorado's Lamont Lester Jr. had three sacks. Tech falls to 0-1 with the four AP votes it received almost certainly gone; next up is No. 20 Tennessee at home on Sep. 12 at 7:00 p.m. ET.

Minnesota Review

Review: Minnesota 59, Eastern Illinois 7

P.J. Fleck's 10th season opened about as cleanly as possible. Minnesota never trailed, outgained Eastern Illinois 495–110 and led 35–7 by halftime. Javon Tracy set a program record with two punt-return touchdowns and added five catches for 98 yards; Darius Taylor ran for three scores, including a 27-yarder in the second quarter. Drake Lindsey was efficient and mistake-free at 19-of-24 for 264 yards and a touchdown — a 6-second-before-halftime throw to tight end Pierce Walsh — extending his interception-free streak. Cole LaCrue threw the Panthers' only touchdown, to Tyren Buggs. The Gophers are 1-0 and host Mississippi State on Sep. 12 in the first meeting between the programs.

College of Wooster Review

Review: Wilmington 22, Wooster 19

Wilmington's 16-point first quarter was the difference, and Wooster spent the rest of the night clawing back without ever getting in front. Junior linebacker Cam Crago, back on defense, blocked two Avery Ernst punts and picked off Miles Hostetler on a deflection to set up a first-and-goal in the fourth quarter. Michael Boyle went 13-of-37 for 245 yards and two touchdowns, both to Chance Bennett (five catches, 115 yards) and first-year Zeek Seraphin; Manny Zelaya punched in a 1-yard score with 13:39 left to make it a one-score game. Wooster sacked Hostetler four times and held Wilmington to 3-of-12 on third down, but was outgained 375–288 and could not slow Kaiden Molnar (15 carries, 170 yards, including a 64-yard opening-drive run) early. Grady Harban led the Scots with nine tackles. Wooster is 0-1 and opens North Coast Athletic Conference play Sep. 12 at Kenyon.
