🤖 AI in the Enterprise

  • Outcome-based pricing arrives. Salesforce's Agentforce Help Agent charges only for resolutions. When a vendor is willing to price on outcomes, it's a real signal about deflection rates — and it reframes every seat-based AI negotiation you're in.
  • Government-grade agent deployment. Agentforce running in IL5 for 9.2 million Army-affiliated users is the largest publicly-named agentic deployment to date and a meaningful credibility marker for regulated industries.
  • Default model swaps are happening quietly. ServiceNow shifting Now Assist to third-party model providers by default, and Microsoft adding OpenAI as an auto-enabled M365 subprocessor, both changed the data path without customers doing anything. Audit your AI data-flow documentation this quarter.
  • Hype vs. reality: A survey of 740 executives at $1B–$20B+ companies found 51.3% claim live AI agents but only ~10% have deployed true multi-agent systems; 70.7% are running assistive AI or below, and 84% report encountering "agent-washing." Deloitte separately found 84% of organizations haven't redesigned jobs around AI. Translation: the constraint is organizational, not model quality. Where it does work — tier-1 support with real tool access and authority — deflection lands at 40–60%, which is genuinely good and genuinely not autonomy.
  • MCP is becoming table stakes. SAP Integration Suite, ABAP, and LeanIX all shipped MCP servers in the same cycle. The integration layer is being rebuilt around agent access, and API governance skills just got more valuable.

✅ Implementation Wins & Losses

  • Win — Germany's Federal Agency for Post and Telecommunications went live on S/4HANA public cloud January 1, 2026: greenfield, 1,000 users, one year end-to-end. Reported success factors were strict fit-to-standard (legacy custom solutions dropped) and direct moderation inside business units (SAP News).
  • Loss — Birmingham City Council / Oracle Fusion remains the cautionary benchmark: a £19M SAP replacement now tracking toward ~£216M through 2026, more than 11x original estimate.
  • Sobering baseline: Panorama's 2026 ERP Report and related research put discrete-manufacturing ERP objective-failure at 73% with average cost overruns near 215%. Use these numbers when someone proposes a compressed timeline.
  • Post-go-live is the real project. Valantic's 2026 SAP study argues the change effort begins after cutover, and that the highest-return programs treated user readiness as strategic from day one rather than as training at the end. This is precisely the gap WalkMe's Q2 release targets.
  • Recovery pattern: A national battery distributor's post-go-live billing failures were stabilized by targeted functional intervention rather than a re-implementation — a reminder that most "failed" go-lives are recoverable with the right hypercare model (case study).

⚠️ Threats & Risks

  • SAP August Patch Day is urgent. 28 new notes including CVE-2026-58231 at CVSS 10.0 — unauthenticated remote code execution, no user interaction required — plus two critical code-injection flaws in SAP MII and unauthenticated memory corruption in ABAP DIAG. Attackers are already exploiting the Commerce Cloud path without a public PoC.
  • Older SAP flaw still being exploited. CVE-2025-42957, the S/4HANA code-injection vulnerability, remains under active exploitation in the wild (SecurityBridge). Unpatched systems from last year are the soft target.
  • Salesforce Experience Cloud misconfiguration is the attack vector of the year. ShinyHunters exfiltrated 4.9M+ Salesforce records from Brinks Home in August by exploiting overly permissive guest user configuration. This is a customer-side config failure, not a Salesforce platform breach — audit your guest user profiles and sharing rules now.
  • Lock-in is compounding across four vendors at once. Analysts note enterprises are accruing switching costs with Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow and AWS simultaneously, faster and deeper than prior software waves. The mitigation that actually works: separate the orchestration layer from the model layer so changing providers is configuration, not rebuild.
  • Consolidation wave accelerating. 68% of IT organizations plan vendor consolidation in 2026, against $3.7T of PE dry powder. Recent deals include Thoma Bravo's $12.3B take-private of Dayforce and Salesforce's acquisition of Contentful. Assume at least one vendor in your stack changes hands within 24 months and check your change-of-control terms.

🤖 AI in the Enterprise

  • The U.S. Army is now one of the largest agentic deployments anywhere. HRC's Agentforce rollout serves 9.2 million people — a scale that will produce genuinely useful public evidence about agent reliability in high-stakes service delivery. Worth tracking for a year.
  • Governance is becoming a product category. Databricks shipped Unity AI Gateway to GA; SAP built its AI Agent Hub on LeanIX; ServiceNow is unifying under Otto. Three vendors, same conclusion: nobody knows what agents are running in their estate, and that's now the bottleneck.
  • Model pluralism is normalizing. Microsoft 365 Copilot now routes between GPT-5.6 and Claude by task, and SAP's managed Joule Studio supports Cursor and Claude Code. The single-model-vendor enterprise stack looks increasingly like a transitional phase.
  • Hype vs. reality — the numbers got worse, not better. 79% of organizations report adoption challenges, up double digits from 2025. Roughly 80% of enterprise AI projects deliver no measurable business value, IBM's CEO study puts initiatives hitting expected ROI at 25%, and Forrester expects enterprises to defer 25% of planned 2026 AI spend into 2027. The honest read: this is not a model-capability problem. 84% of companies haven't redesigned a single workflow around AI — they bolted assistants onto processes designed for humans and are surprised the P&L didn't move.
  • What does work. Tier-1 customer service deflection of 40-60% is repeatedly achievable — but only where the agent has real tool access, real authority to act, and the process was redesigned around it. That's the pattern to copy.
