Joule

🤖 AI in the Enterprise

  • Agentforce is the first agent P&L that's real. 240% ARR growth and $1.5B+ in Agentforce Plan & Solution ARR, on ~29,000 cumulative deals since launch, is the first hard revenue proof point in the agent category. Salesforce's own Agentic Enterprise Index says agent deployments more than doubled year over year. Agentic Enterprise Index
  • Outcome-based pricing is spreading. Agentforce Help Agent, GA in July, deploys in minutes and charges only for resolutions — a pricing model that transfers deployment risk to the vendor and will pressure per-seat AI licensing everywhere. Expect procurement to start demanding it. Help Agent
  • Vendors are converging on the same architecture. SAP's BAIP (context / build / governance), ServiceNow's Otto plus open system-of-action, Databricks' Unity AI Gateway, and Microsoft's connector grounding are all the same three-layer bet: unify data context, give builders a studio, and govern every agent centrally. The differentiator is no longer the model — it's whose governance layer you standardize on.
  • Interoperability is winning over walled gardens. SAP's managed Joule Studio supporting Cursor and Claude Code, ServiceNow defaulting Now Assist to third-party model providers, and Salesforce embedding Claude via Claudeforce all point the same direction: platform vendors would rather own the harness than the model.
  • Hype vs. reality. Vendor-adjacent research claims median agentic ROI around 171% with 7–9 month payback, but independent readings are far bleaker — fewer than 10% of enterprises that experimented with agents have scaled one to measurable value, only ~11% have agents genuinely in production, and roughly 95% of GenAI pilots stall on integration and data readiness, not model capability. The honest take: the failure mode of 2026 is not that agents can't reason, it's that the surrounding estate — master data, entitlements, process definitions — can't support them. Which is precisely why the LeanIX/Signavio/Unity Catalog layer matters more than the agent demo. Deployment guide

⚠️ Threats & Risks

  • RingCentral disclosed that a July social-engineering campaign compromised part of its environment, with attackers publishing data allegedly affecting 1.6 million people — another identity-first breach that bypassed technical SaaS controls entirely. Weekly roundup
  • AI infrastructure is now the supply chain. A supply-chain attack exposed 2,500+ companies in what's being called the largest AI infrastructure breach of 2026 so far, and CVE-2026-64849 in MLflow allows unauthenticated SSRF that can reach cloud metadata and credentials — and in some configurations escalate to RCE. If your data science platform sits inside the enterprise network, patch and audit it like production. CX Today
  • The 2026 licensing reset is real. Oracle, Broadcom and Microsoft are converging on subscription-first structures, portfolio consolidation that removes optimization levers, audit enforcement aligned to new metric definitions, and minimum commitment thresholds that raise baseline spend. Microsoft's removal of automatic EA volume discounts is the template. Block64 analysis
  • Consolidation keeps shrinking negotiating leverage. Broadcom/VMware and Cisco/Splunk already reduced competitive pressure; IBM's March 2026 acquisition of Confluent adds real-time data integration to the same dynamic. Fewer independent vendors means fewer credible walk-away options at renewal. Price increase tracker
  • Agent-layer lock-in is the next lock-in. As SAP, ServiceNow and Salesforce each position their governance layer as the place all agents get registered and monitored, the switching cost migrates from data to orchestration. Worth writing portability requirements — agent definition export, model provider choice — into contracts now, while vendors are still competing on openness. Agentic AI lock-in analysis
