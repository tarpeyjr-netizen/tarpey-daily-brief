🤖 AI in the Enterprise

  • The governance layer is the new battleground. Within one quarter, SAP shipped AI Agent Hub, ServiceNow expanded AI Control Tower, and Databricks launched Unity AI Gateway — three vendors racing to own the registry where every agent in your estate is tracked. Whoever wins that layer owns the account. Pick deliberately; this is a harder decision to reverse than any app choice.
  • ServiceNow gave the market its first hard number: AI ACV past $1B in Q2 2026, with $1M+ Now Assist customers up 130%+ YoY. Everyone else is still quoting adoption percentages.
  • SAP's agent story got real and got bounded. Joule across 35+ solutions, live agents in Ariba and Fieldglass, six more role-based assistants shipping this month, and bidirectional A2A so external agents can call Joule. The constraint is no longer capability — it's whether your master data and process discipline can survive an autonomous actor.
  • Hype vs. reality — the honest take. Survey work this year found 51.3% of organizations claim AI agents in live production, but only ~10% have actually deployed true multi-agent systems; roughly 70.7% are running assistive AI or below — retrieval tools rebranded as agents. The term for it is agent-washing. Gartner still expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be scrapped by 2027, and the cited reasons are organizational, not technical: fragmented data, integration debt, unclear ROI measurement, talent gaps. Sinequa · NC Tech
  • Where agents actually work today: constrained, well-governed domains with human-in-the-loop and clear boundaries — IT ops, employee service, finance operations, onboarding, reconciliation, support triage. If a proposed use case doesn't look like that list, budget for a pilot, not a program.

  • 68% of IT organizations plan vendor consolidation in 2026 — which cuts both ways. Fewer vendors means less sprawl and better pricing today, and less renegotiation leverage in three years. Digital Chiefs
  • The quiet lock-in decision of 2026 is agent architecture. As one analysis puts it, the next phase of competition is about who owns the orchestration standards, agent runtime and developer patterns — and enterprises that haven't defined an agent architecture strategy have already made a lock-in decision without noticing. If you're deploying Joule agents on BAIP, Agentforce on Salesforce, and Otto on ServiceNow with no cross-vendor registry, that's the decision. Kai Waehner
