Enterprise Software Intel — Week of August 14, 2026

🏢 Platform News

S/4HANA

Security dominated the week. SAP's August 11 Patch Day shipped 28 new Security Notes plus updates — 31 items total, five rated Critical. The standouts: an unauthenticated CVSS 10.0 path in SAP Commerce Cloud, two code-injection flaws in Manufacturing Integration & Intelligence, and unauthenticated memory corruption in the ABAP DIAG protocol. Separately, CVE-2025-42957, an S/4HANA code-injection bug, remains under active exploitation — patch now if you haven't. On the business side, SAP shares held above $209 despite a Q2 EPS miss, with cloud growth and margin expansion carrying the story. The 2027 ECC maintenance cliff is now roughly 16 months out; if you're still scoping, you're late.

Joule

Joule Studio 2.0 continues rolling out post-Sapphire, with SAP launching a managed edition that supports Cursor and Claude Code as development front-ends — a notable concession that enterprise agent-building happens in the tools developers already use. The portfolio now claims 200 specialized agents across ~50 domain assistants, up from 40 agents and 2,400 skills at GA. Intent-based development generates PRD, specs, scaffolding and an eval harness from a natural-language description. Watch the gap between "shipped" and "in production at a reference customer" — it's still wide.

SAP Analytics Cloud

QRC3 2026 lands this month (SAC ships February/May/August/November). The Q3 preview adds three chart types — Funnel, Gauge and Sankey — expanded Compass simulation, smarter Data Action filtering and a long-requested search improvement. Follows Q2's asymmetric tables, new Data Panel and live Azure SQL connectivity.

SAP Integration Suite

The MCP Gateway and API-Centric Integration are the strategic pieces here: SAP is folding API management, integration mediation and agent-readiness into one layer so agents can call governed enterprise APIs. On the eventing side, 2026 priorities are a lightweight Advanced Event Mesh adapter for non-SAP endpoints and supported migration from classic Event Mesh to AEM. If you're on EM today, start the migration conversation.

SAP Build

No standalone Build release this week. The direction set at Sapphire holds: Joule for Developers is now embedded across Build Apps, Build Process Automation, Build Code and ABAP, positioning Build as the low-code on-ramp to the agentic stack rather than a separate product line. Monitor the SAP Build community topic page for the next roadmap drop.

WalkMe

Q2 2026 (released May 18) remains the current wave: Contextual AI Assistance, Smart Highlights and a Solutions Gallery, all available in 38 languages with no extra configuration — genuinely useful for global S/4HANA rollouts where post-go-live adoption is where projects quietly bleed value. Sobering data point from WalkMe's own research: 77% of employees abandoned their company's AI tools. Q3 release expected next month.

Signavio

Large-scale data replication now runs through SAP Datasphere with change-data-capture. SAP was named a Leader in Gartner's Process Intelligence Platforms MQ.

LeanIX

The most strategically interesting SAP asset right now. The SAP AI Agent Hub is built on the LeanIX platform, which makes enterprise architects — not IT ops — the designated owners of agent governance at scale. LeanIX now generates rationalization recommendations (retire / tolerate / invest / migrate) from usage, cost, capability mapping and strategic fit, with architects reviewing rather than authoring. SAP was named a 2026 Gartner Peer Insights Customers' Choice for EA Tools. 2026 roadmap: AI-assisted architecture guidance, diagram templates and expanded Agent Hub capability.

BTP

BTP is no longer BTP. Effective June 30, the platform and its partner competency were renamed SAP Business AI Platform (BAIP), consolidating BTP, Business Data Cloud and Business Transformation Management into three layers: context, build (Joule Studio 2.0) and governance (AI Agent Hub). Existing certifications stay valid; updated specializations arrive October 2026, new proficiency requirements January 2027. Read the rename as an architectural signal, not marketing.

Worth validating those claims against your own demand-planning accuracy before budgeting for it.

🤖 AI in the Enterprise

  • The U.S. Army is now one of the largest agentic deployments anywhere. HRC's Agentforce rollout serves 9.2 million people — a scale that will produce genuinely useful public evidence about agent reliability in high-stakes service delivery. Worth tracking for a year.
  • Governance is becoming a product category. Databricks shipped Unity AI Gateway to GA; SAP built its AI Agent Hub on LeanIX; ServiceNow is unifying under Otto. Three vendors, same conclusion: nobody knows what agents are running in their estate, and that's now the bottleneck.
  • Model pluralism is normalizing. Microsoft 365 Copilot now routes between GPT-5.6 and Claude by task, and SAP's managed Joule Studio supports Cursor and Claude Code. The single-model-vendor enterprise stack looks increasingly like a transitional phase.
  • Hype vs. reality — the numbers got worse, not better. 79% of organizations report adoption challenges, up double digits from 2025. Roughly 80% of enterprise AI projects deliver no measurable business value, IBM's CEO study puts initiatives hitting expected ROI at 25%, and Forrester expects enterprises to defer 25% of planned 2026 AI spend into 2027. The honest read: this is not a model-capability problem. 84% of companies haven't redesigned a single workflow around AI — they bolted assistants onto processes designed for humans and are surprised the P&L didn't move.
  • What does work. Tier-1 customer service deflection of 40-60% is repeatedly achievable — but only where the agent has real tool access, real authority to act, and the process was redesigned around it. That's the pattern to copy.

✅ Implementation Wins & Losses

  • Win — Bundesanstalt für Post und Telekommunikation went live on S/4HANA public cloud January 1, 2026: greenfield, 1,000 users, one-year timeline, executed while simultaneously relocating to a new building. Clean-core discipline was the enabler — standard processes, extensions pushed to BTP.
  • Win — Coop Group completed a 15-hour cutover of two very large business-critical ECC systems to S/4HANA with SNP. Downtime windows that short on systems that big are still rare enough to be worth studying.
  • Loss — Zimmer Biomet. A $69M Deloitte work order for S/4HANA was followed by shipment delays and an estimated 1-1.5% revenue decline — roughly $75M annually. A reminder that the true cost of a bad go-live is measured in revenue, not consulting fees.
  • Loss — Major Brands v. Epicor. The beverage distributor is suing Epicor after a go-live date slipped four years. Meanwhile Panorama's 2026 ERP Report puts discrete manufacturing failure rates at 73% with average cost overruns of 215%.
  • The pattern. Post-go-live is where value is won or lost, and it's the phase most consistently under-budgeted. Pair it with WalkMe's finding that 77% of employees abandon company AI tools and you have the real story: adoption, not implementation, is the failure mode.

⚠️ Threats & Risks

  • Patch S/4HANA now. CVE-2025-42957 is being actively exploited in the wild. August Patch Day added five more Critical notes including a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated compromise path in SAP Commerce Cloud and unauthenticated memory corruption in ABAP DIAG.
  • Oracle's 2026 breach keeps widening. OCI, E-Business Suite and Oracle Health were all affected, with credentials and patient records exposed. If you consume any Oracle SaaS, credential rotation and access review are overdue.
  • Vertical software is the new target. Craneware (healthcare finance) disclosed unauthorized data-environment access in July, following Stryker's March incident. Attackers are moving from broad platforms to niche vendors with concentrated, high-value data and thinner security teams — check your fourth-party exposure.
  • Consolidation is compressing buyer leverage. $3.7T in PE dry powder is meeting 68% of IT organizations planning to consolidate vendors in 2026. Thoma Bravo took Dayforce private for $12.3B; IBM closed Confluent; Salesforce bought Contentful. Fewer independent vendors means fewer alternatives at renewal.
  • Agentic lock-in is the lock-in of the next decade. When your agents, their orchestration, their governance layer and their business context all live in one vendor's platform, migration cost stops being about data export. Ask now where the abstraction boundary is — before you have 200 agents in production.

🎓 Training & Certification Finds

  • SAP's exam overhaul is complete. As of end-March 2026, all SAP certifications use open-book, performance-based formats — System-Based Assessment (execute and validate tasks in a live system) and Scenario-Based Assessment (reason through business situations). Memorization-based prep is now actively counterproductive.
  • Salesforce is pruning hard. 24 certifications retire February 1, 2027 — including CPQ Administrator, B2B Solution Architect and Nonprofit Success Pack Consultant — while 16 were renamed on July 24, most gaining "Agentforce." The Platform Administrator exam now allocates 8% to Agentforce AI.
  • Microsoft's largest credential overhaul since 2019. Twelve role-based certifications retire between June and September 2026, including AI-102, DP-100, AI-900, AZ-204 and AZ-500. Critically, there is no automatic migration — you must sit the replacement exam.
  • Free SAP learning is better than most people realize. The former openSAP catalog — 100+ expert-led courses — lives on free and self-paced as SAP Expert Lectures. The Joule Studio Learning Series is a four-part enablement track worth doing now.
  • BAIP note for partners: the BTP competency became the Business AI Platform competency on June 30. Existing certs remain valid, but updated specializations arrive October 2026 and new proficiency requirements activate January 2027 — plan training budget accordingly.
