Week ending Wednesday, August 19, 2026

This Week

Magnus Carlsen went a full tournament without losing a single game — 11 wins, nine draws — to defend his Esports World Cup chess crown in Riyadh on August 17, sweeping Denis Lazavik in the grand final for the $250,000 top prize. CNN's headline summed up the consensus: “Undoubtedly the GOAT.” Elsewhere, Malcolm Gladwell dropped Season 12 of Revisionist History on August 10 while promoting his new eight-part Reconstruction series co-hosted with Barack Obama, and Prime Video's Reacher Season 4 premiered August 12 as Lee Child continues to answer for handing the books to his brother.

Updates

Jerry Saltz — Artist

Update: Fantastic Man published a long profile of Saltz on August 15, framing him as the critic who stripped the intellectual pageantry out of art writing. The piece notes he sees over a thousand exhibitions a year and splits his days between looking at art and writing about it — trading the field's traditional pretension for candour, humour and self-deprecation.

He also has a nonfiction title, Shaken, on the Robert Roberson death-row case in the pipeline.

His thesis: AI isn't a “hegemonic centralizing totalizing force” but an entrepreneurial wave producing “many thousands of winners.”

Magnus Carlsen — Chess Player

Update: Carlsen defended his Esports World Cup chess title on August 17, beating Denis Lazavik in two straight matches in the grand final. CNN's coverage led with “Undoubtedly the GOAT.” It caps a year that already included the FIDE Freestyle World Championship and a blitz-tiebreak win over Arjun Erigaisi at TePe Sigeman in Malmö.

Ethan Mollick — Professor / AI Researcher

Update: Mollick published a practical guide to agentic AI timed to the back-to-school stretch, drawing pickup in Forbes. His advice was blunt: pick Claude or ChatGPT, pay the $20, hand an agent a real task from your real life, then examine the results and ask for changes as you would with a person — rather than simply accepting or rejecting the output. His other point: utility scales with access, so the more you let the tool see, the more you get.
