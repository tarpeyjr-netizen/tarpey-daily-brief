Atlanta, GA

Government

Atlanta's Zoning Review Board voted at its Nov. 5 meeting to approve demolition of 811 Ponce de Leon Place NE, the house where Michael Hardwick was arrested in 1982 under Georgia's anti-sodomy law, a case that reached the U.S. Supreme Court. The owners' lawyer argued that rehabilitation would cost more than demolition, citing black mold, deferred maintenance and a roughly 2016 renovation that altered the home's 1982 appearance, and the owners say they are open to a historic marker. More than 90 LGBTQ+ advocates and community members had signed a letter asking the board to deny or defer the application. The City Council unanimously passed a resolution on Nov. 2 asking, but not requiring, the Urban Design Commission to consider the property for historic designation. If the Department of City Planning pursues a nomination, the commission would have 120 to 180 days to review it, and demolition would be barred during that review.

Science & Environment

The Georgia Department of Transportation is proposing to replace the Peters Street bridge in Downtown Atlanta, a circa-1935 span that GDOT says has fractures in the structure and no longer meets current design standards. The new 703-foot bridge would carry two travel lanes and two bike lanes with sidewalks on both sides, and improvements are planned on Ted Turner Drive SW just east of it. Construction would close the road for 36 months, with a 7.2-mile detour, though no start date has been announced. An open house runs Tuesday, Nov. 10, from 5 to 7 p.m. at Antioch Baptist Church, and written comments are accepted until Dec. 4.

Chicago, IL

Government

The Chicago City Council handed Mayor Brandon Johnson a pointed defeat on Monday, voting 44-6 at a special meeting to direct a $75 million up-front parking meter payment from Stonepeak Partners into the city’s pension debt. Johnson had hoped to use the money to help cover a $363 million pension advance for 2027, and his administration tried to avoid the meeting. Finance Committee Chair Pat Dowell, who negotiated the deal, estimated the move could save the pension funds $213 million over time, with $25.12 million each going to the police and fire funds by Dec. 31. Budget Chair Jason Ervin warned the vote opens a hole in the mayor’s preelection budget and could put youth jobs, libraries and homelessness services at risk. The margin is veto-proof, and the fight arrives as Johnson also faces petition challenges from Willie Wilson’s campaign seeking to keep him off the mayoral ballot.

Science

After a year of punishing storms, the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District has been forced to release billions of gallons of untreated sewage into the Chicago River, according to a Sun-Times Watchdogs investigation. The Racine Avenue pumping station alone has discharged roughly 10 billion gallons, the most since 2019, while the Thornton Reservoir filled for the first time since opening in 2015 and the McCook Reservoir has filled 10 times this year. Storms can dump more than 8.5 inches of rain in 24 hours against sewers designed for about 2 inches, and O’Hare has logged over 37 inches this year. McCook’s expansion to 10 billion gallons of capacity has slipped to 2032, three years later than MWRD told the EPA. Rowers, kayakers and wildlife advocates say the river’s growing recreational use makes the health and ecological risks harder to ignore.

Los Angeles, CA

Government

Los Angeles votes tomorrow in a mayoral runoff that has tightened to a dead heat. A UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies poll co-sponsored by the Los Angeles Times, fielded among 2,197 likely city voters, found incumbent Mayor Karen Bass and Councilmember Nithya Raman each at 40 percent, with 19 percent undecided. In September the same pollster had Raman ahead 39 to 28, so Bass has recovered ground, though 65 percent of voters still view her unfavorably. Raman, a former Bass supporter, edged out reality-TV figure Spencer Pratt for the second runoff slot after a slow mail-ballot count in the June primary. Pratt voters now look like the swing bloc, with 41 percent leaning Bass, 14 percent Raman and 45 percent undecided. Voters named homeless encampments as their top priority (43 percent), ahead of public safety and affordable housing.

Crime & Justice

Los Angeles County's election office has pushed back on a federal list of roughly 20,000 California voters that the Justice Department calls “likely” noncitizens. Only 7,035 of the names had Los Angeles County addresses, and some were duplicates, according to Registrar Dean Logan. In a review of 200 records, county officials called the list “unreliable”: among records that reported a birthplace, 38.9 percent said they were born in the United States, and about 70 percent had no voting history. The Justice Department, which has acknowledged some names may be citizens, nonetheless charged 28 people from the list, all green-card holders, and is asking California to segregate the associated ballots. The state called the request unfeasible so close to the election and a “reckless political stunt,” leaving the county at the center of a legal fight on the eve of the vote.

New York, NY

Government

Election Day is Tuesday, and the last weekend of early voting drew heavy turnout across the five boroughs: more than 104,000 New Yorkers voted on Saturday alone, and over 572,000 city residents have cast ballots since early voting began October 24. Gov. Kathy Hochul and Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman both campaigned in the city on Saturday, she before a union audience in Brooklyn and he in Queens. Hochul pointed to economic growth and labor support, while Blakeman promised change, and a Siena poll from October 28 showed her ahead by a double-digit margin. All state Senate and Assembly seats are also on the ballot, making the vote a test of how the city's electorate leans in the first year of Mayor Zohran Mamdani's term.

Crime & Justice

Family and friends of Siobhan Lynch, 30, are pressing for accountability after a Department of Sanitation truck struck and killed her as she crossed Bay Parkway in Bath Beach, Brooklyn, around 6:40 p.m. on Wednesday. The truck was turning right from Cropsey Avenue onto the seven-lane road, and the driver, a sanitation employee, stayed at the scene; no charges had been filed as of Friday. A DSNY spokesperson called it a terrible tragedy and said the agency is assisting the NYPD investigation. Transportation Alternatives reports at least 30 people were killed or seriously injured on Bay Parkway from 2021 to 2025, and the family says it will push for greater driver accountability and street safety measures.

Jacksonville, FL

Government

Two of Jacksonville's public agencies spent the last week in turmoil. At the Jacksonville Transportation Authority, interim CEO Cleveland Ferguson told reporters that the “buck stopped with the CEO” in the agency's financial crisis, and said he will not seek the permanent job. Days earlier, board member Daniel Vallencourt called for current leadership to resign and said former CEO Nat Ford hid the extent of the problems. Separately, the Jacksonville Housing Authority board fired CEO Cheron Corbett after she refused to resign and asked for a large bonus on top of her $300,000 salary. Together the episodes raise questions about oversight of the city's transit and housing agencies.
