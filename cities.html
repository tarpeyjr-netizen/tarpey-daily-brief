San Francisco-Oakland, CA

Crime & Justice

The federal corruption trial of former Oakland Mayor Sheng Thao, now in its fourth week, has turned to the testimony of the government's key witness, Mario Juarez, a onetime business partner of the Duong family. New evidence shows Juarez secretly lobbied for ABC Security's City Hall contract without registering as a lobbyist, after the firm ranked last of six bidders in a 2022 renewal. City records show that Allied Universal's top-scoring proposal was pulled weeks after Thao took office, and the council soon received a proposal to extend ABC's contract by up to $4 million. Prosecutors have not accused ABC or its owner of wrongdoing. Meanwhile, defense lawyers for Thao, her former partner Andre Jones and David and Andy Duong are pressing Juarez to name the politicians who allegedly got bags of cash from the Duongs after their 2014 waste contract.

Crime & Justice

The San Francisco Sheriff's Office has admitted that an "administrative breakdown" led it to record confidential jail calls between detainees and their lawyers and hand them to prosecutors over two years. The public defender's office says at least 20 of its calls were recorded and shared, and at least 38 phone numbers tied to the office were never properly added to the do-not-record list. Deputy Public Defender Kwixuan Maloof says three of the recorded calls from his number involved homicide cases and covered legal strategy, and that a prosecutor "cannot unhear" them. Since Aug. 27, the sheriff has stopped routinely sharing recordings, and prosecutors must now subpoena any calls they want. Intentionally recording attorney-client conversations is a felony in California, but no charges or discipline have been reported.

Seattle, WA

Government

Progressive advocate Nilu Jenks, former leader of the nonprofit FairVote Washington, won the District 5 special election for Seattle City Council by a wide margin over educator Julie Kang, holding a 17-point lead on election night that grew in later ballot drops. The seat opened when Cathy Moore resigned, and Jenks will take office once results are certified later this month instead of in January. She replaces interim Councilmember Debora Juarez, a frequent critic of Mayor Katie Wilson, so the council's progressive bloc grows to four members and Wilson's agenda should have an easier path. Jenks has said she will push for safer designs on Aurora Avenue N and Lake City Way, a larger civilian-run CARE Department, and on-bill financing for heat pumps. The same election saw the Seattle Transit Measure renewal headed to victory, and Jenks must run again next year when all seven district seats are on the ballot.

Crime

On Nov. 10, Mayor Katie Wilson lifted her pause on new police CCTV cameras in three neighborhoods. Cameras in the Stadium District go live immediately, and installations near Garfield High School in the Central District and in the Capitol Hill nightlife district will follow on a timeline she could not yet give. The decision came a day after the release of a surveillance audit she ordered from NYU's Policing Project, which called for tighter data-retention and privacy guardrails and warned that no agency can guarantee data won't be shared, including with the federal government. Public Safety Committee Chair Bob Kettle called the move "pragmatic," while Councilmember Alexis Mercedes Rinck said she would rather fix internal issues before expanding. The Downtown Seattle Association and the Greater Seattle Business Association back the expansion, and the Community Not Cameras Coalition opposes it, noting that Wilson opposed it during her 2025 campaign.

Cleveland, OH

Crime & Justice

Mayor Justin Bibb’s administration wants to replace Cleveland’s roughly 100 Flock Safety license plate readers with Axon’s system, asking City Council to approve a one-year contract whose cost has not been disclosed. The move follows council’s refusal in May to renew Flock without competitive bids; after two hearings, members granted only a six-month extension on the condition that the city seek other proposals. The administration is also proposing a Skydio “drone as a first responder” contract and a one-year ShotSpotter extension. Opponents in the Flock No Cleveland group say a vendor swap ignores public objections to surveillance, while Safety Committee chair Mike Polensek backs the readers but wants limits on who can access the data. Complicating matters, the Community Police Commission has passed a resolution calling for police to stop using the readers on Jan. 1 until its policy review is done, and it is unclear whether the city will comply.

Government

Cuyahoga County voters approved almost $111 million in additional property tax money for social services last week, passing Issue 11 for seniors, children, homeless residents and food aid, and Issue 10 for the Board of Developmental Disabilities. County Executive Chris Ronayne, re-elected to a second term with 98% against a write-in challenger, said the levy was “first and foremost” about staving off further cuts after a $20.5 million reduction to the 2027 health and human services budget, and the county says it will move slowly on spending the new money. Two big money questions still loom: Greater Cleveland RTA is weighing its first sales tax increase in half a century, possibly on the May ballot, though Ronayne wants a community transportation plan first. Meanwhile, Gateway Economic Development Corp. is out of money for repairs at Progressive Field and Rocket Arena, the Cavaliers are seeking $37 million for upgrades, and the county, city and teams have not identified a revenue source.

Phoenix-Mesa, AZ

Government & Politics

Phoenix voters went to the polls on November 3 to fill four City Council seats, and the early count points to two incumbents winning outright and two open-seat races heading to a March 9, 2027 runoff. In District 6, Kevin Robinson leads Michael Del Prete 64.5% to 35.5%, while District 8's Kesha Hodge Washington holds about 72.5%. The open seats are District 2, where Jim Waring is termed out, and District 4, where Laura Pastor is termed out. In District 4, attorney and school board member Ed Hermes leads with roughly 40% in an eight-candidate field, with the second runoff slot still tight. In District 2, Matt Evans, Julie Read and Danny Mazza sit within about 1,100 votes of one another, and Turning Point Action endorsed all three. Results are unofficial; the council is expected to certify them on November 18, and winners take office in April 2027.

Economics & Business

Phoenix is preparing to end a six-year pause on water shutoffs for seriously delinquent residential accounts. ABC15 reports the restart is set for November 16 for accounts more than four months behind, covering about 9,000 accounts that owe nearly $24 million across water, wastewater, solid waste, taxes and fees. The city says the amount owed and how long an account has been delinquent will set the order, with advance notice and payment plans offered. The move, first announced in early October, comes as the city also weighs tougher conservation steps amid Colorado River shortages. Phoenix has announced resumptions before and then reversed course, so the date is worth confirming with the city.

Miami-Fort Lauderdale, FL

Government

Two close elections are still being counted in Miami-Dade. In Doral, a manual recount put Maureen Porras ahead of Fernanda Gomes for Council Seat 2 by 12 votes, 9,596 to 9,584, according to Supervisor of Elections Alina Garcia. In the congressional race, Republican Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar led Eliott Rodriguez by 1,090 votes after the canvassing board’s final count, below the half-percent threshold that triggers a state-ordered recount. Rodriguez’s campaign filed a federal lawsuit asking Garcia to recuse herself from the canvassing board, citing her son-in-law’s work for Salazar and her description of Salazar as a “dear friend.” Garcia’s office says there is no conflict and she will not step aside; the recount could begin Monday or Tuesday.

Crime & Justice

Former NFL receiver Antonio Brown pleaded guilty Friday to aggravated assault, a reduced charge, and received three years of probation with no jail time. He had been charged with attempted second-degree murder after a May 16, 2025 shooting at a celebrity boxing match in Miami, where prosecutors say he took a handgun from a security guard and fired two shots at a man he had fought earlier. Prosecutors cited the victim’s unwillingness to cooperate and difficulty proving intent, and the report says the deal spares Brown a criminal conviction. His attorney said Brown had faced 30 years with a 20-year mandatory minimum. Probation terms include no contact with the victim and a psychological evaluation.

Atlanta, GA

Government

Atlanta's Zoning Review Board voted at its Nov. 5 meeting to approve demolition of 811 Ponce de Leon Place NE, the house where Michael Hardwick was arrested in 1982 under Georgia's anti-sodomy law, a case that reached the U.S. Supreme Court. The owners' lawyer argued that rehabilitation would cost more than demolition, citing black mold, deferred maintenance and a roughly 2016 renovation that altered the home's 1982 appearance, and the owners say they are open to a historic marker. More than 90 LGBTQ+ advocates and community members had signed a letter asking the board to deny or defer the application. The City Council unanimously passed a resolution on Nov. 2 asking, but not requiring, the Urban Design Commission to consider the property for historic designation. If the Department of City Planning pursues a nomination, the commission would have 120 to 180 days to review it, and demolition would be barred during that review.

Science & Environment

The Georgia Department of Transportation is proposing to replace the Peters Street bridge in Downtown Atlanta, a circa-1935 span that GDOT says has fractures in the structure and no longer meets current design standards. The new 703-foot bridge would carry two travel lanes and two bike lanes with sidewalks on both sides, and improvements are planned on Ted Turner Drive SW just east of it. Construction would close the road for 36 months, with a 7.2-mile detour, though no start date has been announced. An open house runs Tuesday, Nov. 10, from 5 to 7 p.m. at Antioch Baptist Church, and written comments are accepted until Dec. 4.
