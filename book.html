Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

AuthorMark Twain
StylePicaresque Satire / Southern Regionalism
Year1884
Pages~366
Plot Synopsis

Huck Finn, a barely-schooled boy in Missouri, fakes his own death to escape his brutal father and drifts down the Mississippi on a raft with Jim, a man fleeing slavery. Every riverbank town offers a new swindle, feud or sham of respectability, and the king and duke, two charming frauds, hijack the raft and drag them into schemes of rising cruelty. The real drama is inside Huck: his conscience, trained by a slaveholding society, tells him to turn Jim in, while his love for Jim says otherwise.

Why You Should Read It

Jim, you've read this one, and it rewards a second pass with older eyes. Twain's vernacular narration still sounds startlingly alive, and the comedy of the con men sets up a moral turn that lands harder the more you notice how funny the book has been. It's a short, fast read for anyone who likes an American novel that is at once adventure, satire and an argument with the country itself. Come back for the river passages, which are some of the best landscape writing in the language.
