An American Tragedy

AuthorTheodore Dreiser
StyleAmerican Naturalism
Year1925
Pages~860
Plot Synopsis

Clyde Griffiths grows up handing out hymnals on Kansas City street corners for his missionary parents, and he wants out — out of poverty, out of piety, into the shine of hotel lobbies and fast cars. A job at his rich uncle's collar factory in upstate New York puts him within arm's reach of that world, and of Sondra Finchley, the dazzling society girl who seems to promise everything. The trouble is Roberta Alden, the factory girl he has already gotten pregnant, who will not quietly disappear. Out on a lonely lake in the Adirondacks, Clyde's dream and his conscience collide, and the second half of the book becomes a gripping murder trial that asks how much of what happened was ever really his choice.

Why You Should Read It

Jim, this is the great American novel about wanting — about how ambition, class and the glossy promise of money can hollow out an ordinary person who never quite decides to become a villain. Dreiser's prose is famously plain and heavy, but that is the point: he piles up detail until you feel the machinery of desire and circumstance closing around Clyde, and the trial chapters read like the best true-crime you have encountered. It was inspired by a real 1906 case, and it passed its centennial last year, yet its questions about status, social climbing and who gets blamed feel uncomfortably current. Go in ready for a long, slow-burning book — the payoff is one of the most morally unsettling endings in American fiction.
