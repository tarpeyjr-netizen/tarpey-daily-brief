Arrow of God

AuthorChinua Achebe
StyleAfrican Literary Tragedy / Social Realism
Year1964
Pages287
Plot Synopsis

In the fictional Igbo village-group of Umuaro in 1920s Nigeria, Ezeulu is Chief Priest of the god Ulu, a man whose authority rests on a fragile web of ritual, memory, and the loyalty of his people. As British colonial administrators tighten their grip and Christian missionaries chip away at the old order, Ezeulu finds himself squeezed between two worlds, neither of which he fully trusts. A single act of pride, his refusal to bend to the colonial district officer, sets off a chain of events that unravels his standing with his own community. It is a slow-building tragedy about what happens when personal will collides with the machinery of empire and the gods people have always believed in stop answering.

Why You Should Read It

This is Achebe writing at the height of his powers, working the same ground as Things Fall Apart but with a sharper, more interior focus on one man's downfall. If you want a story where cultural collision plays out through a single stubborn, proud protagonist rather than through abstraction, this delivers. The prose is dense with Igbo proverb and idiom, which rewards a slower read than you're probably used to, but there's real payoff in watching how colonial bureaucracy and internal village politics conspire against Ezeulu without either side fully understanding what it's doing. It's a short book that argues its case in a way most 500-page historical novels can't.
