Ancillary Justice

AuthorAnn Leckie
StyleMilitary Space Opera
Year2013
Pages~432
Plot Synopsis

Breq was once a starship — the troop carrier Justice of Toren, an artificial intelligence spread across a vast hull and thousands of “ancillaries,” captured human bodies wired to serve as its soldiers. Now she is the last fragment of that ship, a single body on an icy backwater world, carrying one aim: to kill Anaander Mianaai, the thousand-bodied Lord of the Radch who rules an empire that has been annexing worlds for three millennia. When she stumbles on Seivarden, a former officer of hers frozen for a thousand years and now a wreck, she drags him along against her better judgment. Told in two braided timelines, the novel slowly reveals what happened on the planet Shis’urna twenty years earlier — and that the Lord of the Radch is quietly at war with herself.

Why You Should Read It

Jim, if you liked the scale of Dune and Foundation, this is the modern book that earned its place beside them — it swept the Hugo, Nebula, Arthur C. Clarke and BSFA awards, which no debut had done before. What makes it stick is the narrator: a mind that used to see through twenty bodies at once, now confined to one, grieving in a way that is cold, precise and surprisingly moving. Leckie also does something gutsy with language — the Radchaai don’t mark gender, so Breq calls nearly everyone “she” — and after fifty pages it stops feeling like a trick and starts showing you how an empire thinks. Give the slower first act a little patience; the second half moves like a thriller and sets up a trilogy you’ll want to finish.
