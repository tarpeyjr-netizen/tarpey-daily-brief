Absalom, Absalom!

AuthorWilliam Faulkner
StyleSouthern Gothic / Modernist Stream of Consciousness
Year1936
Pages~320 (Vintage International ed.)
Plot Synopsis

In Mississippi in 1909, Quentin Compson is made to listen as Miss Rosa Coldfield and his own father tell and retell the rise and ruin of Thomas Sutpen, a poor man who arrived in Yoknapatawpha County in 1833 determined to found a dynasty. Sutpen carves a plantation out of the wilderness, but a secret from his past and his refusal to acknowledge his own blood unravel his family in murder and silence. Each narrator guesses at what the others cannot know, until the story becomes as much about how the South invents its own history as about Sutpen himself.

Why You Should Read It

This is Faulkner at his most ambitious, and it rewards a reader who likes to work: long, looping sentences, narrators who contradict each other, and a mystery you assemble yourself. If you enjoyed the layered perspective of Blood Meridian or the moral weight of Beloved, you will find the roots of both here. Give it a slow, undistracted read, keep the genealogy chart handy, and let the second half land. It is widely counted among the great American novels for good reason.
